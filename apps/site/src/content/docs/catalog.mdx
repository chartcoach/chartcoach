Each guideline here is stored as a small record. The writing stays plain, but the structure makes the collection easier to browse, filter, and compare.

A record has a title, a short summary, a set of labels, a few section types, and the references behind it.

## What a guideline includes

Each entry combines:

- a concise **title** and **description** that state the main idea,
- **labels** that make the record easier to narrow down,
- ordered **sections** that keep different kinds of information apart,
- and **references** that show where the guidance comes from.

## Section roles

The sections are meant to be easy to scan. Instead of packing everything into one block of text, each part has a job:

- **advice** — what to do
- **reason** — why it helps
- **context** — where it fits
- **exceptions** — when to be careful or do something else
- **costs** — what you give up by following it
- **mistakes** — common failure modes
- **check** — what to look at when reviewing a chart
- **fix** — ways to respond if something is off

That separation matters because advice often depends on the situation. Two guidelines may sound similar at first, but differ in where they apply or what trade-offs they bring with them.

## References

Inline citations and the references section point back to the source material behind a guideline. That makes it easier to check a claim, compare sources, or keep reading.
