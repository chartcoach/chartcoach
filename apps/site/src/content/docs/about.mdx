Chart Coach is a structured, citable catalog of visualization design guidance.

The goal is to represent design knowledge in a way that is:

- readable by humans,
- usable by machines (retrieval + reasoning),
- context-aware and linkable,
- traceable to sources.

If you're evaluating retrieval strategies (or building tools that give chart feedback), the catalog is meant to be an inspection-friendly ground truth: each guideline has labeled facets, a consistent section structure, and explicit references.

## Design principles

The site is intentionally designed to feel more like an editorial catalog than a generic docs shell.

- **Borrow discipline, not likeness.** The interface should feel calm, coherent, and intentional without imitating any one reference too literally.
- **One signal accent, many neutrals.** Most surfaces stay paper-and-ink quiet so the accent color can do real navigational and semantic work.
- **Metadata should read like editorial apparatus.** Labels, filters, sections, and references are part of the reading experience, not just interface controls.
- **One visual family.** The emblem, homepage icons, and outward-facing marks should look like they were drawn by the same hand.
- **Readable by default.** Display typography establishes hierarchy while body copy stays quiet and legible.

## Accessibility posture

Accessibility is treated as part of the visual system, not as a late check.

- Focus states should always remain visible.
- Light and dark themes should preserve readable contrast.
- Decorative surfaces should stay subtle and low-motion.
- The content hierarchy should remain easy to scan by keyboard, screen reader, and sighted readers alike.

That is also why the site favors restrained decoration over heavy interface chrome: the content should stay primary.

## Brand direction

The current visual direction uses a **catalog-tab** motif: a tabbed card with a small chart window. It is meant to connect the product’s identity with what the product actually does — browsing, organizing, and citing visualization guidance.
