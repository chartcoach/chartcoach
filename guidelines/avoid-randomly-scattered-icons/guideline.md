## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Arrange "at-risk" icons in a contiguous group (e.g., at the bottom or top of the matrix). Do not scatter the icons randomly throughout the grid.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Arrangement fundamentally alters how users process risk magnitude.
*   **The Principle:** **Pattern Recognition vs. Counting.** Grouped displays are easy to assess visually or count. Scattered displays convey the concept of "randomness," but they make it difficult to grasp exactly how large the risk is, leading to poorer choice accuracy.
*   **The Evidence:** Participants viewing static scattered arrays performed significantly worse in identifying the better treatment than those viewing static grouped arrays. Even when animation was used to "settle" scattered icons into a group, it only matched—did not exceed—the performance of the static grouped baseline [@zikmund-fisher_animated_2012].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Assessing the magnitude of a risk or comparing the size of two risks.
*   **Data Type:** Pictographs (icon arrays) showing probability (e.g., 15 out of 100).
*   **Audience:** Patients or users making decisions based on risk probability.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** The specific learning objective is to teach the concept of *randomness* or *uncertainty*, rather than magnitude.
*   **Reason:** Scattering conveys that the outcome is unpredictable, but this comes at the direct expense of understanding the *amount* of risk [@zikmund-fisher_animated_2012].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** You lose the visual metaphor for the stochastic (random) nature of disease occurrence.
*   **The Risk:** Users may perceive the risk as deterministic (e.g., "if I am in the bottom row, I will get the disease") rather than probabilistic.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Using a "shuffling" animation where icons move around to show randomness.
*   **Why it fails:** Shuffling was found to degrade performance and evaluation ratings significantly. It adds confusion, not clarity [@zikmund-fisher_animated_2012].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Do the colored icons appear like "static" or noise across the grid?
*   **The Test:** Can you count the colored icons in under 2 seconds? If not, they are likely too scattered.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Animate the scattered icons "settling" into a group at the bottom. (Note: This is only equal to, not better than, starting with them grouped).
*   **Best Fix:** Re-sort the data so all event icons appear sequentially in a single block.
