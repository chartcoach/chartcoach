## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Use pictograms as recognizable visual cues only when they clearly relate to the data or message.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Pictograms can act as “visual associations” that help people recognize a visualization and are linked to higher-quality recall, without necessarily distracting from understanding.

- **The Principle:** Visual association as a retrieval cue
- **The Evidence:** Objects/HROs were present far more often in the top third most recognizable visualizations than the bottom third, and total fixation time during recognition was high for human recognizable objects; visualizations with pictograms had higher mean description quality than those without across sources (overall 2.01 vs 1.50) [@borkinMemorabilityVisualizationRecognition2016].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Recognize/remember the visualization and its topic
- **Data Type:** Any, especially when many charts share similar templates (low visual distinctiveness)
- **Audience:** General audiences (infographics/news) where quick recognition matters

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The pictogram is decorative and not linked to the chart’s data or message.
- **Reason:** The paper notes that unrelated pictograms can confuse and misdirect attention, potentially harming understanding/recall [@borkinMemorabilityVisualizationRecognition2016].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Space and visual simplicity.
- **The Risk:** Attention spent on pictograms is time not spent on data or message unless the pictogram contributes to them [@borkinMemorabilityVisualizationRecognition2016].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding pictograms to “make it memorable” without integrating them into the meaning.
- **Why it fails:** Viewers may encode the wrong association or misunderstand what the visualization was about [@borkinMemorabilityVisualizationRecognition2016].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The pictogram could be swapped with any other icon without changing the chart’s meaning.
- **The Test:** Ask a viewer what the icon implies about the data; if answers vary widely, the pictogram is not semantically anchored [@borkinMemorabilityVisualizationRecognition2016].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Remove decorative pictograms that don’t correspond to the data/topic.
- **Best Fix:** Use a pictogram that directly encodes or redundantly signals the message (e.g., aligns with the subject/entity being measured) [@borkinMemorabilityVisualizationRecognition2016].
