## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Paginate long tables so the table is shorter than a desktop screen and readers can tell both that more rows exist and that the article continues below.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

A long, unbroken table can make readers miss that there’s content after it; pagination makes the table’s length explicit and keeps the page flow visible, as advised in [@muth_tables_2019].

- **The Principle:** Preserve narrative flow and reveal hidden content
- **The Evidence:** [@muth_tables_2019]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Reading an article without losing the thread, while still accessing full table detail
- **Data Type:** Long tables that would exceed a typical desktop viewport
- **Audience:** General readers in a storytelling context [@muth_tables_2019]

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The table must be scanned as one continuous list without interaction
- **Reason:** Pagination can slow scanning across many rows because not all rows are visible at once [@muth_tables_2019]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Immediate visibility of all rows
- **The Risk:** Readers may not click to later pages and therefore miss rows beyond the first page [@muth_tables_2019]

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Leaving a very tall table unpaginated in the middle of a story
- **Why it fails:** Readers may not notice the story continues below the table [@muth_tables_2019]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The table dominates the page and pushes narrative text far below the fold
- **The Test:** On desktop, scroll: if the table occupies more than one screen and hides the continuation, paginate [@muth_tables_2019]

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Enable pagination to cap visible rows per view [@muth_tables_2019]
- **Best Fix:** Combine pagination with careful sorting so the most important rows appear early (especially if later pages are less likely to be viewed) [@muth_tables_2019]
