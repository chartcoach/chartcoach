## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Do not just show the data points; explicitly tell the reader what the data signifies. Write sentences explaining the trends (e.g., "reaches an annual maximum") directly on the chart near the relevant data points.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
While readers might be able to figure out trends themselves, explicit text ensures they do not miss the point or misinterpret the data. It provides a "whole picture" where no part of the story is missed.
*   **The Principle:** Respecting Reader Time
*   **The Evidence:** [@muth_readers_time_2017] argues that writing out points—even obvious ones like seasonal cycles—saves the reader from having to decipher the chart and ensures they "get it" even if they lack the energy to explore deeply.

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Communicating a specific narrative or finding.
*   **Data Type:** Charts showing trends, cycles, or specific insights (e.g., seasonal fluctuations).
*   **Audience:** Readers who may be "lazy," tired, or unfamiliar with the specific domain.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** Exploratory dashboards or tools intended for discovery.
*   **Reason:** Explicit annotation creates a "lost discovery" effect. As noted in Footnote 1 of [@muth_readers_time_2017], people remember things better when they discover them themselves rather than reading about them.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** The "Lost Learning" effect.
*   **The Risk:** Readers may engage less deeply because the answer is handed to them, potentially reducing retention compared to self-discovery [@muth_readers_time_2017].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Relying solely on the chart title or a paragraph of text below the chart.
*   **Why it fails:** Readers often skip external descriptions. The context must be integrated into the visual field.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Is your chart purely geometric shapes (lines/bars) with no sentences pointing to specific peaks or valleys?
*   **The Test:** If you removed the chart title, would the reader still know the specific insight (e.g., when the peak occurs)?

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Add a subtitle summarizing the main point.
*   **Best Fix:** Add text annotations with pointers directly pointing to the data events (e.g., "Ice cover shrinks to minimum in September") as demonstrated in the Derek Watkins chart analyzed in [@muth_readers_time_2017].
