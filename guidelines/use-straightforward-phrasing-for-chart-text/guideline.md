## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Phrase titles, descriptions, and annotations in the simplest, easiest-to-understand language you can—do not copy official dataset wording if it’s harder to read.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Straightforward phrasing reduces cognitive load and increases comprehension. Translating “official” language into how you’d explain the chart to a friend makes readers faster and less confused.

- **The Principle:** Reduce linguistic complexity to improve comprehension
- **The Evidence:** [@muth_text_in_data_visualizations_2022]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand what the visualization shows and why it matters, quickly
- **Data Type:** Any, especially where dataset variables have bureaucratic names
- **Audience:** Mainstream/general audiences [@muth_text_in_data_visualizations_2022]

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The audience is highly technical and expects formal terminology in prominent text.
  - **Reason:** Simplification may remove precision the audience relies on; still, keep wording as clear as possible within that domain [@muth_text_in_data_visualizations_2022].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** It takes time and iteration to write well.
- **The Risk:** Over-simplifying can accidentally change meaning; you must verify that simpler wording remains accurate [@muth_text_in_data_visualizations_2022].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Copy-pasting a dataset’s formal description into the chart subtitle/notes.
  - **Why it fails:** Readers struggle with jargon and abstractions, delaying understanding [@muth_text_in_data_visualizations_2022].
- **The Wrong Fix:** Keeping complex phrasing because it sounds “more precise.”
  - **Why it fails:** Precision can distract from the message if it’s delivered too early and too prominently [@muth_text_in_data_visualizations_2022].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Titles/descriptions contain long noun phrases or technical terms that aren’t necessary for the first read.
- **The Test:** Pretend you’re describing the chart to a friend in one sentence; if your chart text is more complicated than that, simplify [@muth_text_in_data_visualizations_2022].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Replace abstract dataset terms with concrete phrasing that maps directly to what’s visible (e.g., “The darker…, the more…”).
- **Best Fix:** Rewrite the title as the main takeaway in plain language, and move formal/technical wording to notes or supporting text [@muth_text_in_data_visualizations_2022].
