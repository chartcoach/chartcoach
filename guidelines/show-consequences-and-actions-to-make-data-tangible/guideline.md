## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Show concrete real-life consequences and specific, meaningful actions alongside the data (what happens, where, to whom, and what can change it).

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Abstract metrics become motivating when viewers can map them onto lived experience and plausible choices. Interviews show audiences criticize charts that present risks without guidance, and prefer tangible, local, outcome-focused scenarios and solution-oriented framing that makes “where action could influence the future” visible [@schuster_being_2024]. Editorial practice likewise uses framing to balance urgency with avoidance and to stimulate change by making relevance emotionally legible [@gregory_data_2024]. Even small textual framing—especially titles/captions—can shift a chart from doom to agency by highlighting avoidable outcomes through policy or behavioral action [@koesten_what_2023].

- **The Principle:** Agency and mental simulation (turning numbers into imaginable outcomes plus controllable levers)
- **The Evidence:** [@schuster_being_2024; @koesten_what_2023; @gregory_data_2024]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

Use this when the data is important but feels distant, technical, or emotionally numbing.

- **User Goal:** Understand “what this means for me/us” and what options can change the trajectory.
- **Data Type:** Forecasts, scenarios, risk projections, policy impacts, regional breakdowns, and other abstract indicators (e.g., emissions, warming, infection rates).
- **Audience:** General public, mixed-expertise audiences, or stakeholders who need relevance and next steps.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

Skip or minimize action framing when it would be misleading or inappropriate.

- **Scenario:** You cannot responsibly infer actions from the evidence (high uncertainty, no causal link, or actions are outside the audience’s control).
- **Reason:** “Actionable” guidance becomes advocacy or false certainty and can reduce trust.
- **Scenario:** The purpose is purely descriptive or exploratory for experts (e.g., method validation, internal QA, early-stage analysis).
- **Reason:** Added consequence/action layers can distract from precision and methodological nuance.
- **Scenario:** Safety or ethics concerns (e.g., crisis situations where “actions” could cause harm or panic).
- **Reason:** Over-prescriptive calls to action can be irresponsible.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

Actionable consequence framing trades simplicity and neutrality for relevance and motivation.

- **The Sacrifice:** More space for annotations, captions, and scenario context; slower reading.
- **The Risk:** Oversimplifying causality, implying certainty, politicizing the message, or triggering fatigue if the framing is relentlessly urgent [@gregory_data_2024].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding a generic “What you can do” list that is not tied to the chart’s quantities, location, or timeframe.
- **Why it fails:** Viewers still can’t connect data to outcomes or levers, echoing critiques of non-actionable visuals [@schuster_being_2024].
- **The Wrong Fix:** Using only global or abstract consequences (e.g., “warming is bad”) without local, tangible outcomes.
- **Why it fails:** Reduces personal relevance and makes the data feel distant [@schuster_being_2024].
- **The Wrong Fix:** Relying on dramatic framing without agency (doom-only headlines, catastrophic color schemes).
- **Why it fails:** Can provoke disengagement and fatigue instead of constructive response [@gregory_data_2024].
- **The Wrong Fix:** Hiding the action lever in fine print or a footnote.
- **Why it fails:** Caption/title framing is often what successfully redirects interpretation toward avoidable outcomes [@koesten_what_2023].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The chart shows trends/risks but offers no “so what” beyond alarm; viewers can’t answer “What changes this?” or “What does this affect?”.
- **The Test:** Ask a target viewer to state (1) one concrete consequence, (2) who/where it affects, and (3) one plausible action lever implied by the display. If they can’t do all three within 15 seconds, it’s not tangible/actionable enough [@schuster_being_2024].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a title/caption that explicitly frames agency (e.g., “X outcome is avoidable if Y policy/behavior changes”), and annotate one specific consequence and one lever directly on the graphic [@koesten_what_2023].
- **Best Fix:** Redesign as a consequence-and-levers story: include region-specific impacts, scenario comparisons (e.g., “current path” vs “intervention”), and clear points where action changes the future; balance urgency with a credible path to change to reduce fatigue [@schuster_being_2024; @gregory_data_2024].
