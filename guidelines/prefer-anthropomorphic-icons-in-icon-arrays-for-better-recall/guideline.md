## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Use anthropomorphic icons (restroom person icons, head-and-shoulders outlines, or photos) instead of abstract shapes (blocks/ovals) when your icon array’s goal includes accurate later recall of the risk percentage.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

- **The Principle:** Distinct, human-like forms draw attention and support memory for the numeric message.
- **The Evidence:** In a randomized experiment (n≈1504), recall accuracy differed by icon type; anthropomorphic icons—especially restroom icons and photographs—produced higher accurate recall than blocks (e.g., restroom vs blocks OR≈1.78; photos OR≈1.79) [@zikmund-fisherBlocksOvalsPeople2014].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Remembering a communicated risk estimate (e.g., “your 10-year risk is X%”) after a delay.
- **Data Type:** Single-event probability presented as a part-to-whole icon array (commonly out of 100).
- **Audience:** Mixed-ability audiences, including people with lower numeracy or lower graphical literacy.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** You must avoid any implication that icons are literal individuals (e.g., sensitive contexts where person imagery could be misconstrued).
- **Reason:** Anthropomorphic cues may be undesirable for tone/ethics even if recall improves; the paper shows performance differences but does not assess appropriateness in all contexts [@zikmund-fisherBlocksOvalsPeople2014].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Less neutral/abstract styling; potentially stronger affective reaction.
- **The Risk:** Anthropomorphic icons can introduce unintended associations (e.g., gender cues) that may not match your communication intent [@zikmund-fisherBlocksOvalsPeople2014].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Picking blocks or simple ovals because they are easiest to generate.
- **Why it fails:** Blocks were the lowest-recall condition in this study; convenience can reduce memorability [@zikmund-fisherBlocksOvalsPeople2014].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The array uses generic geometric marks (solid rectangles/ovals) with minimal figure distinctiveness.
- **The Test:** Run a quick delayed recall check (e.g., ask users to report the % later); if accuracy is low, swap to anthropomorphic icons and retest [@zikmund-fisherBlocksOvalsPeople2014].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Replace blocks/ovals with restroom-style person icons.
- **Best Fix:** Use restroom-style icons or head-and-shoulders outlines consistently across the tool, then verify improved recall with your target audience [@zikmund-fisherBlocksOvalsPeople2014].
