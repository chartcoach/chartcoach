## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

If your goal is persuasion, education, engagement, or long-term recall, do not optimize solely for fast and accurate value extraction; allow designs that slow viewers down or differentiate in memory.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

The paper argues that many visualization goals are only tangentially connected to precise decoding. It cites work suggesting benefits of “visual difficulties” for retention, that distinctive/pictorial features can improve memorability, and that “inefficient” designs can encourage contemplation and time spent with the data [@bertiniWhyShouldntAll2020].

- **The Principle:** Higher-level cognitive goals can outweigh perceptual precision
- **The Evidence:** [@bertiniWhyShouldntAll2020]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Remembering a message, being persuaded/motivated, learning, or reflecting.
- **Data Type:** Any, especially narrative or explanatory communication.
- **Audience:** General public, students, stakeholders.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Operational dashboards or contexts where speed/accuracy of extraction is the primary requirement.
- **Reason:** Introducing “difficulty” or ornament can impede rapid, correct lookup [@bertiniWhyShouldntAll2020].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Lower speed and precision for extracting specific values.
- **The Risk:** Viewers may misread details if the design intentionally deemphasizes precise decoding [@bertiniWhyShouldntAll2020].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Making every communication chart look like a plain scatter/dot plot “for objectivity.”
- **Why it fails:** The paper argues that sameness reduces differentiation in memory and ignores goals like engagement and retention [@bertiniWhyShouldntAll2020].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The chart is technically readable but forgettable; viewers cannot recall what they saw shortly after.
- **The Test:** Ask viewers to summarize the message later (not immediately). If recall is poor, a pure-precision design may be misaligned with the goal [@bertiniWhyShouldntAll2020].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add structure that encourages time spent (e.g., richer context or distinctive elements) without changing the underlying data.
- **Best Fix:** Reframe evaluation from “accuracy/time” to the higher-level goal (retention, engagement, motivation) as advocated in the paper [@bertiniWhyShouldntAll2020].
