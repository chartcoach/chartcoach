## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Explicitly cite data sources, list references, and describe methodological choices or limitations directly within the visualization layout.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
According to specific observations of narrative visualizations, including provenance information acts as a rhetorical strategy to signal transparency and trustworthiness. By revealing the source, the designer signals "objectivity" and professional integrity, similar to journalistic codes of ethics. This "provenance rhetoric" strengthens the credibility of the presentation by respecting the audience's need for verification [@hullman_visualization_2011].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** When the user needs to trust the accuracy of a narrative or news visualization.
*   **Data Type:** Any data derived from external studies, census reports, or complex statistical models.
*   **Audience:** Skeptical or general audiences who may not inherently trust the visualization designer.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** Artistic or strictly aesthetic visualizations.
*   **Reason:** If the goal is purely evocative or abstract art rather than information communication, strict provenance might distract from the aesthetic experience.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** Visual clutter and screen real estate.
*   **The Risk:** Over-explaining methodology might overwhelm a lay user or make the graphic seem overly academic.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Hiding sources in a separate "About" page or footer link.
*   **Why it fails:** It removes the rhetorical signal of authority from the immediate visual context of the data [@hullman_visualization_2011].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Look at the graphic in isolation (e.g., as a screenshot).
*   **The Test:** Can you identify where the data came from and who processed it without leaving the image?

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Add a small "Source: [Name]" footer to the chart area.
*   **Best Fix:** Add a brief annotation describing the methodology (e.g., "Based on a survey of...") and specific data citations near the title or legend.
