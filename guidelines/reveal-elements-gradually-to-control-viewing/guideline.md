## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Reveal labels, symbols, and objects in a planned sequence rather than presenting everything at once.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

State changes (newly revealed items) attract attention and help establish viewing order; when multiple elements were revealed together at the start, fixation order was less structured [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997].

- **The Principle:** Attentional capture by onset/state change
- **The Evidence:** [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand a multi-step explanation without missing prerequisites.
- **Data Type:** Animated instructional sequences with multiple on-screen components.
- **Audience:** Broad; especially helpful for low domain knowledge viewers.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Users need an overview map of all components before the sequence begins.
- **Reason:** Simultaneous display can support orientation, but you still risk unclear attention order.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More authoring effort and timing work.
- **The Risk:** Over-fragmentation can make the presentation feel slow or disjointed.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Revealing many items at the same timestamp.
- **Why it fails:** Users’ attention becomes unordered and important items may be skipped [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Viewers miss foundational elements (e.g., initial condition) in recall.
- **The Test:** Ask viewers to describe “what was shown first”; inconsistent answers suggest the reveal order isn’t controlling attention.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Stagger reveals by even a short delay rather than simultaneous onset.
- **Best Fix:** Design a narrative reveal script: one new element at a time, aligned to speech cues and followed by stable viewing time [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997].
