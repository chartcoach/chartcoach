## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Round risk values and avoid decimals whenever possible.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Whole numbers are easier to mentally represent and compare than fractional values, supporting faster comprehension of magnitudes.

- **The Principle:** Preference for whole-object representation in understanding quantities
- **The Evidence:** [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Quickly understand “how big is the risk?”
- **Data Type:** Percentages or frequencies used in patient/public-facing materials
- **Audience:** General public, including low-numeracy audiences

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Small differences are clinically meaningful and rounding would hide them.
- **Reason:** Rounding can collapse distinct risks into the same displayed value. [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Loss of numeric precision.
- **The Risk:** Users may overgeneralize (“these are the same”) when values were different.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Keeping multiple decimal places “for accuracy” (e.g., 29.6%).
- **Why it fails:** Adds complexity without necessarily improving understanding. [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Probabilities include decimals in patient-facing figures.
- **The Test:** Ask “Would rounding change the decision or the recommended action?”

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Round to the nearest whole percent or whole count out of N.
- **Best Fix:** Present rounded values plus a brief note that the number is an estimate (if needed), rather than adding decimals. [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]
