## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When you report risk reduction (absolute or relative), also state the baseline risk and the resulting risk.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Without baseline context, people misjudge effect size; baseline risk anchors interpretation and improves accuracy when assessing changes.

- **The Principle:** Context anchoring for risk interpretation
- **The Evidence:** [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Judging benefit/harm magnitude and making treatment or screening choices
- **Data Type:** Probabilities, percentages, frequency statements, risk reductions
- **Audience:** Patients and lay decision-makers, especially those with weaker probability reasoning [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Baseline risk is unknown or highly variable for the intended audience.
- **Reason:** A single baseline could mislead; you may need tailored baselines or ranges instead. [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More text/space and an extra number (or two) to present
- **The Risk:** If baseline is poorly chosen, users may generalize it to themselves incorrectly [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Reporting only “X% reduction” (relative) with no baseline.
- **Why it fails:** Relative-only descriptions inflate perceived magnitude compared with the same change described with absolute context. [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users react as if small absolute changes are huge because only relative change was shown.
- **The Test:** Ask users to restate “what happens out of 100 people like me” after reading; failure suggests missing baseline context. [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add “baseline: A%; with treatment: B%.”
- **Best Fix:** Present baseline and outcome risk in a single integrated display (numbers and/or a contextual graphic showing part-to-whole). [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]
