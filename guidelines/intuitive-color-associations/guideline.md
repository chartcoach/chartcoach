## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Choose colors that align with the cultural or natural meaning of the data for your specific target audience. However, avoid stereotypical combinations for gender that might confuse or offend.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Using learned associations (e.g., Red=Attention/Stop, Green=Forest) allows the reader to process the chart faster because the meaning is "intuitive" [@muth_colors_2018]. Failing to use these (or reversing them) creates cognitive interference (e.g., using Blue for Republicans and Red for Democrats in a US context would confuse readers).

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Immediate recognition of categories.
*   **Data Type:** Data with strong real-world or cultural links (Politics, Nature, Sentiment).
*   **Audience:** A specific cultural group (associations vary by culture).

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** Gender Data.
*   **Reason:** @muth_colors_2018 advises avoiding the stereotypical pink-blue combination. Instead, use a cold color for men (blue/purple) and a warmer color for women (yellow/orange/warm green) to differentiate without reinforcing stereotypes.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** You cannot simply use your corporate brand colors if they contradict the data's intuitive colors.
*   **The Risk:** Cultural associations are not universal (e.g., Red is "lucky" in China but "danger" in the West). You must know your audience.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Using random colors for political parties.
*   **Why it fails:** Readers have to constantly check the legend because the colors don't match their mental model.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** A map of forests colored red.
*   **The Test:** Show the chart without a legend. Can the user guess which category is which based on color alone?

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Swap the palette to match natural/learned tones (e.g., Water = Blue).
*   **Best Fix:** Research the specific cultural color meanings of your target audience before assigning the palette.
