## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Do not present a complex, continuous stream of information. Break the content into bite-size segments and require the user to click "Continue" to proceed to the next step.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
When both visual and verbal channels are overloaded by essential processing demands (Type 2 Overload), the user may not have enough time to organize and integrate the information before new information arrives.
*   **The Mechanism:** Segmenting allows the user to "digest" a portion of the mental model. Between segments, the user completes the cognitive processes of organizing and integrating without the pressure of incoming data [@mayer_nine_2003].
*   **The Evidence:** This is the *Segmentation Effect*. In tests involving scientific explanations, segmented presentations yielded a median effect size of 1.36 over continuous presentations [@mayer_nine_2003].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Learning a multi-step process or a complex causal chain.
*   **Data Type:** High-intrinsic load material (conceptually complex data) presented via narrated animation or video.
*   **Audience:** Novices who lack the prior knowledge to quickly chunk the information themselves.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** The content is simple or slow-paced.
*   **Reason:** Frequent interruptions can frustrate users if the cognitive load is low enough to process the stream continuously.
*   **Scenario:** Assessing fluency or speed.
*   **Reason:** If the goal is to test reaction time or real-time monitoring, segmentation breaks the fidelity of the simulation.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** The presentation takes longer to consume due to pauses and interaction time.
*   **The Risk:** Breaking the flow at illogical points can disrupt the narrative or causal links.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Providing a generic "Pause" button.
*   **Why it fails:** Novice users often do not know *when* they need to pause to process information. Built-in breaks (hard stops) are more effective for learning than user-initiated pauses.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** A timeline or video bar that runs uninterrupted for several minutes on a complex topic.
*   **The Test:** Watch the content. If you feel like you are "falling behind" or missing details because the next sentence started too soon, segmenting is needed.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Insert "Click to Continue" buttons after every 1–2 sentences or distinct visual steps.
*   **Best Fix:** Structure the content logically so that each segment represents a complete sub-component of the system (e.g., "The intake stroke" of an engine).
