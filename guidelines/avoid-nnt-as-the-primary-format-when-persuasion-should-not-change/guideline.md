## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Do not choose between ARR and NNT expecting one to be more persuasive; pick the format based on understanding and clarity needs.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Across many comparisons, ARR and NNT produced little or no difference in persuasiveness, so persuasion is not a reliable differentiator between these two formats.

- **The Principle:** Persuasiveness equivalence (ARR vs NNT)
- **The Evidence:** ARR vs NNT showed little/no difference in persuasiveness (SMD 0.05, 95% CI -0.04 to 0.15) in [@aklUsingAlternativeStatistical2011].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Decide whether to adopt an intervention (hypothetical choice/intention).
- **Data Type:** Intervention effects where you are deciding whether to present ARR or NNT.
- **Audience:** Consumers and professionals (subgroups showed little difference in [@aklUsingAlternativeStatistical2011]).

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** You are comparing RRR to absolute formats (ARR or NNT).
- **Reason:** The review found RRR is more persuasive than both ARR and NNT; this rule is only about ARR vs NNT [@aklUsingAlternativeStatistical2011].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** You lose a simple “pick the more convincing number” heuristic.
- **The Risk:** Over-focusing on persuasion may distract from selecting the format that improves understanding.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Switching from ARR to NNT (or vice versa) to “nudge” uptake.
- **Why it fails:** [@aklUsingAlternativeStatistical2011] indicates minimal persuasiveness difference between ARR and NNT on average.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The design rationale says “use NNT because it persuades more” (or “use ARR because it persuades more”) without evidence.
- **The Test:** Verify whether the goal is persuasion; if yes, note that ARR vs NNT is unlikely to move it per [@aklUsingAlternativeStatistical2011].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** If you already use one, add the other only if it improves comprehension for your audience.
- **Best Fix:** Prefer event rates plus ARR as a primary explanation of effect, and add NNT as a secondary translation if needed, aligning selection with the outcome patterns in [@aklUsingAlternativeStatistical2011].
