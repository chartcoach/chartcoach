## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Use more saturated (visually stronger) colors for important parties in the story, and keep less important parties more muted—especially when a party becomes newly significant and should no longer look “minor.” [@muth_partycolors_2018]

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Color intensity creates visual hierarchy: more saturated colors attract attention and feel heavier, helping readers notice key parties first. The post illustrates how newsrooms increased saturation for AfD as it became a major parliamentary force, because a previously desaturated treatment no longer matched its importance. [@muth_partycolors_2018]

- **The Principle:** Visual salience through saturation
- **The Evidence:** [@muth_partycolors_2018]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Identify major parties quickly in overviews, trackers, and summary charts/maps.
- **Data Type:** Election results where some parties are dominant and others are marginal (or where one party’s relevance changes over time).
- **Audience:** General readers scanning election coverage. [@muth_partycolors_2018]

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Every party shown is equally central to the analysis (e.g., a focused local chart where small parties matter as much as big ones).
- **Reason:** Emphasis-by-saturation would bias attention away from categories that the reader should treat as equally important. [@muth_partycolors_2018]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** A strictly “neutral” look; you are making an editorial emphasis choice. [@muth_partycolors_2018]
- **The Risk:** Over-emphasizing a party can be interpreted as giving it undue prominence; under-emphasizing can hide relevant information. [@muth_partycolors_2018]

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Keeping an emerging major party in a pale/desaturated color because that’s what you used when it was small.
  - **Why it fails:** The visual weight no longer matches real-world relevance in the story. [@muth_partycolors_2018]
- **The Wrong Fix:** Making many parties equally saturated in a crowded palette.
  - **Why it fails:** Everything competes for attention, reducing hierarchy and scanability. [@muth_partycolors_2018]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** A major party doesn’t stand out from “other” parties, or a minor party grabs disproportionate attention due to vivid color. [@muth_partycolors_2018]
- **The Test:** Step back or squint: the parties you consider most important should be the ones your eye lands on first. [@muth_partycolors_2018]

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Increase saturation (and/or darken slightly) for the party that should be visually prominent; reduce saturation for less important parties. [@muth_partycolors_2018]
- **Best Fix:** Define a hierarchy system in your palette (e.g., “major parties = stronger, minor/other = muted”) and update it when party importance changes between elections, while keeping party identity colors recognizable. [@muth_partycolors_2018]
