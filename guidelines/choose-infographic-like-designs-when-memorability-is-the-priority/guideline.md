## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When the main goal is memorability, adopt infographic-style design choices rather than a uniform report-style template.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

In the studied corpus, infographic-source visualizations were more memorable overall; the authors suggest these venues may use design features (e.g., bold, pictorial, varied aesthetics) that improve distinctiveness and memory.

- **The Principle:** Style-driven distinctiveness in real-world distributions
- **The Evidence:** Infographics had the highest memorability scores among the four source categories, and this remained true even after removing pictorial visualizations [@borkinWhatMakesVisualization2013a].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Make the visualization itself stick in memory (e.g., outreach, public communication)
- **Data Type:** Any presented in a competitive attention environment
- **Audience:** Broad public audiences

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** You require strict uniform aesthetics (e.g., standardized reporting).
- **Reason:** The paper notes that uniform publication styles may reduce uniqueness; if brand consistency is the priority, you may accept lower memorability [@borkinWhatMakesVisualization2013a].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Template uniformity and potentially minimalist aesthetics.
- **The Risk:** Higher memorability as measured does not guarantee better comprehension of the underlying data [@borkinWhatMakesVisualization2013a].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Assuming “infographic-like” means only adding decoration.
- **Why it fails:** The study’s results connect memorability to multiple factors (pictorial cues, color, density, distinctiveness), not a single decorative layer [@borkinWhatMakesVisualization2013a].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The chart looks like a standard report graphic with subdued, uniform styling.
- **The Test:** Compare against an infographic-style alternative; if the standard version is visually interchangeable with many others, it likely underuses the attributes correlated with memorability in the paper [@borkinWhatMakesVisualization2013a].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Increase distinctiveness via richer color and more structured/annotated composition.
- **Best Fix:** Redesign with an infographic-like composition that introduces distinctive structure and (when relevant) pictorial cues while keeping the data message intact [@borkinWhatMakesVisualization2013a].
