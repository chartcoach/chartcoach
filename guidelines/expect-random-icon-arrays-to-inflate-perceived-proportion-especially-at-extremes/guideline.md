## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Assume random (scattered) icon arrays will produce upward-biased first impressions—avoid them when overestimation would be harmful.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Under a 10-second deadline, random arrangements produced larger mean overestimates than sequential ones for most proportions tested, and relative inaccuracy for random arrays showed a pronounced U-shape—higher at low and high percentages (e.g., 6% and 70%) [@anckerEffectArrangementStick2011]. This implies random scattering can make small or large risks look larger than they are at first glance.

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Form an initial impression of magnitude (how big is the risk?).
- **Data Type:** Icon arrays showing uncommon events (single-digit %) or common events (≥60–70%).
- **Audience:** Patients/consumers making quick judgments.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The goal is to intentionally create a conservative (higher) first impression.
- **Reason:** If you want to err on the side of higher perceived magnitude, the bias may align with the communication goal, though it remains a distortion [@anckerEffectArrangementStick2011].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Using sequential layouts may reduce the immediate salience that random scattering can create.
- **The Risk:** If you use random layouts, you risk systematically inflating perceived magnitude, potentially steering choices based on misperception [@anckerEffectArrangementStick2011].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Assuming “random vs sequential is cosmetic” and won’t change perceived magnitude.
- **Why it fails:** The same proportion was often judged differently depending on arrangement; within-person differences were significant for 6% and 29% [@anckerEffectArrangementStick2011].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Viewers’ quick estimates cluster above the true percentage, especially for very low or high depicted values.
- **The Test:** Show the graphic briefly and collect estimates; compare mean signed error for random vs sequential—random should not show materially higher overestimation if unbiased perception is required [@anckerEffectArrangementStick2011].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Convert scattered affected icons into a contiguous block.
- **Best Fix:** Use sequential layouts for extreme probabilities where the paper found the strongest relative inaccuracies with random layouts [@anckerEffectArrangementStick2011].
