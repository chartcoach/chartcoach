## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Reserve 3-D graphs primarily for situations where you are showing the data to others, not for private use.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Participants systematically shifted toward selecting 3-D graphs (volume/surface) when the scenario changed from self-use to presenting to others.

- **The Principle:** Use depth cues as a communicative/attention device
- **The Evidence:** In Experiment 2, 3-D volume and surface graphs were chosen more for “other” than “self”; Experiment 3 found no strong overall preference difference between 2-D and 3-D for “other,” indicating 3-D becomes comparatively more acceptable in presentation contexts [@levyGratuitousGraphicsPutting1996].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Communicate findings to a group
- **Data Type:** 2-D quantitative data where 3-D is optional
- **Audience:** Viewers who may benefit from a more salient display

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The audience needs an immediate, correct reading and is sensitive to confusion from extra cues
- **Reason:** 2-D was preferred for immediate-decision scenarios [@levyGratuitousGraphicsPutting1996].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Potential simplicity and straightforward reading
- **The Risk:** Added dimensionality may be seen as decorative rather than informational, and could distract from the message

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding 3-D for every chart in a deck regardless of purpose
- **Why it fails:** Preferences in the study were conditional on scenario; 3-D was not uniformly preferred [@levyGratuitousGraphicsPutting1996].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** 3-D appears even in internal-only analytic views
- **The Test:** Label each chart “for self” vs “for others”; if “for self,” verify that 3-D is actually necessary.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Replace 3-D versions in analytic views with 2-D equivalents.
- **Best Fix:** Use 3-D sparingly and only in presentation slides where memorability or salience is explicitly a goal [@levyGratuitousGraphicsPutting1996].
