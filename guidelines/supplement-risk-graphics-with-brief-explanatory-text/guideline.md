## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Whenever you present risk or probability graphics to patients, add brief explanatory text that states the intended takeaway.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Users often prefer and better understand graphics when supported by explanatory text, which helps them form correct conclusions rather than guessing.

- **The Principle:** Guided interpretation of unfamiliar quantitative graphics
- **The Evidence:** [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Interpreting risk graphs (e.g., survival curves, bar charts) correctly
- **Data Type:** Risk/probability visuals used in patient education or decision aids
- **Audience:** Lay users, including those with limited graphical literacy [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The graphic is used in an expert-only environment where conventions are shared and text would clutter critical monitoring.
- **Reason:** Experts may not need interpretive scaffolding, and space/time can be constrained. [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More space and potential visual clutter
- **The Risk:** Poorly written text can bias interpretation or oversimplify uncertainty [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding more numbers or a longer caption instead of a clear interpretive sentence.
- **Why it fails:** The problem is not only missing detail; it’s difficulty mapping the visual to meaning. [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users can point to the chart but can’t state what it means for their decision.
- **The Test:** Ask users to explain the chart in one sentence; if they can’t, the graphic needs interpretive text. [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add one takeaway sentence (e.g., “With treatment, about X out of 100 avoid Y.”).
- **Best Fix:** Integrate text callouts tied to the exact marks/regions in the graphic that support the inference. [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]
