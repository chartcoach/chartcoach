## Use multi-messaging between text and graphics <!-- role: advice -->

Coordinate text (prompts, captions, or panel copy) with corresponding graphical updates and annotations so each channel contributes different information about the same narrative point. Keep the text and the visual emphasis synchronized across steps.

## Aligned redundancy increases clarity without overloading a single channel <!-- role: reason -->

Data stories can be hard to follow if the narrative is only in prose or only in the chart. Multi-messaging makes the message more robust: the text can state the claim while the graphic shows the evidence and the highlighted regions.

**Mechanism:** Parallel, aligned channels reduce inference burden and help viewers connect explanations to specific visual evidence.

**Evidence:** An interactive slideshow case study demonstrates narrative clarity through coordinated text and chart annotations that provide related but different information (multi-messaging), reinforced by animated updates and details-on-demand [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010].

**Notes:** Multi-messaging is distinct from repeating the same sentence; it is redundancy with complementary detail.

## Where multi-messaging is most effective <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand key observations and how they arise from the data.
- **Task:** Interpret trends, exceptions, or causal explanations grounded in a chart.
- **Data:** Time series or other data where narrative claims depend on specific segments.
- **Chart Setting:** Slideshows, annotated charts, or longform narratives with accompanying text panels.
- **Audience:** General readers who need claims connected to visible evidence.
- **Success Criterion:** Readers can restate the claim and point to the visual evidence that supports it.

## When multi-messaging can be counterproductive <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** Space constraints force text to obscure the crucial data marks. **Why:** The added channel blocks the evidence it is meant to clarify.

## Costs of multi-messaging <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Space and production time for writing, annotation, and synchronization. **Risk:** Misalignment between text and visual emphasis undermines trust and comprehension. **Mitigation:** Keep a tight coupling between each text statement and the highlighted visual region.

## Common multi-messaging mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **Mistake:** Writing generic text that does not reference any visible feature in the chart. **Why it fails:** Viewers cannot connect explanation to evidence.
- **Mistake:** Changing the chart without updating the corresponding text. **Why it fails:** The narrative becomes inconsistent across channels.

## Checks for alignment between channels <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Readers read the text but do not look at the relevant part of the chart, or vice versa. **Quick Check:** For every narrative sentence, identify the exact mark, region, or annotation that it maps to. **Stronger Test:** Have users paraphrase the narrative and indicate the supporting visual evidence.

## Fixes when text and chart feel disconnected <!-- role: fix -->

- Add annotations tied to the exact features referenced in the text.
- Update the graphic and the text together in each step so they remain synchronized.
- Move long explanations into a separate panel so the chart remains readable.
- Reduce text to memorable fact-like statements when paragraphs become terminology-heavy.
