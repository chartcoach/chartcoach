## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Create "harmonious grays" by starting with pure gray and overlaying it with your main palette color.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Pure neutral grays can feel disconnected from a vibrant color palette. Tinting them creates a cohesive visual system.
*   **The Principle:** Color Harmony.
*   **The Evidence:** [@muth_colorguide_2018] cites a *Smashing Magazine* introduction suggesting "creating harmonious grays" by overlaying the main color on pure grays to make the palette feel more harmonious.

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Building a custom color palette from scratch.
*   **Task:** Selecting background, axis, or context colors that sit behind the data.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** Strict corporate branding guidelines.
*   **Reason:** You may be required to use specific neutral hex codes.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** The gray will no longer be truly neutral, which might subtly influence perception of other colors.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Using default #CCCCCC or #808080 alongside a warm or cool palette.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **The Test:** Look at the palette as a whole. Does the gray look "dead" compared to the active colors?

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Add a small percentage of transparency of your primary color on top of your gray in your design software.
