## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When the goal is accurate risk judgment, show the risk as a part-to-whole relationship (numerator in context of denominator), not as a standalone numerator.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Displays showing only the numerator emphasize the risk and can drive avoidance behavior, while part-to-whole context improves accuracy of judgment.

- **The Principle:** Foreground/background salience in risk graphics
- **The Evidence:** [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Accurately understanding probability magnitude (not merely motivating behavior)
- **Data Type:** Probabilities/percent risks
- **Audience:** Patients/consumers making decisions with risk information [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The primary goal is to motivate risk-avoidance behavior change rather than precise judgment.
- **Reason:** Numerator-only displays can be more persuasive by emphasizing the risk. [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Slightly more visual complexity (must depict denominator/background)
- **The Risk:** Users may find part-to-whole graphics less visually “simple” or less familiar [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Using a bar height for “10%” with no 0–100% context.
- **Why it fails:** It pushes attention to the numerator and can distort magnitude judgments. [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The graphic shows only the “at risk” group with no obvious “out of” frame.
- **The Test:** Ask “Out of how many?” If the display doesn’t answer immediately, it’s not part-to-whole. [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add an explicit 0–100% frame or denominator reference directly in the graphic.
- **Best Fix:** Redesign as a display that visually encodes numerator and denominator together (so the risk reads as a fraction of the whole). [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]
