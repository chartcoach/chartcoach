## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Pick one of the paper’s narrative visualization genres (magazine style, annotated chart, partitioned poster, flow chart, comic strip, slide show, or video) and design deliberately within its constraints; combine genres only with a clear purpose.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

A genre determines the number of frames (scenes) and how viewers are ordered through them; that ordering is the backbone of narrative flow. Explicit genre choices reduce ambiguity about how a reader should traverse the content and how much control you, versus the reader, should have.

- **The Principle:** Narrative flow emerges from frame structure and ordering conventions.
- **The Evidence:** [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand a data-backed story (not just explore freely).
- **Data Type:** Any, especially when you must communicate a sequence of observations.
- **Audience:** General/public audiences, or mixed-expertise audiences consuming journalism/education content.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** You are building an analysis tool for open-ended diagnostics and hypothesis generation.
- **Reason:** The paper characterizes these as strongly reader-driven experiences where genre-like ordering is not the priority [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Less freedom to “just add” interactions or panels ad hoc.
- **The Risk:** Over-constraining the reader if a more reader-driven structure is needed.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Mixing slide-show pacing, poster-like multipanel layouts, and drill-down interactions without clarifying traversal.
- **Why it fails:** Viewers lose orientation because the narrative order becomes unclear [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** A viewer can’t tell where to start, what comes next, or what “unit” constitutes a step in the story.
- **The Test:** Ask someone to point to the “start” and the “next step” without instruction; hesitation indicates genre/ordering ambiguity [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add an establishing entry state and a visible ordering cue (e.g., progress indicator or clear pathing).
- **Best Fix:** Rebuild the piece around one primary genre and only then layer interaction/messaging appropriate to that genre [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010].
