## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When summarizing uncertainty for decisions, report a credible interval that reflects not only observed variability but also threats to internal validity, external validity, and the strength of the underlying science.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Confidence intervals reflect sampling variability under statistical assumptions; decision makers also face uncertainty from study flaws, generalizability limits, and weak scientific foundations. Credible intervals are meant to integrate those sources into the uncertainty users actually need [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Knowing how far to trust predicted outcomes (risk/benefit, effect sizes).
- **Data Type:** Quantitative estimates used for decisions (e.g., annual probability of an outcome).
- **Audience:** Decision makers who must act under residual uncertainty.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Threats to validity and pedigree are negligible and well-justified.
- **Reason:** In rare cases, credible and confidence intervals may be effectively the same because additional uncertainty sources are minimal [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Requires expert judgment and can be uncomfortable to state explicitly.
- **The Risk:** If organizations do not protect transparent reporting, intervals may be used in unfair performance evaluations [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Publishing only statistical intervals while implying that they reflect “all uncertainty.”
- **Why it fails:** It understates uncertainty when biases and generalizability limits exist, encouraging overconfidence or backlash when surprises occur [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The interval looks narrow despite known design limitations or weak measures.
- **The Test:** Ask whether the interval changes when you account for study conduct, population differences, and foundational science strength; if not considered, it’s not a credible interval [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a second interval labeled as incorporating validity/pedigree judgments, alongside the confidence interval.
- **Best Fix:** Follow a structured protocol (identify outcome, summarize variability, internal validity, external validity, science strength) and then state the resulting credible interval explicitly [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].
