## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When you give a yes/no call to action, explicitly communicate (a) uncertainty in evidence (how well experts discriminate states) and (b) the decision rule’s caution or tradeoffs (the action threshold).

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Categorical advice always reflects both discrimination ability and a value-laden threshold. Without seeing both, recipients misattribute false alarms or misses to “bad science” or “bad motives,” undermining trust and decision quality [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Deciding whether to act (evacuate, undergo treatment, sell/buy).
- **Data Type:** Binary/categorical recommendations produced from uncertain signals.
- **Audience:** People who must follow or evaluate expert calls to action.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The message is purely descriptive and does not recommend action.
- **Reason:** If you are not triggering a threshold decision, you may not need to disclose the implied decision rule (but you still should communicate relevant uncertainty) [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More message space and cognitive load than a simple directive.
- **The Risk:** Revealing thresholds can trigger disagreement about values and accountability [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Presenting the recommendation as if it followed “directly” from the science.
- **Why it fails:** It hides the threshold tradeoff, so audiences cannot interpret apparent errors (unnecessary action vs missed danger) [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** After an outcome, people argue only about whether experts “were right,” not about the acceptable tradeoff.
- **The Test:** Ask users to explain why the recommendation could reasonably be made even if the adverse event does not occur; if they cannot, the threshold logic is missing [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a sentence stating the tradeoff (e.g., “We prefer false alarms over missed events”) alongside the evidence strength.
- **Best Fix:** Publish or reference an explicit decision rule so recipients know what β embodies, leaving uncertainty focused on knowledge (d′) [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].
