## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Before concluding that the graphing calculator changed problem solving, check whether strategy differences are better explained by (1) the task’s knowledge demands and (2) students’ access to that knowledge.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

In Mesa’s study, major differences appeared across problems (not across tool conditions): students’ strategies and success aligned with what knowledge was “at stake” and whether they had it available (e.g., linking roots ↔ factored form for polynomials), while tool presence produced no major overall strategic shift [@mesaSolvingProblemsFunctions2008].

- **The Principle:** Tool affordances are mediated by domain knowledge and task structure.
- **The Evidence:** [@mesaSolvingProblemsFunctions2008]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Interpret observed behaviors (exploration, verification, time use) in tech vs. no-tech settings.
- **Data Type:** Protocols, videos, think-alouds, or classroom observations of function tasks.
- **Audience:** Researchers analyzing technology use; instructors interpreting student work.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The tool introduces a capability that is impossible otherwise (e.g., a representation or computation students cannot feasibly produce by hand within the constraints).
- **Reason:** In that case, the tool may be the dominant causal factor regardless of prior knowledge.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More analysis work—requires specifying “knowledge at stake” and coding evidence of availability.
- **The Risk:** Underestimation of subtle tool-mediated shifts if you stop at knowledge explanations.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Attribute increased exploration or different representations directly to the calculator.
- **Why it fails:** Mesa observed exploration explode mainly when students lacked key mathematical principles; the calculator mainly increased the *rate* of trials, not conceptual progress [@mesaSolvingProblemsFunctions2008].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Students with and without calculators choose similar high-level approaches, but succeed/fail based on whether they invoke key concepts.
- **The Test:** Identify the minimal conceptual moves required by the task (e.g., inequality ↔ graph regions; roots ↔ factors). Then mark whether each pair demonstrates these moves; if outcomes track these moves more than tool access, the knowledge explanation dominates [@mesaSolvingProblemsFunctions2008].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add an analysis layer that codes “knowledge invoked/available” alongside “tool actions.”
- **Best Fix:** Redesign the study to include targeted prompts/interventions that surface the needed knowledge and then re-examine whether the tool adds incremental benefit beyond that knowledge [@mesaSolvingProblemsFunctions2008].
