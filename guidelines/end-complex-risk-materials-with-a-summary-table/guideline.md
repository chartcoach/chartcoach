## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When a treatment has many risks and benefits, end with a summary table listing the key attributes and their statistics.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Multi-page or lengthy explanations are hard to remember; summary tables reduce cognitive burden and support comparison across options, and were associated with increased interest in seeking more information and discussing treatment with clinicians.

- **The Principle:** Externalize memory and enable side-by-side comparison
- **The Evidence:** Adding a summary table increased interest in further information/doctor discussion and endorsement behaviors in the cited chemoprevention study [@fagerlinHelpingPatientsDecide2011].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Compare multiple options with multiple attributes (harms and benefits).
- **Data Type:** Multi-attribute decision sets (e.g., several risks + several benefits).
- **Audience:** Patients reviewing written materials or decision aids, or after a long conversation.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** None stated in the paper.
- **Reason:** Not specified.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Requires careful selection and formatting of attributes; takes page/screen space.
- **The Risk:** If overloaded, the table can recreate the “too much information” problem.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Distributing risks/benefits across multiple pages with no final consolidation.
- **Why it fails:** Patients struggle to remember and compare dispersed information [@fagerlinHelpingPatientsDecide2011].
- **The Wrong Fix:** A summary that lists attributes but omits the actual risk/benefit statistics.
- **Why it fails:** It doesn’t reduce the cognitive burden of comparing quantitative trade-offs [@fagerlinHelpingPatientsDecide2011].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users must flip pages or scroll back and forth to compare options.
- **The Test:** Ask, “Can someone compare options without revisiting earlier sections?” If not, add a summary table.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a compact end-of-document table with each option as a column and each key risk/benefit as a row.
- **Best Fix:** Combine a summary table with the paper’s other supported practices (plain language, absolute risks, consistent denominators) [@fagerlinHelpingPatientsDecide2011].
