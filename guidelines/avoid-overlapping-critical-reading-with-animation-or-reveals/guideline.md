## Separate label reading time from competing animation and reveals <!-- role: advice -->

Allow dedicated time for reading after a label or caption is cued, and avoid starting new motion or reveals during that reading interval.

## Visual competition can prevent integration of labels with the main visual message <!-- role: reason -->

When labels appear and motion starts at the same time, attention is pulled toward the dynamic element, which can prevent fixations on newly revealed text and reduce identification and comprehension.

**Mechanism:** Reading requires stable fixations, while motion onset induces attentional capture and tracking; the two demands conflict, so one stream is dropped or delayed.

**Evidence:** Eye tracking showed that motion onset could shift attention away before a revealed label was fixated, and some viewers did not follow a fast-moving animated element while they were reading a label; in recall testing, propositions tied to poorly attended or uncued elements were missed [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997].

**Notes:** This is especially important when labels are long or conceptually dense.

## When reading/animation separation matters most <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Correctly identify objects and understand their role in a sequence.
- **Task:** Map labels/captions to visual entities; follow a narrated explanation.
- **Data:** Content with named entities (objects) and key actions.
- **Chart Setting:** Mixed speech + labels/captions + animation in a short time window.
- **Audience:** Low domain-knowledge viewers or any audience encountering unfamiliar terminology.
- **Success Criterion:** Viewers can correctly name and describe key steps immediately after viewing.

## When temporal separation is less necessary <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** The label is trivial (single familiar word) and the motion is the primary message. **Why:** The comprehension benefit comes from tracking the action rather than reading.

## Tradeoffs of reserving reading time <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** The presentation may become longer or feel slower. **Risk:** Over-pausing can reduce pacing and engagement. **Mitigation:** Reserve reading time only for terms and propositions that are important or unfamiliar.

## Common mistakes that reintroduce attentional clashes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **Mistake:** Revealing multiple labels and starting motion at the same moment. **Why it fails:** Viewers cannot establish a consistent viewing order and may miss key text.
- **Mistake:** Using fast transient animations to “point” to something while text appears. **Why it fails:** The animation can steal the fixation needed to read.

## Quick checks for reading/animation clashes <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Users report “I saw it, but I couldn’t read it,” or omit named concepts in free recall. **Quick Check:** Pause at each label and see whether it can be read comfortably before the next change occurs. **Stronger Test:** Ask users to write down all named entities and compare omissions to moments where motion overlapped label appearance.

## What to do instead when timing is tight <!-- role: fix -->

- Add a short pause after label/caption onset before initiating motion.
- Reveal labels first, then animate the corresponding object/action.
- Replace long labels with a shorter label plus a later caption segment.
- Split the sequence into more frames so only one reading demand occurs at a time.
