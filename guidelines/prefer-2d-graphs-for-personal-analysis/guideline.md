## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When a graph is for your own use (analysis/exploration), prefer 2-D simple or area graphs over 3-D graphs.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Participants consistently judged 2-D graphs as more appropriate for self-directed understanding, while reserving 3-D more for showing others.

- **The Principle:** Match dimensionality to intended social function (self vs. presentation)
- **The Evidence:** In Experiment 2, simple/area graphs were chosen more for “self,” and 3-D volume/surface graphs more for “others”; Experiment 3 shows the same self/other interaction pattern [@levyGratuitousGraphicsPutting1996].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Personally understand what’s going on in the data
- **Data Type:** Standard 2-D quantitative data (depth adds no new variable)
- **Audience:** The analyst themself

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The primary objective is memorability (even for oneself) or you are preparing a figure intended to stand out later
- **Reason:** 3-D was preferred for memory-focused scenarios [@levyGratuitousGraphicsPutting1996].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Reduced distinctiveness
- **The Risk:** If you later need to recall the pattern without the graph, a purely 2-D chart may be less salient

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Defaulting to 3-D “because the tool makes it easy” during exploratory analysis
- **Why it fails:** Participants did not view 3-D as the best choice for self-focused understanding [@levyGratuitousGraphicsPutting1996].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The chart includes perspective/3-D volume even though you are the only intended viewer
- **The Test:** Ask: “Am I trying to understand, or trying to impress?” If it’s understand, remove 3-D and reassess readability.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Switch the chart type from 3-D to the analogous 2-D simple/area version.
- **Best Fix:** Choose between 2-D line (for trends) and 2-D bar (for details) depending on the analytic question [@levyGratuitousGraphicsPutting1996].
