## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Do not rely on text alone to communicate risk or health scenarios. Always supplement written descriptions with quantitative graphics.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Textual information is susceptible to "anecdotal reasoning," where users are swayed by vivid stories or irrelevant details rather than the statistical facts. Providing a graphic alongside the text anchors the user's understanding in the data, reducing the influence of less-relevant textual information and improving decision-making accuracy [@ancker_rethinking_2007].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Making a treatment decision or understanding a health risk.
*   **Data Type:** Scenarios involving statistical probabilities mixed with descriptive outcomes.
*   **Audience:** General health consumers, especially those prone to affective (emotional) decision making.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** The user has very low graphical literacy and finds charts confusing/intimidating.
*   **Reason:** While graphics generally help, users with extremely poor graphical literacy may simply ignore complex charts. (However, simple graphics are still generally preferred) [@ancker_rethinking_2007].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** Layout space and design complexity.
*   **The Risk:** If the graphic and text contradict (or seem to), trust in the information may decrease.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Using a generic stock image or decorative icon next to the text.
*   **Why it fails:** The graphic must be *quantitative*—it must encode the actual risk or probability to serve as a cognitive anchor.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Is the page a wall of text?
*   **The Test:** Cover the text. Is there a visual element that still conveys the magnitude of the risk or benefit?

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Add a simple bar chart or pie chart summarizing the numbers mentioned in the text.
*   **Best Fix:** Integrate an icon array or risk ladder directly adjacent to the relevant paragraph to provide a visual check against the narrative.
