## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When uncertainty, bias, or rhetorical framing matters, do not rely solely on ultra-clean, precision-driven visual styles that can imply undue authority.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

The paper argues that overly precise/minimalist visualizations can project an “imprimatur of authority and objectivity,” potentially discouraging critique and masking uncertainty; it notes that “messier” designs (e.g., sketchy/uncertainty-conveying renderings) can increase willingness to question or better appreciate uncertainty [@bertiniWhyShouldntAll2020].

- **The Principle:** Visual rhetoric affects perceived certainty and authority
- **The Evidence:** [@bertiniWhyShouldntAll2020]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understanding a phenomenon with uncertainty, missing data, or contested interpretation.
- **Data Type:** Any where uncertainty/subjectivity is relevant.
- **Audience:** Public-facing communication, policy contexts, or any skeptical/critical reading environment.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Regulated reporting or engineering contexts where convention demands clean, standard, audit-friendly displays.
- **Reason:** Departing from conventional “clean” forms may be unacceptable for the context even if it would better cue uncertainty [@bertiniWhyShouldntAll2020].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Some immediacy and “professional” minimalism.
- **The Risk:** Viewers may perceive the display as less polished or less credible if the context prizes conventional cleanliness [@bertiniWhyShouldntAll2020].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Assuming that more precise encodings automatically make a message more truthful or ethically safer.
- **Why it fails:** The paper warns that precision aesthetics can increase uncritical acceptance rather than improve understanding [@bertiniWhyShouldntAll2020].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The design looks authoritatively definitive even though the underlying data/story is uncertain or partial.
- **The Test:** Ask: “Could a viewer walk away thinking this is objective truth rather than a contingent view?” If yes, reconsider the rhetoric [@bertiniWhyShouldntAll2020].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add cues that foreground uncertainty or interpretation rather than pure exactness.
- **Best Fix:** Choose a representation style that supports critical reading and uncertainty appreciation, consistent with the paper’s argument about rhetorical force [@bertiniWhyShouldntAll2020].
