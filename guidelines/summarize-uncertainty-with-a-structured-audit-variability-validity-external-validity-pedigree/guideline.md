## Provide a standardized uncertainty profile, not an unstructured narrative <!-- role: advice -->

When reporting scientific results for decisions, summarize uncertainty in a structured profile that covers variability, internal validity threats, external validity threats, and the strength of the underlying science. Present the profile in consistent headings so users can compare across studies or updates.

## Why structured audits reveal hidden and omitted uncertainty sources <!-- role: reason -->

Scientific discourse often contains either too much detail or omits “goes without saying” uncertainties, forcing users to guess. A standardized profile makes recurring uncertainty sources visible, reduces reliance on intuition, and supports consistent interpretation across domains and time.

**Mechanism:** Standard fields prompt systematic disclosure and make it easier for users to locate the uncertainty types that matter to their decision, rather than inferring them from narrative cues.

**Evidence:** Communicating uncertainty requires uncovering uncertainties scientists may omit and reducing them to decision-relevant elements, which varies by decision context [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014]. A proposed protocol structures uncertainty reporting into variability, internal validity, external validity, strength of science, and a final uncertainty summary to minimize burden while educating users [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

**Notes:** A structured profile supports both transparency and comparability, even when precise quantification is limited.

## When decision makers must rely on your evidence summary <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Decide how much to trust a result and whether to seek more information.
- **Task:** Evaluate evidence quality and applicability across contexts.
- **Data:** Study findings, field trials, model outputs, synthesized estimates with known limitations.
- **Chart Setting:** Policy briefs, evidence dashboards, trial registries, model documentation pages.
- **Audience:** Non-specialists comparing multiple sources; specialists outside the field.
- **Success Criterion:** Users can identify key uncertainty sources without reading a full methods section.

## When not to use a full audit profile <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** The audience needs only the gist for a robust decision that is insensitive to plausible uncertainty. **Why:** Extra detail can impose unnecessary cognitive burden [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## Tradeoffs and risks <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** More reporting work and more page real estate. **Risk:** The profile can be mistaken for a guarantee that all uncertainties are captured. **Mitigation:** Include an explicit statement of whether unknown/omitted factors may remain.

## Common failure modes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **Mistake:** Listing only statistical variability while omitting validity and applicability concerns. **Why it fails:** Users infer unwarranted certainty from narrow statistical summaries [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].
- **Mistake:** Providing a long narrative of caveats without structure. **Why it fails:** Users cannot compare across sources and may miss critical uncertainty types [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## Quick tests <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Readers cannot tell whether uncertainty comes from data noise, bias, generalization, or weak theory.\
**Quick Check:** Can a reader point to where each of the four uncertainty categories is addressed?\
**Stronger Test:** Compare two studies using the same template and see whether users can correctly identify which is more decision-relevant and why.

## What to do instead <!-- role: fix -->

- Create a short table with labeled rows for variability, internal validity, external validity, and strength-of-science pedigree.
- Add a final “decision-grade uncertainty” summary statement that integrates the audit into a bounded range when feasible.
- State which uncertainty sources are routinely ignored in the field and whether that matters for this decision.
- Provide links to deeper methods for users who need to drill down beyond the profile.
