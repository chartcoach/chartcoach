## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Add topic-relevant visual imagery that is integrated with the chart to increase long-term memorability.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Explain the principle at work here. Connect the rule to human perception or clear communication. Cite your sources.

- **The Principle:** Additional visual encoding supports later retrieval.
- **The Evidence:** In a study comparing Nigel Holmes-style embellished charts vs. matched plain versions, long-term recall (after ~2–3 weeks) was significantly better for embellished charts across subject, categories, trend, and value message, while on-screen description accuracy was not worse [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

This advice is designed for specific moments.

- **User Goal:** Remembering the topic and key takeaways after days/weeks (e.g., presentations, mass media reading, persuasive communication).
- **Data Type:** General charted data (bar/line/pie as used in the study).
- **Audience:** Broad or lay audiences who may not have the chart available later.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

List the specific scenarios where you should ignore this rule.

- **Scenario:** Settings where memorability is not a goal and the chart must support purely immediate use.
- **Reason:** The paper cautions that imagery is contentious and may not be appropriate as a general principle even though it improved long-term recall in their task context [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

Be honest about the downsides.

- **The Sacrifice:** Extra design effort and need for artistic skill to create integrated imagery.
- **The Risk:** Imagery can unintentionally introduce or amplify a value message (bias) [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

Describe the common anti-patterns or "lazy fixes" people use that don't actually solve the problem.

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding generic decoration that is not connected to the chart’s subject.
- **Why it fails:** The study’s embellished charts used imagery tightly connected to subject/details; unrelated embellishment is not supported by the paper’s evidence as improving recall [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Imagery feels interchangeable (could be swapped with another topic) without changing meaning.
- **The Test:** Ask a viewer (after exposure) what the chart was about; if they recall the picture but not the topic, the imagery may not be sufficiently topic-linked (the study’s benefit was for recalling both) [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Replace decorative imagery with a single, clear image that directly cues the chart’s subject.
- **Best Fix:** Integrate imagery into the data representation (so recalling the image cues the chart structure/trend), as in Holmes-style designs used in the experiment [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].
