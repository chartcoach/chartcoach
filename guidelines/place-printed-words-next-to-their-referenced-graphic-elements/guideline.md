## Align printed words with the corresponding visuals <!-- role: advice -->

Place printed words within or immediately adjacent to the parts of the graphic they describe rather than separating text and graphics.

## Why spatial alignment reduces cognitive load <!-- role: reason -->

When text and corresponding visuals are separated, learners must spend limited cognitive resources scanning and matching, which adds incidental processing unrelated to understanding the system itself. Integrating words with the relevant visual elements reduces search and supports building connections between verbal and pictorial representations.

**Mechanism:** Reduced visual scanning lowers incidental processing demands, freeing capacity for selecting, organizing, and integrating the essential explanation.

**Evidence:** Transfer was higher for integrated (aligned) text-and-animation presentations than for separated presentations, consistent with a spatial contiguity effect [@mayerNineWaysReduce2003].

**Notes:** This method targets overload caused by confusing presentation of essential material, not by adding extraneous content.

## When learners must map text to visuals <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand what each part of a visual represents and how it relates to the explanation.
- **Task:** Match descriptions to graphic elements while learning the mechanism.
- **Data:** Labeled components and relations in a graphic or animation.
- **Chart Setting:** Screen or page with both graphics and printed explanatory text.
- **Audience:** Learners who may not know where to look in the graphic.
- **Success Criterion:** Reduced confusion and improved transfer.

## When separation may be unavoidable <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** Space constraints or formatting rules prevent placing labels near the relevant visual elements. **Why:** The aligned layout cannot be implemented, so other methods must reduce scanning and confusion.

## Tradeoffs of integrating text into graphics <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Visual cleanliness and space for the graphic itself. **Risk:** Over-labeling can clutter the image and hinder perception. **Mitigation:** Keep printed text minimal and tightly tied to the essential elements.

## Common spatial contiguity failures <!-- role: mistakes -->

**Mistake:** Putting all explanatory text in a single block far from the visual it describes. **Why it fails:** It forces scanning and memory holding to connect words and pictures.

## Quick checks for misalignment <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Readers repeatedly look back and forth to find what the text refers to. **Quick Check:** For each key phrase, verify that the referenced visual element is immediately identifiable without searching. **Stronger Test:** Observe a small sample of learners and note whether they can match text to elements without hesitation.

## What to do if you cannot fully align <!-- role: fix -->

- Signal correspondences with simple cues that indicate what text refers to what visual element.
- Reduce the amount of on-screen text so mapping demands are smaller.
- Segment the explanation so only the currently relevant labels/text are displayed.
- Use narration instead of on-screen text when an animation must be watched continuously.
