## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Plan 20–60 minutes (not a few minutes) to build or refine a palette, and revisit it later with fresh eyes and/or feedback before finalizing.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Explain the principle at work here. Connect the rule to human perception or clear communication.

- **The Principle:** Iteration reveals hidden failures (contrast, meaning, colorblind safety) and improves judgment
- **The Evidence:** Muth states that palette creation is time-consuming, commonly involves multiple reversals (liking a color, then realizing it doesn’t fit sentiment or accessibility), and suggests feedback and revisiting after time to see issues more clearly [@muth_good_color_palettes_2024].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

This advice is designed for specific moments.

- **User Goal:** Publish a polished, accessible, appropriate chart without last-minute surprises
- **Data Type:** Any chart with multiple categories or strong communication constraints
- **Audience:** Public-facing work where misinterpretation or poor accessibility has high cost

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

List the specific scenarios where you should ignore this rule.

- **Scenario:** You’re using a proven default palette and have minimal constraints (few categories, standard background)
- **Reason:** The incremental value of extended iteration may be low when requirements are already met [@muth_good_color_palettes_2024].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

Be honest about the downsides.

- **The Sacrifice:** More time upfront and potential delays
- **The Risk:** Over-iterating can lead to decision fatigue or unnecessary changes when the palette is already good enough [@muth_good_color_palettes_2024].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

Describe the common anti-patterns or "lazy fixes" people use that don't actually solve the problem.

- **The Wrong Fix:** Treating palette choice as a last-minute 3-minute styling step
- **Why it fails:** Problems like low contrast or colorblind conflicts often appear only after seeing the palette in the actual chart [@muth_good_color_palettes_2024].
- **The Wrong Fix:** Falling in love with one color and forcing the rest to match even when it breaks sentiment or accessibility
- **Why it fails:** The palette ends up inappropriate or indistinguishable, requiring rework anyway [@muth_good_color_palettes_2024].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** You keep making small tweaks but still feel uncertain; new issues appear after each adjustment (contrast, salience, meaning)
- **The Test:** Pause and re-evaluate later; ask a colleague to identify categories quickly and note confusions; re-run your palette checks [@muth_good_color_palettes_2024].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Stop tweaking and validate with a checker; change only the failing colors rather than redoing everything [@muth_good_color_palettes_2024].
- **Best Fix:** Build a simple iteration loop: draft → test in chart at small sizes → colorblind/contrast check → adjust → rest overnight → final validation [@muth_good_color_palettes_2024].
