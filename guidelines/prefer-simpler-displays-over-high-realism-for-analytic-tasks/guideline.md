## Use the least realistic depiction that still supports the decision task <!-- role: advice -->

When the task is analytic (comparison, relative position, inference), choose a simplified depiction that emphasizes task-relevant cues over photorealism or high-fidelity imagery. Do not add realism unless it measurably improves task performance for the target decision.

## Realism can attract attention and inflate credibility without improving accuracy <!-- role: reason -->

High-quality imagery can increase perceived scientific credibility or preference while drawing attention to irrelevant details, slowing or degrading performance. This creates a mismatch between what users like and what helps them decide correctly.

**Mechanism:** Visually rich depictions increase salience of non-essential detail and can trigger a “high-quality image” bias, shifting attention and judgment away from task-relevant variables.

**Evidence:** Articles and displays accompanied by high-quality scientific images receive higher credibility ratings than simpler figures, even when the image is not diagnostic for the claim [@padillaDecisionMakingVisualizations2018]. More realistic geospatial depictions can increase time on task and focus attention on irrelevant information compared to simpler displays, including for expert users in some contexts [@padillaDecisionMakingVisualizations2018].

**Notes:** Preference is not a reliable proxy for performance; both experts and novices can show these effects.

## When this applies <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Make an accurate inference or comparison from a visual display.
- **Task:** Relative position judgments, extracting patterns, comparing conditions, selecting a route/option.
- **Data:** Spatial or scientific information that can be shown as either realistic imagery or abstract encoding.
- **Chart Setting:** Decision support, reports, briefings, educational graphics.
- **Audience:** Novices and experts; especially users prone to being distracted by detail.
- **Success Criterion:** Faster, more accurate task completion with less distraction.

## Exceptions <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** The task requires recognition of real-world objects or landmarks and realism is necessary for correct identification. **Why:** Abstraction can remove the cues needed for object/scene recognition.

## Costs <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Reduced “wow factor” and potentially lower initial engagement. **Risk:** Users may mistrust simplified graphics if they expect realism. **Mitigation:** Explain the purpose of abstraction (task focus) in a short caption.

## Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **Mistake:** Choosing realism because users say they “prefer” it. **Why it fails:** Preference can diverge from accuracy and speed.
- **Mistake:** Adding detailed textures, 3D effects, or imagery that is not used by the task. **Why it fails:** It increases distraction and visual complexity.

## Check <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Users fixate on irrelevant details or take longer without higher accuracy. **Quick Check:** Ask what evidence users used for their answer; if they cite decorative details, realism is interfering. **Stronger Test:** Compare simplified vs realistic versions on response time and accuracy for the actual task.

## Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- Remove or mute decorative textures, 3D rendering, and non-task imagery.
- Increase emphasis on the variables required for the judgment (clear encodings, labels).
- Provide realism only as an optional secondary view if needed for orientation.
- Replace realism with schematic encodings that directly support the target comparison.
