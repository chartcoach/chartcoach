## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Use a bar graph when you want viewers to focus on comparing legend-defined groups (z) within each x-axis category (x moderates z–y comparisons).

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Grouped bars encourage chunking by proximity within each x category, supporting within-category comparisons across the legend variable, which changes the interaction framing viewers spontaneously describe.

- **The Principle:** Proximity-based chunking emphasizes within-group sets of bars.
- **The Evidence:** Viewers produced more z–y interaction descriptions for bar graphs than for line graphs when summarizing three-variable graphs [@shahBarLineGraph2011].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Compare group differences (legend variable) inside each x-axis level.
- **Data Type:** Three-variable data with multiple levels of x and z.
- **Audience:** Mixed audiences when you want “within each category, groups differ” to be the default reading.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Your primary goal is to convey how y changes smoothly over x for each group.
- **Reason:** Line graphs more strongly cue trend-by-group (x–y interaction framing) than bars [@shahBarLineGraph2011].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Trend perception over x can be less dominant than in line graphs.
- **The Risk:** Viewers may focus on category-by-category differences and underemphasize cross-x trend narratives [@shahBarLineGraph2011].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Using bars expecting people to automatically describe trend lines or continuous change.
- **Why it fails:** The visual grouping is by clusters, not by continuous traces, shifting what seems “important” [@shahBarLineGraph2011].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Summaries sound like “for low x, group A > group B; for medium x…” rather than “as x increases…”
- **The Test:** Collect short written “main point” statements; if many are within-category comparisons across groups, the bar format is driving z–y-by-x framing as intended [@shahBarLineGraph2011].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** If trend-over-x is required, add explicit instructions to describe how y changes across x for each group.
- **Best Fix:** Switch to a line graph when trend-by-group is the intended takeaway [@shahBarLineGraph2011].
