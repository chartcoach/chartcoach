## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Do not assume that simpler is better; allow for some decorative or standard elements ("chartjunk") if it enhances the user's subjective appreciation of the graph.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
There is a disconnect between design theory (which values efficiency) and user reality (which values familiarity and standard aesthetics). Simplicity can be perceived as "too simple."
*   **The Principle:** **The "Moral Act" vs. User Preference.** While Tufte views minimalism as a moral imperative for truth, users view standard graphics as more persuasive and beautiful.
*   **The Evidence:** Participants rated standard charts with lower data-ink ratios (more "ink") as more beautiful and persuasive. The paper supports Norman's view that "simplicity is highly overrated" and that complexity can enhance attractiveness [@inbar_minimalism_2007].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Persuasion, marketing, or public communication where the "attractiveness" of the product matters.
*   **Audience:** General public or users with no prior training in minimalist design theory.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** Time-critical monitoring tasks.
*   **Reason:** The paper acknowledges previous research (Gillan & Richman, 1994) showing high data-ink ratios can improve response times, even if users don't *like* the look as much [@inbar_minimalism_2007].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** The chart contains "useless" ink that does not convey data.
*   **The Risk:** The design may be criticized by visualization purists or adherents to Tufte's philosophy.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Forcing users to learn minimalist graphs by making them perform data extraction tasks first.
*   **Why it fails:** The study showed that even after "acquainting" users with the minimalist graph through tasks, they still preferred the standard graph [@inbar_minimalism_2007].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Is the chart stripped to the bare minimum (only data)?
*   **The Test:** Ask, "Does this look professional/finished?" If the answer is "It looks empty," you have prioritized efficiency over preference.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Re-enable standard formatting options (ticks, borders) in your plotting software.
*   **Best Fix:** Adopt a design that users find "beautiful" (often implying symmetry and standard structures) rather than mathematically efficient [@inbar_minimalism_2007].
