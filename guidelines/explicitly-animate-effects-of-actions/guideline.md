## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

For complex actions, animate a visible reaction or state change that shows the effect of the action, not just the action itself.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Complex animated actions were poorly recalled when the effect was not shown (e.g., forming the protein‑pyrimidine complex; light energy interaction). In the redesigned version, adding explicit combination/state-change cues (new combined object, color/shape change, reaction effect) improved recall of these propositions [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997].

- **The Principle:** Comprehension relies on perceivable cause→effect mapping
- **The Evidence:** [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand causal mechanisms and multi-step processes.
- **Data Type:** Scientific/technical process animations with interactions.
- **Audience:** Especially novices who can’t infer implications from subtle motion.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The action’s effect is already visually unambiguous without additional reaction cues.
- **Reason:** Extra effects may be redundant and distracting.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More animation complexity and production time.
- **The Risk:** Over-dramatized effects can mislead about magnitude or nature of the change.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Showing two entities overlap/touch without indicating a new state.
- **Why it fails:** Viewers may not infer “combination” or “trigger” and misattribute what happened [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users misdescribe the interaction (e.g., what was struck, where energy came from).
- **The Test:** Ask users to explain the causal chain in one sentence; incorrect links indicate the effect wasn’t perceptible.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a clear state change (color/shape) at the moment of interaction.
- **Best Fix:** Create an explicit before/after: a combined object for “complex formed,” and a distinct reaction cue when energy strikes, then show the subsequent separation/repair step [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997].
