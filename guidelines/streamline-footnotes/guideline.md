## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Cut footnotes down to the basics necessary to understand the map and use links for sources and complex methodology.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Complex predictions involve many assumptions (e.g., heat index, relative humidity factors). Listing all these details in the chart footer creates visual clutter. Transparency is important, but you can achieve it by linking to the data source. This allows you to "cut the footnote down to the basics of what’s necessary to understand the map in front of us" [@mintzer_fix_my_chart_text_elements_2024].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Presenting a clean, accessible visualization for public consumption.
*   **Data Type:** Modeled data or projections with complex inputs (e.g., climate forecasts).
*   **Audience:** Digital readers who can click a link for deeper context.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** Static print media.
*   **Reason:** Readers cannot click links; essential methodological caveats must be printed on the page.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** Immediate access to the granular details of the calculation without leaving the page.
*   **The Risk:** A skeptical reader might view a sparse footnote as a lack of rigor if they don't click the source link.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Writing a paragraph-long essay in the chart footer explaining every variable (e.g., defining "Daily Maximum Air Temperature" and "shaded enclosure").
*   **Why it fails:** It overwhelms the visual hierarchy and intimidates the reader.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Is your footnote longer than your chart title?
*   **The Test:** Check the word count. If the footnote dominates the bottom 20% of the image, it needs trimming.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Identify sentences that explain "how" the data was gathered and replace them with a hyperlink on the Source label.
*   **Best Fix:** Keep only the definitions crucial for interpreting the visual (e.g., "Blue <30 Summer Days") and link the rest.
