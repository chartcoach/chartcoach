## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Add the comparison data that best supports your chart’s stated point, and remove comparisons that do not serve that point.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Comparisons are what make data interpretable: they create perspective (vs. past, other places, peers, averages, outliers) and allow the chart to function as evidence for the headline claim, as argued and exemplified in [@muth_better_charts_2017].

- **The Principle:** Meaning-through-comparison (context-building)
- **The Evidence:** [@muth_better_charts_2017]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Decide whether something is “a lot,” “growing,” “unusual,” or “more than X.”
- **Data Type:** Any chart where a single series alone would be hard to interpret (e.g., sales over time without product peers).
- **Audience:** Readers who need context to understand significance, not just raw numbers.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The intended point is about the absolute level of a single measure and adding comparisons would distract (e.g., a chart intended to show only one figure clearly).
- **Reason:** Extra series can dilute attention and obscure the central message; Muth emphasizes choosing comparisons based on the hypothesis you want to prove [@muth_better_charts_2017].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Simplicity—more lines/bars increase visual complexity.
- **The Risk:** Poorly chosen comparisons can send the reader toward the wrong question or imply a story you didn’t intend [@muth_better_charts_2017].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding every available related series “for completeness.”
- **Why it fails:** The reader loses the intended thread; the comparison set must be selected to match the point (hypothesis) [@muth_better_charts_2017].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The chart shows only one series and the reader can’t tell whether it is impressive or not; or it shows many series with no clear reason.
- **The Test:** Ask: “What should the reader compare this to in order to understand my headline claim?” If you can’t answer, your comparison set is missing or misaligned [@muth_better_charts_2017].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add one key comparator series/baseline that directly supports the headline claim.
- **Best Fix:** Revisit the headline (point) and rebuild the comparison set so every included series has a job: helping the reader judge the main data in service of that point [@muth_better_charts_2017].
