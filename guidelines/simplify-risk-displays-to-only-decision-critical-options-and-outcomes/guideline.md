## Remove nonessential options and statistics from risk displays <!-- role: advice -->

When presenting risks and benefits for a decision, show only the options and outcome statistics that are essential for that patient’s choice, even if that reduces completeness.

## Extra information can distract and reduce understanding <!-- role: reason -->

Dense presentations can overload attention and obscure the key tradeoff, while simpler displays can improve comprehension and make viewers more responsive to the actual magnitude of benefit.

**Mechanism:** Reducing choice set and redundant outcomes lowers cognitive load and helps viewers focus on the most decision-relevant comparison.

**Evidence:** Simpler presentations that focused on the decision-relevant comparison improved understanding and increased sensitivity to the magnitude of risk reduction compared with more complex multi-option, multi-outcome displays [@fagerlinHelpingPatientsDecide2011].

**Notes:** Simplification can be done either by choosing essential information in advance or by narrowing the choice based on patient preferences and clinical relevance.

## When simplification applies <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Choose between treatments with different benefit–harm profiles.
- **Task:** Compare options and perceive small differences accurately.
- **Data:** Multiple outcomes and multiple treatment options, some redundant or clinically irrelevant.
- **Chart Setting:** Decision aids, clinic printouts, or dashboards that default to exhaustive output.
- **Audience:** Patients with limited numeracy or under stress; clinicians communicating quickly.
- **Success Criterion:** Users correctly identify the incremental benefit of the relevant option and do not confuse it with unrelated outcomes.

## When not to follow it <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** The patient explicitly wants a comprehensive view and demonstrates ability to navigate it without confusion. **Why:** Omitting information may reduce perceived transparency for some users.

## Tradeoffs and risks <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Completeness and the ability to answer uncommon follow-up questions from the same display. **Risk:** Over-simplifying can hide a relevant option or outcome for a specific patient. **Mitigation:** Make it easy to access additional detail outside the primary display.

## Common failure modes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **Mistake:** Displaying every possible therapy option regardless of clinical relevance. **Why it fails:** Users may spend effort comparing options that are not real choices.
- **Mistake:** Including multiple overlapping outcomes that require interpretation (for example, survival plus multiple mortality breakdowns) without a clear decision purpose. **Why it fails:** Redundancy increases cognitive load and confusion.

## Quick tests <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Users cannot state the main incremental benefit or confuse which option is being compared. **Quick Check:** Ask, “What is the one choice you are deciding between right now?” and see if the display makes that obvious. **Stronger Test:** Compare comprehension questions between the simplified and full versions.

## What to do instead <!-- role: fix -->

- Restrict the display to the patient’s actual decision set rather than all theoretically available options.
- Show only the primary outcome(s) needed for the tradeoff being made.
- Present treatments one at a time or as a binary comparison when that matches the clinical decision.
- Provide a separate section or link for expanded detail rather than embedding it in the main graphic.
