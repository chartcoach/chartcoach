## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Treat the total number of icons (denominator) as a consequential design choice; do not change 100 vs 1,000 icons casually when communicating the same underlying risk.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

The experiments showed that larger denominators can make otherwise equivalent risks and risk reductions feel larger/more serious. In visual conditions, icon arrays with 1,000 (vs 100) increased perceived helpfulness of screening (and tended to increase perceived seriousness), indicating a denominator-driven perception shift.

- **The Principle:** Denominator sensitivity (ratio-bias–like effects) in perceived magnitude
- **The Evidence:** [@galesicUsingIconArrays2009]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Form a judgment about seriousness of a medical risk or helpfulness of a treatment
- **Data Type:** Proportions communicated via “X out of N” (including icon arrays)
- **Audience:** General audiences where perception and affect may influence decisions

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The communication goal is explicitly to standardize on a specific population unit (e.g., always “per 1,000”) across materials for consistency.
- **Reason:** Consistent denominators can reduce confusion across comparisons; the paper does not test cross-document consistency, but it supports that denominator choice affects perception and thus should be standardized if neutrality is desired [@galesicUsingIconArrays2009].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Flexibility in layout and simplicity (1,000-icon arrays are larger and denser)
- **The Risk:** A 1,000-icon array may look overwhelming, while a 100-icon array may understate perceived impact

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Switching denominators to “fit the page” (using 100 in one place and 1,000 elsewhere for the same risk)
- **Why it fails:** It can unintentionally change perceived seriousness/helpfulness even when the underlying risk is identical [@galesicUsingIconArrays2009].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Two graphics show the same percentage but elicit different “this feels big” reactions.
- **The Test:** Hold the percentage constant and swap denominators (100 vs 1,000) in a pilot; if ratings shift, denominator is influencing perception.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Standardize denominators within a document (pick 100 or 1,000 and stick to it).
- **Best Fix:** Select a denominator deliberately for your intended interpretive unit (e.g., “out of 1,000 people”) and keep it consistent across baseline and treatment arrays and across related materials [@galesicUsingIconArrays2009].
