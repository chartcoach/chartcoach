## Choose restroom-style person icons when preference and acceptability matter <!-- role: advice -->

Use restroom-style person icons in icon arrays when you want the display to be rated as more helpful, clearer, and more desirable to see.

## Preference can depend on icon form even with identical data <!-- role: reason -->

Even when the risk numbers and array layout are the same, icon form changes how acceptable and usable the display feels, which can affect willingness to engage with the information.

**Mechanism:** People-like icons can increase perceived relevance and ease of interpretation, improving subjective evaluations of the graphic.

**Evidence:** Mean graph preference ratings (clarity/helpfulness/desire to see the format) differed by icon type, with the highest ratings for restroom icons and photographs compared with other icon types. [@zikmund-fisherBlocksOvalsPeople2014]

**Notes:** The preference pattern held across lower and higher numeracy and graphical literacy groups.

## Use when you need buy-in for risk displays <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Feel that the risk graphic is clear and helpful.
- **Task:** Evaluate and accept a risk display format for future use.
- **Data:** Percentage risk displayed as an icon array with event vs non-event icons.
- **Chart Setting:** Patient education materials, risk calculators, decision aids.
- **Audience:** Mixed numeracy; mixed graphical literacy.
- **Success Criterion:** Higher subjective ratings of clarity/helpfulness/preference.

## Exceptions for preference-optimized icon choice <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** The icon must avoid human depictions for cultural, institutional, or product policy reasons. **Why:** The preference advantage is tied to more anthropomorphic iconography.

## Tradeoffs of optimizing for preference <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Preference-optimized icons may not be the only determinant of understanding. **Risk:** Higher preference could be mistaken for higher comprehension without measuring outcomes. **Mitigation:** Pair preference testing with recall and calibration measures.

## Common mistakes in preference-driven icon selection <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **Mistake:** Picking icon types purely because they are easy to render (e.g., blocks) without checking user ratings. **Why it fails:** Users rate some icon types as less helpful/less desirable even with identical risk information.
- **Mistake:** Assuming a “fun” face-based design will be preferred. **Why it fails:** Smile/frown faces were not among the most preferred icon types.

## Quick checks for preference differences <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Users describe the graphic as unhelpful or say they would not want risk information shown this way. **Quick Check:** Ask the three evaluation items (clarity/helpfulness/desire to see) and compare mean scores across icon candidates. **Stronger Test:** Randomized between-subjects test of icon types with identical data and layout.

## What to do instead if restroom icons are not available <!-- role: fix -->

- Use another anthropomorphic option (e.g., head-and-shoulders outlines) and re-test preference in your audience.
- Keep the same icon type across contexts to build familiarity and reduce format friction.
- Emphasize the numeric risk estimate alongside the array to maintain perceived usefulness even if icon preference is lower.
- If photographs are considered, standardize styling (cropping, background, color treatment) so comparisons are not confounded by image variation.
