## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
If you visually highlight one specific object in a pair (making it the visual "figure"), you must structure your accompanying text so that highlighted object is the **grammatical subject** (the "target").

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Visual perception and linguistic spatial descriptions share an underlying asymmetry.
*   **The Principle:** Functional Role Assignment. In the relation "A is above B," A is the "target" (figure) and B is the "reference" (ground). The visual system similarly marks the location of the attentional spotlight as "special."
*   **The Evidence:** @roth_asymmetric_2012 demonstrated compatibility effects between visual attention and linguistic framing. When attention was manipulated to focus on one object, verification was faster if that object was the linguistic target. Mismatches (attending to A while verifying "B is below A") caused slower responses.

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Reading charts with annotated insights or captions.
*   **Data Type:** Scatterplots, maps, or diagrams where specific points are highlighted against others.
*   **Audience:** General audiences relying on text annotations to interpret data.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** Fixed Reference Objects.
*   **Reason:** In geography or layouts with massive landmarks, the landmark is almost always the reference (ground). It is awkward to say "The lake is to the right of the boat" even if the boat is highlighted.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** You may need to use passive voice or inverse spatial terms (e.g., "below" instead of "above") to maintain alignment.
*   **The Risk:** Using "unnatural" linguistic phrasing (e.g., "The building is right of the bike") just to match visual highlighting can feel awkward, as noted by @roth_asymmetric_2012 citing Talmy.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Highlighting a data point (A) but writing the caption about the baseline (B). (e.g., Visually glowing "2023 Revenue" but writing "2022 Revenue was lower than 2023").
*   **Why it fails:** The viewer's attention is drawn to "2023," establishing it as the visual target. The text forces them to mentally switch roles to make "2022" the target, fighting the visual cue.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Look at what is red, bold, or glowing. Is that the very first noun in your sentence?
*   **The Test:** If the highlighted object is "A" and the other is "B," does your sentence start "A is..."? If it starts "B is...", you have a mismatch.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Rewrite the text. Change "B is below A" to "A is above B."
*   **Best Fix:** Change the visual highlighting to match the preferred linguistic structure (e.g., if the story is really about B, move the highlight to B).
