## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Use bold weights only to emphasize titles or a few key words/values; keep longer chart text (descriptions, notes, annotations) in regular or medium weight.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Bold (and especially black) weights draw attention and feel more assertive; using them sparingly creates a clear typographic hierarchy and keeps longer text easier to read [@muth_fonts_2022].

- **The Principle:** Visual hierarchy through weight contrast
- **The Evidence:** [@muth_fonts_2022]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Noticing what matters first (headline, key numbers) while still reading supporting text comfortably
- **Data Type:** Charts/tables with annotations, notes, and multiple text elements
- **Audience:** General readers scanning quickly

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Very short, UI-like text where stronger weight is part of the intended emphasis (e.g., a headline-only element).
- **Reason:** The post notes boldness is attention-grabbing and can be appropriate for titles; the exception is when the text is short and meant to dominate [@muth_fonts_2022].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Less immediate punch for non-title text.
- **The Risk:** If you underuse bold, some key callouts may not stand out without other hierarchy cues [@muth_fonts_2022].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Setting most labels, annotations, and notes in bold to “improve readability.”
- **Why it fails:** It collapses hierarchy—everything competes for attention and longer bold text becomes harder to read comfortably [@muth_fonts_2022].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Too many elements shout at once; you can’t tell what’s primary vs secondary text.
- **The Test:** Identify (in 2 seconds) the single most important text element. If multiple elements compete equally, you likely overused bold [@muth_fonts_2022].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Change body annotations/notes back to regular (400) or medium weight; keep bold for only the key phrase/number [@muth_fonts_2022].
- **Best Fix:** Define a weight system (e.g., title bold, labels regular, notes regular/medium) and apply it consistently across chart and table elements [@muth_fonts_2022].
