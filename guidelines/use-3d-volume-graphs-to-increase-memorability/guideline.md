## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When memorability is the priority, prefer 3-D volume graphs over 2-D graphs.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Participants systematically associated added depth (especially volume-style 3-D) with making the figure more memorable, and chose 3-D more often under explicit “remember later” instructions.

- **The Principle:** Use perceived salience to support recall
- **The Evidence:** Across experiments, 3-D graphs were preferred more for memorability than immediate use; in Experiment 3, the 3-D volume graph was significantly preferred in the “memory” condition (p < 0.01) [@levyGratuitousGraphicsPutting1996].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Remember the content later without referring back to it
- **Data Type:** 2-D quantitative data where 3-D depth would be redundant (i.e., depth is decorative)
- **Audience:** Viewers who must later compare recalled results against other information (e.g., competing presentations)

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The viewer needs maximum immediate clarity/decision accuracy
- **Reason:** 2-D graphs were preferred for “now/immediate decision” scenarios [@levyGratuitousGraphicsPutting1996].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Simplicity; potentially more visual “ink”/complexity
- **The Risk:** Viewers may perceive or interpret values less straightforwardly than in 2-D, even if they find it memorable [@levyGratuitousGraphicsPutting1996].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Using any 3-D style interchangeably (e.g., surface effects) when aiming for memorability
- **Why it fails:** Participants showed different preferences for volume vs. surface 3-D, preferring volume more strongly [@levyGratuitousGraphicsPutting1996].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The chart is 2-D and looks similar to surrounding charts, with little to make it distinctive
- **The Test:** If the design brief says “must be remembered,” compare a 2-D area version against a 3-D volume version with users for perceived memorability.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Switch from a 2-D area chart to the analogous 3-D volume style (keeping axes and data mapping unchanged).
- **Best Fix:** Use a 3-D *volume* rendering (not a floating surface) if you add depth cues specifically to support memorability [@levyGratuitousGraphicsPutting1996].
