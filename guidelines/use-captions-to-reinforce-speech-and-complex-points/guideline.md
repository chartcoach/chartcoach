## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When speech conveys complex or central information, present it concurrently as a caption (and time it to the narration).

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

In the original presentation, propositions given only in speech were generally poorly recalled (e.g., “photoreactivation”, “protein‑pyrimidine complex”). Adding captions (and aligning them with the speech) in the re-authored version improved recall significantly [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997].

- **The Principle:** Redundant textual support for complex verbal information
- **The Evidence:** [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Learn and retain specialized terms and key relationships.
- **Data Type:** Technical instructional explanations with unfamiliar vocabulary.
- **Audience:** Novices or mixed-knowledge audiences.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The screen is already text-heavy and the caption would obscure essential visuals.
- **Reason:** Added text may create clutter that interferes with attention.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Screen space and visual simplicity.
- **The Risk:** Too much captioning can slow viewing or compete with visuals.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding captions but not aligning them to the exact narrated moment.
- **Why it fails:** Viewers don’t know which visual event the caption belongs to, weakening reinforcement [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Viewers recall objects but not the named process/relationship.
- **The Test:** Ask for immediate recall of key terms; low recall indicates insufficient caption reinforcement.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a brief caption for the key term/relationship and display it during the narrated phrase.
- **Best Fix:** Restructure the sequence so the caption appears alone (or with minimal motion) while the narration delivers the complex point [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997].
