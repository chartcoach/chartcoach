## Use progress indicators to show structure and position <!-- role: advice -->

Use a progress bar, timebar, or checklist-style tracker to make the story’s overall structure visible and to show the reader’s current position. Let these elements also serve as navigation when the narrative has multiple frames.

## Visible structure reduces getting-lost costs <!-- role: reason -->

Narrative visualizations often mix guidance with interaction, which can invite digressions. A persistent structural indicator anchors the reader, helping them understand scope and return to the narrative after exploration.

**Mechanism:** Structural cues externalize “where am I?” and “how much is left?” so attention can stay on the content and transitions.

**Evidence:** Examples of interactive slideshows and structured presentations use progress bars and checklist-like overviews as visual structuring devices that orient viewers and support navigation across segments [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010].

**Notes:** A checklist can double as an establishing shot by previewing the topics and frames before the narrative begins.

## When structural indicators are especially helpful <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Move through a multi-part explanation without losing track of scope.
- **Task:** Stepwise reading, revisiting earlier segments, or skipping to a topic.
- **Data:** Multi-topic or multi-time narratives where the viewer may explore subsets.
- **Chart Setting:** Slideshows, longform interactives, tabbed narratives, or scrolly-like sequences.
- **Audience:** General audiences and first-time viewers.
- **Success Criterion:** Readers can jump to a segment and still understand where it fits.

## When not to add progress structure <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** The visualization is intended as a single-frame, reader-driven analysis tool with no narrative segmentation. **Why:** A progress metaphor implies an intended path that may not exist.

## Tradeoffs of progress/UI scaffolding <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Screen space and visual simplicity. **Risk:** Poorly designed trackers can distract or look like decoration without function. **Mitigation:** Tie the indicator to real navigation and keep it persistent and lightweight.

## Common failures with trackers <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **Mistake:** Adding a progress bar that updates but does not allow navigation. **Why it fails:** Readers still cannot recover from disorientation or skip to relevant segments.
- **Mistake:** Hiding the structure entirely and relying on users to infer segmentation from content changes. **Why it fails:** Readers may not realize segments exist or may abandon mid-way.

## Quick checks for structure clarity <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Readers cannot tell how long the narrative is or what topics it covers. **Quick Check:** After a brief scan, ask someone to list the major segments; if they cannot, add structure cues. **Stronger Test:** Observe whether readers can leave a segment and return without restarting.

## Fixes when readers get lost <!-- role: fix -->

- Add a persistent progress bar that both shows position and allows direct navigation.
- Use a checklist overview screen to preview sections and provide random access.
- Pair time sliders with narrative steps when time is a primary organizing dimension.
- Keep the indicator consistent across frames so it also functions as a continuity cue.
