## Set per-item time limits based on pilot completion time for standardized visualization tests <!-- role: advice -->

Choose a per-item time limit using pilot timing results so test administration is standardized without making the assessment a speed test.

## Why pilot-based limits support standardization without undue pressure <!-- role: reason -->

A standardized limit prevents uncontrolled variation in administration time while still allowing enough time for typical respondents to attempt items. Pilot-based calibration helps avoid a limit that is too strict (turning the test into speed) or too lenient (reducing standardization).

**Mechanism:** Timing calibration reduces variance in effort and context, keeping scores more comparable across test takers.

**Evidence:** VLAT set a 25-second per-item limit based on pilot work and observed that tryout participants could complete the full test within the allotted time window, supporting feasibility for non-expert test takers [@leeVLATDevelopmentVisualization2017].

**Notes:** Timing should be evaluated alongside item difficulty to avoid penalizing harder visualizations disproportionately.

## When to apply this <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Standardize an online visualization literacy assessment.
- **Task:** Define time limits that preserve comparability.
- **Data:** Any; more complex charts may need more time.
- **Chart Setting:** Remote/online administration where total time must be bounded.
- **Audience:** General-population adults with varied reading speed and confidence.
- **Success Criterion:** Most participants finish without rushing, and timing does not dominate errors.

## When not to enforce a strict per-item limit <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** The purpose is diagnostic tutoring or exploratory learning rather than standardized measurement. **Why:** Strict limits can suppress reflection and learning behaviors.

## Tradeoffs <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Some thoughtful respondents may be constrained on complex items. **Risk:** Time pressure may disproportionately affect people with slower reading speed or accessibility needs. **Mitigation:** Provide accommodations or alternative forms where appropriate.

## Common mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

**Mistake:** Reusing a time limit from another test without piloting on your actual items and audience. **Why it fails:** Visual complexity and vocabulary demands vary, causing unintended speed effects.

## Quick checks <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Many responses occur near the timeout boundary or show increased omissions due to time. **Quick Check:** Plot response-time distributions and identify items with frequent timeouts. **Stronger Test:** Run a second pilot with adjusted timing and compare item statistics to confirm reduced speed artifacts.

## What to do if time pressure is distorting results <!-- role: fix -->

- Increase the per-item time limit for the whole test or for specific chart types.
- Simplify item stems and vocabulary to reduce reading-time burden.
- Reduce total item count to keep total session time acceptable without strict per-item caps.
- Offer an untimed accommodation mode and report scores separately.
