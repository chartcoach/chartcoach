## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Increase skepticism and bias controls when financial or nonfinancial conflicts of interest or strong prejudices are present.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

The paper argues that interests and prejudices can increase bias (u), which mathematically decreases PPV; thus significant findings in conflicted contexts are less likely to be true.

- **The Principle:** Incentives and commitment shift reporting/analysis toward “findings,” lowering PPV
- **The Evidence:** [@ioannidisWhyMostPublished2005]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Assess credibility of published claims
- **Data Type:** Studies in areas with strong commercial, career, or theoretical stakes
- **Audience:** Readers, guideline panels, journal editors

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Strongly standardized, low-discretion research environments with robust oversight
- **Reason:** Bias may be constrained, though it is not eliminated. [@ioannidisWhyMostPublished2005]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More verification effort; slower translation into practice
- **The Risk:** May be perceived as distrustful toward entire fields or teams. [@ioannidisWhyMostPublished2005]

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Assuming disclosure alone removes bias
- **Why it fails:** Bias mechanisms can persist even when conflicts exist and are known. [@ioannidisWhyMostPublished2005]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Strong claims aligned with sponsor or investigator interests, with limited transparency about outcomes/analyses
- **The Test:** Ask: “Could incentives increase u (selective reporting, analytic flexibility, interpretation spin)?”

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Interpret effect claims as provisional and seek independent confirmation
- **Best Fix:** Enforce stronger standards (protocol adherence, comprehensive reporting) and emphasize totality of evidence rather than isolated significant results. [@ioannidisWhyMostPublished2005]
