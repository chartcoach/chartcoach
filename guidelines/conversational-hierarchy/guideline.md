## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Be conversational first and precise later. Use colloquial wording for the text people read first (the title). Move precise definitions (dates, methodologies, standard deviations) to less prominent text elements like descriptions or notes.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Precise wording can distract from the actual message. Readers are more likely to remember a strong statement like "Rent prices are up" than a precise definition like "Price estimates for multi-family rentals... increased." Prioritizing the message helps the audience grasp the "so what" immediately [@muth_text_in_data_visualizations_2022].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Quickly understanding the takeaway of a visualization.
*   **Data Type:** Journalism or public-facing reports.
*   **Audience:** Mainstream or general audiences.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** Scientific papers or technical specifications.
*   **Reason:** In these contexts, precision is often valued higher than immediate accessibility or narrative impact.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** You lose the immediate "scientific" appearance of having the full methodology in the title.
*   **The Risk:** If the footnote is too small, a user might miss critical caveats about the data's limitations.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Using the official dataset name or axis variable name as the chart title (e.g., "Change in X variable 2020-2022").
*   **Why it fails:** It forces the reader to interpret the data to find the story, rather than being told the story upfront.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Does your title contain jargon like "median," "standard deviation," or specific date ranges?
*   **The Test:** Imagine describing the visualization to a friend. Would you say the words currently in your title? If not, rewrite it.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Move the precise dates and units to the subtitle/description.
*   **Best Fix:** Rewrite the title as a declarative sentence summarizing the trend (e.g., "Is higher than ever" instead of "Peaks").
