## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Add annotations to small multiple line charts, but keep them short and information-dense.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Annotations guide the reader’s eye and provide context; in small multiples, space is limited, so brevity prevents text from crowding out the data while still improving interpretability [@muth_small_multiple_line_charts_2024].

- **The Principle:** Attention guidance with minimal text footprint
- **The Evidence:** [@muth_small_multiple_line_charts_2024]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand why a change happened or which part of the line matters
- **Data Type:** Time series with notable events, peaks, or breaks
- **Audience:** General readers who benefit from contextual cues

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The annotation cannot be made short without losing essential meaning.
- **Reason:** Overlong text will compete with the chart; move detail to surrounding narrative instead of forcing it into panels [@muth_small_multiple_line_charts_2024].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Less space for the plot area; less room for multiple callouts.
- **The Risk:** Tiny text or crowded labels reduce readability [@muth_small_multiple_line_charts_2024].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Writing full sentences in-panel (including unnecessary verbs).
- **Why it fails:** Text overwhelms the small panels; readers can’t quickly scan the multiples [@muth_small_multiple_line_charts_2024].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Annotations dominate the panel or overlap lines/axes.
- **The Test:** Remove verbs and nonessential words; if meaning remains, the original was too long [@muth_small_multiple_line_charts_2024].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Compress wording (e.g., “U.S. highest in Q3”), use abbreviations readers understand, and drop unnecessary verbs.
- **Best Fix:** Keep only the most important annotation(s) per chart/panel and, if needed, move additional explanation into the chart description or surrounding text [@muth_small_multiple_line_charts_2024].
