## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When a label/caption is being cued (especially by speech), avoid introducing new motion or simultaneous reveals until viewers have time to read it.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

In the study, motion often pulled attention away before users fixated and read newly revealed text, reducing attention to important labels (e.g., repaired DNA label missed when motion began). Reading time can be comparable to the duration of spoken terms, so competing animation can cause attentional clashes [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997].

- **The Principle:** Limited serial visual attention; competition between tracking and reading
- **The Evidence:** [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Identify objects correctly; learn terminology.
- **Data Type:** Labeled diagrams/animations with synchronized narration.
- **Audience:** Novices, or any audience encountering complex terms.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The text is trivial (very short) and the motion is the primary message.
- **Reason:** Reading demand is low, so competition is less harmful.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Slower pacing; longer runtime.
- **The Risk:** Over-pausing may feel tedious if overused.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Keeping the animation running while “hoping” users will read the label later.
- **Why it fails:** Attention is captured by motion; users may never return to the label at the right time [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users can describe the motion but cannot name the labeled elements.
- **The Test:** Immediate free-recall: ask users to list key terms right after; missing labeled terms indicates insufficient reading time.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a brief pause after the label/caption appears.
- **Best Fix:** Rescript timing so speech cue + label reveal happen together, then leave the screen stable for reading before the next motion/reveal [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997].
