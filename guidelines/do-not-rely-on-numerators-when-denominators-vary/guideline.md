## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

If denominators differ across groups, do not present event counts in a way that supports numerator-only comparison; visually encode the full denominators so “out of how many” is unavoidable.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Across both studies, when participants received only numeric statements and denominators differed, they systematically over- or under-estimated treatment effectiveness in the direction predicted by numerator salience (e.g., many overestimated when the untreated numerator was much larger). Adding icon arrays reduced these errors by shifting attention to denominators [@garcia-retameroIconArraysHelp2010].

- **The Principle:** Numerator salience drives denominator neglect
- **The Evidence:** Denominator size manipulations produced predictable misestimation under numbers-only; icon arrays attenuated this by highlighting denominators [@garcia-retameroIconArraysHelp2010].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Judge effect size (e.g., risk reduction) correctly rather than be impressed by raw counts.
- **Data Type:** Two-group frequency statements with unequal N.
- **Audience:** Broad public audiences; especially important when older adults are included.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Denominators are equal and explicitly constant across groups.
- **Reason:** When denominators matched, participants were much more accurate even without visual aids, so numerator emphasis is less likely to mislead in the specific way studied [@garcia-retameroIconArraysHelp2010].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Extra design effort and space to encode denominators.
- **The Risk:** Overly dense visuals may overwhelm some viewers if not kept legible.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Highlighting the numerator with bold type, callouts, or prominent placement while leaving denominators as small, secondary text.
- **Why it fails:** It amplifies exactly the attentional imbalance (numerator over denominator) that produced biased estimates in the studies [@garcia-retameroIconArraysHelp2010].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The largest visual/typographic emphasis is on the event count, not the total at risk.
- **The Test:** Remove denominators mentally—if the display still “works,” it likely permits numerator-only reasoning and risks denominator neglect.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add icon arrays that explicitly show totals for each group.
- **Best Fix:** Rebalance salience so the denominator is perceptually primary (as the whole array) and the numerator is a marked subset within it, consistent with the mechanism demonstrated in the paper [@garcia-retameroIconArraysHelp2010].
