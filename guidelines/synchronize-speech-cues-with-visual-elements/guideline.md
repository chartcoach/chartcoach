## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Cue objects, labels, and animations in the speech track exactly when they appear or occur on screen, and explicitly name what the viewer should attend to.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Eye tracking showed viewers re-fixated objects/labels when they were cued in speech (e.g., pyrimidine dimer; photolyase). Recall data showed propositions presented only visually (without speech reference) were poorly recalled, while speech-cued animations were well recalled [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997].

- **The Principle:** Cross-modal cueing to direct attention and support encoding
- **The Evidence:** [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Follow a narrated explanation and retain key steps.
- **Data Type:** Narrated animations or demonstrations.
- **Audience:** Particularly important for low domain knowledge audiences.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Silent viewing contexts (no audio) or environments where audio can’t be used.
- **Reason:** Speech cues are unavailable; rely on visual reveals instead.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More scripting and tight timing coordination.
- **The Risk:** Poor synchronization can create confusion or missed cues.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Generic narration that describes concepts without pointing to the on-screen element.
- **Why it fails:** Viewers may not attend to the correct object/action, harming recall [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users recall general ideas but misattribute what interacted with what (e.g., what light struck).
- **The Test:** Ask viewers immediately: “What did the narration just refer to on screen?” If they can’t point to it, synchronization is off.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add explicit naming in narration (“this photolyase…”, “the pyrimidine dimer…”).
- **Best Fix:** Re-time reveals/animation beats so each narrated reference coincides with the relevant on-screen change, then leave time for viewers to look [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997].
