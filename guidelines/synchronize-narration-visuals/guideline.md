## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Present visual illustrations and their corresponding verbal explanations at the exact same time. Do not show the animation first and then explain it (or vice versa).

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
If visuals and audio are separated by time (successive presentation), the learner must hold a mental representation of one while processing the other (Type 5 Overload).
*   **The Mechanism:** Simultaneous presentation eliminates the need for "representational holding" in working memory. The learner can immediately integrate the visual and verbal inputs [@mayer_nine_2003].
*   **The Evidence:** This is the *Temporal Contiguity Effect*. Simultaneous presentations produced robust learning gains over successive ones (Median Effect Size 1.30) [@mayer_nine_2003].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Connecting a visual event to its meaning.
*   **Data Type:** Narrated animations, videos, or interactive cause-and-effect diagrams.
*   **Audience:** Particularly important for users with low spatial ability who struggle to hold mental images in memory.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** The visual content is extremely complex and the user controls the pace.
*   **Reason:** If the user is segmented (see Guideline: Segmenting), small gaps are acceptable because the segments are short enough to hold in memory.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** Requires precise editing and timing of audio/video tracks.
*   **The Risk:** If the sync is slightly off, it can be more confusing than a complete separation.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Showing a full video clip, then stopping it to discuss what happened.
*   **Why it fails:** The user has to recall the video from memory to understand the explanation.
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Giving a long lecture intro before showing the chart.
*   **Why it fails:** The verbal information decays before the visual anchor appears.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Are you watching a silent animation, followed by a screen of text or a talking head explaining it?
*   **The Test:** Does the narration say "As you can see..." but nothing is happening on screen at that moment?

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Edit the timeline so the voice-over plays exactly when the motion occurs.
*   **Best Fix:** Ensure the narration explicitly references visual changes *as* they occur (e.g., "As the piston moves up..." while the piston moves up).
