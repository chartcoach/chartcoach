## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Use bar graphs when the viewer needs to focus on detailed relationships among individual data points.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Bars visually separate each data point as its own element, and participants preferred bars more for details than trends.

- **The Principle:** Use discrete marks to foreground point-by-point comparisons
- **The Evidence:** In Experiment 2, bar graphs were chosen more for details than trends, the opposite of line graphs [@levyGratuitousGraphicsPutting1996].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Inspect specific values; compare individual points carefully
- **Data Type:** A set of values across an ordered or categorical x-axis
- **Audience:** Viewers who must discuss “knitty gritty” specifics rather than overall shape

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The goal is rapid comprehension of overall trend or gist
- **Reason:** Participants preferred line graphs for trends/gist [@levyGratuitousGraphicsPutting1996].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Clear depiction of continuous trend/shape
- **The Risk:** The overall pattern may be harder to summarize quickly than with a line

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Using bars but expecting viewers to infer trend easily in a quick-glance situation
- **Why it fails:** Bars were not preferred for trend-focused scenarios in these studies [@levyGratuitousGraphicsPutting1996].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Viewers describe the chart as “busy” or fail to summarize direction quickly
- **The Test:** Give a 2-second exposure and ask for the gist; if the goal is gist and answers are poor, bars are likely the wrong choice.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** If you must keep bars, reduce visual complexity (avoid added 3-D) and keep comparisons straightforward.
- **Best Fix:** Switch to a simple line graph when gist/trend is the true goal; otherwise keep bars for detail tasks [@levyGratuitousGraphicsPutting1996].
