## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When communicating medical risk reductions to general audiences, present both the numbers and an icon array—do not rely on numbers alone.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

The review reports that low-numeracy participants showed much higher rates of over- and underestimation with inconsistent denominators when given numerical information only, and that icon arrays reduced these errors disproportionately for low-numeracy people [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand how much a treatment changes risk (relative risk reduction).
- **Data Type:** Event rates for treated vs. untreated groups.
- **Audience:** Patients/general public with variable numeracy; especially low numeracy.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** You have strong evidence your audience has high numeracy and you must minimize space to a single line of text.
- **Reason:** The paper’s main added value of icon arrays is largest for those disadvantaged by low numeracy; high-numeracy groups showed fewer baseline errors [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More screen/print space and production effort.
- **The Risk:** Viewers may attend only to the picture and ignore exact numbers (which may matter for some tasks) [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Simplifying by deleting denominators (e.g., reporting only “5 died vs 80 died”).
- **Why it fails:** It amplifies numerator salience and worsens denominator neglect, particularly among low-numeracy viewers [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Your communication asks for interpreting a proportion but provides only text/numbers.
- **The Test:** Pilot with low-numeracy users: if many answers track the larger numerator rather than the larger proportion, the design is failing [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add icon arrays that show the full group and highlight affected individuals.
- **Best Fix:** Provide paired icon arrays and keep the numeric ratios adjacent so users can cross-check picture and number [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].
