## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Use line graphs when you need viewers to quickly see general trends or the “gist.”

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Connecting points with a line emphasizes continuity and overall direction, and participants reliably preferred line graphs for trend-focused scenarios.

- **The Principle:** Use connected marks to foreground overall direction
- **The Evidence:** In Experiment 2, participants preferred line graphs more for trends than details; Experiment 1 similarly showed a simple line graph preferred for “gist” and “trends” scenarios [@levyGratuitousGraphicsPutting1996].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Identify direction, pattern, or overall movement at a glance
- **Data Type:** Ordered quantitative series (e.g., sequence of measurements)
- **Audience:** People who need a quick summary rather than point-by-point inspection

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The task is to inspect specific point-to-point values or detailed relationships among individual data points
- **Reason:** Participants preferred bar graphs more for details than trends [@levyGratuitousGraphicsPutting1996].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Emphasis on individual point comparisons
- **The Risk:** Viewers may gloss over exact magnitudes of specific points because the line privileges overall shape

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Switching to bars “because they’re familiar” when the question is explicitly about trend
- **Why it fails:** In these studies, bars were not the preferred choice for trend/gist-focused scenarios [@levyGratuitousGraphicsPutting1996].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Viewers’ eyes are pulled to individual columns rather than the overall path/shape
- **The Test:** Ask viewers to state the trend in one sentence after a 2–3 second glance; if they struggle, consider a line graph.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Convert bars to a simple line chart using the same x-order and y-scale.
- **Best Fix:** Use a simple line graph (avoid unnecessary 3-D effects) when the goal is “gist”/trend reading [@levyGratuitousGraphicsPutting1996].
