## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Do not default “Other,” “Multiracial,” or similar people-based categories to gray if it reduces their visual importance relative to other groups.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Gray commonly functions as a de-emphasis color; using it for a category of people can communicate “these people are less important,” which is especially harmful in sensitive topics like race/ethnicity.

- **The Principle:** Equal-importance encoding for people categories
- **The Evidence:** [@muth_race_ethnicity_colors_2024]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** View all groups as equally legitimate parts of the dataset
- **Data Type:** Race/ethnicity category breakdowns with an “Other”/“Multiracial” bucket
- **Audience:** General public and stakeholders represented by the data

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Your story explicitly requires de-emphasizing a category as contextual background (not as a value judgment)
- **Reason:** The post notes grayscale can be used as a backdrop for what’s not the focus, but frames it as an exception that should be used carefully [@muth_race_ethnicity_colors_2024].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** You lose a convenient neutral color that often helps reduce clutter.
- **The Risk:** Giving every category equal chroma can make the chart feel busier and require stronger layout/labeling.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Keeping gray but arguing it’s “neutral” and therefore fair
- **Why it fails:** Neutral in aesthetics can still be hierarchical in attention; gray often reads as “less important” in visual systems [@muth_race_ethnicity_colors_2024].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The “Other/Multiracial” segment fades into the background while other groups pop.
- **The Test:** Squint at the chart: if the gray category is the first to disappear, it’s likely being diminished [@muth_race_ethnicity_colors_2024].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Assign “Other/Multiracial” a non-gray hue with comparable saturation/lightness to other categories.
- **Best Fix:** Redesign the palette to equalize perceived prominence across categories (and validate with peers by asking whether any group feels visually sidelined) [@muth_race_ethnicity_colors_2024].
