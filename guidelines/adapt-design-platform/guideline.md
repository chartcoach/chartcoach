## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Adapt the visualization's complexity, format, and emotional tone to the specific publication medium. Do not publish the same graphic across print, mobile, and social channels without modification.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Different platforms dictate different modes of consumption. A visualization designed for the deep focus of a printed report fails on social media where attention spans are short and screens are small.

*   **The Principle:** Platform Context & Audience Expectation. Mobile and social platforms require "bite-sized," standalone graphics that prioritize emotional resonance and simplicity to grab attention [@schuster_being_2024]. Conversely, print and desktop environments allow for complex, analytical layouts that support deeper investigation [@koesten_what_2023].
*   **The Evidence:** Research indicates that social media audiences, particularly younger demographics, prefer quick, captioned summaries and bold visuals (large fonts, bright colors) [@schuster_who_2023]. Producers at major outlets like *Scientific American* explicitly alter designs to be concise for mobile while reserving complex layouts for print [@koesten_what_2023].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

*   **User Goal:** When maximizing reach and engagement across multiple channels (e.g., promoting a white paper on Instagram).
*   **Data Type:** Complex datasets that need to be simplified for top-of-funnel awareness but detailed for bottom-of-funnel analysis.
*   **Audience:** Divergent demographics; younger users often engage with platform-tailored summaries (e.g., TikToks, Instagram cards), while older audiences may engage more with full-length articles [@schuster_who_2023].

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

*   **Scenario:** Regulatory or Compliance Reporting.
*   **Reason:** Legal requirements often mandate a specific, standardized format regardless of how the document is distributed.
*   **Scenario:** Archival Data Repositories.
*   **Reason:** The goal is data preservation and fidelity, not engagement or narrative resonance.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

*   **The Sacrifice:** Production time. Creating bespoke versions for Instagram, a PDF report, and a mobile web view requires significantly more effort than a "create once, publish everywhere" approach.
*   **The Risk:** Fragmentation. Nuance may be lost in the "bite-sized" social versions, potentially leading to oversimplification of the data.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

*   **The Wrong Fix:** Taking a screenshot of a complex dashboard or print chart and posting it directly to social media.
*   **Why it fails:** The text becomes illegible on mobile screens, and the density of information overwhelms a user scrolling through a feed, failing to hook their attention [@schuster_who_2023].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

*   **Visual Sign:** Use the "Squint Test" on a mobile device. If you cannot discern the main message or read the title without zooming in, the design is not adapted for the platform.
*   **The Test:** Check the asset against the native aspect ratio of the target platform (e.g., 9:16 for Stories/Reels, 1:1 or 4:5 for Feed) to ensure no critical info is cropped or obscured by UI elements.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

*   **Quick Fix:** Crop the original visualization to focus strictly on the primary trend or insight, removing all secondary legends and annotations. Increase the font size of the main takeaway.
*   **Best Fix:** Create a "Data Card" series or a short animation specifically for social media that highlights one clear message per frame, leaving the complex analysis for the linked full report [@schuster_who_2023].
