## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
If you only have two values to show, consider mentioning the number in the article text instead of creating a pie chart.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
A two-value pie chart effectively only shows one value (your value and the difference between it and 100%). Visualizing a single number often adds unnecessary complexity when text suffices [@muth_pie_charts_2018].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Communicating a single percentage or binary split.
*   **Data Type:** Binary data (e.g., Yes/No, Remaining/Used).
*   **Audience:** Readers reading a narrative or article.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** You need to break up a wall of text with a visual element.
*   **Reason:** The chart serves an aesthetic/pacing purpose rather than a strictly analytical one.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** You lose a visual anchor on the page.
*   **The Risk:** The statistic might get lost in a dense paragraph.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Making the chart huge to justify its existence.
*   **Why it fails:** It wastes screen real estate on low-density information.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** A circle split into just two chunks.
*   **The Test:** Read the sentence containing the stat. Does the chart add any new information or context?

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Keep the chart small.
*   **Best Fix:** Delete the chart and style the number as a "Big Number" or bold text in the narrative [@muth_pie_charts_2018].
