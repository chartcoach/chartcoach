## Align narration with the moment the referenced object or action is shown <!-- role: advice -->

Cue objects, labels, and animations in the speech track at the moment they appear or occur in the visual presentation.

## Temporal alignment supports cross-media integration and guides what to look at next <!-- role: reason -->

When speech names a visual element while it is present, it can trigger re-fixation and help viewers integrate audio and visual information; when visuals are not referenced in speech, they are more likely to be missed or poorly recalled.

**Mechanism:** Spoken references act as attentional pointers, increasing the probability of fixations on the referenced visual element and supporting integration across modalities.

**Evidence:** Eye tracking showed viewers re-fixated labeled objects when they were cued in speech, and recall was better for propositions where animation/image content was reinforced by speech cues than for visuals shown without speech reference [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997].

**Notes:** This is a timing rule, not a scripting rule; the same words can fail if misaligned.

## When speech-visual synchronization is most important <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand an instructional sequence from a single viewing.
- **Task:** Map narration phrases to specific objects, labels, and actions.
- **Data:** Multiple possible referents (several objects on screen).
- **Chart Setting:** Narrated animation with reveals and moving elements.
- **Audience:** Low domain-knowledge viewers or anyone new to the terminology.
- **Success Criterion:** Accurate immediate recall of key actions and entities.

## When strict synchronization can be relaxed <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** The narration is providing a brief preview or summary rather than referencing a specific on-screen element. **Why:** Viewers do not need to bind the sentence to a precise visual referent.

## Tradeoffs of tight synchronization <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Authoring and editing become more time-intensive. **Risk:** Overly rigid timing can make narration feel unnatural. **Mitigation:** Keep alignment for key referential phrases even if surrounding narration is looser.

## Common mistakes in speech-visual timing <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **Mistake:** Narrating a concept while the corresponding visual is not yet present or has already changed. **Why it fails:** Viewers cannot reliably bind the audio to the correct visual referent.
- **Mistake:** Showing important visual changes without mentioning them in speech. **Why it fails:** Viewers may not infer the intended meaning and may not recall the proposition.

## Quick checks for synchronization issues <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Users recall an action but attach it to the wrong object (or report confusion about what the narration referred to). **Quick Check:** Scrub through the timeline and verify that each noun phrase in speech has exactly one clear on-screen referent at that moment. **Stronger Test:** Run a single-viewing recall test and inspect which propositions missing correspond to uncued visuals.

## What to do instead if timing cannot be perfectly aligned <!-- role: fix -->

- Add a brief pause or slow-down at the moment the speech references a complex element.
- Use a caption that repeats the key referential phrase while the element is visible.
- Reduce the number of simultaneous on-screen candidates at cue moments by staging reveals.
- Replace ambiguous narration with more specific referents that match the visual (e.g., name the object shown).
