## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

State the main restatement of what the data shows as a text annotation on the chart (e.g., “Only 4–5% are ever solved”), not as disconnected body text.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Placing the key message where the reader is already looking fuses interpretation with the visual evidence, so the chart “says clearly what it means” without extra hunting for meaning in surrounding paragraphs. This follows the approach in [@mintzer_simple_data_2024] of distinguishing “restatements of the core data” and tying them closely to the graphic via annotations.

- **The Principle:** Connect interpretation to evidence at the point of attention
- **The Evidence:** [@mintzer_simple_data_2024]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Quickly understand the central takeaway (especially a stark gap/difference)
- **Data Type:** Simple, high-contrast story in time (e.g., reported vs. solved counts across years)
- **Audience:** General readers who skim and need the point made immediately

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The chart has many equally important takeaways that would require many callouts.
- **Reason:** Excess annotations would clutter the chart and reduce readability, undermining the simplicity [@mintzer_simple_data_2024].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Less open space in the plotting area.
- **The Risk:** Over-assertive annotations can crowd the data or feel editorial if too many claims are added.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Putting the key takeaway only in a subtitle/paragraph (“only 5% solved”) while leaving the chart unlabeled.
- **Why it fails:** Readers must bounce between text and graphic; the data doesn’t “speak for itself” as intended in [@mintzer_simple_data_2024].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** A reader can look at the chart and still not know what the main point is without reading surrounding text.
- **The Test:** Hide the subtitle/footnote and ask: “Is the key takeaway still obvious from the chart and its on-chart text?”

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add one concise annotation stating the primary conclusion (e.g., the solved-share percentage).
- **Best Fix:** Place the annotation adjacent to the relevant area/segment so it visually “pins” the takeaway to the data [@mintzer_simple_data_2024].
