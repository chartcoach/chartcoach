## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Make your chart descriptions redundant. Repeat the subject of the chart (e.g., "ice cover," "size," "sq. km") multiple times within the chart area itself—on axes, in annotations, and in labels. Use different synonyms for the same concept.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Readers often skip introductory descriptions and dive straight into the visual. If the context is only in the title, they may become confused. Synonyms help different audiences connect with the data (e.g., "extent" vs. "cover").
*   **The Principle:** Redundant Encoding / Safety Net
*   **The Evidence:** [@muth_readers_time_2017] notes that readers use descriptions as a "Plan B." By placing terms like "ice cover" and "size" six times within the chart, the designer pushes information to the reader without forcing them to read the introduction.

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Immediate comprehension without reading pre-amble text.
*   **Data Type:** Any standalone visualization.
*   **Audience:** Broad audiences including non-native speakers (who may recognize one synonym but not another).

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** Highly minimalist design aesthetics or strict Tufte-style "data-ink" reduction where redundancy is viewed as clutter.
*   **Reason:** Redundancy increases word count and visual noise.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** Minimalism.
*   **The Risk:** The chart becomes text-heavy.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Putting all context in a single paragraph above the chart ("Plan B").
*   **Why it fails:** Readers habitually avoid reading blocks of text before looking at images.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Look at the chart axes and labels.
*   **The Test:** If you cover the title and description, does the chart still contain words that tell you what the subject matter is?

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Add units to your axis labels (e.g., "Size in sq. km" instead of just numbers).
*   **Best Fix:** Use annotations that include the subject noun (e.g., instead of "It drops in March," write "Ice cover drops in March") [@muth_readers_time_2017].
