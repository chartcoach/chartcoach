## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Add annotations to orient readers to your perspective and labels to point them toward potential solutions or answers.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Maps and charts can be abstract without specific reference points. "Bonus text" in the form of annotations helps frame the narrative. In the source example, an annotation ("My city of Winter Haven...") orients the reader to the author's problem, while city labels (e.g., "Chicago: 35 days") point them "towards potential solutions to your question" [@mintzer_fix_my_chart_text_elements_2024].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Telling a specific story or answering a personal question with data.
*   **Data Type:** Geospatial data or scatterplots where specific points represent key entities.
*   **Audience:** Readers who need guidance on where to look or what the data implies for them.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** A dashboard intended for neutral, unguided exploration.
*   **Reason:** Pre-selected annotations might bias the user's own discovery process.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** Clean whitespace on the map or chart area.
*   **The Risk:** Cluttering the visual field if too many annotations are added.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Leaving the map entirely blank of text to keep it "clean."
*   **Why it fails:** The reader sees the patterns but doesn't know which specific data points (cities) are the relevant takeaways.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Is the data area devoid of text?
*   **The Test:** Remove the title. Can the reader still guess the story based on the highlighted points? If not, you need annotations.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Add labels to the top 3-5 data points that represent the "best" or "worst" outcomes.
*   **Best Fix:** Add a narrative annotation linking the data to the user's context (e.g., "Start here" or "Escape to here").
