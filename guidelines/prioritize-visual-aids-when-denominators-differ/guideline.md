## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

If the options you compare have different denominators (different group sizes), add an icon array (or equivalent display that makes totals visible) instead of relying on numbers alone.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

The reviewed experiments show systematic distortions when denominators are inconsistent: people overestimate benefit when the treated group is smaller and underestimate when it is larger, consistent with denominator neglect. Adding icon arrays reduced these errors and increased correct risk-reduction estimates in the inconsistent-denominator conditions [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Estimate or compare treatment effectiveness (risk reduction) from study results.
- **Data Type:** Two ratios with unequal denominators (e.g., 5/100 vs. 80/800).
- **Audience:** Broad public, especially low-numeracy audiences.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Denominators are already equal and the task is simple enough that people can rely on numerator comparisons without error.
- **Reason:** The paper reports far fewer errors when denominators match, because proportional reasoning is less necessary for correctness in those cases [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Additional design and space to depict different group sizes.
- **The Risk:** If the audience has low graph literacy, the incremental benefit of the visual aid may be smaller [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Presenting unequal-denominator ratios in text only and expecting viewers to normalize mentally.
- **Why it fails:** It encourages focus on absolute event counts, producing predictable over/underestimation patterns [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Your communication contains ratios with different totals but no graphic that makes those totals visually salient.
- **The Test:** Replace your ratios with just the two numerators; if the message would “feel the same,” you’re likely not supporting denominator processing [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add an icon array for each option sized to the option’s denominator.
- **Best Fix:** Redesign the presentation so viewers can directly see each option’s total and affected subset before comparing options [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].
