## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

If you intend to communicate a value message (an editorial stance), use integrated imagery to make that message more salient.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Explain the principle at work here. Connect the rule to human perception or clear communication. Cite your sources.

- **The Principle:** Semantic cues in imagery make interpretive framing easier to notice and remember.
- **The Evidence:** Participants described and recalled “value messages” significantly better for the embellished Holmes charts than for plain equivalents in both description and recall measures [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

This advice is designed for specific moments.

- **User Goal:** Grasping the author’s intended point (not just the numbers).
- **Data Type:** Charts used in communication contexts where framing is part of the objective (e.g., magazine-style explanatory graphics).
- **Audience:** General audiences who may not do deep analytic reading.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

List the specific scenarios where you should ignore this rule.

- **Scenario:** You must avoid perceived editorializing and want to minimize value-laden interpretation.
- **Reason:** The paper highlights that strong imagery often conveys values and may bias interpretation, intentionally or unintentionally [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

Be honest about the downsides.

- **The Sacrifice:** Reduced appearance of neutrality.
- **The Risk:** The imagery may over-amplify one interpretation of the data, leading viewers to conflate the picture’s connotation with the data itself [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

Describe the common anti-patterns or "lazy fixes" people use that don't actually solve the problem.

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding emotionally loaded imagery when the goal is simply to inform.
- **Why it fails:** The study shows imagery increases detection/recall of value messages; if you didn’t intend one, you may still create one [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Viewers describe the chart in moral/emotional terms not present in the numeric claim.
- **The Test:** Ask viewers “Is the author trying to communicate a message beyond the data?” If most say yes, the imagery is functioning as framing (as in the study’s Holmes charts) [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Remove or neutralize imagery elements with strong positive/negative connotations.
- **Best Fix:** If a value message is intentional, ensure it is consistent with the textual framing (title/labels) so the chart’s argument is transparent rather than accidental [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].
