## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Make the decision-critical marks or regions visually salient (e.g., by highlight color or emphasis) so viewers’ attention lands there first.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

- **The Principle:** Salient items attract attention automatically, guiding what gets compared first.
- **The Evidence:** The paper describes salience-driven attention and shows that highlighting plus annotation can guide comprehension toward intended comparisons [@zacksDesigningGraphsDecisionMakers2020].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Finding “what matters” quickly (largest, biggest change, threshold exceedance).
- **Data Type:** Multi-element charts where many comparisons are possible.
- **Audience:** Time-limited decision-makers and novice viewers.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** When all items are equally important and highlighting would bias interpretation.
- **Reason:** Salience can distort priorities by making one element seem more important than the data warrants [@zacksDesigningGraphsDecisionMakers2020].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Neutrality; you are editorializing the viewing order.
- **The Risk:** Over-highlighting can cause viewers to ignore context or outliers.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Highlighting many elements at once.
- **Why it fails:** If everything is salient, nothing is; attention is no longer guided [@zacksDesigningGraphsDecisionMakers2020].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Viewers’ eyes don’t land on the intended feature; they start elsewhere (often at a large or bright but irrelevant element).
- **The Test:** Show the chart for 2 seconds, hide it, and ask “what stood out?” If the answer isn’t your target, salience is misallocated.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Reduce competing salience (mute noncritical colors, simplify background).
- **Best Fix:** Highlight only the primary comparison and support it with nearby annotation so the viewer knows why it’s highlighted [@zacksDesigningGraphsDecisionMakers2020].
