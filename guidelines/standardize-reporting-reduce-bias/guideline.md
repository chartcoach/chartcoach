## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Adhere to universally agreed, stereotyped definitions, outcomes, and analytical modes. Avoid creating multifarious outcomes or flexible analysis plans.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Flexibility in research designs, definitions, and analytical modes increases the potential for **bias ($u$)**—specifically, the manipulation of analysis to transform "negative" results into "positive" ones. According to Corollary 4 in [@ioannidis_why_2005], the greater the flexibility, the less likely the research findings are to be true. Fields with standardized conduct (like RCTs with common standards) have higher positive predictive values than fields with experimental or flexible methods.

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Designing a study or reporting results to maximize credibility.
*   **Data Type:** Studies with complex datasets allowing multiple analytical paths (e.g., observational epidemiology, microarrays).
*   **Audience:** Peer reviewers and consumers of evidence.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** Genuine exploratory research where no standards exist yet.
*   **Reason:** In nascent fields, standard definitions may not have been developed. (However, these should be explicitly labeled as hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory).

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** You lose the ability to "dredge" data for a "publishable" significant result.
*   **The Risk:** You may find no significant relationships (the "null field" scenario), which is scientifically valid but professionally unrewarding.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Selective outcome reporting.
*   **Why it fails:** Reporting only the "best" results (e.g., using the one analytical method that yielded significance) inflates bias and invalidates the PPV [@ioannidis_why_2005].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Does the study use unique, obscure, or "custom" scales/metrics rather than standard ones (e.g., death)?
*   **The Test:** Check if the protocol was registered upfront. If the analysis deviates from a pre-registered plan without explanation, flexibility bias is likely.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Report all outcomes and analyses attempted, not just the significant ones.
*   **Best Fix:** Pre-register the study protocol, including specific definitions and analytical modes, to eliminate post-hoc flexibility.
