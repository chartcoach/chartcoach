## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Write the chart’s headline as a clear statement of the one key takeaway (your hypothesis) before you refine the chart’s visuals.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

A declarative headline tells readers what to look for and anchors the entire design process around a single intended message, preventing aimless “pretty chart” exploration or paralysis about what to show, as described by Muth’s workflow in [@muth_better_charts_2017].

- **The Principle:** Headline-as-hypothesis (message-first chart design)
- **The Evidence:** [@muth_better_charts_2017]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand the chart’s main point quickly and remember it afterward.
- **Data Type:** Any dataset where multiple facts could be shown (especially exploratory or multi-series data).
- **Audience:** General readers who did not participate in your analysis and need immediate orientation.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** You are producing an exploratory chart for yourself or a team where the purpose is open-ended discovery.
- **Reason:** A fixed takeaway headline can prematurely narrow investigation; Muth’s approach is aimed at making charts for communication, where a point is required [@muth_better_charts_2017].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Less flexibility to showcase multiple interesting findings in one graphic.
- **The Risk:** If your headline claim is wrong or unprovable with the available data, you must revisit the premise and potentially redo the chart [@muth_better_charts_2017].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Using a vague, topic-only headline (e.g., “iPhone sales over time”).
- **Why it fails:** It doesn’t tell readers what matters, and it doesn’t guide later choices like comparisons, color emphasis, or annotations [@muth_better_charts_2017].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The headline reads like a label of the subject rather than a claim.
- **The Test:** Ask: “After reading only the headline, does a reader know what they should look for in the chart?” If not, rewrite it as a point [@muth_better_charts_2017].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Rewrite the headline into a single sentence that states the most important insight you want to communicate.
- **Best Fix:** Re-evaluate the chart’s role in the surrounding text/article and restate the headline to match that purpose, then adjust the chart’s comparisons, emphasis, and explanations to support it [@muth_better_charts_2017].
