## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

For explanatory communication, highlight the specific data pattern you want remembered (using a single accent color within a mostly neutral palette) and add a one-sentence annotation that states the conclusion.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Focusing directs attention and interpretation to one intended pattern among many plausible patterns, making that pattern more likely to be encoded and later recalled.

- **The Principle:** Guided attention and message anchoring
- **The Evidence:** Focused (decluttered+focused) designs led to higher recall of the focus-relevant pattern in both redraw-from-memory and typed free-response conclusions; participants were ~2.5–3× more likely to recall the intended conclusion vs. non-focused designs [@ajaniDeclutterFocusEmpirically2022a].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** “What should they remember?” after a slide, handout, or brief exposure
- **Data Type:** Charts where multiple interpretations are possible (multi-series lines, multi-category bars, stacked bars)
- **Audience:** Viewers who may not share the presenter’s “curse of knowledge” about which pattern matters [@ajaniDeclutterFocusEmpirically2022a]

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The purpose is exploration, brainstorming, or inviting the audience to surface their own interpretations.
- **Reason:** A single highlighted message can prematurely constrain interpretation and discussion [@ajaniDeclutterFocusEmpirically2022a].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Some neutrality—your chart becomes more explicitly rhetorical.
- **The Risk:** Some viewers may perceive the focused chart as having an “agenda,” which can reduce perceived trustworthiness for certain individuals even though the study found no strong overall trustworthiness differences by design [@ajaniDeclutterFocusEmpirically2022a].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Highlighting too many items (multiple accent colors) or writing a long paragraph of annotation.
- **Why it fails:** It recreates clutter and dilutes what should be the single “sticky” takeaway, undermining the attention-directing mechanism [@ajaniDeclutterFocusEmpirically2022a].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** No single element stands out as “the point,” or several elements compete as highlights.
- **The Test:** Ask a viewer (or yourself after 30 seconds) to state the conclusion in one sentence; if answers vary widely, the chart likely lacks focus (the paper found many different conclusions without focus) [@ajaniDeclutterFocusEmpirically2022a].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Convert everything to neutral tones and apply one accent color to only the key marks/series.
- **Best Fix:** Add a concise one-sentence headline/annotation whose key words match the highlight color, explicitly stating the intended conclusion [@ajaniDeclutterFocusEmpirically2022a].
