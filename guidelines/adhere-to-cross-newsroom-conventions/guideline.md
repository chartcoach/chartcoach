## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Follow the dominant color assignments used by other major newsrooms for political parties, even if those assignments seem arbitrary or lack historical logic. Do not invent new color schemes if a consensus already exists.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Consistency across newsrooms reduces cognitive load for the reader. While the assignment of Red to Republicans and Blue to Democrats in the US was originally arbitrary (and solidified partly because "Red begins with R"), it has become a rigid standard since the 2000 election due to television and internet reinforcement. Deviating from these established norms confuses readers who have been trained to associate specific colors with specific parties [@muth_partycolors_2018].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Quick recognition of political data.
*   **Data Type:** Election reporting in countries with established media consensuses (e.g., US, Germany).
*   **Audience:** A mass audience consuming news from multiple sources.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** A country with no established consensus.
*   **Reason:** In the UK, for example, color usage for parties like the DUP or UKIP is inconsistent across newsrooms. In such chaotic environments, you must make your own best judgment based on distinctiveness [@muth_partycolors_2018].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** Creative freedom and potentially logical semantic mapping (e.g., Red usually means labor/socialism internationally, but means Conservative in the US).
*   **The Risk:** You perpetuate a color scheme that might not make sense historically, but is required for current usability.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Swapping colors to align with international standards rather than local conventions (e.g., making US Democrats Red because they are the "left" party).
*   **Why it fails:** It fights against the ingrained mental models of the local audience.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Your map looks "inverted" compared to CNN, The New York Times, or local equivalents.
*   **The Test:** Compare your legend to the Wikipedia article for that election. Wikipedia often aggregates the most common consensus colors.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Swap the palette to match the "mainstream" media colors.
*   **Best Fix:** Audit the top 3-5 news outlets in the region and align your palette with the majority choice.
