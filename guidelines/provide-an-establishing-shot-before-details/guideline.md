## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Start with an establishing shot (overview/splash screen) that introduces the scope and topic before presenting dense detail.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

The paper highlights establishing shots and overview screens as visual structuring tactics that orient readers early and prepare them to follow subsequent narrative steps.

- **The Principle:** Early orientation reduces disorientation when complexity increases.
- **The Evidence:** [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Quickly understand “what this is” and “what’s covered.”
- **Data Type:** Dense, multi-part narratives (multiple sections/tabs/frames).
- **Audience:** General audiences, first-time viewers.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** A single annotated chart with minimal complexity.
- **Reason:** The cost of an extra entry screen may outweigh benefits when orientation is already obvious [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Takes screen/time away from data.
- **The Risk:** If the establishing shot is too generic, users may still not understand what actions to take next [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Dropping users directly into an interactive view with no narrative entry point.
- **Why it fails:** The paper’s case study analysis notes lack of orientation and no example story reduces engagement and comprehension [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users ask “What am I looking at?” before they ask substantive questions about the data.
- **The Test:** Hide all explanatory text; if the first screen doesn’t communicate topic and scope, you need a better establishing shot [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a short intro panel/title + one representative visual summary.
- **Best Fix:** Use a dedicated splash/overview with a clear “start” affordance and preview of sections (e.g., checklist-style overview) [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010].
