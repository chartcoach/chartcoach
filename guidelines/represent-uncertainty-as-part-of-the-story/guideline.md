## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Explicitly represent **uncertainty**—mark where values are **extrapolated/forecast**, state **margins of error or confidence limits**, and note doubts or inferential limits.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

The paper observes uncertainty is often conveyed through textual means (e.g., “leap-of-faith” annotations) and functions rhetorically to signal impartiality and temper interpretation.

- **The Principle:** Uncertainty cues limit over-interpretation and supports credibility
- **The Evidence:** [@hullmanVisualizationRhetoricFraming2011a]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand how strong a conclusion is
- **Data Type:** Forecasts, estimates, sampled data, model outputs, or any extrapolated series
- **Audience:** Non-expert readers who may otherwise treat point values as certain

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Purely descriptive counts with no estimation and minimal risk of inferential overreach
- **Reason:** Uncertainty messaging may distract when there is no meaningful uncertainty to communicate.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Simplicity and narrative punch
- **The Risk:** Readers may dismiss the entire graphic if uncertainty is presented without clear interpretation guidance.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Hiding uncertainty only in a footnote while the main story asserts a strong conclusion
- **Why it fails:** The rhetorical force remains in the main framing; uncertainty must be visible where conclusions are drawn [@hullmanVisualizationRhetoricFraming2011a].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** A forecast looks identical to observed data, or estimates look like exact measurements
- **The Test:** Identify the point where the visualization switches from observed to inferred—if a reader can’t see it, you’re not representing uncertainty.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a clear textual callout at the transition to forecast/estimate.
- **Best Fix:** Combine visible in-chart uncertainty cues (plus text) and explain what uncertainty means for the intended takeaway.
