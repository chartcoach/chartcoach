## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Avoid brown and olive hues when coloring race, ethnicity, or people-based categories.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Brown/olive shades can evoke skin tones and can be perceived negatively or less favorably than other colors, which risks making people unhappy with how their group is depicted.

- **The Principle:** Minimize color choices that trigger unwanted identity associations or dislike
- **The Evidence:** [@muth_race_ethnicity_colors_2024]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Compare groups while keeping depiction neutral and respectful
- **Data Type:** Categorical palettes for people-based groupings (race/ethnicity)
- **Audience:** General audiences, especially including data subjects

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Brown/olive is mandated by a strict existing brand palette and cannot be changed
- **Reason:** The post frames these as considerations rather than absolute prohibitions; constraints may force compromise, but you should mitigate via other design choices [@muth_race_ethnicity_colors_2024].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** You reduce available distinct hues, which can make large category sets harder to color.
- **The Risk:** Substitutes may become too similar or too pastel, affecting distinguishability.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Choosing “nicer” browns (warmer/caramel) and assuming the problem is solved
- **Why it fails:** Even pleasant browns can still read as skin-adjacent and carry the same issue for race/ethnicity categories [@muth_race_ethnicity_colors_2024].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** One or more categories are a brown/olive swatch that stands out as “skin-like” compared to the rest of the palette.
- **The Test:** Convert the palette into a row of swatches and ask, “Do any read as skin/earth tones tied to a group?” If yes, reconsider [@muth_race_ethnicity_colors_2024].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Swap brown/olive for a non-skin-associated hue with similar lightness (e.g., teal, violet, rose).
- **Best Fix:** Rebalance the full palette so categories remain equally prominent and culturally unburdened by skin-tone reminders [@muth_race_ethnicity_colors_2024].
