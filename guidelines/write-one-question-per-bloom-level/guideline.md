## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

For each visualization, ask **six questions total**—one per Bloom level—so the evaluation is comprehensive but not overwhelming.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

The paper positions a six-question structure as a practical alternative to high-volume think-aloud or “report everything” protocols, compressing evaluation into a manageable set while still sampling multiple facets of understanding. Their case studies use one question per level to reveal where designs differ and where they do not [@burnsHowEvaluateData2020].

- **The Principle:** Coverage with minimal burden
- **The Evidence:** [@burnsHowEvaluateData2020]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Compare two visualization designs (original vs redesign) for what they afford in understanding
- **Data Type:** Static charts (the paper’s demonstration uses static stimuli)
- **Audience:** Remote/online study contexts where long interviews are impractical [@burnsHowEvaluateData2020]

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** You need fine-grained diagnosis within one level (e.g., multiple distinct trend types)
- **Reason:** One question per level may not capture subtle affordance differences within that level [@burnsHowEvaluateData2020]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Less depth within each level because you’re sampling broadly
- **The Risk:** A poorly chosen single question can under-represent that level’s construct [@burnsHowEvaluateData2020]

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding many similar low-level questions (multiple value lookups) and calling it “comprehensive”
- **Why it fails:** You increase volume without expanding measured facets of understanding [@burnsHowEvaluateData2020]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Your questionnaire is long but repetitive (many retrieval/comparison items)
- **The Test:** Count tasks per level; if some levels have zero tasks, coverage is incomplete [@burnsHowEvaluateData2020]

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Replace redundant retrieval questions with one Comprehension and one Evaluation prompt
- **Best Fix:** Rebuild the instrument as six prompts aligned to the paper’s level definitions and example tasks [@burnsHowEvaluateData2020]
