## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When the goal is to preserve users’ accuracy for feature-to-part correspondences during a transformation, avoid rotation; use transformations that do not require part-by-part attentional tracking.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

- **The Principle:** Rotational transformations uniquely force a narrow attentional “spotlight” onto a single part, reducing binding capacity.
- **The Evidence:** A scaling (expand/shrink) imagery task produced high feature-part capacity (K≈1.6), similar to the static condition, while rotation and needle-rotation produced K≈1 [@xuCapacityVisualFeatures2015].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand that features stay attached to parts through a transformation (e.g., instructional diagrams, stepwise explanations).
- **Data Type:** Multi-part objects with categorical features (colors, tokens, labels) whose correct attachment is essential.
- **Audience:** Students/novices learning spatial or structural concepts.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Rotation is the concept being taught/tested and cannot be replaced.
- **Reason:** The task requires rotation specifically, even though it will constrain feature binding [@xuCapacityVisualFeatures2015].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Less faithful depiction of rotational phenomena if you substitute another transformation.
- **The Risk:** Users may not practice the exact rotational skill you ultimately care about.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Making rotation “easier” (e.g., slower) while still requiring multiple bindings.
- **Why it fails:** The core limitation is capacity for binding under rotation/tracking, which remains around one in these paradigms [@xuCapacityVisualFeatures2015].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users must imagine continuous rotation while remembering multiple attached features.
- **The Test:** Swap features between parts in a comprehension check; if users only detect swaps involving one salient part, rotation is likely driving attentional narrowing [@xuCapacityVisualFeatures2015].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Replace rotation segments with static before/after frames for binding-critical steps.
- **Best Fix:** Use a non-rotational transformation (e.g., scale change) when transformation is needed but rotation is not essential, keeping attention available for multiple bindings [@xuCapacityVisualFeatures2015].
