## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Shuffle your color assignments for every new project. Do not permanently link one racial category to one specific color (e.g., do not always make the Black category pink or the Asian category green).

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
All colors come with associations. By rotating colors, you prevent any single racial group from being permanently linked to specific adjectives (e.g., "competent" blue or "dangerous" red). It forces you to question "first thoughts" and unconscious biases regarding which colors "feel right" for a group [@muth_race_ethnicity_colors_2024].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Establishing editorial guidelines for a publication or dashboard suite.
*   **Audience:** Repeat readers who might learn to associate specific colors with specific groups over time.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** A single multi-part story or report.
*   **Reason:** Within a single document or article series, consistency aids readability. The "shuffling" should happen between different unrelated projects.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** You lose "brand consistency" for specific data categories.
*   **The Risk:** Designers must actively select new palettes rather than relying on a default template.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Always using the exact same "random" assignment (e.g., alphabetical assignment often results in the same colors).
*   **Why it fails:** It creates a systemic bias, even if unintentional.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Check your last three visualizations. Was the "Black" category the same color in all of them?
*   **The Test:** If you feel resistance to changing a color (e.g., "But yellow feels right for this group"), you are likely acting on bias and *must* change it.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Manually swap the color definitions in your visualization tool.
*   **Best Fix:** Create multiple distinct categorical palettes and rotate through them for different articles or reports.
