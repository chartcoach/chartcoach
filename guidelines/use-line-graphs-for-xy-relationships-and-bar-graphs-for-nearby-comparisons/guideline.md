## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Use line graphs when the task is understanding x–y relationships; use bar graphs when the task is comparing magnitudes of elements in close proximity.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

The paper cites findings that line graphs facilitate extraction of x–y relationships, while bar graphs ease comparisons of nearby elements, aligning chart choice with how people read these structures.

- **The Principle:** Task–chart cognitive fit
- **The Evidence:** [@bornerDataVisualizationLiteracy2019]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Relationship reading (line) vs. magnitude comparison (bar)
- **Data Type:** Quantitative y values over an ordered x (including time) vs. categorical comparisons
- **Audience:** General users performing common literacy tasks

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** When users must perform additional computation while reading (e.g., multi-step arithmetic from the chart)
- **Reason:** The paper notes comprehension becomes harder with computation, so you may need simpler restructuring or additional support rather than relying on chart type alone [@bornerDataVisualizationLiteracy2019].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Less stylistic variety; fewer exotic chart types
- **The Risk:** Misuse if x is not meaningfully ordered (line graphs imply continuity/order)

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Using a line graph for unordered categories because it “shows a trend”
- **Why it fails:** It suggests a false ordered/continuous relationship that the data may not support [@bornerDataVisualizationLiteracy2019].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users interpret slopes between categories that have no real ordering.
- **The Test:** Confirm the x-axis is meaningfully ordered/continuous for lines; if not, use bars for comparison tasks [@bornerDataVisualizationLiteracy2019].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Switch line-to-bar when the goal is comparing discrete categories.
- **Best Fix:** Re-derive the visualization from the stated insight need: relationship tasks → line; local magnitude comparison → bar (or other position-based comparison) [@bornerDataVisualizationLiteracy2019].
