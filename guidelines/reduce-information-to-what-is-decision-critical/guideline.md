## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Limit risk/benefit content to what is most critical for the patient’s decision; remove redundant outcomes and irrelevant options.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Too much information can distract users and reduce sensitivity to the magnitude of risk reduction; simpler, more focused presentations improved understanding and sensitivity in studies using Adjuvant! Online-style outputs.

- **The Principle:** Reduce cognitive overload to improve comprehension and discrimination
- **The Evidence:** Simpler presentations (binary choice focus; survival-only) improved understanding and sensitivity to risk reduction magnitude [@fagerlinHelpingPatientsDecide2011].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Choose among treatments where the realistic choice is effectively a small set (often two).
- **Data Type:** Multi-option decision aids with multiple endpoints (e.g., survival, cancer mortality, other-cause mortality).
- **Audience:** Patients facing emotionally and cognitively demanding cancer decisions.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** None stated in the paper.
- **Reason:** Not specified.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Less completeness; fewer secondary outcomes and edge-case options shown.
- **The Risk:** Omitting information that some patients consider personally important if you don’t first clarify preferences.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Showing every available option and every endpoint “for completeness” by default.
- **Why it fails:** It increases processing demands and can reduce understanding of what matters most [@fagerlinHelpingPatientsDecide2011].
- **The Wrong Fix:** Including redundant or clinically irrelevant options for a given patient.
- **Why it fails:** It adds noise and complicates comparisons [@fagerlinHelpingPatientsDecide2011].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users must parse multiple bars/rows for options they would never choose or that are not clinically relevant.
- **The Test:** Ask, “Could the decision be accurately made by comparing fewer options or fewer outcomes?” If yes, simplify.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Hide or collapse non-relevant options and redundant endpoints; present the primary decision as a smaller choice set.
- **Best Fix:** Design the display around the patient’s actual decision (often a binary comparison) and present only the key outcome(s) needed to judge trade-offs [@fagerlinHelpingPatientsDecide2011].
