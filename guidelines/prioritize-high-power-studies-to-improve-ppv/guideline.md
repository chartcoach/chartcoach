## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Increase statistical power (1 − β) to reduce false findings and raise the probability that a significant result is true.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

In the paper’s framework, PPV increases with power; small studies tend to have low power, making significant results less likely to reflect true relationships. The corollary explicitly links small sample sizes (lower power) to lower truth probability.

- **The Principle:** Power increases the true-positive yield relative to false positives
- **The Evidence:** [@ioannidisWhyMostPublished2005]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Produce findings that are likely to replicate
- **Data Type:** Any inferential study where significance is used to claim a relationship
- **Audience:** Study designers, funders, IRBs, editors

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Early exploratory/hypothesis-generating work where the purpose is screening rather than claiming truth
- **Reason:** Exploration may proceed with smaller studies, but claims should be framed accordingly. [@ioannidisWhyMostPublished2005]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Larger sample sizes, higher cost and time
- **The Risk:** Very large studies may detect statistically significant but trivial effects. [@ioannidisWhyMostPublished2005]

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** “We got p < 0.05, so power doesn’t matter”
- **Why it fails:** Low power inflates the proportion of significant findings that are false. [@ioannidisWhyMostPublished2005]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Small-N studies making strong claims about effects expected to be modest
- **The Test:** Ask whether the study plausibly had adequate power for the effect size of interest

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Increase sample size or reduce noise to raise power
- **Best Fix:** Reserve strong causal/clinical claims for adequately powered, low-bias designs (or meta-analyses of such studies). [@ioannidisWhyMostPublished2005]
