## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Ensure that higher probabilities or more severe risks are located physically higher on the vertical axis or display.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Individuals are sensitized to height as a metaphor for magnitude. There is an intuitive understanding that events located higher on a "risk ladder" or vertical graph represent a greater risk than events located toward the bottom [@lipkus_numeric_2007].

*   **The Principle:** Metaphorical Mapping (Up = More).
*   **The Evidence:** [@lipkus_numeric_2007] cites research on risk ladders showing that people easily comprehend this vertical arrangement.

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Comparing the severity of a specific hazard against other known hazards.
*   **Data Type:** Comparative risks (e.g., Radon levels vs. Smoking).
*   **Audience:** Homeowners, patients, laypeople.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** Text-heavy lists where reading order (top-to-bottom) is the primary interaction.
*   **Reason:** In standard reading lists, the first item (top) is often read first, but "ranking" rules usually dictate the "winner" or "highest value" goes on top anyway, so this rarely needs breaking.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** It may require vertical space that isn't available in landscape layouts.
*   **The Risk:** If the axis is not clearly labeled, users might assume a standard list format rather than a quantitative scale.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Inverting the y-axis so "Rank #1" (lowest risk) is at the top.
*   **Why it fails:** Visual magnitude conflicts with the conceptual ranking.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Look at the item at the top of the chart.
*   **The Test:** Is the item at the top the "riskiest" or "largest"? If not, is the axis clearly inverted?

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Sort data in descending order.
*   **Best Fix:** Use a "Risk Ladder" design where the y-axis explicitly represents the volume of risk/harm.
