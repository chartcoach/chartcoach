## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Use static visualizations without interaction techniques when assessing basic visualization reading and interpretation, and add simple grids to support value reading on axes when appropriate.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Interaction can add extra tasks (discovering and operating interactions) that are not part of “reading and interpreting visually represented data,” and users may have limited ability to detect interactions initially. VLAT therefore omitted interaction and instead used grids to support axis value reading while keeping the construct focused.

- **The Principle:** Reduce construct-irrelevant task demands
- **The Evidence:** [@leeVLATDevelopmentVisualization2017]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Measuring baseline comprehension of charts/graphs
- **Data Type:** Charts with Cartesian axes or common static encodings
- **Audience:** Non-expert users, first-time viewers

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** You are explicitly measuring ability to learn/use interactive visualization tools or interaction techniques (e.g., brushing, sorting).
- **Reason:** Then interaction is part of the skill and should be included as content. [@leeVLATDevelopmentVisualization2017]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** You cannot measure interaction-related literacy or tool proficiency.
- **The Risk:** Static-only items may under-represent how people actually explore modern dashboards. [@leeVLATDevelopmentVisualization2017]

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding “helpful” interactive features (hover tooltips, filtering) to make items easier.
- **Why it fails:** It changes what is being measured and can shift the task toward reading text/tooltips rather than interpreting marks. [@leeVLATDevelopmentVisualization2017]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Item success depends on finding/using a control rather than interpreting the chart itself.
- **The Test:** Try answering each item with interaction disabled; if the intended task cannot be completed, you are likely testing interaction, not literacy. [@leeVLATDevelopmentVisualization2017]

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Remove interactions and replace with static aids like gridlines for reading values.
- **Best Fix:** If interaction must remain, redesign the construct and blueprint to include interaction discovery/use as explicit assessed tasks. [@leeVLATDevelopmentVisualization2017]
