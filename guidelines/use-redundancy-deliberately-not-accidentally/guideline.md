## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Use redundancy (repeated labels, disaggregated similar values, repeated marks) only when you intentionally want to amplify emphasis—and verify it doesn’t collapse into noise.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

The paper identifies redundancy as a mapping rhetoric technique that can emphasize by repetition or disaggregation, but can also create visual noise; it changes rhetorical force by making certain concepts feel more prevalent.

- **The Principle:** Repetition increases perceived importance and salience
- **The Evidence:** [@hullmanVisualizationRhetoricFraming2011a]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Notice and remember a particular consequence or theme
- **Data Type:** Narrative graphics where emphasis is part of the communication goal
- **Audience:** Broad audiences scanning quickly

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** When precision decoding and low cognitive load are the priority
- **Reason:** Redundancy can obscure structure and overwhelm decoding [@hullmanVisualizationRhetoricFraming2011a].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Space and simplicity
- **The Risk:** Readers may perceive the visualization as propagandistic if repetition feels manipulative.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Repeating the same claim in many nearby labels instead of summarizing once
- **Why it fails:** The repetition reads as rhetorical amplification rather than evidence [@hullmanVisualizationRhetoricFraming2011a].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The repeated words dominate attention more than the data relationships
- **The Test:** Temporarily collapse repeated labels into one summary; if comprehension improves without losing meaning, redundancy was accidental.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Consolidate repeated labels into a single annotation.
- **Best Fix:** Keep redundancy only where it supports a clearly stated narrative emphasis, and counterbalance with provenance/definitions.
