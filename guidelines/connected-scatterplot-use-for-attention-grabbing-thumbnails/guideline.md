## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When the goal is to draw initial attention among multiple chart thumbnails, prefer a connected scatterplot over a dual-axis line chart.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Viewers preferentially inspected connected scatterplots earlier when given a choice set, indicating an attention/engagement advantage in initial viewing [@harozConnectedScatterplotPresenting2016].

- **The Principle:** Preferential viewing driven by novelty/challenge in visual form
- **The Evidence:** In a thumbnail-selection task, participants spent a larger share of early viewing time on connected scatterplots than on dual-axis line charts, indicating prioritized attention [@harozConnectedScatterplotPresenting2016].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Decide what to look at first (browsing).
- **Data Type:** Paired time series where both CS and DALC are viable encodings.
- **Audience:** Naive viewers choosing among multiple visuals (e.g., news or dashboard gallery view).

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The primary goal is immediate, effortless comprehension rather than attraction.
- **Reason:** Participants also reported connected scatterplots as the most difficult to understand in post-task discussion [@harozConnectedScatterplotPresenting2016].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Some readers may find the chart harder at first glance.
- **The Risk:** Attention may be driven by confusion rather than productive engagement.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Using a connected scatterplot without ensuring readability, assuming attention guarantees understanding.
- **Why it fails:** The same study context revealed perceived difficulty, especially with “loopy” connected scatterplots [@harozConnectedScatterplotPresenting2016].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Viewers click/open the connected scatterplot first but quickly back out or express confusion.
- **The Test:** In a small pilot, measure which chart people open first and ask which was hardest; if CS is both first and hardest, add guidance.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add brief on-chart instruction (“time follows the line”) and clear direction cues.
- **Best Fix:** Pair the connected scatterplot with focused annotations that explain the key shape features you want the viewer to notice [@harozConnectedScatterplotPresenting2016].
