## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Present complex multimedia explanations in bite-size segments and let the learner advance to the next segment.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Segmenting provides time for organizing and integrating selected words and images before new material arrives, reducing overload when essential processing demands are high in both channels.

- **The Principle:** Segmentation effect
- **The Evidence:** Better transfer occurs when a narrated animation is presented in learner-controlled segments rather than continuously [@mayerNineWaysReduce2003].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Build understanding of a multi-step process (not just recall).
- **Data Type:** Dense, fast-paced causal sequences (e.g., many steps, unfamiliar terms).
- **Audience:** Learners likely to be overloaded by continuous pacing.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The core learning objective depends on perceiving uninterrupted motion or timing relationships that would be distorted by pauses.
- **Reason:** Interrupting the sequence can change the nature of what is being learned (timing/flow).

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Takes longer and may feel less “smooth.”
- **The Risk:** Poor segment boundaries can fragment the explanation.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Keeping the presentation continuous but adding a “replay” button only at the end.
- **Why it fails:** It doesn’t create processing time at the moment it’s needed—between steps [@mayerNineWaysReduce2003].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Viewers miss steps because the next step starts before the previous one makes sense.
- **The Test:** If users frequently pause/rewind during specific transitions, those are likely segment points.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Insert short, explicit breaks after logical steps.
- **Best Fix:** Convert the explanation into learner-controlled segments (e.g., “Continue” after each step) so processing can complete before new information arrives [@mayerNineWaysReduce2003].
