## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Assign each political party a color that is sufficiently different from the other party colors, and keep those assignments consistent across all your election charts and maps. [@muth_partycolors_2018]

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Colors act as fast category cues; if party colors are too similar, readers confuse categories, and if assignments change between graphics or publishers, readers must relearn the mapping each time. The post argues that the specific hue matters less than (1) being clearly distinct and (2) being consistent across newsrooms, to avoid confusion. [@muth_partycolors_2018]

- **The Principle:** Categorical separability + learned color-to-category mapping
- **The Evidence:** [@muth_partycolors_2018]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Quickly identify which party is which across multiple election visuals and compare shares/regions without re-decoding.
- **Data Type:** Categorical parties (often multiple) shown repeatedly across charts/maps (results, seat shares, regional breakdowns, trackers).
- **Audience:** General audiences reading election coverage across time and outlets. [@muth_partycolors_2018]

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** You are producing a one-off, isolated graphic with no need for cross-chart or cross-outlet continuity.
- **Reason:** Consistency carries less value if readers won’t see the color mapping again; you may prioritize other constraints (e.g., strict house style) as long as categories remain distinct. [@muth_partycolors_2018]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** You may not be able to use parties’ exact brand/logo colors or traditional “political colors” if they collide with others. [@muth_partycolors_2018]
- **The Risk:** Enforcing consistency can lock in imperfect choices and make later changes disruptive (readers have already learned the mapping). [@muth_partycolors_2018]

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Using party brand colors even when major parties end up nearly identical (e.g., similar reds), reducing discriminability.
  - **Why it fails:** Readers can’t reliably tell parties apart at a glance. [@muth_partycolors_2018]
- **The Wrong Fix:** Letting color assignments drift between charts, articles, or publishers (same party shown with different hues).
  - **Why it fails:** Readers must repeatedly relearn the legend, increasing confusion and cognitive load. [@muth_partycolors_2018]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Two parties look “the same” in bars/legend chips or are easily confused on maps; or the same party appears in different colors across related visuals. [@muth_partycolors_2018]
- **The Test:** Place all party swatches side-by-side (or in a simple “color wheel”/palette overview) and verify no overlaps in appearance; then compare against your previous election graphics to confirm stable assignments. [@muth_partycolors_2018]

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Adjust hues/lightness/saturation so the closest pair becomes more clearly separated while keeping existing party-to-color mapping intact where possible. [@muth_partycolors_2018]
- **Best Fix:** Establish a single, documented party color palette for your newsroom (or project) and reuse it everywhere; only introduce new colors when a new party becomes relevant, ensuring it does not conflict with existing ones. [@muth_partycolors_2018]
