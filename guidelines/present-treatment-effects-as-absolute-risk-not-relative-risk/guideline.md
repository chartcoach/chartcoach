## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Present benefits and harms using absolute risks; avoid presenting relative risk (or NNT) as the main/only format.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Relative risk makes changes feel larger than they are and can lead patients (and even clinicians) to view treatments as more favorable than warranted, whereas NNT is difficult to understand.

- **The Principle:** Prevent framing-driven inflation of perceived effect size
- **The Evidence:** Relative risk increases perceived impact and favorability; NNT is hardest to understand and should not be the sole format [@fagerlinHelpingPatientsDecide2011].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Judge whether a treatment’s benefits outweigh its risks.
- **Data Type:** Risk reduction, side-effect risk, survival/mortality probabilities.
- **Audience:** Patients making “preference sensitive” decisions.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** None stated in the paper.
- **Reason:** Not specified.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Absolute changes may look “small” and feel less compelling.
- **The Risk:** Users may underestimate importance if you don’t also provide baseline risk and clear context.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Leading with “50% risk reduction” without stating the baseline and resulting absolute risk.
- **Why it fails:** It can bias patients toward treatment by exaggerating perceived benefit [@fagerlinHelpingPatientsDecide2011].
- **The Wrong Fix:** Reporting only NNT.
- **Why it fails:** Patients often do not understand NNT well enough to use it in decisions [@fagerlinHelpingPatientsDecide2011].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Statements like “cuts risk by X%” with no baseline risk and no “from A to B” values.
- **The Test:** Ask, “Could a reader tell what happens to 100 people with vs without treatment?” If not, it’s not truly absolute-risk communication.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add baseline and treated risks (e.g., “from 4 in 100 to 2 in 100”).
- **Best Fix:** Make absolute risk the primary framing everywhere; if you include relative risk or NNT, treat them as secondary and always pair with absolute risks [@fagerlinHelpingPatientsDecide2011].
