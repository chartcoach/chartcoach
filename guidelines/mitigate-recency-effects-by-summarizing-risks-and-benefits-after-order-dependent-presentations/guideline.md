## End with a brief recap of both risks and benefits <!-- role: advice -->

After presenting both benefits and harms (in any order), conclude with a short summary that restates the key risks and benefits together.

## Presentation order can bias judgments via recency <!-- role: reason -->

People weight the most recently presented information more heavily, which can shift perceived likelihood and worry even when both risks and benefits were read minutes apart.

**Mechanism:** A final recap refreshes earlier information in working memory, reducing imbalance caused by recency.

**Evidence:** When risks were presented after benefits, people perceived the risks as more worrisome and more common, consistent with a recency effect; a concluding summary is suggested to reduce this order-driven bias [@fagerlinHelpingPatientsDecide2011].

**Notes:** This is especially relevant in long conversations or multi-page materials where early content fades.

## When recap is needed <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Make an informed tradeoff between benefits and harms.
- **Task:** Integrate multiple attributes into a decision.
- **Data:** Multiple risk/benefit statistics spread across sections or pages.
- **Chart Setting:** Counseling sessions, brochures, or web pages where ordering is unavoidable.
- **Audience:** Patients under cognitive load or emotional stress.
- **Success Criterion:** Users recall and weigh both risks and benefits rather than mainly the last section.

## When not to follow it <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** Only one attribute (only risk or only benefit) is being communicated. **Why:** There is no balancing information to refresh.

## Tradeoffs and risks <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Adds time and repetition. **Risk:** A recap that selectively emphasizes one side can become another framing device. **Mitigation:** Keep the recap symmetrical in structure and wording across risks and benefits.

## Common failure modes <!-- role: mistakes -->

**Mistake:** Ending immediately after the last category presented (for example, finishing on harms). **Why it fails:** The last items can dominate perception due to recency.

## Quick tests <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Users remember only the last-presented set of outcomes. **Quick Check:** Ask users to name one key benefit and one key harm after the explanation. **Stronger Test:** Randomize order in a pilot and see whether judgments shift; if so, your recap is insufficient.

## What to do instead <!-- role: fix -->

- Add a final short section titled to indicate it contains both risks and benefits.
- Restate each key outcome using the same units and time horizon.
- Keep the recap to only the decision-relevant outcomes to avoid overload.
- Use a summary table when there are many attributes to refresh at once.
