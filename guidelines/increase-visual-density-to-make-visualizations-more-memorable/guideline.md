## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

If memorability is your goal, do not default to sparse layouts—use a higher visual density of elements.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Denser visuals provide more features and distinctive structure for recognition memory, improving detectability of repeats in rapid viewing.

- **The Principle:** Feature richness supports recognition
- **The Evidence:** High visual density (“3”) yielded higher memorability than low density (“1”) across the studied visualizations [@borkinWhatMakesVisualization2013a].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Recognize/remember the visualization later
- **Data Type:** Any where added elements can increase distinctiveness
- **Audience:** Viewers exposed briefly (e.g., scanning feeds, slide decks)

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The goal is not memorability-as-image but something else (e.g., controlled evaluation of other attributes).
- **Reason:** The study explicitly measures memorability of the visualization as an image and notes this does not equate to comprehension [@borkinWhatMakesVisualization2013a].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Minimalism and potentially speed of parsing.
- **The Risk:** Added density may create “clutter”; higher memorability does not guarantee users understand the underlying data [@borkinWhatMakesVisualization2013a].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Removing many elements to “clean up” a chart when the actual requirement is to be remembered.
- **Why it fails:** The paper found the opposite relationship for memorability: sparse charts were less memorable in aggregate [@borkinWhatMakesVisualization2013a].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Large empty areas with few marks; the chart looks “bare.”
- **The Test:** Compare side-by-side with other charts; if yours has noticeably fewer visual elements/structure, it likely has lower density than the more memorable examples [@borkinWhatMakesVisualization2013a].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add structural elements that increase feature count (e.g., more annotated components or additional distinct regions).
- **Best Fix:** Increase density in a way that creates distinctive structure rather than uniform repetition, making the overall image easier to discriminate in memory [@borkinWhatMakesVisualization2013a].
