## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Do not use randomly arranged stick-figure arrays when users must compare two risks or detect moderate differences between proportions quickly.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Random arrangements increase estimation error enough that viewers can confuse moderately different proportions. In the study, among respondents who saw both 29% and 40% random arrays, 26% ranked them incorrectly (estimating 29% as larger than 40%), showing that an 11-point difference may not be reliably visible at first glance with random layouts [@anckerEffectArrangementStick2011].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Compare “before vs after” risks or choose between options based on which proportion is larger.
- **Data Type:** Two or more icon arrays intended to support discrimination of differences on the order of ~10 percentage points.
- **Audience:** General health consumers, especially mixed numeracy/education populations.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The comparison is explicitly supported by something other than visual estimation (e.g., users are not expected to compare by eye).
- **Reason:** This paper tests unlabeled, time-limited first impressions; if the task is not visual comparison under time pressure, this specific risk may be less relevant [@anckerEffectArrangementStick2011].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** You lose the “random-looking” distribution some viewers find intuitive or realistic.
- **The Risk:** A more orderly design may be perceived as less true-to-life, even while improving comparability [@anckerEffectArrangementStick2011].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Keeping random layouts but increasing the number of icons, assuming “more icons = easier comparison.”
- **Why it fails:** The study used a large array (240 icons) and still found substantial confusion and variability with random arrangements [@anckerEffectArrangementStick2011].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users disagree on which of two random icon arrays shows the larger proportion.
- **The Test:** Give viewers a brief exposure (similar to first-impression use) and ask them to rank the two; if misranking is common, the design is failing [@anckerEffectArrangementStick2011].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Change both displays to sequential (contiguous block) arrangements.
- **Best Fix:** Standardize arrangement across all compared graphics (same grid, same orientation, same block placement) so differences are encoded by an easily judged contiguous area [@anckerEffectArrangementStick2011].
