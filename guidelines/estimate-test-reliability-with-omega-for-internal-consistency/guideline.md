## Estimate internal consistency with reliability coefficient omega for the final instrument <!-- role: advice -->

Compute reliability coefficient omega for the finalized item set to quantify the internal consistency of test scores.

## Why omega supports consistent score interpretation <!-- role: reason -->

Reliability reflects how much observed score variation is attributable to the underlying skill rather than random error. Omega provides an internal consistency estimate suitable for practical instrument development, supporting the validity argument that scores are stable and interpretable.

**Mechanism:** A higher omega indicates that items cohere around a common measured construct, reducing random measurement noise in total scores.

**Evidence:** The VLAT’s final 53-item form reported acceptable internal consistency using coefficient omega (ω ≈ .76), supporting that scores were consistent and not unduly driven by random error [@leeVLATDevelopmentVisualization2017].

**Notes:** Reliability evidence complements, but does not replace, content validity evidence.

## When to apply omega <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Report and justify score consistency for an assessment.
- **Task:** Reliability evaluation of a finalized (or near-final) item set.
- **Data:** Completed test response data from a tryout group.
- **Chart Setting:** Multi-item test intended to yield a total score.
- **Audience:** Researchers/practitioners interpreting and using scores.
- **Success Criterion:** Reliability high enough for intended decision use.

## When omega is not sufficient <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** You need evidence of stability over time (test–retest) rather than internal consistency. **Why:** Omega does not measure temporal stability.

## Tradeoffs <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Omega summarizes overall consistency but does not diagnose which tasks/types are weak. **Risk:** High omega can mask multidimensionality (multiple skills). **Mitigation:** Complement omega with per-task reviews and dimensionality analyses if needed.

## Common mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

**Mistake:** Reporting reliability without specifying the final item set used to compute it. **Why it fails:** Reliability is a property of scores from a specific instrument form and sample.

## Quick checks <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Total scores fluctuate heavily with small item removals or appear inconsistent with item performance. **Quick Check:** Compute omega and compare it after removing clearly problematic items to see sensitivity. **Stronger Test:** Evaluate dimensionality and/or compute reliability for meaningful subscores if the blueprint implies multiple skill components.

## What to do if omega is low <!-- role: fix -->

- Remove or rewrite items with negative or near-zero discrimination.
- Increase the number of quality items per task to reduce measurement noise.
- Tighten the construct definition and remove items that measure off-construct skills.
- Split the instrument into separate subtests if multiple distinct skills are present.
