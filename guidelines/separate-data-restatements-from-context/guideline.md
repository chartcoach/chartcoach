## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Draw a clear distinction between text that restates the core data and text that adds extra context. Place "restatements" (like specific values) as annotations directly on the chart, and move "added information" (like methodology or backstory) to the subtitle or footnotes.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Text serves different functions in a visualization. Keeping data-centric text close to the data reinforces the visual message, while moving context to the edges prevents clutter.
*   **The Principle:** Visual Hierarchy.
*   **The Evidence:** @mintzer_simple_data_2024 states that "restatements of the core data" (e.g., only 5% of thefts get solved) should be "closely connected to the chart itself," while "other information" should be moved further away to the subtitle or footnote to "influence how readers interpret the chart" without crowding the visual.

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** When the chart needs to tell a clear statistical story while also providing necessary background context.
*   **Data Type:** Visualizations accompanied by both statistical highlights (percentages, totals) and qualitative context (laws, suspect history).

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** When the context is the primary insight.
*   **Reason:** If the "added info" contradicts or completely changes the meaning of the visual data immediately, it may need to be an annotation to ensure it isn't missed.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** You cannot put every interesting fact directly in the center of the view.
*   **The Risk:** Readers might skim the chart and miss the context if they do not read the subtitles or footnotes.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Dumping all text (values, history, and methodology) into a single paragraph description or scattering them randomly as annotations.
*   **Why it fails:** It flattens the hierarchy, making it hard to distinguish between "what is happening" (the data) and "why it matters" (the context).

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Does your chart look like a document with a graphic inserted, or a graphic with supporting text?
*   **The Test:** Identify a piece of text. Is it a specific number visible in the chart? If yes, is it touching the chart? Is it a fact about the data source? If yes, is it in the footer or subtitle?

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Cut text that explains "why" or "who" and paste it into the chart description/subtitle.
*   **Best Fix:** Audit every text element. If it repeats a number shown in the line/bar, turn it into a direct label/annotation. If it explains the background, move it to the title block or footer.
