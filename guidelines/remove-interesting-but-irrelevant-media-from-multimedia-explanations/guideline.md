## Weed extraneous material from the lesson <!-- role: advice -->

Eliminate interesting but irrelevant material (such as background music or irrelevant video clips) from a multimedia explanation.

## Why concision improves transfer <!-- role: reason -->

Extraneous additions can prompt learners to devote limited cognitive resources to incidental processing that does not support understanding the target system. When capacity is diverted to processing irrelevant sounds or visuals, less remains for selecting, organizing, and integrating the essential explanation needed for meaningful learning.

**Mechanism:** Removing extraneous material reduces incidental processing, preserving working memory capacity for essential model-building processes.

**Evidence:** Learners achieved higher transfer from concise narrated animations than from embellished versions containing irrelevant sounds or clips, consistent with a coherence effect [@mayerNineWaysReduce2003].

**Notes:** The extraneous material can be topically related yet still not directly support the instructional goal.

## When extra media competes with core explanation processing <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand the mechanism, not be entertained.
- **Task:** Extract and integrate causal steps from words and pictures.
- **Data:** Dense explanations where working memory is likely strained.
- **Chart Setting:** Narrated animation/video with added music, decorative visuals, or irrelevant clips.
- **Audience:** Learners with limited prior knowledge or limited capacity.
- **Success Criterion:** Better problem-solving transfer from the core lesson.

## When some extra material may be unavoidable <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** The environment requires non-instructional elements that cannot be removed (e.g., mandated branding segments). **Why:** The rule cannot be fully applied, so other load-reduction methods are needed to protect essential processing.

## Tradeoffs of weeding <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Lower perceived “richness” or entertainment value. **Risk:** Over-pruning can remove context learners find motivating even if it is not instructionally essential. **Mitigation:** Keep only elements that directly serve the learning goal.

## Common coherence anti-patterns <!-- role: mistakes -->

**Mistake:** Adding “engaging” background music during the explanation. **Why it fails:** It can consume cognitive resources as learners process sound that does not support the causal model.

## How to detect coherence problems quickly <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Learners recall the added clips or sounds but cannot explain the system or solve transfer questions. **Quick Check:** Identify any audio/visual element you could remove without changing the causal explanation; those elements are candidates for weeding. **Stronger Test:** Compare transfer scores for concise versus embellished versions.

## What to do if you must keep embellishments <!-- role: fix -->

- Add signals that guide attention to essential words and visual elements.
- Segment the lesson so essential processing occurs in smaller chunks with less competition.
- Align any necessary text directly with the corresponding visual element to reduce scanning.
- Avoid redundant presentation of the same words in multiple formats during animation.
