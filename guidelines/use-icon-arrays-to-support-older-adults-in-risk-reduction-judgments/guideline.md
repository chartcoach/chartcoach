## Add icon arrays to numerical risk reduction information for older audiences <!-- role: advice -->

When your audience includes older adults, accompany numerical treated-versus-untreated outcomes with icon arrays representing each group’s total and affected cases. Use the arrays as a parallel representation rather than replacing the numbers.

## Why icon arrays help older adults without harming younger adults <!-- role: reason -->

Older adults show lower accuracy than younger adults when interpreting numerical risk reduction information, especially when the task requires integrating numerators and denominators. Icon arrays reduce the integration burden by making the denominator and subset relationship visually available, improving accuracy similarly across age groups.

**Mechanism:** The display offloads part-to-whole computation and makes the relevant class inclusion (affected within total) perceptually explicit, reducing reliance on effortful calculation.

**Evidence:** Across two studies, older adults were less accurate than younger adults when information was presented numerically, and adding icon arrays increased accuracy for older adults while also improving (or maintaining high) accuracy for younger adults, with no evidence that the benefit depended on age group [@garcia-retameroIconArraysHelp2010]. This pattern held in both a within-subject laboratory design and a between-subject web survey design [@garcia-retameroIconArraysHelp2010].

**Notes:** The benefit targets understanding (correct estimation of relative risk reduction), not persuasion.

## When age-sensitive risk communication applies <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand the magnitude of treatment benefit from outcome data.
- **Task:** Translate “x out of N” treated and “y out of M” untreated into an understanding of risk reduction.
- **Data:** Binary outcomes with denominators that may be equal or unequal across groups.
- **Chart Setting:** Patient-facing materials or clinical decision aids where older adults are common users.
- **Audience:** Older adults and mixed-age populations with varied numeracy.
- **Success Criterion:** Higher accuracy of risk reduction understanding for older adults without reducing comprehension for younger adults.

## When not to prioritize icon arrays for age-related support <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** The material must fit severe space constraints that prevent showing the whole denominator meaningfully. **Why:** Truncated or overly compressed arrays can undermine the part-to-whole cue that drives the accuracy benefit.

## Tradeoffs of adding icon arrays for older audiences <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** More layout space and potentially longer viewing time. **Risk:** If arrays are too small or visually dense, they may be hard to read for some older users. **Mitigation:** Keep arrays legible and ensure the numeric values remain available.

## Common ways this fails in practice <!-- role: mistakes -->

**Mistake:** Assuming a single numeric presentation is equally interpretable across age groups in risk reduction tasks. **Why it fails:** Older adults show lower accuracy with numerical-only formats and are more likely to misinterpret treatment effects when denominators must be taken into account [@garcia-retameroIconArraysHelp2010].

## Quick checks for age-related comprehension gaps <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Older users’ estimates of treatment benefit are systematically higher or lower when denominators differ, compared with equal-denominator versions. **Quick Check:** Test one scenario with equal denominators and one with unequal denominators; a larger drop for older users indicates vulnerability. **Stronger Test:** Run a brief comprehension check asking for expected deaths out of 1000 with and without treatment; compare derived risk reduction accuracy across age bands.

## What to do instead if icon arrays are not feasible <!-- role: fix -->

- Keep denominators equal across groups in the presented data so the comparison does not require adjusting for unequal totals.
- Provide the risk reduction in a form that reduces computation demands during interpretation (while still showing the underlying group outcomes).
- Separate the treated and untreated outcomes into clearly labeled blocks so each can be interpreted as a part-to-whole statement before comparison.
- Use a simplified set of scenarios per page/screen to reduce cognitive load during benefit interpretation.
