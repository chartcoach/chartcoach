## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Apply minimalist design principles moderately; ensure the design remains recognizable as a standard chart type (the MAYA principle).

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Users resist extreme deviations from what they consider "typical" graphs. However, they are open to *moderate* innovation.
*   **The Principle:** **MAYA (Most Advanced Yet Acceptable).** There is a "sweet spot" between novelty and typicality.
*   **The Evidence:** In experimental Condition 3, while 0% of participants chose the extreme minimalist graph (Graph D), approximately 41% preferred a "moderate" minimalist graph (Graph C) that increased the data-ink ratio but maintained symmetrical appearance and classical aesthetic attributes [@inbar_minimalism_2007].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Creating a modern, clean look without alienating the user base.
*   **Data Type:** Standard quantitative comparisons.
*   **Audience:** Users who may be bored by default Excel charts but confused by abstract data art.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** Pure data exploration where "ink" impacts reading speed (though this paper focuses on subjective preference, not speed).
*   **Reason:** The paper cites Gillan & Richman (1994) regarding performance speed, but focuses its own findings on the user's subjective *dislike* of the resulting aesthetic [@inbar_minimalism_2007].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** You cannot achieve the theoretical maximum "data-ink ratio."
*   **The Risk:** You settle for a "middle ground" design that is neither purely traditional nor purely efficient.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Adopting Tufte's "inter-quartile range" box plot style for bar charts.
*   **Why it fails:** The paper indicates that extreme versions of minimalism (Graph D) were consistently the least preferred option across all conditions [@inbar_minimalism_2007].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Does the chart look significantly different from what a user sees in a standard newspaper?
*   **The Test:** Compare your design to a standard Excel/PowerPoint output. If it is unrecognizable as the same chart type, you have gone too far.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Add back a bounding box or standard grid lines.
*   **Best Fix:** Use "Graph C" style: Cleaner than the default (less chartjunk), but distinct enough to still look like a bar chart [@inbar_minimalism_2007].
