## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Treat extreme percentages as vulnerable to systematic bias: expect low values to be read too high and high values to be read too low.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Signed error patterns showed consistent overestimation for low presented values (e.g., \<25%) and underestimation for high presented values (e.g., >75%) across multiple experiments and mark types (bars and dots), indicating a systematic bias rather than random noise.

- **The Principle:** Systematic memory/perceptual bias for extremes in magnitude reproduction
- **The Evidence:** Comparisons between lowest and highest quartiles (Q1 vs Q4) repeatedly showed low-value overestimation and high-value underestimation [@mccolemanNoMarkIsland2021].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Accurately interpret very small or very large proportions (near 0% or near 100%).
- **Data Type:** Percent/proportion values displayed as height/position.
- **Audience:** Any audience, especially when extremes drive decisions.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The decision does not depend on extremes (only mid-range values matter).
- **Reason:** The bias described is specifically about low vs high regions of the scale [@mccolemanNoMarkIsland2021].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** You may need to avoid strong claims based on extreme values alone.
- **The Risk:** Without accounting for bias, the audience may systematically misjudge near-zero or near-ceiling performance.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Assuming extreme values are “obvious” and therefore safe from misreading.
- **Why it fails:** The experiments show consistent directional errors at low and high ends [@mccolemanNoMarkIsland2021].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Your key takeaways rely on values in the bottom quartile (\<25%) or top quartile (>75%).
- **The Test:** Ask whether a small value being remembered a few points higher (or a large value a few points lower) would change the conclusion.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Avoid phrasing that depends on exactness at the extremes; communicate conclusions in ways robust to a few-point bias.
- **Best Fix:** Re-encode or re-structure the presentation so critical judgments don’t depend on single extreme marks being read with high fidelity, aligning with the bias patterns documented in the paper [@mccolemanNoMarkIsland2021].
