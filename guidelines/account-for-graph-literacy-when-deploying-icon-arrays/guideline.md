## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Do not assume icon arrays will help everyone equally—design and test with the audience’s graph literacy in mind.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

The review reports that graph literacy moderates the benefit of icon arrays: participants with higher graph literacy showed a larger increase in correct risk-reduction estimates when icon arrays were added than those with lower graph literacy [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Correctly infer treatment risk reduction from combined numeric + visual displays.
- **Data Type:** Proportions shown as icon arrays (including unequal denominators).
- **Audience:** Mixed-skill public audiences where graph literacy may vary substantially.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** You have verified (through testing or known context) that the target audience has high graph literacy for the relevant display type.
- **Reason:** The moderating concern is most critical when graph literacy is unknown or low [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More user research, iteration, and possibly additional explanatory elements.
- **The Risk:** Overengineering for low graph literacy can increase clutter and slow comprehension for high graph-literacy users [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding an icon array and assuming comprehension is solved without testing.
- **Why it fails:** The paper indicates some viewers lack the graph-related knowledge needed to translate the visual pattern into a correct interpretation [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users misinterpret which option is safer/effective even with the icon array present.
- **The Test:** Run a quick comprehension check: ask users to state the risk reduction implied by the display; large gaps between low- and high-graph-literacy users indicate the display is not universally accessible [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add clearer labeling around totals and affected counts so users can map icons to quantities.
- **Best Fix:** Redesign and test iteratively for low graph literacy, potentially supplementing with additional support or training-oriented presentation, as suggested by the review’s caution about limited benefit for low graph literacy users [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].
