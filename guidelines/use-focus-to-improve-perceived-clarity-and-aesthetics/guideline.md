## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When communicating a specific takeaway, add focus cues (a highlighted subset plus a brief annotation) to make the chart feel clearer and more visually appealing.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Focus cues reduce interpretive ambiguity (“why am I looking at this?”) and give the viewer a perceptual anchor, improving subjective clarity and aesthetics judgments.

- **The Principle:** Reduce interpretive search and ambiguity
- **The Evidence:** Ajani et al. found focused designs rated significantly higher than cluttered on aesthetics and professionalism, and trending higher on clarity; focused also tended to rate higher than decluttered on aesthetics/clarity (trend-level) [@ajaniDeclutterFocusEmpirically2022a].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Quick comprehension and a clear “so what”
- **Data Type:** Any standard explanatory chart used in presentations or reports
- **Audience:** General viewers evaluating charts quickly (e.g., meeting/presentation contexts)

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** You must maintain strict neutrality or avoid steering interpretation (e.g., contentious decisions, audits).
- **Reason:** Viewers may interpret emphasis and narrative text as persuasion rather than explanation [@ajaniDeclutterFocusEmpirically2022a].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Additional space for annotation and a stronger authorial voice.
- **The Risk:** If the highlighted takeaway is debatable, viewers may react negatively to the perceived “pushiness,” harming reception [@ajaniDeclutterFocusEmpirically2022a].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding emphasis without a clear statement (highlighting alone), leaving viewers to guess why it’s emphasized.
- **Why it fails:** The chart signals “this matters” but doesn’t resolve “what does it mean?”, limiting the clarity benefit the study associated with combined focus techniques [@ajaniDeclutterFocusEmpirically2022a].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Viewers comment that the chart is “vague,” “unclear,” or they describe different takeaways.
- **The Test:** Remove the annotation and see whether the highlighted element still clearly implies the intended conclusion; if not, you need the textual focus cue as well [@ajaniDeclutterFocusEmpirically2022a].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a short sentence stating the conclusion near the chart.
- **Best Fix:** Pair a single accent highlight with a matching-color annotation that explicitly names the intended pattern [@ajaniDeclutterFocusEmpirically2022a].
