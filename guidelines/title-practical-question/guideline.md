## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Replace descriptive, variable-based titles with the specific practical question the visualization is intended to answer.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
A visualization is often created to answer a concrete question that interests the creator or the audience. According to [@mintzer_fix_my_chart_text_elements_2024], the title is the "perfect place to spell out" this purpose. Explicitly stating the question or the answer provides "crystal-clear purpose and perspective," which is the foundation of a beautiful visualization.

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** When the chart is designed to solve a specific problem (e.g., "Where can I escape the heat?").
*   **Data Type:** Exploratory data that has been filtered to find a "sweet spot" (like "Goldilocks counties").
*   **Audience:** General readers who need immediate context on why the chart exists.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** Strictly academic or technical reporting.
*   **Reason:** In these contexts, neutral descriptions of variables (e.g., "projected temperature variances") are often required over narrative framing.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** You lose the formal, scientific tone of a traditional figure caption.
*   **The Risk:** The title may feel too subjective if the analysis is intended to be purely neutral.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Listing every variable and filter in the title (e.g., "Most Comfortable 4 & 5 Star Summer Places for Sunbirds by Lowest Number of 90F+ Summer Days").
*   **Why it fails:** It forces the reader to parse the methodology before understanding the goal [@mintzer_fix_my_chart_text_elements_2024].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Does the title look like a database query or a sentence?
*   **The Test:** Read the title aloud. If it sounds like a robot listing columns, it needs changing. If it sounds like a human asking a question, it passes.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Reword the title to ask the question you had in mind when finding the data.
*   **Best Fix:** Change "Variable X by Variable Y" to "Where can [Subject] find [Goal]?" (e.g., "Where can a Florida 'sunbird' escape the heat?").
