## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Design quantitative health communication as a whole system (patient + provider + artifact), not as a test of the patient’s math ability.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Good outcomes depend on how internal skills coordinate with external representations and social communication, so better artifacts and communication can compensate for weak individual skills and reduce cognitive burden.

- **The Principle:** Distributed cognition in health communication
- **The Evidence:** [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Using numbers to follow regimens, interpret personal data, or make treatment/screening decisions
- **Data Type:** Doses/schedules, lab values, risks/benefits, probabilities, tables/graphs in documents or devices
- **Audience:** Patients/consumers (often mixed literacy/numeracy), plus clinicians who must explain the information

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** You are building a formal assessment instrument intended to measure individual numeracy skill.
- **Reason:** The goal is to isolate the individual’s competence rather than optimize real-world performance. [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More design and testing effort across artifacts and workflows
- **The Risk:** Over-optimizing for ease may reduce fidelity or omit detail some users want [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Blaming misunderstandings solely on “low numeracy” and leaving confusing artifacts unchanged.
- **Why it fails:** Even highly skilled users can perform poorly with poorly explained or poorly designed representations. [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users can read values on a device but fail when the same values are shown in a different artifact (e.g., a table).
- **The Test:** Present the same quantity in two artifact formats and see whether users recognize them as identical. [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add provider-facing and patient-facing explanations that explicitly connect the number to the task (what to do with it).
- **Best Fix:** Redesign the artifact and the communication touchpoints together so the representation, navigation, and explanation jointly support the task. [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]
