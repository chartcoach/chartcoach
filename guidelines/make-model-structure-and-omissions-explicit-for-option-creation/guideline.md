## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When helping people create options, communicate an explicit model of how the system works (variables and causal links) and state what important factors or relationships may be missing or routinely ignored.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Option creation depends on understanding processes, not just outputs. Models carry uncertainty in variables, relationships, and omissions; neglecting those omissions can underestimate uncertainty and block discovery of better options [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Designing interventions, policies, or contingencies under uncertain system behavior.
- **Data Type:** Complex systems with interacting factors (e.g., risk pathways, communication networks, environmental systems).
- **Audience:** Officials, planners, and stakeholders developing new response options.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The decision is robust to any plausible model structure.
- **Reason:** If outcomes do not change under model variations, detailed structure may add complexity without decision value [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More effort to build, validate, and explain the model structure.
- **The Risk:** Overconfidence if audiences treat the diagram as complete rather than a best current representation [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Sharing only model outputs (predicted distributions) without showing the underlying structure.
- **Why it fails:** Users cannot see why uncertainty exists, where leverage points are, or which omissions might create surprises [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users ask “What drives this?” or propose options that contradict known causal links.
- **The Test:** Ask users to identify at least one leverage point and one major “unknown/omitted” factor after viewing the model; if they cannot, structure/omissions are not communicated [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a simple influence diagram of major variables and arrows, plus a short “not included” list.
- **Best Fix:** Provide the influence diagram paired with a sensitivity narrative describing which uncertainties and omissions most affect outcomes and which disciplines may be missing from the model [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].
