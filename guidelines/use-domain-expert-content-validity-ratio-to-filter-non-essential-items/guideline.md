## Use Content Validity Ratio (CVR) from domain experts to filter non-essential items <!-- role: advice -->

Have multiple domain experts rate whether each item is essential to the target construct and remove items with non-positive Content Validity Ratio (CVR).

## Why expert essentialness ratings strengthen content validity <!-- role: reason -->

Content validity depends on whether items are judged to represent the construct adequately. CVR provides a quantitative summary of expert agreement, enabling transparent retention decisions and creating explicit validity evidence for the instrument’s content relevance.

**Mechanism:** Aggregating independent expert judgments reduces idiosyncratic item inclusion and provides defensible, auditable item filtering.

**Evidence:** Items for VLAT were evaluated by five visualization experts using Lawshe’s CVR method, and items with CVR ≤ 0 were removed to retain only items judged essential by a majority of experts [@leeVLATDevelopmentVisualization2017].

**Notes:** CVR supports filtering; it does not replace empirical item analysis with test takers.

## When this applies <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Ensure items reflect the intended visualization literacy construct.
- **Task:** Screen an item pool before tryout.
- **Data:** Any, as long as experts can judge task relevance and clarity.
- **Chart Setting:** Test development workflow before pilot/tryout.
- **Audience:** Expert reviewers with relevant domain experience.
- **Success Criterion:** Retained items are broadly judged essential to the construct.

## When not to over-rely on CVR <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** Expert consensus is expected to be low because the construct is intentionally broad or contested. **Why:** CVR-based filtering may discard valuable diversity in item types.

## Tradeoffs <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Recruiting experts and administering reviews takes time. **Risk:** Experts may overvalue items aligned with their subfield and undervalue others. **Mitigation:** Use diverse experts (academia/industry) and follow with empirical item analysis.

## Common mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

**Mistake:** Keeping all items regardless of low or negative CVR “to have more data.” **Why it fails:** Non-essential items dilute construct representation and can weaken validity arguments.

## Quick checks <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Experts frequently rate items as “useful but not essential” or “not necessary.” **Quick Check:** Compute CVR per item and flag items with CVR ≤ 0 for removal or rewrite. **Stronger Test:** Re-run expert review after revision to confirm improved essentialness.

## What to do instead of keeping low-CVR items <!-- role: fix -->

- Rewrite stems to better match the intended task and chart encoding.
- Replace the visualization-task pairing with a more suitable one from the blueprint.
- Remove ambiguous wording identified by experts and re-test the revised item.
- Drop the item and add a new one targeting the same blueprint cell with clearer task alignment.
