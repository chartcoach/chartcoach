## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Use an open-ended, topic-only title (state the topic and scope) when you want the visualization to be read without a pushed stance.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Open-ended titles were the dominant form people produced as “default” and “neutral” titles, and they avoid highlighting one aspect that can cue a specific interpretation; slanted titles, by contrast, significantly shifted what viewers reported as the visualization’s “main message.”

- **The Principle:** Framing via title cues guides message extraction
- **The Evidence:** Viewers’ perceived main message differed significantly by title slant, even for the same chart (χ²=27.06, p\<.001) [@kongFramesSlantsTitles2018].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Form an initial understanding of what the chart is about without being steered to one “side”
- **Data Type:** Controversial, multi-variable charts where different variables can support opposing conclusions
- **Audience:** General-public readers, especially in news/social contexts [@kongFramesSlantsTitles2018]

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** You are intentionally writing an editorialized narrative visualization meant to advocate
- **Reason:** A topic-only title may under-communicate the intended argumentative message [@kongFramesSlantsTitles2018].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Less immediate takeaway; readers may only recall the topic rather than a concrete message.
- **The Risk:** The chart may feel “directionless” or require more effort to interpret [@kongFramesSlantsTitles2018].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Calling a title “neutral” while subtly selecting one side’s statistic (a disguised slant).
- **Why it fails:** Even “statistics-looking” titles can steer interpretation by emphasizing one variable/trend [@kongFramesSlantsTitles2018].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The title names a specific comparative claim that aligns with one policy stance.
- **The Test:** Ask: “Does this title merely name the topic/timeframe, or does it imply what conclusion to draw?” [@kongFramesSlantsTitles2018]

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Replace the claim with a topic + scope (e.g., topic and date range).
- **Best Fix:** Use a topic-only title and move any interpretive claim into clearly-labeled commentary elsewhere (separated from the title) [@kongFramesSlantsTitles2018].
