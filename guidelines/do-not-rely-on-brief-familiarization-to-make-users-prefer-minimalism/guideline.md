## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

If you need users to accept a minimalist chart, don’t rely on a brief one-off familiarization (e.g., a few data extraction tasks) to change their preference.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

In the experiment, a condition intended to increase familiarity—having participants perform several data extraction tasks using the minimalist chart before rating—did not change the overall preference pattern: participants still strongly preferred the standard bar chart. This suggests light exposure may be insufficient to overcome preference for familiar conventional formats.

- **The Principle:** Familiarity effects may be strong; minimal exposure may not shift subjective preference.
- **The Evidence:** Data extraction tasks did not modify the clear preference for the standard bar-graph [@inbarMinimalismInformationVisualization2007].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Driving adoption of a new chart style within a product, report template, or organizational standard.
- **Data Type:** Quantitative bar-chart style comparisons where a minimalist alternative is unfamiliar.
- **Audience:** Users likely encountering the minimalist chart for the first time [@inbarMinimalismInformationVisualization2007].

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Your aim is competence (ability to read the chart) rather than preference (liking).
- **Reason:** The paper’s manipulation and outcomes were about stated preference/ratings, not long-term learning or objective comprehension improvements in your context [@inbarMinimalismInformationVisualization2007].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More substantial onboarding or a phased rollout may be required if you still want to introduce minimalist formats.
- **The Risk:** Underinvesting in change management can produce persistent dislike and lower acceptance of the visualization [@inbarMinimalismInformationVisualization2007].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding a short “try it once” exercise and assuming preference will follow.
- **Why it fails:** In the study, that approach did not meaningfully shift preferences away from the standard bar chart [@inbarMinimalismInformationVisualization2007].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** After minimal training, users still request the “normal bar chart” or rate the minimalist version lower on beauty/clarity/ease.
- **The Test:** Measure preference before and after a brief familiarization task; if preference doesn’t move, assume brief familiarization is insufficient [@inbarMinimalismInformationVisualization2007].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Offer both chart styles and let users choose, rather than forcing the minimalist version after brief training.
- **Best Fix:** Use a gradual introduction with intermediate designs and repeated exposure, and re-test acceptance at each step instead of expecting a single familiarization activity to work [@inbarMinimalismInformationVisualization2007].
