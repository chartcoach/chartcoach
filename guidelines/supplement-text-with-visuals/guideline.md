## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
When creating patient education materials, specifically for diverse or older populations, accompany text and numerical data with well-designed pictures or visual aids.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
*   **The Principle:** Literacy Compensation. Populations with lower health literacy, numeracy, and graph literacy struggle to process raw text and numbers. Visuals serve as a cognitive scaffold, bypassing the need for complex calculation or high-level reading skills.
*   **The Evidence:** In a study of 502 veterans, @rodriguez_health_2013 found high levels of inadequate health literacy, particularly among African Americans and older participants. The authors conclude that "the use of well-designed pictures in educational materials can also potentially overcome some of these literacy limitations."

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Understanding health risks, medication adherence, or treatment options.
*   **Data Type:** Quantitative health information (probabilities, dosages) or procedural instructions.
*   **Audience:** Populations with high risk of low literacy, specifically older adults and minority groups who may have experienced educational disparities.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** communicating with highly specialized expert audiences (e.g., surgeons, researchers).
*   **Reason:** In high-expertise contexts, simplified pictures may add clutter or be perceived as patronizing, though clarity remains important.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** Layout space. Adding pictures requires significantly more page real estate than text alone.
*   **The Risk:** If the pictures are poorly designed or culturally ambiguous, they may confuse rather than clarify.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Using decorative clip art that does not relate to the data.
*   **Why it fails:** Decorative elements distract without providing the cognitive scaffolding needed to understand the quantitative concept.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Is the document walls of text with no visual anchor?
*   **The Test:** Remove the text. Can a user still understand the core concept or action required based on the imagery alone?

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Add an icon array or simple illustration next to the key numerical figure.
*   **Best Fix:** Redesign the layout to prioritize the visual explanation, using text as a caption or support rather than the primary lead.
