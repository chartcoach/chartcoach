## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

In review, explicitly distinguish what the visualization **denotes** (literally encodes) from what it **connotes** (implicitly suggests via cultural/perceptual codes).

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

The paper explains that viewing codes (cultural, perceptual, textual, aesthetic) guide interpretation, and connotation can transfer secondary meanings that alter the story without changing the denoted data.

- **The Principle:** Connotation arises from internalized conventions (“codes”)
- **The Evidence:** [@hullmanVisualizationRhetoricFraming2011a]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand the intended story without unintended value judgments
- **Data Type:** Any narrative visualization using metaphor, irony, or stylistic framing
- **Audience:** Diverse audiences with varied cultural associations

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** When the goal is explicitly satirical or metaphor-driven and the connotation is the message
- **Reason:** Removing connotation would remove the intended rhetorical layer [@hullmanVisualizationRhetoricFraming2011a].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Some expressive design freedom
- **The Risk:** Over-correcting may make the piece dull or reduce engagement.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Assuming “it’s just a chart” so only denotation matters
- **Why it fails:** The paper’s examples show connotation can add strong evaluative meaning (e.g., via titles and visual metaphors) [@hullmanVisualizationRhetoricFraming2011a].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Readers’ takeaways include moral/affective judgments not supported by the encoded quantities
- **The Test:** Write two sentences: (1) “This chart shows…” (denotation), (2) “This chart suggests that…” (connotation). If (2) is surprising or undesirable, revise.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Neutralize loaded titles/captions or add clarifying annotation that limits the implied judgment.
- **Best Fix:** Test alternative metaphors/layouts that preserve the denoted story while avoiding unwanted cultural associations.
