## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Choose chart types based on the judgment task: use bar charts for comparisons, line graphs for trends over time, and pie charts for proportions.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Different charts support different perceptual operations (comparison, trend detection, part-to-whole judgment), improving interpretability when aligned to the task.

- **The Principle:** Task-chart fit for perceptual efficiency
- **The Evidence:** [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Compare risks, see change over time, or judge proportions
- **Data Type:** Risk magnitudes across groups (bars), across time (lines), or part-to-whole (pies)
- **Audience:** Public/patients using decision aids or educational materials

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The audience is unfamiliar with a chart type used in your domain (e.g., survival curves for patients without guidance).
- **Reason:** Unfamiliarity reduces comprehension unless supported with explanation. [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** You may need multiple charts instead of one.
- **The Risk:** Overusing a “preferred” chart type can hide key patterns (e.g., using bars for time trends).

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Using a single chart type everywhere for consistency.
- **Why it fails:** Consistency can come at the expense of comprehension if the chart doesn’t match the task. [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Viewers ask questions that indicate the chart doesn’t support the intended reading (e.g., “Is this increasing over time?” on a bar chart of years).
- **The Test:** Write the user’s question in one sentence; verify the chart directly answers it.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Swap to a chart type that matches the core task.
- **Best Fix:** If multiple tasks exist, separate into small multiples (comparison chart + trend chart) with consistent scales and labeling. [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]
