## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Add a clear explanation of what the graph means and what conclusion to draw, especially for unfamiliar formats (e.g., survival curves).

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Instructions and verbal explanations improve comprehension and can change how viewers attend to key parts of the display.

- **The Principle:** Guided interpretation increases correct mapping from visual to meaning
- **The Evidence:** [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand risk over time or compare outcomes between options
- **Data Type:** Survival/mortality curves, complex genetic risk displays, or other unfamiliar graphs
- **Audience:** Patients/general public with limited graph literacy

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The audience is expert and the graph is standard in their practice.
- **Reason:** Extra explanation can be redundant and may distract. [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More space and time in the communication.
- **The Risk:** Over-explaining can reduce attention to underlying numbers/details.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding only a short caption with no interpretive guidance.
- **Why it fails:** Users may not know what visual features (slopes, gaps, endpoints) are relevant. [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users misread axes, confuse survival with mortality, or focus only on endpoints.
- **The Test:** Ask users to explain in words what the graph shows; if they can’t, add guidance.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a short “How to read this” note and one sentence stating the key takeaway.
- **Best Fix:** Provide step-by-step interpretive instructions integrated near the graph (and, when relevant, spoken explanation in clinical use). [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]
