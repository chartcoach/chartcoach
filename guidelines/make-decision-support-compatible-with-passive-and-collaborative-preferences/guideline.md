## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Design decision-support visuals so they remain useful whether the patient takes a passive or collaborative role, not only an active “self-directed” role.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

If most users behave passively in real encounters but prefer collaboration, decision materials that assume high patient autonomy or initiative may not be used effectively at the point of care.

- **The Principle:** Fit decision aids to actual decision-role behavior
- **The Evidence:** In this sample, most veterans reported a passive usual role (51%) and preferred a collaborative role (54%), with relatively few reporting an active role (usual 15%, preferred 12%) [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Supporting shared decisions during a clinical visit
- **Data Type:** Options and tradeoffs (benefits/harms) typically presented visually
- **Audience:** Veterans in outpatient primary care [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013]

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** An intervention is explicitly designed for self-management outside visits with motivated, highly active patients
- **Reason:** The assumed interaction model differs from the in-clinic shared decision setting described [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Less emphasis on deep exploration and more on guided conversation structure
- **The Risk:** Highly engaged patients may want more depth than the visit-oriented format provides [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Creating materials that require the patient to drive the comparison unaided (multiple steps, many choices, self-navigation)
- **Why it fails:** Many patients report being passive in actual encounters, so the material may not be acted upon without clinician guidance [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The aid reads like an independent worksheet rather than a clinician-patient conversation tool.
- **The Test:** Ask: “Could a clinician use this in 2–3 minutes to guide a collaborative decision even if the patient is passive?” If no, it mismatches observed roles [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a short “talking points” summary of the key comparison.
- **Best Fix:** Redesign as a conversation-first decision aid that supports collaboration during visits and does not require high patient initiative to extract the main point [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].
