## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Use pictographs (icon arrays of 100) instead of text or tables to present comparative risk/benefit frequencies when you need parents to understand both the overall takeaway and the numbers.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Graphical presentation increases the salience and interpretability of probabilistic information and improves both “gist” (which option is better/worse) and “verbatim” (the actual frequencies) understanding.

- **The Principle:** Visual + verbal reinforcement and increased salience of probabilities
- **The Evidence:** Parents randomized to pictographs had higher rates of adequate gist and verbatim understanding than those receiving text or tables [@taitEffectFormatParents2010].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand which option has higher/lower risks and benefits, and recall/identify the actual frequencies
- **Data Type:** Risks/benefits expressed as frequencies out of 100 (percent-style probabilities), including multiple outcomes
- **Audience:** Parents/guardians (mixed numeracy) considering pediatric clinical research participation

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** You cannot allocate enough space to show a 100-unit pictograph clearly.
- **Reason:** If the icon array becomes too small to parse, the advantage over text/tables may be lost (the paper’s tested pictographs were large and explicitly explained) [@taitEffectFormatParents2010].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More screen/page space than a compact table or sentence
- **The Risk:** If users are not oriented to how to read pictographs, comprehension gains may not materialize (the study provided explanations/examples before use) [@taitEffectFormatParents2010].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Switching from text to a table and assuming it will meaningfully improve understanding.
- **Why it fails:** In this study, text and tables produced similar gist and verbatim understanding, while pictographs outperformed both [@taitEffectFormatParents2010].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users still confuse which drug increases/decreases each outcome or cannot report frequencies accurately.
- **The Test:** Ask users to answer both (1) “Which option has more/less of outcome X?” and (2) “Out of 100, how many?”; if many miss these, the format is not supporting gist + verbatim comprehension as intended [@taitEffectFormatParents2010].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Replace the table/text block with a 100-unit pictograph per outcome using two colors for presence/absence.
- **Best Fix:** Provide pictographs plus a brief upfront legend/example explaining how to interpret the icon arrays (as done in the study) [@taitEffectFormatParents2010].
