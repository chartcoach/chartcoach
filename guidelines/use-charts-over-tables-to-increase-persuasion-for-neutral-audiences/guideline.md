## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When your audience is neutral or only weakly opinionated, present statistical evidence as charts (e.g., bar/line charts) rather than as tables.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Charts increase the likelihood of positive attitude change relative to tables for neutral/weakly polarized participants. In the experiments, chart-based treatments produced higher “positive change” rates than table-based treatments across three topics, and the aggregated attitude-change advantage favored charts.

- **The Principle:** Presentation format can change persuasion likelihood and attitude change (attitude is malleable when not strongly polarized).
- **The Evidence:** [@pandeyPersuasivePowerData2014]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Be persuaded by evidence in a message; form or shift an attitude.
- **Data Type:** Statistical comparisons/trends that can be expressed as bar and line charts (the study used both).
- **Audience:** People who start neutral/weakly polarized (e.g., pre-attitude in {-1, 0, 1} on a Likert-style scale).

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Your target audience is strongly negative toward the advocated position.
- **Reason:** For negatively polarized participants, tables showed higher persuasion likelihood and larger attitude change than charts in the study’s aggregated results. [@pandeyPersuasivePowerData2014]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** You may lose the ability to communicate exact numeric values as directly as a table.
- **The Risk:** If users distrust the data/source or question the message logic, the chart format alone won’t overcome skepticism. [@pandeyPersuasivePowerData2014]

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Assuming charts are universally more persuasive for everyone.
- **Why it fails:** The study found the direction of the effect depends on initial attitude; negatively polarized viewers often moved more with tables. [@pandeyPersuasivePowerData2014]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The audience segment you care about is not becoming more favorable despite seeing clear chart patterns.
- **The Test:** Measure pre/post attitude (same single-item question) and segment results by initial attitude; verify the “positive change” share is higher with charts for NWP users. [@pandeyPersuasivePowerData2014]

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Replace the tabular evidence blocks with bar/line charts while keeping the same explanatory text and numbers/trends.
- **Best Fix:** Run an A/B test (charts vs tables) with pre/post attitude and analyze specifically the neutral/weakly polarized subgroup to confirm charts increase persuasion likelihood in your setting. [@pandeyPersuasivePowerData2014]
