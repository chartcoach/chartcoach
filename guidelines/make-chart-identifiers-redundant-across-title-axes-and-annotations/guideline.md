## Repeat what the chart is about and what the units mean across the graphic <!-- role: advice -->

Use the same core subject terms and unit cues in multiple places (annotations, axis labels, and in-chart text) so readers are reminded what they’re looking at while they scan. Prefer simple rephrasing and synonyms rather than relying on a single title or caption to carry all context [@muth_readers_time_2017].

## Redundancy helps skimmers avoid getting lost and misreading the measure <!-- role: reason -->

Many readers start by looking at the marks, not by reading the chart description, so they can lose context about what the quantities represent. Repeating the subject and units throughout the chart keeps the meaning “in view” during scanning, reduces reliance on a single description as a fallback, and improves comprehension for varied vocabularies (including non-native speakers) [@muth_readers_time_2017].

**Mechanism:** Redundant cues reduce context loss during scanning and lower the chance that readers interpret the y-axis measure or topic incorrectly.

**Evidence:** Repeating the subject (e.g., “ice cover” and related phrases) and reinforcing units within the chart makes the description less necessary and helps readers understand what they’re seeing without confusion [@muth_readers_time_2017].

**Notes:** Using multiple phrasings can increase the chance that at least one term matches a reader’s mental model.

## Use this when readers might not read the caption or title first <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Maintain understanding of what the chart measures while scanning trends and annotations.
- **Task:** Interpret quantities correctly (what variable, what unit, what scope).
- **Data:** Measures that could be confused with related concepts (e.g., area vs temperature) or where units matter.
- **Chart Setting:** Embedded charts in articles, social sharing, presentations, and mobile where people “dive in” visually.
- **Audience:** Broad audiences, including non-native speakers and casual readers.
- **Success Criterion:** Readers can accurately name the measured concept and units without consulting the caption [@muth_readers_time_2017].

## When not to do it <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** Space constraints are so severe that repeated text would crowd the data and make the chart unreadable (common in very small mobile embeds). **Why:** Redundancy can become clutter that competes with the marks for attention [@muth_readers_time_2017].

## Tradeoffs you accept <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** More copywriting and layout work, and less room for the data marks. **Risk:** Excess repetition can feel wordy or visually noisy if not edited tightly [@muth_readers_time_2017]. **Mitigation:** Repeat only the subject and unit cues that prevent likely misunderstandings.

## What people often do wrong <!-- role: mistakes -->

**Mistake:** Putting the full context only in the chart title/caption and leaving axes and annotations context-free. **Why it fails:** Readers who skim the marks first may misunderstand what the values represent or forget the subject while exploring [@muth_readers_time_2017].

## Fast ways to verify it worked <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** A reader asks “What is this measuring?” or “What are the units?” after looking at the plot. **Quick Check:** Hide the title/caption and see if the in-chart text and axes still make the subject and units obvious. **Stronger Test:** Ask someone to describe what the chart is about after a brief glance without reading the caption [@muth_readers_time_2017].

## What to do instead if it’s not working <!-- role: fix -->

- Add short unit hints to the axis label (for example, include the unit abbreviation and what it represents).
- Use brief annotation text that repeats the subject term near the most important parts of the data.
- Swap in a familiar synonym where a key term may not be widely understood, while keeping the meaning consistent.
- If the chart is too cramped, reduce annotation count and reserve redundancy for the most error-prone concepts (subject and units) [@muth_readers_time_2017].
