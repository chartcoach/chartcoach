## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Do not simplify or restrict quantitative visuals solely because your audience is older; use direct literacy/numeracy/graph-literacy considerations rather than age alone.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

In this study, once relevant covariates were accounted for, age was not associated with differences in health literacy, objective numeracy, or graph literacy—so age-based design assumptions can misclassify individuals.

- **The Principle:** Demographic proxies can fail after accounting for confounders
- **The Evidence:** After adjustment, the study found no age differences in health literacy, objective numeracy, or graph literacy; only subjective numeracy differed unadjusted, and age effects were not significant in multivariate tests [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Tailoring patient-facing materials to ability
- **Data Type:** Any quantitative information shown via numbers or graphs
- **Audience:** Mixed-age veteran populations where cognition is screened/considered [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013]

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** You have strong local evidence that older patients in your setting have unaddressed sensory/cognitive impairments that affect reading/interpretation
- **Reason:** The study excluded some impairments and screened for cognition; different settings may have different constraints [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Less ability to “quickly” tailor by an easy demographic variable
- **The Risk:** Without measurement or testing, you may miss real ability differences that exist in your specific population [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Automatically removing graphs or numbers for older audiences
- **Why it fails:** Age alone did not predict lower health literacy, objective numeracy, or graph literacy after adjustment in this veteran sample [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Design decisions are justified with “older people won’t understand this” without evidence.
- **The Test:** Ask: “What measured or observed ability constraint is this change addressing?” If the only answer is age, you’re using a weak proxy [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Remove age-based assumptions from the design rationale and replace them with specific comprehension goals.
- **Best Fix:** Test comprehension across the intended audience and iterate based on observed misunderstandings rather than age categories [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].
