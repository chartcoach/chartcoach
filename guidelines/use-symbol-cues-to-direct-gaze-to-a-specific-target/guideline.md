## Use an arrow or similar symbol to point attention to a specific object or region <!-- role: advice -->

Use a symbol cue, such as an arrow, when you need viewers to shift attention to a precise object or location without relying on motion tracking.

## Symbol meaning can transfer attention from the cue to the intended target <!-- role: reason -->

Viewers may not fixate on the symbol itself for long; instead, they use the symbol’s learned meaning to shift gaze toward the referenced object or area, supporting the intended viewing sequence.

**Mechanism:** A directional symbol provides an explicit spatial instruction that resolves ambiguity about where to look next.

**Evidence:** Eye tracking showed viewers shifted from the main object to a newly revealed arrow and followed it downward, leading attention toward the next relevant visual element rather than staying on the symbol itself [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997].

**Notes:** Symbol cues are particularly useful when multiple items are visible and the next target is not moving.

## When symbol cues are most helpful <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Find the next relevant element in a complex scene.
- **Task:** Shift attention to a specific target area.
- **Data:** Multiple co-present objects with one intended focus.
- **Chart Setting:** Animated instructional visuals with staged reveals.
- **Audience:** Low domain-knowledge viewers or time-pressured viewers.
- **Success Criterion:** Viewers fixate the intended target promptly after the cue appears.

## When symbols may not work well <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** The audience cannot be assumed to understand the symbol convention. **Why:** The cue may not translate into the intended attentional shift.

## Tradeoffs of symbol cues <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Additional visual elements add clutter. **Risk:** Symbols can be mistaken as part of the domain content rather than a guidance device. **Mitigation:** Keep symbols visually distinct from domain objects and use them sparingly.

## Common mistakes with symbol cues <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **Mistake:** Using multiple simultaneous arrows or cues. **Why it fails:** The cue itself becomes ambiguous and reintroduces attentional competition.
- **Mistake:** Pointing to a region that immediately changes or moves away. **Why it fails:** The cue does not stabilize attention on a readable/inspectable target.

## Quick checks for symbol cue effectiveness <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Users miss the target element the cue was intended to highlight. **Quick Check:** Ask a viewer “What is the arrow pointing to?” immediately when it appears. **Stronger Test:** Verify via a small user test that the next fixations after the cue include the intended target.

## What to do instead if symbols are ambiguous <!-- role: fix -->

- Replace the symbol with a brief reveal or highlight directly on the target object.
- Add a synchronized speech cue that names the target while the cue is shown.
- Reduce the number of simultaneous on-screen candidates at the cue moment.
- Move the target label/caption closer to the object to reduce search.
