## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When a user must map numbers to physical units (e.g., servings), add direct visual cues that show what the unit means.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Users often struggle with applying serving-size/servings-per-container information; concrete visual cues reduce the inference and navigation burden.

- **The Principle:** External representations reduce cognitive load in quantitative interpretation
- **The Evidence:** [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Correctly translating label quantities into real portions or totals
- **Data Type:** Nutrition labels and other unit-based quantitative instructions
- **Audience:** Patients/consumers with mixed numeracy and document literacy [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The unit is already unambiguous and physically standardized in the user’s workflow.
- **Reason:** Extra cues may be redundant and consume limited space. [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More packaging/screen real estate
- **The Risk:** Poorly designed cues could imply false precision or confuse users if portions vary [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding only more text describing the unit.
- **Why it fails:** The difficulty is often in mapping text-and-numbers to action, not in reading ability alone. [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users misapply “per serving” vs “per container” or can’t show what a serving looks like.
- **The Test:** Ask users to demonstrate the serving amount after reading; failure indicates missing cues. [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a simple visual marker indicating serving boundaries (or equivalent unit cues).
- **Best Fix:** Combine unit cues with simplified totals and reduced extraneous information so users can compute and act correctly. [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]
