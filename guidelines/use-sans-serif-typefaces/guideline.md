## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Set your data visualization text—including labels, numbers, and tooltips—in a sans-serif typeface.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Sans-serif typefaces (like Roboto, Lato, or Arial) look cleaner and are generally easier to skim than serif fonts, particularly when displaying numbers. According to [@muth_fonts_2022], serif fonts can disrupt the visual flow required for quick data scanning, whereas sans-serifs provide the clarity needed for charts.

*   **The Principle:** Legibility and Skimmability
*   **The Evidence:** [@muth_fonts_2022] notes that major publications like *The Economist* and *Bloomberg* predominantly use sans-serifs for data.

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

*   **User Goal:** Rapid information retrieval and scanning.
*   **Data Type:** All charts and tables, especially those with dense numerical data.
*   **Audience:** General audiences reading on screens (web).

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

*   **Scenario:** Visualization Headlines or Titles.
*   **Reason:** Serif fonts can look more "classy," "traditional," or "serious/professional." They can help set a visualization apart or match an organization's branding if they already use serif text heavily [@muth_fonts_2022].
*   **Scenario:** Specific "Vibe" or Storytelling.
*   **Reason:** Some designers use serifs (e.g., Federica Fragapane) to create a "literary" or "map explorative" vibe, or handwritten fonts for a playful/comic feel [@muth_fonts_2022].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

*   **The Sacrifice:** You lose the "traditional" or "book-like" aesthetic associated with serifs.
*   **The Risk:** The design might look generic if using very common sans-serifs (like Arial or Roboto) without customization.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

*   **The Wrong Fix:** Using a "Slab Serif" or "Monospace" font indiscriminately.
*   **Why it fails:** While distinct, slab serifs can be heavy, and monospace fonts give every letter the same width, resulting in a "typewriter" look that may not fit modern design standards [@muth_fonts_2022].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

*   **Visual Sign:** Does the text have small decorative strokes at the ends of characters (feet)?
*   **The Test:** Zoom out. If the text looks cluttered or the numbers are hard to distinguish quickly, you may be using a complex serif font.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

*   **Quick Fix:** Switch the font family to a standard web-safe sans-serif like Arial, Helvetica, or Verdana.
*   **Best Fix:** Use a high-quality free sans-serif optimized for UI, such as Roboto, Lato, Open Sans, or Source Sans Pro [@muth_fonts_2022].
