## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Do not visualize a number in isolation; always add relevant comparison data to establish context and scale.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Visualizing a single number (e.g., sales for one year) provides no insight into whether that number is good, bad, or normal.
*   **The Principle:** Relatability and Relative Perception.
*   **The Evidence:** Comparisons turn raw data into insights. [@muth_better_charts_2017] states that you only understand success (e.g., iPhone sales) when seeing it alongside relevant benchmarks (e.g., other Apple products, global averages, or historical data).

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Proving a hypothesis (e.g., "This product is successful").
*   **Data Type:** Single metrics that lack inherent meaning without a baseline.
*   **Audience:** Readers unfamiliar with the baseline data.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** The number is an absolute limit or target known to everyone.
*   **Reason:** If the metric is "0 traffic fatalities," the goal is absolute, not relative.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** The chart becomes more complex and requires more data collection.
*   **The Risk:** Choosing the wrong comparison (e.g., comparing a luxury product to a budget product) can create a misleading narrative.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** plotting a single line or bar without any reference points.
*   **Why it fails:** The reader asks "Is this a lot? Is it a little?" and gets no answer [@muth_better_charts_2017].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Does the chart only show the subject of the article and nothing else?
*   **The Test:** Ask "Compared to what?" If the chart doesn't answer that, it's incomplete.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Add data from the previous year or a global average.
*   **Best Fix:** Determine the specific point you want to make (e.g., "growth" or "market dominance") and select the comparison data that best proves that specific point (e.g., historical returns vs. competitor sales) [@muth_better_charts_2017].
