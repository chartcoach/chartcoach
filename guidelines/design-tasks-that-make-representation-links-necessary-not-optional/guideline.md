## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Design function problems so that solving requires using explicit links between representations (e.g., inequality solution sets ↔ relative position of graphs; roots ↔ factors), not just symbolic procedures.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Mesa’s results show that success depended on whether students could connect symbolic and graphical representations. In the inequality task, groups who used the symbol–graph connection could produce solutions; a group that stayed in symbolic inequality manipulations failed despite having calculator access [@mesaSolvingProblemsFunctions2008].

- **The Principle:** Representational coordination is a bottleneck for problem progress.
- **The Evidence:** [@mesaSolvingProblemsFunctions2008]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Promote robust understanding of functions through coordinated representations.
- **Data Type:** Tasks on inequalities, intersections, transformations, and constructing functions from conditions.
- **Audience:** Teachers and curriculum/task designers.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The learning objective is deliberately procedural fluency in symbolic manipulation (e.g., practicing algebraic solving).
- **Reason:** Enforcing representational links may distract from the targeted procedural objective.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Tasks may become less straightforward to grade with a single “canonical” method.
- **The Risk:** Students may rely on visual resemblance without articulating underlying structure unless prompted [@mesaSolvingProblemsFunctions2008].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Provide representation-rich problems but allow progress via aimless parameter tweaking or purely symbolic routines.
- **Why it fails:** Students may not engage the intended connections; Mesa observed extensive trial-and-error guided by “resemblance” rather than structure until prompted [@mesaSolvingProblemsFunctions2008].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Student work shows either only algebraic transformations with no reference to graph meaning, or only graph matching with no structural explanation.
- **The Test:** Require students to state which graph feature corresponds to which symbolic feature (e.g., “these are the zeros because…”)—if they can’t, the task is not enforcing the link [@mesaSolvingProblemsFunctions2008].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add prompts that force mapping (e.g., “mark intersection x-values and explain how they appear in the equations”).
- **Best Fix:** Redesign constraints so the representation link is necessary to determine a correct answer (e.g., require verifying intervals of inequality via graph-relative positioning, not just endpoints) [@mesaSolvingProblemsFunctions2008].
