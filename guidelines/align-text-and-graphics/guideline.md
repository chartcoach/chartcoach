## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Place printed words physically close to the corresponding parts of the graphic they describe. Do not separate text and visuals (e.g., caption at bottom, image at top).

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
When text is separated from the graphic (Type 4 Overload), the user must scan back and forth to connect the label to the object.
*   **The Mechanism:** This scanning creates "incidental processing" (visual search). Integrating text reduces this load, allowing cognitive resources to focus on understanding the relationship between the text and the image (essential processing) [@mayer_nine_2003].
*   **The Evidence:** This is the *Spatial Contiguity Effect*. Integrated presentations outperform separated presentations (Effect Size 0.48) [@mayer_nine_2003].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Identifying parts of a system or reading data values on a chart.
*   **Data Type:** Diagrams, maps, charts, and static illustrations with labels.
*   **Audience:** All users.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** The graphic is too dense to accommodate text without occlusion.
*   **Reason:** If placing text on the graphic obscures critical data, readability suffers. (Consider connecting lines/leaders in this case).

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** Layouts become more difficult to design; "clean" whitespace is reduced.
*   **The Risk:** The graphic can look cluttered if not typeset carefully.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Using a legend (Key: A = Intake, B = Piston).
*   **Why it fails:** Legends force the eye to ping-pong between the key and the graphic, maximizing cognitive load.
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Placing all text in a scrolling box next to the animation.
*   **Why it fails:** This disconnects the visual from the verbal explanation.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Are you using a legend? Are captions located at the bottom of the screen far from the image?
*   **The Test:** Track your eye movements. If you look up and down repeatedly to understand one concept, the rule is broken.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Drag labels directly onto the image or chart.
*   **Best Fix:** Embed the text into the visual design, using lines or proximity to explicitly link the word to the object.
