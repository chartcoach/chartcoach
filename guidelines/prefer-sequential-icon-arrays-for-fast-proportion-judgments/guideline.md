## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Use a sequential (block/edge-aligned) arrangement of affected stick figures when you want viewers to quickly estimate the proportion.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Sequential arrangements create an immediately legible part-to-whole region, while random arrangements force viewers to mentally aggregate many noncontiguous marks, increasing error and variability under time pressure. In the study, random arrays produced larger and more variable inaccuracies than sequential arrays for most tested proportions under a 10-second viewing deadline [@anckerEffectArrangementStick2011].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Rapid “at-a-glance” estimation or comparison of proportions.
- **Data Type:** Part-to-whole percentages shown as colored icons (e.g., affected vs unaffected).
- **Audience:** Patients/consumers, including people with lower numeracy or education.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** You explicitly want to emphasize randomness/unpredictability rather than enable accurate proportion estimation.
- **Reason:** The paper notes that random arrangements can be perceived as more “realistic,” even though estimation accuracy is worse [@anckerEffectArrangementStick2011].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Sequential blocks may feel less “realistic” or less like outcomes are randomly distributed.
- **The Risk:** Users may infer clustering or a non-random process from the block-like presentation, even if none is intended [@anckerEffectArrangementStick2011].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Randomly scattering affected icons to “look more natural” while still expecting precise reading.
- **Why it fails:** Random scattering increases both overestimation and person-to-person variability, especially at low and high percentages [@anckerEffectArrangementStick2011].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users’ quick estimates swing widely for the same graphic, or skew high for random arrays.
- **The Test:** Run a short “first-impression” test (brief exposure) and compare variance/overestimation between random vs sequential versions; random should not be used if it meaningfully increases error [@anckerEffectArrangementStick2011].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Convert the random scatter to a contiguous block along an edge/corner.
- **Best Fix:** Use sequential arrays consistently across materials when proportion reading is the primary task, and reserve random layouts only when the message is about unpredictability rather than magnitude [@anckerEffectArrangementStick2011].
