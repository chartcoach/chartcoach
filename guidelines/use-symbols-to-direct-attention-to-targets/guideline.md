## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Use a directional symbol (such as an arrow) to redirect attention to a specific object or area when the next focus is not naturally salient.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

In the eye tracking results, the revealed arrow symbol pulled attention away from the prior focus and guided fixations downward toward the repaired DNA; users often followed the arrow rather than fixating on the arrow itself [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997].

- **The Principle:** Learned symbol meaning guides attentional shifts
- **The Evidence:** [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Find the next relevant object/state in a sequence.
- **Data Type:** Multi-region screens where important information is spatially separated.
- **Audience:** General; helpful when viewers may otherwise stay on the previous animated element.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The target is already strongly cued by motion/reveal and the symbol would be redundant.
- **Reason:** Extra cues can clutter and compete for attention.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Visual simplicity; additional on-screen elements.
- **The Risk:** Misplaced symbols can send attention to the wrong object.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Using a symbol without a clear target placement or direction.
- **Why it fails:** The symbol’s learned meaning won’t map cleanly, so attention won’t transfer as intended.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Viewers look at the symbol but not where it points, or they look somewhere else.
- **The Test:** Freeze-frame and ask “What should you look at next?” If answers vary, the symbol isn’t guiding reliably.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Reposition/rotate the symbol so its direction clearly aligns with the intended target.
- **Best Fix:** Reveal the symbol as a distinct step in the sequence, then reveal/animate the target immediately after to confirm the intended shift [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997].
