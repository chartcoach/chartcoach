## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Do not trust your own judgment that a graph is "clear." Run quick, informal experiments by showing variations to a few people and asking them to answer specific questions about the data.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Designers suffer from the "curse of knowledge"—once you know the message, you cannot un-know it, making it impossible to simulate the perspective of a confused novice. You literally cannot see the graph through the eyes of a non-expert without external feedback.

*   **The Principle:** User-Centered Design / The Curse of Knowledge
*   **The Evidence:** It is impossible to turn off your own expertise. Experiments, even simple ones, are the only way to validate that the visual pattern maps to the intended message for the audience [@zacks_designing_2020].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

*   **User Goal:** Ensuring the message is received accurately.
*   **Data Type:** Any.
*   **Audience:** Any audience that does not share the designer's exact background knowledge.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

*   **Scenario:** Personal visualizations.
*   **Reason:** If you are the only consumer of the chart, your own mental model is the only one that matters.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

*   **The Sacrifice:** Time. Testing requires iteration and gathering feedback.
*   **The Risk:** Skipping this step risks releasing a visualization that makes sense to you but is incomprehensible to the audience (e.g., the confusing hurricane cone of uncertainty) [@zacks_designing_2020].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

*   **The Wrong Fix:** staring at the graph longer or asking a fellow expert/colleague who already knows the data.
*   **Why it fails:** It reinforces the curse of knowledge rather than breaking it.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

*   **Visual Sign:** N/A (This is a process check).
*   **The Test:** Ask a user: "What is the main takeaway of this chart?" If their answer differs from your intent, the design has failed.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

*   **Quick Fix:** Show two designs to a colleague outside your project and ask "Which is easier to read?"
*   **Best Fix:** Conduct a formal A/B test or user interview using the specific questions you want the graph to answer (User-Centered Design) [@zacks_designing_2020].
