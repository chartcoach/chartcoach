## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When presenting treatment effects, display two separate icon arrays—one for the treated group and one for the untreated group—alongside the corresponding numeric statements.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

The task requires comparing two ratios (“deaths out of total”) across groups. Separate arrays make each group’s numerator and denominator jointly visible, reducing reliance on numerator-only comparisons that drive denominator neglect. In both studies, providing icon arrays for the two groups improved accuracy and reduced systematic over/underestimation when denominators differed [@garcia-retameroIconArraysHelp2010].

- **The Principle:** Visual alignment of two part-to-whole relationships
- **The Evidence:** Adding icon arrays (two arrays, one per condition) increased correct risk reduction estimates and eliminated the denominator-neglect pattern [@garcia-retameroIconArraysHelp2010].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Compare “with treatment” vs “without treatment” outcomes.
- **Data Type:** Two-group event frequencies (e.g., deaths) used to infer relative risk reduction.
- **Audience:** Patients/general public, including older adults.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The communication goal is only a single-group risk (no comparison).
- **Reason:** The paper’s demonstrated benefit is for two-group comparisons where denominators can differ and neglect can distort perceived treatment effect [@garcia-retameroIconArraysHelp2010].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Requires space for two visuals rather than one.
- **The Risk:** If poorly aligned or inconsistently scaled, the comparison may become confusing.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Combining both groups into a single mixed array without clear separation.
- **Why it fails:** It can obscure which denominator belongs to which numerator, weakening the part-to-whole cues that the paper leveraged to reduce neglect [@garcia-retameroIconArraysHelp2010].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Viewers can see deaths but can’t easily tell the total in each group separately.
- **The Test:** Ask users to report “out of how many” for treated and untreated—if either total is missed, the layout likely isn’t supporting the comparison.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Split into two clearly labeled arrays (treated vs untreated).
- **Best Fix:** Present the two arrays in a consistent side-by-side layout with each array encoding its own denominator and affected subset, mirroring the paper’s effective format [@garcia-retameroIconArraysHelp2010].
