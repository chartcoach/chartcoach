## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Ruthlessly remove any material—music, decorative graphics, or interesting trivia—that does not directly support the instructional goal.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Every sensory input consumes cognitive capacity. Extraneous material creates "incidental processing" demands. If the learner uses resources to process background music or irrelevant video clips, they have fewer resources for the essential material (Type 3 Overload).
*   **The Mechanism:** "Weeding" makes the presentation concise and coherent. It prevents the learner from being primed to process irrelevant details [@mayer_nine_2003].
*   **The Evidence:** This is the *Coherence Effect*. Students learn better when extraneous material is excluded (Median Effect Size 0.90) [@mayer_nine_2003].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Deep understanding of the core material.
*   **Data Type:** Multimedia presentations, educational videos, and data visualizations.
*   **Audience:** All audiences, particularly those with limited working memory capacity.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** Engagement/Entertainment is the primary goal, not learning.
*   **Reason:** If the goal is emotional impact rather than knowledge transfer, music and decoration can set a mood.
*   **Scenario:** Very low-load tasks.
*   **Reason:** If the task is incredibly simple, "seductive details" might increase arousal without causing overload (though the paper advises caution).

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** The presentation may feel "dry" or "boring" to a superficial observer.
*   **The Risk:** Designers often fear that without bells and whistles, users will lose interest.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Lowering the volume of background music.
*   **Why it fails:** Even quiet music requires auditory processing. The paper specifically notes that adding background music negatively impacts learning.
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Adding "interesting facts" to spice up a chart.
*   **Why it fails:** These distract from the causal chain being explained.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Are there decorative icons, background loops, or music playing?
*   **The Test:** Ask, "If I remove this element, does the explanation break?" If the answer is no, remove it.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Mute background music. Delete decorative clip art.
*   **Best Fix:** Re-edit the content to be as lean as possible, focusing exclusively on the causal chain of the topic.
