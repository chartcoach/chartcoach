## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

If your title does not fully explain what the viewer should take away, add supporting text (e.g., a short paragraph or labels) that makes the meaning explicit.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

When viewers can’t get enough from the title, they shift attention to other textual elements, and those elements are then what gets recalled.

- **The Principle:** Semantic scaffolding for encoding and recall
- **The Evidence:** During recognition, titles had the highest total fixation time, and when titles were not sufficient (e.g., short/less informative), participants relied on other text such as paragraphs; in recall, textual elements (title, labels, paragraphs) were most frequently mentioned overall, with news visualizations especially relying on paragraph text [@borkinMemorabilityVisualizationRecognition2016].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understanding what the chart shows and recalling the message later
- **Data Type:** Any, especially charts with ambiguous encodings or unfamiliar topics
- **Audience:** General public contexts (news, infographics) where message clarity is paramount

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The visualization is meant to be explored under strict space constraints where added text would materially reduce readability of the data encodings.
- **Reason:** Added text consumes space and can compete for attention; the paper shows people spend substantial time on text [@borkinMemorabilityVisualizationRecognition2016].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Visual simplicity and available plotting area.
- **The Risk:** Viewers may over-index on prose and under-examine the data [@borkinMemorabilityVisualizationRecognition2016].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding lots of prose that repeats context but still doesn’t explain the takeaway.
- **Why it fails:** Text is heavily fixated; if it doesn’t encode the message, attention is spent without improving recall quality [@borkinMemorabilityVisualizationRecognition2016].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The chart’s meaning is unclear without reading external context.
- **The Test:** Hide surrounding article text and ask if the in-chart text (title + supporting text) is enough to summarize the message [@borkinMemorabilityVisualizationRecognition2016].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a one-sentence supporting statement clarifying what the data represents.
- **Best Fix:** Combine a message-bearing title with concise supporting text (paragraph/labels) that resolves ambiguity and highlights the key point [@borkinMemorabilityVisualizationRecognition2016].
