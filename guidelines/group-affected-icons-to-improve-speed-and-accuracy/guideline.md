## Cluster affected icons to support fast, accurate reading <!-- role: advice -->

In icon arrays that represent how many people are affected in a population, group the affected icons together when you want faster processing and more accurate estimates.

## Why clustered icons reduce counting and scanning burden <!-- role: reason -->

Scattered affected marks require more visual search and mental aggregation. Grouping creates a single contiguous region that can be rapidly approximated and counted, improving speed and accuracy.

**Mechanism:** Clustering reduces visual search demands and supports quick numerosity judgments by turning many discrete marks into a coherent block.

**Evidence:** Grouping affected individuals together in icon displays is identified as the approach that best supports accuracy in numeric estimates and speed of processing compared with distributing affected icons throughout the array. [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]

**Notes:** Random distribution can communicate randomness, but it is not optimal for speed/accuracy goals.

## When to cluster icons <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand the magnitude of a risk and compare options quickly.
- **Task:** Estimate “how many out of N” are affected; compare two risks.
- **Data:** Frequencies that can be mapped to an icon grid.
- **Chart Setting:** Decision aids, brochures, screens with limited time for reading.
- **Audience:** General public; mixed numeracy and graph literacy.
- **Success Criterion:** Faster, more accurate recall or estimation of the displayed frequency.

## When not to follow it <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** The communication goal is to emphasize the randomness of who is affected. **Why:** Clustering can imply systematic grouping rather than random occurrence.

## Tradeoffs and risks <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** You may lose an intuitive cue about randomness. **Risk:** Viewers might infer that affected people share a location or subgroup characteristic. **Mitigation:** Add a caption clarifying that the cluster is a display choice, not a real grouping.

## Common failure modes <!-- role: mistakes -->

**Mistake:** Randomly sprinkling affected icons while asking viewers to estimate exact counts. **Why it fails:** It increases scanning effort and reduces speed and accuracy.

## Quick tests <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Readers take noticeably longer to answer “how many are affected?” than expected for the grid size. **Quick Check:** Time a colleague estimating the count from the display; clustered versions should be faster. **Stronger Test:** Compare error rates on “how many out of N” questions between clustered and scattered layouts.

## What to do instead <!-- role: fix -->

- Cluster affected icons into a contiguous block within the grid.
- Add a numeric label alongside the icons (e.g., “7 out of 100”) to anchor interpretation.
- Reduce grid size or change the denominator if the array is too dense to read.
- Switch to a bar-based display if exact counting remains difficult at the chosen scale.
