## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Include relevant pictograms or human-recognizable objects (HROs) in your visualization to serve as visual hooks.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Contrary to minimal design philosophies that view illustrations as "chart junk," [@borkin_beyond_2016] found that pictograms do not hinder the memory or understanding of a visualization. In fact, visualizations with pictograms had statistically higher description quality scores. These objects act as "visual associations" that aid in retrieving the visualization from memory. Eye-tracking showed that while pictograms attract attention, they do not distract users from processing the data or message.

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Creating memorable content that needs to be recalled later.
*   **Data Type:** Thematic data where visual metaphors are clear (e.g., using icons of people for demographic data).
*   **Audience:** General audiences, infographics, and media publications.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** The pictogram is semantically unrelated to the data.
*   **Reason:** [@borkin_beyond_2016] noted that pictograms unrelated to the data or message can produce confusion and misdirect attention.
*   **Scenario:** High-precision scientific analysis.
*   **Reason:** While distinctiveness helps recall, standard scientific conventions often prioritize density and precision over visual hooks.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** Screen real estate and "data-ink" ratio.
*   **The Risk:** If the design quality is low or the metaphor is weak, the visualization may be perceived as unprofessional or confusing.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Adding generic clip art that doesn't match the subject matter.
*   **Why it fails:** The benefit comes from semantic association (linking the visual to the topic). Random art creates cognitive interference.
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Using pictograms to replace axes or labels entirely.
*   **Why it fails:** Pictograms are hooks, not replacements for necessary context.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Are there recognizable objects (people, icons, logos) in the chart area?
*   **The Test:** Ask a viewer what the image relates to. If they mention the object (e.g., "the apple chart") and it aligns with the data topic (fruit sales), it is working.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Add a relevant icon next to the title or legend.
*   **Best Fix:** Integrate the pictogram into the chart design (e.g., Isotype charts) where the object represents the unit of data.
