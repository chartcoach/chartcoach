## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Put a clear, informative title at the top of the visualization, and have it communicate the main message.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Titles attract attention during viewing and become a primary cue people use to recognize and later describe what the visualization was about.

- **The Principle:** Text as a semantic retrieval cue
- **The Evidence:** Viewers fixated titles frequently during encoding (titles fixated 59% of the time when present) and titles were the most-mentioned element in recall descriptions; titles at the top were fixated more than titles at the bottom, and visualizations with titles had higher description quality than those without titles [@borkinMemorabilityVisualizationRecognition2016].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Remembering the topic and takeaway; recognizing the visualization later
- **Data Type:** Any (especially when the visual form is not visually distinctive)
- **Audience:** General audiences and mixed-expertise viewers

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The surrounding medium already provides a stable, adjacent headline that will be seen with the chart every time.
- **Reason:** The title’s role as an in-chart cue is less necessary if a reliable external semantic cue is guaranteed (the paper’s finding concerns within-visualization text cues) [@borkinMemorabilityVisualizationRecognition2016].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Less space for the data region.
- **The Risk:** A long title can dominate attention and reduce time spent examining the data [@borkinMemorabilityVisualizationRecognition2016].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Using a short or vague title (e.g., a single word topic label) that does not convey the message.
- **Why it fails:** Viewers then rely on other text (paragraphs/labels) to infer meaning; recall quality depends heavily on having a meaningful semantic hook [@borkinMemorabilityVisualizationRecognition2016].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The title reads like a category label rather than a takeaway.
- **The Test:** Ask a colleague to read only the title for 2 seconds and say the message; if they can’t, the title is not doing its job [@borkinMemorabilityVisualizationRecognition2016].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Rewrite the title to include the main conclusion/trend, not just the subject.
- **Best Fix:** Move the title to the top and make it message-specific so it can serve as a recognition and recall cue [@borkinMemorabilityVisualizationRecognition2016].
