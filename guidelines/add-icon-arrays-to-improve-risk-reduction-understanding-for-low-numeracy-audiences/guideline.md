## Add icon arrays alongside numbers for risk reduction tasks <!-- role: advice -->

Add icon arrays in addition to numerical risk information when readers may have low numeracy and need to understand risk reduction accurately.

## Why icon arrays raise accuracy for risk-reduction understanding <!-- role: reason -->

Icon arrays provide a concrete mapping from a probability to a count of affected people out of a fixed whole, which can reduce calculation and translation demands when interpreting risk reduction.

**Mechanism:** Showing affected and unaffected individuals visually supports converting percentages or ratios into frequencies and comparing “with” versus “without” treatment outcomes.

**Evidence:** Adding icon arrays to numerical descriptions increased the proportion of participants who accurately estimated relative risk reduction, with particularly strong benefit for participants with relatively lower numeracy within both older-adult and student samples [@galesicUsingIconArrays2009].

**Notes:** The accuracy gain was observed across multiple medical scenarios in the study.

## When to use icon arrays as an add-on to numeric risk information <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand how much a treatment or screening changes risk.
- **Task:** Estimate or compare risk “with” versus “without” treatment and infer risk reduction.
- **Data:** Two probabilities/risks for the same outcome (baseline and treated).
- **Chart Setting:** Patient-facing or lay-audience communication where misunderstanding has decision consequences.
- **Audience:** Mixed numeracy, including people below typical numeracy levels (e.g., older adults).
- **Success Criterion:** Higher rate of correct interpretation of risk reduction.

## When not to follow this exactly <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** You are not communicating quantitative risk or risk reduction (e.g., purely qualitative counseling with no numeric claims). **Why:** The benefit tested is specifically about accurate understanding of numeric risks and their reduction.

## Tradeoffs and risks <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Additional space and visual complexity versus numbers-only text. **Risk:** Readers may interpret the display as making the risk feel smaller or less serious than the same information presented numerically. **Mitigation:** Ensure the communication goal is comprehension accuracy rather than maximizing perceived urgency.

## Common failure modes <!-- role: mistakes -->

**Mistake:** Using icon arrays as decoration without mapping them to a clear denominator and outcome state. **Why it fails:** The tested benefit depends on icon arrays acting as a faithful, countable representation of risk.

## Quick tests <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Readers can restate the headline percentage but cannot answer “out of 1,000, how many are affected with vs. without treatment?”\
**Quick Check:** Ask a small sample to compute (or select) the treated and untreated counts out of the same denominator after viewing the display.\
**Stronger Test:** Compare accuracy on the same questions with numbers-only versus numbers-plus-icon-arrays in a brief A/B test.

## What to do instead <!-- role: fix -->

- Add explicit “out of N people” counts next to the numeric risks for both treatment conditions.
- Provide both treated and untreated values in the same denominator to support direct comparison.
- Reduce the task to a single comparison question (e.g., “How many fewer out of N?”) if the current material requires multi-step calculation.
- If space is limited, show one icon array for baseline and one for treated risk, each with the same denominator.
