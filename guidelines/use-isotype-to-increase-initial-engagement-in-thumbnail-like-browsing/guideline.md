## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When your goal is to entice viewers to click/open/inspect a chart among competing items, use an ISOTYPE-style stacked pictograph chart rather than a plain bar chart.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Pictographic data marks increase initial selection and viewing in a browsing-like setting, shifting early attention toward the visualization.

- **The Principle:** Visually distinctive, meaningful marks draw early attention in competition
- **The Evidence:** In a 3×3 grid browsing task, subjects preferentially opened ISOTYPE charts earlier than bar charts or text, with ISOTYPE dominating early views (Exp. 5) [@harozISOTYPEVisualizationWorking2015a].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Decide what to inspect next (triage/selection)
- **Data Type:** Simple categorical summaries that can be shown as small thumbnails
- **Audience:** General readers skimming collections (e.g., dashboards, article lists, small multiples menus)

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The primary success metric is fastest accurate extraction (not attracting attention)
- **Reason:** Engagement benefits do not justify adding distracting non-data imagery; also, speed tasks showed no advantage except that superfluous images slow people down [@harozISOTYPEVisualizationWorking2015a].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More design effort (icon selection, consistent pictograph set)
- **The Risk:** If pictographs become superfluous decoration rather than data marks, they can harm memory and speed (as shown in other experiments) [@harozISOTYPEVisualizationWorking2015a].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding a big decorative picture to make a bar chart “more engaging”
- **Why it fails:** Superfluous imagery was empirically associated with worse memorability and slower performance; engagement gains in Exp. 5 came from ISOTYPE data marks, not decoration [@harozISOTYPEVisualizationWorking2015a].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** In thumbnail form, the chart’s primary marks are clearly pictographic and represent the quantities
- **The Test:** In a grid of mixed content, measure which items users click first; compare ISOTYPE vs bar thumbnails for the same topics [@harozISOTYPEVisualizationWorking2015a].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Replace plain bars with stacked pictograph marks for the same values.
- **Best Fix:** Create a consistent ISOTYPE vocabulary (distinct icons per topic/category) and keep pictographs strictly tied to data encoding (no decorative backgrounds) [@harozISOTYPEVisualizationWorking2015a].
