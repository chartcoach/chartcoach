## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

After presenting risks and benefits (in any order), give a short final review that brings both back into focus.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

The order of information can change perceived risk and knowledge (a recency effect), even when both sets are read within minutes; summarizing helps counteract the last-presented information dominating judgment.

- **The Principle:** Reduce recency-driven framing bias
- **The Evidence:** Presenting risks after benefits increased perceived worry/commonness; order influenced knowledge and perceptions; summarizing may minimize recency effects [@fagerlinHelpingPatientsDecide2011].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Make a balanced decision based on both harms and benefits.
- **Data Type:** Any combined risk/benefit explanation across multiple attributes.
- **Audience:** Patients receiving sequential explanations over time or across pages/screens.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** None stated in the paper.
- **Reason:** Not specified.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Takes more time/space at the end of the encounter/material.
- **The Risk:** Oversimplified summaries could omit nuances if not chosen carefully.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Assuming “they just read it” means order won’t matter.
- **Why it fails:** The paper reports meaningful recency effects within minutes [@fagerlinHelpingPatientsDecide2011].
- **The Wrong Fix:** Ending immediately after the final section (often risks), leaving that as the last impression.
- **Why it fails:** It can amplify recency-weighting of what came last [@fagerlinHelpingPatientsDecide2011].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The last page/screen/topic is one-sided (all risks or all benefits) with no closing synthesis.
- **The Test:** Ask a reader immediately after: “What do you remember most?” If they recall mostly the last section, add a summary.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a brief “In summary” section restating the main benefit(s) and main risk(s).
- **Best Fix:** End with a structured recap (or a summary table) that places risks and benefits side-by-side [@fagerlinHelpingPatientsDecide2011].
