## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Provide concise introductory framing and a closing summary/synthesis that states the main takeaways of the narrative visualization.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

The paper’s design-space observations note under-utilization of introductory text and summary/synthesis in narrative visualizations, and case critiques suggest that without synthesis, exploration-heavy pieces can leave readers without meaningful conclusions.

- **The Principle:** Framing and synthesis help convert explored facts into a coherent narrative argument.
- **The Evidence:** [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Leave with clear conclusions and remembered points.
- **Data Type:** Multi-part, interactive, or information-dense stories.
- **Audience:** General audiences.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Purely exploratory tools where the system should avoid asserting conclusions.
- **Reason:** A forced synthesis may conflict with user-led analysis goals [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Additional text and authoring time; potential reduction in perceived neutrality.
- **The Risk:** Overconfident summaries can feel unsupported if not clearly linked to evidence in the visualization [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Ending immediately after interactions without restating what was learned.
- **Why it fails:** Readers may remember interface actions but not the narrative conclusion [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users can describe what they clicked but not what the story concluded.
- **The Test:** Ask viewers for the “one sentence takeaway” after use; if answers vary wildly from intended message, add/strengthen synthesis [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a final slide/panel with 2–4 bullet takeaways tied to prior visuals.
- **Best Fix:** Provide a narrative wrap-up that references key evidence points and offers a structured handoff to continued exploration (if applicable) [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010].
