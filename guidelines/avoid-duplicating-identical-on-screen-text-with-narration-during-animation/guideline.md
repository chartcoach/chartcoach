## Eliminate redundant on-screen text during narrated animation <!-- role: advice -->

When an animation is shown with narration, do not also display the same words as concurrent on-screen text.

## Why redundancy can hurt learning with animation <!-- role: reason -->

Presenting the same verbal stream in both spoken and printed forms during animation can prompt learners to allocate capacity to reading and reconciling duplicate information, adding incidental processing that competes with essential processing needed to understand the system. With animation already demanding visual resources, removing redundant text helps preserve capacity for selecting and organizing the animation and integrating it with narration.

**Mechanism:** Eliminating duplicated verbal streams reduces incidental processing and visual-channel competition during animation viewing.

**Evidence:** Transfer was higher for nonredundant narrated animations than for redundant versions that also included identical on-screen text, consistent with a redundancy effect in multimedia explanations with animation [@mayerNineWaysReduce2003].

**Notes:** The negative effect is tied to competition with animation in the visual channel.

## When identical words are presented twice during animation <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Learn an explanation from narrated animation.
- **Task:** Integrate spoken explanation with dynamic visuals.
- **Data:** Stepwise causal explanation with changing visuals.
- **Chart Setting:** Animation plus narration, with optional caption-like text that duplicates narration.
- **Audience:** Learners likely to split attention between reading and watching.
- **Success Criterion:** Better transfer without changing the explanation content.

## When redundancy may not be harmful <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** There is no animation or competing visual display. **Why:** Without animation, on-screen text may not overload the visual channel and can support learning alongside narration.

## Tradeoffs of removing on-screen text <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Reduced support for learners who prefer reading or need text access in the moment. **Risk:** Sound-off contexts can block access to the words. **Mitigation:** Provide text in a way that does not coincide with animation viewing demands.

## Common redundancy mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

**Mistake:** Treating captions as harmless even when they exactly duplicate narration over a busy animation. **Why it fails:** The captions compete for visual resources needed to process the animation.

## Quick redundancy diagnostics <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Learners alternate between reading captions and watching the animation, missing visual transitions. **Quick Check:** If the on-screen text is verbatim narration while animation is active, redundancy is present. **Stronger Test:** Compare transfer between redundant and nonredundant versions under the same timing and content.

## What to do instead of verbatim duplication <!-- role: fix -->

- Use narration with animation and remove verbatim on-screen text.
- Segment the lesson so any necessary printed words appear when the animation is not demanding attention.
- Align minimal printed labels with visual elements rather than duplicating full sentences.
- Signal key elements in the animation so learners can follow narration without needing duplicate text.
