## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Do not use generic titles that merely describe the dataset. Use the title to explicitly state the question the table answers or the conclusion it draws.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Data interpretation should not be a secret. By stating the goal "loud and clear" in the title, you guide the reader’s interpretation immediately. It transforms a spreadsheet-like list into a communicative visualization [@mintzer_compact_tables_2024].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Communicating a specific narrative or finding (e.g., "Which city hosted the most games?").
*   **Data Type:** Any table used in a publication or presentation context.
*   **Audience:** General audiences who need to understand the "so what" of the data quickly.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** Database exports or technical appendices.
*   **Reason:** These require neutral, descriptive metadata for archival or search purposes.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** Neutrality. An active title frames the data, potentially downplaying other possible interpretations.
*   **The Risk:** If the data is updated but the title remains static, the statement may become factually incorrect.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Using titles like "List of [Noun] by [Noun]" (e.g., "Euro 2024 host cities and their games").
*   **Why it fails:** It describes the container, not the content's value.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Does the title end in a question mark or contain a verb?
*   **The Test:** Read the title. Does it tell you what to look for in the rows below?

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Rewrite "Sales by Region" to "Which Region Sold the Most?"
*   **Best Fix:** Frame the title as the primary insight or the specific question the user asked.
