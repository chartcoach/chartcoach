## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Diagnose what the audience misunderstands about the system (missing factors, wrong relationships, unrecognized uncertainty), then communicate only the pieces needed to repair those specific gaps.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

People rely on mental models to create and evaluate options, but these models can omit factors, misunderstand relationships, or ignore uncertainty—especially in dynamic or nonlinear systems. Targeted communications avoid overwhelming users while correcting decision-relevant misconceptions [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understanding “how it works” well enough to plan and invent responses.
- **Data Type:** System/process explanations, not just numeric forecasts.
- **Audience:** Lay or mixed audiences with partial, intuitive models.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** A crisis alert where there is no time to diagnose mental model gaps.
- **Reason:** Immediate action may require a minimal directive; targeted education can follow in supporting materials [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Requires research (elicitation/user testing) to learn audience beliefs first.
- **The Risk:** If the diagnosis is wrong, you may “fix” a problem users don’t have and miss the real one [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Dumping comprehensive background science “just in case.”
- **Why it fails:** It overwhelms recipients, repeats what they know, and obscures the few uncertainties that actually block good decisions [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users remember trivia but still cannot explain the key mechanism or uncertainty.
- **The Test:** Ask users to explain the causal story in their own words and to state what is uncertain; compare to the expert model to see if the targeted gaps closed [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Rewrite the message around the single most decision-relevant misconception or unknown.
- **Best Fix:** Use a mental-models approach: elicit audience beliefs, map them against an expert influence diagram, and produce a targeted uncertainty communication validated by testing [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].
