## Explicitly reinforce the time interval attached to each risk <!-- role: advice -->

Whenever you present a risk, repeatedly specify the time interval it covers (for example, 5-year risk, 10-year risk, lifetime risk) in the same sentence or label as the number.

## People often ignore time and misjudge magnitude <!-- role: reason -->

Viewers can focus on the size of the ratio and neglect the period over which events accrue, which can bias interpretations of severity and treatment effectiveness.

**Mechanism:** Repeated time labels keep the denominator-in-time salient, reducing “time neglect” where longer horizons feel inherently more severe even when comparisons are inappropriate.

**Evidence:** People can ignore the time element of risk, and perceptions of treatment effectiveness can be biased by the length of time displayed in survival curves (for example, 15-year vs 5-year) [@fagerlinHelpingPatientsDecide2011].

**Notes:** Visual displays often do not make time salient, so text reinforcement is critical even when a chart is used.

## When time-horizon reinforcement applies <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Correctly interpret the magnitude of risk and compare options fairly.
- **Task:** Compare risks presented over different time spans or interpret survival curves.
- **Data:** Time-bounded risks (5-year, 10-year) and cumulative/lifetime risks; survival over time.
- **Chart Setting:** Static charts, pictographs with textual legends, survival curves, or any mixed-format counseling.
- **Audience:** Patients and clinicians who may default to “bigger percent = bigger problem” without time context.
- **Success Criterion:** Users can state the time interval associated with each number and avoid comparing mismatched horizons.

## When not to follow it <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** All risks shown share exactly the same time horizon and it is unavoidably obvious in every label. **Why:** Repetition adds clutter without improving interpretation.

## Tradeoffs and risks <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** More labeling and less space for other content. **Risk:** If time horizons are inconsistent across the display, repetition can reveal confusing design rather than fix it. **Mitigation:** Standardize time horizons whenever the comparison requires it.

## Common failure modes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **Mistake:** Putting the time horizon only in a footnote or legend. **Why it fails:** Users may never integrate that detail into their interpretation of the number.
- **Mistake:** Comparing “lifetime risk” in one place with “10-year risk” in another without making the mismatch explicit. **Why it fails:** Users may treat them as directly comparable magnitudes.

## Quick tests <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Users cannot answer “over what time period?” for a risk statistic. **Quick Check:** Circle every numeric risk and confirm the time interval is adjacent to it. **Stronger Test:** Ask users to compare two risks and listen for whether they spontaneously mention time.

## What to do instead <!-- role: fix -->

- Attach the time interval directly to every numeric label (for example, “10-year survival: 77 out of 100”).
- Use the same time horizon across options when the goal is side-by-side comparison.
- If multiple horizons are informative, present them explicitly as separate, clearly labeled values.
- Add a brief verbal recap that repeats the time horizon along with the key risks and benefits.
