## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When persuading an audience that starts strongly opposed to your position, present statistical evidence in tables rather than charts.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

In the experiments, negatively polarized participants showed higher persuasion likelihood with tables than with charts across all three topics; the aggregated results showed substantially more positive change for tables. Attitude change also favored tables in the aggregated analysis for this group.

- **The Principle:** Initial attitude moderates how evidence format affects persuasion.
- **The Evidence:** [@pandeyPersuasivePowerData2014]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Reconsider a stance that is initially against the advocated claim.
- **Data Type:** The same statistics can be expressed either as a chart or as a table.
- **Audience:** Negatively polarized viewers (e.g., pre-attitude in {-3, -2}). [@pandeyPersuasivePowerData2014]

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Your audience is neutral/weakly polarized.
- **Reason:** For neutral/weakly polarized participants, charts consistently increased persuasion likelihood relative to tables. [@pandeyPersuasivePowerData2014]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Tables are typically slower to scan for patterns than charts, which may reduce “at-a-glance” comprehension.
- **The Risk:** If the table is large or dense, readers may disengage before processing the evidence.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Switching to tables but removing contextual framing and narrative.
- **Why it fails:** The study’s persuasive messages combined context + evidence statements + data display; the format effect was tested within that full message structure. [@pandeyPersuasivePowerData2014]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Opposed viewers are not moving (or are reacting negatively) after chart exposure.
- **The Test:** Segment by initial attitude (NP vs others) and compare pre/post attitude change between table and chart treatments; verify tables produce more “positive change” among NP viewers. [@pandeyPersuasivePowerData2014]

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Convert the chart panels into compact tables while keeping the same explanatory statements and numbers/trends.
- **Best Fix:** Run a targeted experiment on your opposed segment (NP) to validate that tables outperform charts for your topic and message framing. [@pandeyPersuasivePowerData2014]
