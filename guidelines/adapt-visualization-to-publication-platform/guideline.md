## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Adapt your visualization’s framing, format, and complexity to the platform or publication outlet (e.g., concise standalone graphics for mobile/social; more detailed, analytical layouts for print/reports).

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Different outlets impose different constraints (screen size, attention span, interaction patterns) and come with different audience expectations; matching the format to the medium increases comprehension and engagement by reducing friction and emphasizing the right level of detail.

- **The Principle:** Medium-fit framing and cognitive load management
- **The Evidence:** Platform-tailored formats and framing were repeatedly emphasized by practitioners and editors, including simplifying and focusing on one clear message for social media while supporting deeper analysis in reports/print [@schuster_being_2024; @koesten_what_2023; @schuster_who_2023].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

Use this whenever the same data story may appear in multiple outlets or must succeed in a specific distribution channel.

- **User Goal:** Quickly grasp the main takeaway (social/mobile) or analyze and verify details (print/report/news)
- **Data Type:** Any; especially multi-variable or narrative-heavy analyses that must be condensed or expanded
- **Audience:** Platform-segmented audiences (e.g., quick-summary seekers on social; detail-oriented readers in reports) [@schuster_who_2023]

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** A single canonical graphic must be reused unchanged across outlets (e.g., legal filings, standardized corporate templates)
- **Reason:** Consistency, auditability, or governance requirements outweigh platform optimization.
- **Scenario:** The platform demands high fidelity detail (e.g., expert community forums, technical dashboards on large screens)
- **Reason:** Over-simplification would block the audience’s analytical task and reduce trust.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More production time (multiple versions, re-layout, re-copywriting, re-exporting for formats)
- **The Risk:** Message drift across versions (different emphasis can accidentally change interpretation) or oversimplification that removes essential context [@schuster_who_2023]

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Posting a print/report graphic unchanged on social media (tiny text, dense legends, multiple messages)
- **Why it fails:** Mobile viewers can’t read it quickly; attention is lost before the point lands [@koesten_what_2023; @schuster_who_2023]
- **The Wrong Fix:** “Making it social” by adding decoration (bright colors/animations) without reducing complexity
- **Why it fails:** Visual noise increases cognitive load and still doesn’t produce a single clear takeaway [@schuster_who_2023]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Text and labels look cramped on mobile; multiple competing focal points; the viewer can’t restate the main message in one sentence.
- **The Test:** Do a platform simulation: view at typical device size and speed (e.g., phone screen; 3-second glance). If the core takeaway isn’t legible and obvious, it’s not platform-fit [@schuster_who_2023].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Increase font sizes, reduce annotations, remove secondary series, and rewrite the title to state one clear message tailored to the outlet.
- **Best Fix:** Create platform-specific versions: a concise standalone card/clip for social/mobile and a fuller, annotated analytical version for print/report, with consistent underlying data and aligned wording to prevent message drift [@schuster_being_2024; @koesten_what_2023; @schuster_who_2023].
