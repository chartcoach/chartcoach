## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Group related words into separate, visually distinct zones instead of intermixing all words across the canvas.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Grouping reduces the need to mentally separate unrelated words and supports “one-topic-at-a-time” processing. In the paper’s time-constrained category identification task, layouts that separated semantic groups (e.g., columns or other clear groupings) produced substantially higher scores than standard Wordle-style intermixing, and eye-tracking showed far more within-group fixation sequences for grouped layouts [@hearstEvaluationSemanticallyGrouped2020].

- **The Principle:** Spatial grouping supports chunking and reduces cross-topic interference.
- **The Evidence:** Grouped layouts outperformed Wordle layouts; eye-tracking showed fixations clustered within categories in grouped designs [@hearstEvaluationSemanticallyGrouped2020].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Quickly gist topics/themes from a set of words.
- **Data Type:** Words that belong to distinct semantic categories/topics.
- **Audience:** Viewers doing analytic interpretation (not just decorative viewing), especially under time pressure.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Your groups are not semantically coherent or are heavily overlapping.
- **Reason:** The paper notes results were shown for semantically distinct categories and cautions that overlap may reduce effectiveness [@hearstEvaluationSemanticallyGrouped2020].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Less “surprise”/free-form look compared to typical Wordle-style clouds.
- **The Risk:** If grouping is incorrect or unclear, users may infer false structure.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Place groups loosely in different areas but without clear boundaries (no visible demarcation).
- **Why it fails:** Prior thematic layouts without clear visual separation can leave users unaware of the grouping principle; the paper emphasizes groups should be visually distinct [@hearstEvaluationSemanticallyGrouped2020].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Words from different topics are intermingled so the viewer must constantly switch contexts to infer meaning.
- **The Test:** Watch whether a viewer can work through one group at a time; if their gaze/attention must hop across the whole cloud to solve one topic, grouping is likely insufficient (consistent with the eye-tracking pattern reported) [@hearstEvaluationSemanticallyGrouped2020].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Rearrange so each topic’s words are adjacent (tight local clusters).
- **Best Fix:** Partition the canvas into explicit zones so each semantic group is visually separated (by space, color, or both) [@hearstEvaluationSemanticallyGrouped2020].
