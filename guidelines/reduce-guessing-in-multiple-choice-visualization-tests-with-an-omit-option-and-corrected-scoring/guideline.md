## Reduce guessing in multiple-choice visualization tests with an Omit option and corrected scoring <!-- role: advice -->

Provide an explicit “Omit” option for each item and score with a correction-for-guessing method so test takers can avoid random guessing.

## Why omitted guessing reduces measurement error <!-- role: reason -->

Random guessing introduces noise that inflates or distorts scores, especially in timed multiple-choice settings. Allowing omission and correcting for guessing reduces construct-irrelevant variance so the score better reflects actual reading and interpretation skill.

**Mechanism:** Encouraging omission for guesses reduces random correct responses; correction-for-guessing adjusts scores to account for expected chance performance.

**Evidence:** VLAT administration included an “Omit” option for each item and used correction-for-guessing scoring to address guessing-related error in selected-response items [@leeVLATDevelopmentVisualization2017].

**Notes:** Clear instructions are required so test takers know when to omit rather than guess.

## When to use this approach <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Measure visualization literacy with selected-response items without inflating scores via chance.
- **Task:** Standardized testing with time limits per item.
- **Data:** Any, as long as items are multiple-choice or true/false.
- **Chart Setting:** Online test administration where guessing is likely.
- **Audience:** Non-experts; variable confidence levels.
- **Success Criterion:** Reduced guessing noise and more interpretable score distributions.

## When not to use it <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** The test purpose is formative practice where encouraging attempts is more important than score precision. **Why:** Omit options may discourage engagement and reduce learning attempts.

## Tradeoffs <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Some test takers may overuse “Omit,” reducing information per person. **Risk:** Omit behavior may correlate with confidence rather than skill. **Mitigation:** Provide practice items and explicit guidance on omitting only when answers are pure guesses.

## Common mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

**Mistake:** Adding an Omit option but scoring omissions the same as wrong answers without correction. **Why it fails:** It removes the incentive structure intended to reduce guessing noise.

## Quick checks <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** High omission rates on items that should be solvable from the chart or widespread rapid responding. **Quick Check:** Monitor per-item time and omission frequency for anomalies. **Stronger Test:** Compare score distributions and item statistics with and without correction-for-guessing to see whether guessing is inflating outcomes.

## What to do if omission behavior becomes problematic <!-- role: fix -->

- Add clearer instructions and a short practice section demonstrating when to omit.
- Review and rewrite items with unclear stems or weakly supported encodings that drive omission.
- Adjust time limits if omissions are driven by insufficient reading time.
- Use additional attention checks to identify random clickers separately from cautious omitters.
