## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Represent individual risk statistics using pictographs (icon arrays) rather than bar charts, pie charts, or text alone.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Pictographs visually represent risk as a frequency while simultaneously showing the numerator (those affected) and the denominator (those not affected). This format helps prevent decision bias.
*   **The Principle:** Numerator/Denominator Visibility and Frequency Framing.
*   **The Evidence:** Research indicates pictographs are comprehended more quickly and accurately than other formats. They are superior to pie charts for "verbatim knowledge" (exact numbers) and superior to bar charts for "gist knowledge" (main point) [@fagerlin_helping_2011].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
Use this when communicating individual statistics or probabilistic information to patients, particularly those with low numeracy.
*   **User Goal:** Understanding the likelihood of a specific outcome (e.g., side effects).
*   **Data Type:** Probabilistic data (e.g., "X out of 100").
*   **Audience:** Patients making medical decisions, regardless of education level.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** Showing changes in risk patterns over time.
*   **Reason:** It is difficult to show temporal changes effectively with a static pictograph compared to a line graph [@fagerlin_helping_2011].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** Space efficiency. Pictographs require displaying all units (e.g., 100 icons) rather than a single bar or line.
*   **The Risk:** Missing the time element. Pictographs often relegate time details to textual legends, which users may miss [@fagerlin_helping_2011].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Using pie charts.
*   **Why it fails:** While pie charts show part-to-whole, pictographs are shown to be more effective for verbatim knowledge retention [@fagerlin_helping_2011].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Are you using geometric areas (bars/slices) to represent people?
*   **The Test:** Can you count the individual units representing the denominator in the graphic? If not, it is not a pictograph.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Switch the chart type to a 10x10 icon grid.
*   **Best Fix:** Use a 10x10 grid where each square represents "1 out of 100 individuals," clearly distinguishing the affected population with a contrasting color.
