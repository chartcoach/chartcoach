## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Do not automatically strip visual embellishments if your goal is basic on-screen comprehension (subject, categories/values, and trend) and viewers have time to look.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Explain the principle at work here. Connect the rule to human perception or clear communication. Cite your sources.

- **The Principle:** Viewers can allocate attention to data even when non-data imagery is present.
- **The Evidence:** Participants’ description accuracy for subject, categories, and trend did not differ between embellished (Holmes) and plain charts; total description time also did not significantly differ [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

This advice is designed for specific moments.

- **User Goal:** Understanding the main topic, categories/values, and overall trend while the chart is visible.
- **Data Type:** Standard chart types with matching underlying data (as in the paper’s paired designs).
- **Audience:** General readers/typical chart consumers.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

List the specific scenarios where you should ignore this rule.

- **Scenario:** You must enforce a strict time limit on viewing.
- **Reason:** The authors note their study did not constrain viewing time; under time pressure, looking at imagery could reduce attention available for data [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

Be honest about the downsides.

- **The Sacrifice:** Less visual simplicity and potentially less “minimalist” styling.
- **The Risk:** Imagery can convey additional value-laden interpretation beyond the data [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

Describe the common anti-patterns or "lazy fixes" people use that don't actually solve the problem.

- **The Wrong Fix:** Treating all non-data ink as harmful without testing comprehension for the intended task.
- **Why it fails:** In the paper’s task (high-level chart comprehension), embellishment did not reduce accuracy or time [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Stakeholders argue “chart junk” necessarily harms understanding despite no task evidence.
- **The Test:** Run a short comprehension check (subject/categories/trend) with the chart visible; if performance is comparable, removing imagery may be unnecessary for comprehension (as found in the study) [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Keep imagery but ensure data marks and labels remain readable and unchanged from the underlying data encoding.
- **Best Fix:** Pair an embellished version (for engagement/recall) with a plain version (for analytic use) when the context includes both needs; the paper motivates differing goals though it does not prescribe a universal style [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].
