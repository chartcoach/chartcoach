## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Select visualization types for assessment by triangulating (1) what users learn (e.g., K–12 curricula), (2) what common tools can produce, and (3) what major media outlets publish—then keep types that appear across sources.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Content is valid only if it reflects what the target population is expected to encounter and be able to interpret. VLAT operationalizes this by surveying curricula, authoring tools, and news outlets, then choosing chart types supported by at least two sources to match “non-expert adult” exposure.

- **The Principle:** Content representativeness for the target population
- **The Evidence:** [@leeVLATDevelopmentVisualization2017]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Building a “general visualization literacy” test for everyday users
- **Data Type:** Common chart types and common real-world contexts
- **Audience:** Non-expert adults who encounter visualizations via tools and media

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** You are assessing specialized professional literacy (e.g., genomics, finance terminals).
- **Reason:** The relevant chart ecosystem differs from general-public exposure; VLAT’s sources are tuned to general users. [@leeVLATDevelopmentVisualization2017]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Time spent surveying sources and counting frequencies.
- **The Risk:** You may omit niche but important charts for certain domains because they are not frequent in general sources. [@leeVLATDevelopmentVisualization2017]

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Picking chart types based only on personal preference or what’s fashionable in visualization research.
- **Why it fails:** It can misalign the test with what non-experts actually see and have been taught. [@leeVLATDevelopmentVisualization2017]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Stakeholders ask “why these charts?” and you cannot cite an exposure rationale.
- **The Test:** For each included chart type, document at least two independent sources of prevalence (learned/tooled/published), as VLAT did. [@leeVLATDevelopmentVisualization2017]

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a simple frequency table from your chosen sources and prune outliers.
- **Best Fix:** Replicate VLAT’s three-source survey method and reselect the final set based on cross-source presence. [@leeVLATDevelopmentVisualization2017]
