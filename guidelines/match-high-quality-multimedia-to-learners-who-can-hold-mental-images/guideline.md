## Individualize designs that rely on holding mental images <!-- role: advice -->

Use designs that depend on holding and manipulating mental images (such as simultaneous narration and animation) with learners who have strong skill at maintaining visual representations.

## Why spatial ability changes who benefits <!-- role: reason -->

Learners vary in the cognitive effort required to hold and manipulate mental images in working memory. When a lesson requires maintaining visual representations while integrating with narration, learners with higher spatial ability can devote more capacity to essential processing, whereas learners with lower spatial ability may remain overloaded and gain less from the design.

**Mechanism:** Differences in the efficiency of representational holding affect how much capacity remains for selecting, organizing, and integrating.

**Evidence:** High-spatial learners showed substantially better transfer from simultaneous presentations than from successive ones, while low-spatial learners performed similarly low across both formats, consistent with a spatial ability interaction effect [@mayerNineWaysReduce2003].

**Notes:** This is a learner-selection strategy rather than a direct redesign method.

## When the lesson depends on mental image holding <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Learn a process from narrated animation.
- **Task:** Maintain and integrate transient visual events with narration.
- **Data:** Dynamic visuals where important information appears briefly.
- **Chart Setting:** Simultaneous narration-plus-animation or other designs requiring active visual maintenance.
- **Audience:** Mixed population with varying spatial ability.
- **Success Criterion:** Avoid leaving a subgroup overloaded and unable to benefit.

## When you should not rely on learner selection <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** You must support all learners regardless of spatial ability. **Why:** Restricting the design to only high-spatial learners does not address overload for others.

## Tradeoffs of individualizing by spatial ability <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Broad accessibility and uniform delivery. **Risk:** Misclassification can deny support to learners who need it. **Mitigation:** Prefer designs that reduce holding demands for everyone when possible.

## Common individualization failure modes <!-- role: mistakes -->

**Mistake:** Assuming simultaneous narration and animation will help all learners equally. **Why it fails:** Learners with lower spatial ability may still be overloaded and show little benefit.

## Checks for spatial-ability sensitivity <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** A subgroup fails to improve under the supposedly better multimedia format. **Quick Check:** Compare performance distributions rather than only averages across conditions. **Stronger Test:** Test for an interaction between spatial ability and presentation format on transfer.

## What to do instead of relying on spatial ability <!-- role: fix -->

- Segment the lesson so transient visuals are processed in smaller chunks with pauses.
- Pretrain component names and behaviors to reduce essential load during integration.
- Synchronize at a fine grain (short alternating corresponding chunks) to reduce holding demands.
- Weed extraneous material so more capacity is available for essential processing.
