## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Use grey (or very light, near-grey tints) for less important elements and reserve strong colors for the most important data points.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

De-emphasizing context with grey increases the salience of highlight colors and calms the overall visual impression; Muth calls grey “the most important color” for these roles [@muth_colors_2018].

- **The Principle:** Visual hierarchy through reduced salience.
- **The Evidence:** [@muth_colors_2018]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Notice the key message first (highlights) while still having context available.
- **Data Type:** Charts with a main focus plus supporting/background series, annotations, or unselected states.
- **Audience:** Readers who need quick takeaway.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Grey would reduce readability because contrast becomes too low against the background.
- **Reason:** Muth stresses maintaining sufficient contrast, especially for text and light colors like grey [@muth_colors_2018].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Some context elements may feel less “present” or engaging.
- **The Risk:** Overusing grey can make the chart feel cold; Muth suggests warm greys or very light alternative tints to mitigate this [@muth_colors_2018].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Coloring everything equally to avoid “boring” greys.
- **Why it fails:** Nothing stands out; the reader can’t tell what matters most [@muth_colors_2018].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The chart has no clear focal point; all series/categories compete.
- **The Test:** Squint—if your highlight does not pop immediately against subdued context, your hierarchy is weak [@muth_colors_2018].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Turn secondary series/gridlines/labels into grey tones; keep one highlight color.
- **Best Fix:** Redesign the palette so only the key categories are colored and everything else uses warm grey or very light tints with adequate contrast [@muth_colors_2018].
