## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When communicating risk, include interactive or reflective elements that make users actively process the numbers (not just read them).

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Active information processing (e.g., answering questions, drawing/interacting with representations) improves comprehension and satisfaction with risk information.

- **The Principle:** Active processing improves risk understanding
- **The Evidence:** [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understanding personal risk and what changes it
- **Data Type:** Probabilities and risk representations (e.g., icon-like “people” representations)
- **Audience:** Patients using electronic tools, portals, or decision aids [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Time-critical clinical contexts where interaction would slow essential action.
- **Reason:** The cognitive benefit may not justify the added steps. [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More development effort and user time
- **The Risk:** Poorly designed “game-like” interactions may distract or frustrate users [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding interactivity that changes visuals but doesn’t prompt meaningful reasoning.
- **Why it fails:** Motion/novelty alone doesn’t ensure users connect representations to the underlying quantities. [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users click around but can’t answer basic “what does this mean for me?” questions after.
- **The Test:** Include a short comprehension question; if performance doesn’t improve, the interaction isn’t supporting processing. [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a single reflective prompt that requires interpreting the displayed risk.
- **Best Fix:** Use interaction that reveals the meaning of the probability (e.g., letting users see which subset of a population representation is affected). [@anckerRethinkingHealthNumeracy2007]
