## Add icon arrays when the audience may struggle with the message language <!-- role: advice -->

When risk information must be presented in a language that part of the audience may not speak fluently, include icon arrays that visually encode the totals and the affected subset for each group. Keep the visuals aligned with the same quantities described in the text.

## Why icon arrays help when language proficiency is limited <!-- role: reason -->

Limited language proficiency can impair comprehension of verbally described numeric relationships, making ratio biases more likely. Visual displays can provide an alternate channel for extracting the needed proportional relations, reducing dependence on parsing the verbal description.

**Mechanism:** The visualization provides a language-light representation of the same ratio structure, enabling proportion-based comparison without full reliance on the narrative text.

**Evidence:** In an immigrant sample, denominator-neglect-style errors were more pronounced when numeric risk-reduction information was presented in a nonnative language; adding icon arrays reduced these errors and improved accuracy, with stronger gains when the language was nonnative [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].

**Notes:** The effect is most relevant when risks are communicated using frequencies that require ratio interpretation.

## When language is a barrier in risk communication <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand treatment benefit/harms to make or support a decision.
- **Task:** Interpret and compare risks expressed as frequencies or ratios.
- **Data:** Two-group outcomes with totals and event counts.
- **Chart Setting:** Public health materials, clinic handouts, translated or partially translated resources.
- **Audience:** Immigrants or multilingual populations with limited nonnative language proficiency.
- **Success Criterion:** More accurate risk-reduction judgments despite language constraints.

## When icon arrays may not be sufficient <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** The audience’s graph literacy is low enough that the icon array will not be understood. **Why:** Visual-aid effectiveness is moderated by graph literacy, so some viewers benefit less even when language barriers exist [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].

## Tradeoffs of using icon arrays for language accessibility <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Additional space and design effort, especially when creating multilingual layouts. **Risk:** Mismatch between text quantities and visual quantities can confuse readers who rely on either channel. **Mitigation:** Maintain one-to-one correspondence between the numbers and the icons.

## Common mistakes in cross-language risk visuals <!-- role: mistakes -->

**Mistake:** Using a visual that is not keyed to the same denominators as the numeric statement. **Why it fails:** It prevents readers from mapping the picture to “out of how many,” undermining proportional understanding.

## Quick checks for language-robust risk displays <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** A reader who cannot read the text cannot still tell what each group total is and how many are affected. **Quick Check:** Hide the narrative text and verify the icon arrays still communicate group totals and affected counts. **Stronger Test:** Test comprehension with bilingual users under time pressure and confirm correct proportional comparisons.

## What to do instead when visuals cannot carry the message <!-- role: fix -->

- Provide the same risk information in the audience’s native language when feasible.
- Reduce linguistic complexity in the numeric description so the key denominators are easy to locate and match to the visual.
- Add simple labels on the icon arrays indicating total group size and affected count.
- Use additional supports for interpreting visuals when the audience is unfamiliar with graphical displays.
