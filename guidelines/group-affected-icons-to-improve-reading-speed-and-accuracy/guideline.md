## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When using icon arrays to show how many are affected, group the affected icons together rather than scattering them randomly.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Grouping supports faster and more accurate estimation by reducing visual search and counting effort.

- **The Principle:** Reduced visual search cost improves estimation
- **The Evidence:** [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Estimate “how many out of N” are affected
- **Data Type:** Frequencies/proportions shown with icons (e.g., 10×10 arrays)
- **Audience:** General public or patients

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The message requires conveying randomness of who is affected.
- **Reason:** Random scatter may better imply stochastic occurrence, even if it slows counting. [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Less visual representation of randomness.
- **The Risk:** Viewers may infer clustering in reality if the grouping appears spatially meaningful.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Randomly distributing affected icons without labels or counts.
- **Why it fails:** Increases estimation difficulty and invites miscounts. [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users visibly “hunt” across the grid to count cases.
- **The Test:** Time how long it takes a user to answer “how many are affected?”—if slow, consider grouping.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Re-render the icon array with contiguous affected icons.
- **Best Fix:** Combine grouped icons with an explicit “X out of N” label to prevent misreading. [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]
