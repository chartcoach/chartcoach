## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Use trend animation primarily for live presentations or storytelling contexts where engagement is a priority and a narrator controls the focus.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Animation evokes an emotional response and keeps the audience interested. When paired with a presenter, the downsides of animation (not knowing where to look) are mitigated because the speaker directs the eye.
*   **The Principle:** **Guided Attention**. The presenter "primes" the observer to look at a particular part of the chart before a significant event occurs.
*   **The Evidence:** Participants in the study found animation "fun," "exciting," and "emotionally touching." While it was prone to errors in analysis, it was the fastest technique for presentation tasks and strongly preferred by users for its entertainment value [@robertson_effectiveness_2008].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** communicating a specific narrative or insight to a passive audience (e.g., a TED talk).
*   **Data Type:** Dynamic multi-dimensional data where the "movement" helps tell the story.
*   **Audience:** Non-expert audiences or groups where maintaining attention is critical.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** The user needs to make critical decisions based on the data.
*   **Reason:** The "excitement" of animation can mask data errors. Users in the study sometimes "lost" the data points or failed to notice reversals because they were distracted by the movement [@robertson_effectiveness_2008].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** Analytical rigor. Users may feel they understand the data better than they actually do (illusion of competence).
*   **The Risk:** The audience might focus on the "balls flying everywhere" rather than the actual trends.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Letting the audience watch the animation silently without guidance.
*   **Why it fails:** Without a narrator saying "watch the blue circle," the user’s eye wanders, and critical trends are missed.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Is the screen chaotic with many moving parts?
*   **The Test:** Remove the audio/narration. Does the visualization still clearly convey the specific insight? If not, the animation relies entirely on the presenter (which is acceptable for presentations, but not for standalone tools).

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Highlight or select the specific entities relevant to the story, greying out the background noise during the animation.
*   **Best Fix:** Ensure the animation is accompanied by a strong narrative script that cues the audience on where to look *before* the movement happens.
