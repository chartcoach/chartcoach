## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Use plain language in all written and verbal explanations of risks, benefits, and options.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Plain language reduces literacy barriers by removing jargon and structuring information so people can process it correctly.

- **The Principle:** Reduce cognitive and literacy load
- **The Evidence:** Plain-language materials improve understanding [@fagerlinHelpingPatientsDecide2011].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand treatment options well enough to weigh trade-offs.
- **Data Type:** Any risk/benefit description, especially numerically supported evidence.
- **Audience:** Patients, including those with limited health literacy or numeracy.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** None stated in the paper.
- **Reason:** Not specified.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Less technical precision and fewer discipline-specific terms.
- **The Risk:** Oversimplification if key clinical distinctions are removed.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Keeping clinical/statistical jargon and merely adding a brief definition once.
- **Why it fails:** Patients still can’t reliably process the information in real time [@fagerlinHelpingPatientsDecide2011].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Dense blocks of text; frequent acronyms; multiple unfamiliar terms per sentence.
- **The Test:** Replace all medical/statistical jargon with everyday words; if meaning changes substantially, the original was too jargon-heavy.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Remove jargon and shorten sentences; add headings and whitespace.
- **Best Fix:** Rewrite as a logically sequenced explanation focused on the decision, using plain language and supportive layout [@fagerlinHelpingPatientsDecide2011].
