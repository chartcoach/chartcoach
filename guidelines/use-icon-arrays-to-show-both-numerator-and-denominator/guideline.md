## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Add icon arrays next to numeric risk statements so viewers can see both the count affected (numerator) and the total at risk (denominator).

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Icon arrays visually encode the whole set and the affected subset, making the denominator harder to ignore and reducing denominator neglect—especially when group sizes differ. The reviewed studies show that adding icon arrays increased accuracy and reduced over/underestimation in treatment risk reduction judgments when denominators were inconsistent [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Judge treatment benefit/harm (e.g., risk reduction) accurately.
- **Data Type:** Ratios/proportions (events out of totals), especially with two groups (treated vs. untreated).
- **Audience:** General public and patients, particularly those with lower numeracy or limited language proficiency.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Your audience has very low ability to interpret graphs (low graph literacy) and you cannot provide support.
- **Reason:** The paper reports that people with low graph literacy benefit less from icon arrays than those with higher graph literacy [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More space and visual complexity than numbers alone.
- **The Risk:** Viewers with low graph literacy may still misunderstand or fail to use the display effectively [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Showing only the number harmed/benefited (numerator) without a visible total.
- **Why it fails:** It invites denominator neglect by making the foreground count salient while hiding the background at-risk population [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The visualization makes the event count pop out but doesn’t visually communicate the size of the group it came from.
- **The Test:** Ask a viewer to explain “out of how many?” for each option without rereading the text; if they can’t, the denominator is not visually supported [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add an icon array representing the full denominator for each option and highlight the affected icons.
- **Best Fix:** Provide paired icon arrays (one per option) so both denominators are visibly comparable and the part-to-whole relation is explicit [@garcia-retameroUsingVisualAids2012].
