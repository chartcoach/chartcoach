## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Add concise annotations and visual highlights (ranges, arrows, contours) only where they help prove the point or provide context the chart cannot encode.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Annotations both guide attention and add information beyond the central statement—especially explanations that answer “why,” which the chart’s marks alone often can’t convey, as described in [@muth_better_charts_2017].

- **The Principle:** Augment-the-graphic (guided attention + external context)
- **The Evidence:** [@muth_better_charts_2017]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand what happened at a notable change, crossover, or period—and why it matters.
- **Data Type:** Trend charts with meaningful events (e.g., a crossover where one product overtakes another).
- **Audience:** Readers who need narrative context to interpret the visual evidence.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The chart is intended to be a neutral reference graphic and readers must draw their own conclusions without narrative framing.
- **Reason:** Annotations can over-direct interpretation; Muth frames them as a way to support a specific point and add “why” context when needed [@muth_better_charts_2017].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Visual simplicity—annotations add clutter and consume space.
- **The Risk:** Over-annotation can crowd the chart and distract from the main series [@muth_better_charts_2017].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Annotating many points because they are “interesting,” without tying them to the headline point.
- **Why it fails:** The reader loses focus; annotations should reinforce the intended message or supply essential missing context [@muth_better_charts_2017].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Text boxes and arrows compete with the data or make it hard to see the main pattern.
- **The Test:** Remove annotations temporarily: if the headline point becomes unclear, keep the minimal set that restores clarity; if nothing changes, delete them [@muth_better_charts_2017].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Keep only one or two annotations at the most important moment(s) supporting the point.
- **Best Fix:** Rewrite annotations to be shorter and pair them with subtle highlights (e.g., a light range) so they guide attention without overwhelming the data [@muth_better_charts_2017].
