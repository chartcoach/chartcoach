## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When presenting quantitative information to African American veteran patients, do not rely on graphs alone—add comprehension scaffolding (explicit takeaways and guided reading cues).

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

In this veteran primary-care sample, African Americans scored lower than Whites on graph literacy, indicating a higher likelihood of misreading graphical displays without support.

- **The Principle:** Unequal graphical literacy creates unequal access to information
- **The Evidence:** African Americans had lower graph literacy than Whites, including in adjusted analyses; older African Americans showed lower graph literacy than older Whites, and younger African Americans showed lower graph literacy than younger Whites [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Interpreting risk/benefit visuals or trends to make a health decision
- **Data Type:** Quantitative comparisons commonly graphed in patient education
- **Audience:** African American veterans in outpatient primary care [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013]

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** You have direct evidence (testing) that the specific graph format is well understood by the specific patient group
- **Reason:** Group-level averages may not apply to a tested, local design and audience [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More space for explanations and guided cues
- **The Risk:** Added text may feel repetitive to some readers [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding more chart types (multiple panels) to “clarify”
- **Why it fails:** Lower graph literacy means complexity can amplify confusion instead of resolving it [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The chart’s meaning depends on the viewer independently extracting relationships or making inferences.
- **The Test:** Ask intended users to answer the exact questions the graph is supposed to resolve (e.g., “Which option has lower risk?”); high error rates indicate insufficient scaffolding [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a one-sentence “What this means” takeaway next to the graph.
- **Best Fix:** Redesign the visual so the intended conclusion is explicit and then verify comprehension with African American veterans in the target age group(s) [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].
