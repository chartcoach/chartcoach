## Display a caption for narration that is complex or central to the message <!-- role: advice -->

When a spoken proposition is important or conceptually dense, present the same information concurrently as an on-screen caption.

## Speech-only propositions are more likely to be lost than propositions reinforced by text and visuals <!-- role: reason -->

Viewers can miss or fail to retain information delivered only in narration, especially when the message is central and must be integrated with visuals; concurrent captions provide a second channel for encoding and support immediate recall.

**Mechanism:** Redundant textual representation increases the chance the proposition is encoded and provides a stable reference while visuals change.

**Evidence:** In free recall, propositions delivered only in the speech track were generally poorly recalled, while propositions reinforced by labels/captions were better recalled; redesign that added captions improved recall significantly compared to the original multimedia sequence [@faradayDesigningEffectiveMultimedia1997].

**Notes:** Captions are especially useful for naming processes and describing key relationships that are not visually explicit.

## When caption reinforcement is most needed <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Remember key terms and causal/procedural relationships after a single viewing.
- **Task:** Encode and later reproduce named processes and relationships.
- **Data:** Important concepts not easily inferred from the image alone.
- **Chart Setting:** Short narrated animation where speech content is dense.
- **Audience:** Low domain-knowledge viewers.
- **Success Criterion:** Improved immediate free recall of central propositions.

## When captions may be unnecessary <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** The narration is simple and the visual change makes the proposition obvious without text. **Why:** Additional captions may add clutter without improving recall.

## Tradeoffs of adding captions <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Screen space and visual simplicity. **Risk:** Too much text can compete with the animation for attention. **Mitigation:** Keep captions brief and time them to appear when the visual is stable.

## Common captioning mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **Mistake:** Using captions that introduce new information not present in speech or visuals. **Why it fails:** Viewers may not know what to anchor the text to, increasing confusion.
- **Mistake:** Showing captions while motion is drawing attention elsewhere. **Why it fails:** Viewers may not read the caption during the limited display interval.

## Quick checks for caption effectiveness <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Users recall objects but omit named processes or key relationship statements. **Quick Check:** Identify the two or three most central propositions and verify each appears as both speech and caption. **Stronger Test:** Compare recall of those propositions before/after adding captions in a small pilot.

## What to do instead if you cannot add captions <!-- role: fix -->

- Convert key speech-only propositions into brief on-screen labels tied to specific objects.
- Simplify the narration so each sentence maps to an immediately visible action or state.
- Split the explanation into two shorter segments with a reset frame between them.
- Add an explicit visual state change that externalizes the proposition.
