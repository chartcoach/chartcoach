## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Use icons, decorative shapes, and stylistic embellishments only if they communicate data or essential context; remove anything that doesn’t.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Extra visual elements compete with the data for attention. When non-data ink is ambiguous or excessive, viewers split in whether they find it helpful or irritating, increasing the chance of distraction and misreading (mixed reactions to icons/colors in a bar chart: @saske_multidimensional_2025). At the same time, aesthetics can serve communication goals—drawing attention, lowering perceived barriers, or building emotional connection—when it is intentionally aligned with the message (practitioner perspectives: @schuster_who_2023; editorial design practice: @gregory_data_2024).

- **The Principle:** Signal over decoration (reduce cognitive load; keep attention on encoded values).
- **The Evidence:** @saske_multidimensional_2025; @schuster_who_2023; @gregory_data_2024

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

This advice is designed for specific moments.

- **User Goal:** Quickly compare values, detect differences, or interpret change without decoding extra symbols.
- **Data Type:** Categorical comparisons (e.g., country rankings, before/after markers) where the main message is in position/length.
- **Audience:** Broad or mixed audiences where tolerance for “visual extras” varies and misinterpretation risk is higher.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

List the specific scenarios where you should ignore this rule.

- **Scenario:** The audience needs an approachable, low-threshold style to reduce intimidation or increase willingness to engage.
- **Reason:** A deliberately simple, hand-drawn or friendly aesthetic can support uptake even if it isn’t strictly necessary for decoding values (@schuster_who_2023).
- **Scenario:** The goal includes emotional resonance or narrative engagement (e.g., climate communication) and design elements help audiences relate to the stakes.
- **Reason:** Aesthetics can be an intentional part of the message delivery, not mere decoration (@gregory_data_2024).
- **Scenario:** An icon encodes a real variable or clarifies meaning (e.g., a symbol that unambiguously denotes the measured phenomenon).
- **Reason:** The “extra” element is no longer decorative; it functions as an annotation or redundant cue that supports comprehension (consistent with mixed viewer responses when icons were present: @saske_multidimensional_2025).

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

Be honest about the downsides.

- **The Sacrifice:** Less visual flair and potentially less immediate “wow” or attention-grabbing appeal.
- **The Risk:** Over-stripping can make the piece feel sterile or reduce engagement for audiences who benefit from approachable styling or narrative tone (@schuster_who_2023; @gregory_data_2024).

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

Describe the common anti-patterns or "lazy fixes" people use that don't actually solve the problem.

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding multiple icons, badges, flags, drop shadows, patterns, and extra colors “to make it interesting.”
- **Why it fails:** Viewers differ in what they find helpful versus “too much,” so excess styling can irritate, distract, or create interpretive clutter (@saske_multidimensional_2025).
- **The Wrong Fix:** Using icons that are metaphorical but not defined (no legend/annotation) or that don’t map cleanly to the data.
- **Why it fails:** Ambiguous symbols introduce decoding work without improving accuracy, competing with the primary encodings.
- **The Wrong Fix:** Treating “aesthetics vs. clarity” as a binary and adding decoration without a communication objective.
- **Why it fails:** Aesthetics can help when purposeful, but becomes noise when untethered from message and audience needs (@gregory_data_2024).

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Your eye lands on icons, illustrations, or ornamental elements before it lands on the data marks; or viewers ask what symbols mean.
- **The Test:** For every non-data element, ask: “What specific question does this help answer?” If you can’t state a concrete interpretive benefit in one sentence, remove it.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Delete purely decorative elements (extra icons, gradients, shadows, unnecessary flags) and limit the palette to what encodes categories or emphasis.
- **Best Fix:** Convert remaining visuals into explicit, meaningful annotations—define icons, tie them to a variable or context, and use them sparingly as redundant cues; if engagement is a goal, apply aesthetics consistently to support the narrative rather than layering unrelated embellishments (@schuster_who_2023; @gregory_data_2024).
