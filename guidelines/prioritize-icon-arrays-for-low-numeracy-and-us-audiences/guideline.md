## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Default to including icon arrays when your audience is likely to have low numeracy—especially in US-facing materials.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

In the study’s probabilistic national samples, US participants had more difficulty estimating treatment risk reduction from numbers alone than German participants, and icon arrays were particularly beneficial in closing this gap (bringing inaccurate estimates to similar levels across countries). Low-numeracy participants also showed the largest improvements when icon arrays were present [@garcia-retameroCommunicatingTreatmentRisk2009].

- **The Principle:** Visual scaffolding reduces numeracy-related comprehension disparities
- **The Evidence:** [@garcia-retameroCommunicatingTreatmentRisk2009]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand treatment benefit (relative risk reduction) correctly
- **Data Type:** Comparative risk information for treatment vs no treatment
- **Audience:** Populations with wide numeracy variation; audiences expected to have lower numeracy (highlighted in the US sample) [@garcia-retameroCommunicatingTreatmentRisk2009]

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Expert-only settings where users reliably compute ratios and space is severely limited.
- **Reason:** The marginal benefit of icon arrays may not justify the space/complexity tradeoff in that constrained expert context (the paper’s strongest motivation is aiding low numeracy) [@garcia-retameroCommunicatingTreatmentRisk2009].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Additional design and layout effort to incorporate pictographs well
- **The Risk:** If the icon arrays are too small or poorly reproduced, they may not convey denominators clearly.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Assuming a single numeric statement is “good enough” for everyone.
- **Why it fails:** Numeric-only formats produced substantial inaccuracies among low-numeracy participants and higher error rates in the US sample when denominators differed [@garcia-retameroCommunicatingTreatmentRisk2009].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Materials rely solely on numeric fractions or counts without a part-to-whole visual.
- **The Test:** User test with low-numeracy participants: if denominator-mismatch conditions yield systematic over- or underestimation, add icon arrays [@garcia-retameroCommunicatingTreatmentRisk2009].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a pair of icon arrays that mirror the numeric comparison (treatment vs no treatment).
- **Best Fix:** Design the icon arrays so denominators are visually obvious (full set of icons shown) and event icons are clearly marked, enabling accurate risk-reduction judgments across numeracy levels [@garcia-retameroCommunicatingTreatmentRisk2009].
