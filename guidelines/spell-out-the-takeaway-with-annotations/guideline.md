## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Write the specific points you want readers to notice directly on the chart (as annotations), even if they could technically figure them out on their own.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Explaining the intended reading reduces interpretation work and prevents readers from missing or misreading the message; the chart “respects readers’ time” by making the author’s intended insights explicit rather than leaving them to be discovered by exploration alone [@muth_readers_time_2017].

- **The Principle:** Make the intended message explicit
- **The Evidence:** [@muth_readers_time_2017]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Quickly understand the main story/insight of the chart without extended exploration.
- **Data Type:** Explanatory charts where a few key patterns matter (e.g., seasonal cycles in a time series).
- **Audience:** General readers, workshop participants, or anyone with limited time/attention [@muth_readers_time_2017].

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** You are deliberately designing for open-ended exploration or “discovery,” where you want readers to find insights themselves.
- **Reason:** Spelling out conclusions can reduce self-discovery (the post notes a potential “lost learning effect”) [@muth_readers_time_2017].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More writing, editing, and layout work; more on-chart text.
- **The Risk:** Over-annotation can crowd the graphic or make it feel like “everything has already been said,” reducing exploratory value [@muth_readers_time_2017].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Relying on a caption/description alone to communicate the key point.
- **Why it fails:** Many readers won’t read the description until they’re already confused; the insight arrives too late to save time [@muth_readers_time_2017].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The chart “works” only after reading the caption; the takeaway isn’t visible during a quick scan.
- **The Test:** Hide the title/description and ask: can someone state the intended takeaway from the chart itself in a few seconds? If not, you likely didn’t spell it out [@muth_readers_time_2017].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add 1–3 short callouts that state the primary pattern(s) you want noticed (e.g., where typical maximum/minimum occurs) [@muth_readers_time_2017].
- **Best Fix:** Integrate a narrative layer into the chart: place concise, well-positioned annotations at the relevant locations so the insight is read in-place, not inferred later [@muth_readers_time_2017].
