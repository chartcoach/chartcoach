## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Present narration and the corresponding visual (animation step) at the same time, not in separate blocks.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Simultaneous presentation reduces the need to hold a verbal or visual representation in working memory while waiting for the corresponding information, freeing capacity for selecting, organizing, and integrating.

- **The Principle:** Synchronizing / Temporal contiguity effect
- **The Evidence:** Learners show better transfer when animation and narration are simultaneous rather than successive [@mayerNineWaysReduce2003].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Connect what is said with what is shown to understand causal relations.
- **Data Type:** Stepwise explanations where each narrated statement maps to a visual change.
- **Audience:** Learners likely to be overloaded by holding one stream while waiting for the other.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The lesson is already structured as very short alternating micro-segments (few seconds each) that minimize holding demands.
- **Reason:** The paper notes the temporal contiguity disadvantage is reduced when successive presentations are broken into bite-size alternating segments [@mayerNineWaysReduce2003].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Requires tighter production and timing alignment.
- **The Risk:** Poor synchronization (mismatched timing) can confuse rather than help.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Playing the full narration first and then playing the full animation (or vice versa).
- **Why it fails:** It increases representational holding demands and can overload working memory [@mayerNineWaysReduce2003].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users say “I heard it, but I didn’t see it,” or “I saw it, but I don’t remember what was said.”
- **The Test:** For each key sentence, verify the matching visual event occurs concurrently (or within the same moment).

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Move narration cues to align with the relevant visual moments.
- **Best Fix:** Edit the lesson so narration and animation are designed together, with step-by-step synchronization throughout [@mayerNineWaysReduce2003].
