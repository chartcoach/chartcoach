## Add an action threshold and concrete advice to risk ladders <!-- role: advice -->

When using a risk ladder to communicate risk magnitude, include a clearly marked action threshold and a short description of what actions are recommended at different risk levels.

## Why thresholds and advice improve decisions and reduce anxiety <!-- role: reason -->

A risk ladder can convey magnitude, but people also need interpretive standards for what the magnitude implies. Action thresholds and guidance connect the magnitude display to decisions and can prevent confusion or undue worry.

**Mechanism:** Thresholds provide a decision reference point, and advice reduces ambiguity about what to do given a level of risk.

**Evidence:** Risk ladder work in environmental risk communication showed that including recommended actions appropriate to levels of radon risk eased anxiety and promoted good decision making, and an example ladder includes an explicit action standard and advice. [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]

**Notes:** The threshold should not replace communicating the absolute magnitude; it should contextualize it.

## When to add thresholds and advice <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Decide whether to take a protective action based on risk level.
- **Task:** Interpret magnitude against a standard; choose an action.
- **Data:** Risks that can be ordered and compared on a ladder.
- **Chart Setting:** Consumer-facing hazard communication, screening/prevention guidance.
- **Audience:** General public; varying numeracy.
- **Success Criterion:** Users can state whether action is needed and what action is recommended.

## When not to follow it <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** There is no accepted standard for what action should be taken at a given risk level. **Why:** A threshold would imply false certainty or consensus.

## Tradeoffs and risks <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Adding advice increases text and design complexity. **Risk:** Users may treat the threshold as a strict cliff and ignore differences above it. **Mitigation:** Communicate both the threshold and the absolute magnitude.

## Common failure modes <!-- role: mistakes -->

**Mistake:** Showing a ladder of risks without stating what any level means for action. **Why it fails:** Users may understand rank but remain unable to decide what to do.

## Quick tests <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Users can identify “higher risk” but cannot say whether they should act. **Quick Check:** Ask “At what value would you act, and what would you do?” using only the ladder. **Stronger Test:** Test whether users’ chosen actions match the intended guidance across several ladder positions.

## What to do instead <!-- role: fix -->

- Mark a decision threshold directly on the ladder with a label stating what it means.
- Add a brief advice block describing actions for ranges of risk levels.
- Provide a short statement clarifying that risks above the threshold can still differ materially.
- Use a different decision aid format if actionable thresholds cannot be justified.
