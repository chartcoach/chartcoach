## Titles at the top improve attention and recall <!-- role: advice -->

Place a clear, message-carrying title at the top of the visualization. Use the title to express what the viewer should remember about the data.

## Why top titles improve recall quality <!-- role: reason -->

Viewers allocate substantial attention to text, and especially titles, during encoding and recognition, making titles strong semantic hooks for later recall.

**Mechanism:** A prominent title provides a semantic association that supports recognition and helps viewers reconstruct the visualization’s message during recall.

**Evidence:** Titles were fixated frequently during encoding and were the most commonly mentioned element in recall descriptions; visualizations with titles had higher average recall-description quality than those without, and top-positioned titles were fixated and described more than bottom-positioned titles [@borkinMemorabilityVisualizationRecognition2016].

**Notes:** When titles are missing or overlooked, viewers fall back to other text (e.g., paragraphs, labels), which can reduce consistency of what is recalled.

## When this applies to your visualization <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Remember the visualization’s topic and main takeaway later.
- **Task:** Recognition and free-form recall of what the visualization communicates.
- **Data:** Any dataset where a key message or conclusion should be retained.
- **Chart Setting:** Static or lightly interactive charts intended for communication (reports, news, infographics, slides).
- **Audience:** Mixed or general audiences who may not deeply study the chart.
- **Success Criterion:** Viewers can restate the message and at least one supporting detail after viewing.

## When not to follow it <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** The surrounding narrative (e.g., adjacent text) already provides a stable, persistent title equivalent and the figure itself must be title-free by format. **Why:** The title’s role as an in-figure semantic hook is reduced.

## Tradeoffs and risks <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Space that could otherwise be used for data marks. **Risk:** A vague or non-informative title can become the dominant remembered content, displacing the intended message. **Mitigation:** Keep the title short but specific about the takeaway.

## Common failure modes with titles <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **Mistake:** Using a generic title that names only the topic. **Why it fails:** Viewers may recall the topic but not the conclusion or trend.
- **Mistake:** Placing the title in an unexpected location (e.g., bottom). **Why it fails:** It is fixated and recalled less often than top titles.

## Quick tests <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Viewers can say what the chart is “about” but not what it shows. **Quick Check:** Hide the chart and ask a colleague to restate the takeaway after reading only the title. **Stronger Test:** Run a short recall prompt after a brief exposure and score whether the takeaway is correctly reproduced.

## What to do instead <!-- role: fix -->

- Move the title to the top of the figure area and keep it visually prominent.
- Rewrite the title to encode the main conclusion, not just the subject.
- If a title cannot be used, add a short explanatory paragraph or labels that explicitly state the message.
- If space is limited, shorten other non-essential surrounding text to preserve a top title.
