## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Define variables and categories unambiguously, including **membership criteria** and known **overlaps** between categories.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Ambiguity can allow users to choose definitions that align with their own viewing codes; this can increase interpretive variance and make a designer-intended reading less verifiable.

- **The Principle:** Ambiguity shifts interpretation control to user assumptions
- **The Evidence:** [@hullmanVisualizationRhetoricFraming2011a]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Correctly interpret categories and comparisons
- **Data Type:** Demographics, taxonomies, grouped measures where overlaps are plausible
- **Audience:** Mixed-knowledge audiences with differing prior beliefs

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** When strategic ambiguity is essential to invite multiple legitimate readings
- **Reason:** Some narrative pieces intentionally leave room for interpretation; clarity may undermine the intended dialectic experience [@hullmanVisualizationRhetoricFraming2011a].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More annotation/space
- **The Risk:** Over-defining can slow reading and reduce engagement.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Relying on presumed shared knowledge (“everyone knows what this category means”)
- **Why it fails:** Knowledge assumptions vary widely and can interact with cultural codes to change meaning [@hullmanVisualizationRhetoricFraming2011a].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Readers ask “what counts as X?” or interpret categories as mutually exclusive when they’re not
- **The Test:** Have someone paraphrase each category definition; if they invent criteria, you’re ambiguous.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add short parenthetical definitions in a legend or tooltip.
- **Best Fix:** Include explicit overlap notes (e.g., “may overlap with …”) and a linked methodology description.
