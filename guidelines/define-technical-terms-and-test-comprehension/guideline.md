## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Define key terms that look familiar but have expert-specific meanings, and user-test whether recipients interpret them as intended.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Misunderstandings arise when experts use common words in uncommon ways (e.g., “precipitation” having a technical threshold), and clarity is an empirical question resolved by testing with users rather than assuming shared meaning [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Correctly interpreting forecasts, warnings, and evidence summaries.
- **Data Type:** Any message relying on defined thresholds, categories, or technical criteria.
- **Audience:** Nonexperts and cross-disciplinary experts.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** A specialized internal report for a homogeneous expert audience using standardized definitions.
- **Reason:** Shared technical conventions may already exist and be enforced (though ambiguity can still occur) [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More space and time for definitions and testing.
- **The Risk:** Oversimplified definitions can omit important boundary conditions [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Using a technically correct term without explaining the operational definition.
- **Why it fails:** Users supply their own everyday meaning, leading to systematic misinterpretation of the uncertainty or category [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users make confident but inconsistent interpretations of the same term.
- **The Test:** Ask users to paraphrase the term and apply it to an example; failure indicates you need a clearer definition [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add parenthetical operational definitions (what counts, where measured, what threshold).
- **Best Fix:** Replace the term with a more familiar equivalent phrasing that conveys the intended category and boundary (validated by user testing) [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].
