## Use pictograms as message-aligned memory cues <!-- role: advice -->

Add pictograms only when they clearly connect to the visualization’s topic or message and can serve as a memorable cue.

## Why pictograms can help without distracting <!-- role: reason -->

Pictograms can act as visual associations that aid recognition and are linked to better recall descriptions, while not necessarily consuming the most attention during encoding.

**Mechanism:** A recognizable object can provide a fast visual retrieval cue that supports recognition and helps viewers reconstruct what the visualization was about.

**Evidence:** Human recognizable objects (including pictograms) were far more common in the most recognizable visualizations than in the least recognizable, and visualizations with pictograms had higher average recall-description quality than those without [@borkinMemorabilityVisualizationRecognition2016].

**Notes:** The benefit depends on pictograms being “used appropriately,” i.e., aligned with the data/message rather than arbitrary decoration.

## When this applies to your visualization <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Remember and recognize the visualization later.
- **Task:** Recognition and qualitative recall of topic/message.
- **Data:** Any, especially when topic can be anchored to concrete objects (organizations, products, countries, domains).
- **Chart Setting:** Communication-focused graphics (news, infographics, explainers).
- **Audience:** General audiences who benefit from quick visual cues.
- **Success Criterion:** Higher recognition and accurate recall of the message.

## When not to follow it <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** A pictogram cannot be clearly tied to the data or message. **Why:** It can misdirect attention and increase confusion about what the visualization is about.

## Tradeoffs and risks <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Space and visual simplicity. **Risk:** Viewers may remember the pictogram more than the data/message if the mapping is unclear. **Mitigation:** Ensure the pictogram reinforces the message rather than competing with it.

## Common failure modes <!-- role: mistakes -->

**Mistake:** Adding a pictogram that is unrelated to the data or conclusion. **Why it fails:** It creates a memorable but misleading association.

## Quick tests <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** Viewers recall the object but misstate the topic or conclusion. **Quick Check:** Ask someone to describe the visualization from memory; see whether the pictogram leads to the right topic. **Stronger Test:** Compare recall-description quality for versions with and without the pictogram.

## What to do instead <!-- role: fix -->

- Replace the pictogram with a short message annotation if the connection is primarily semantic.
- Use a pictogram only as part of message redundancy (paired with explicit text stating the takeaway).
- Simplify or remove competing decorative graphics that do not support the data/message.
- Emphasize key data marks with labels so recall is anchored to the data as well as the cue.
