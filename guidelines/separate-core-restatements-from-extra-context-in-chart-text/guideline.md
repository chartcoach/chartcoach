## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Classify your text into (1) restatements of the core data and (2) added context, then keep (1) as on-chart annotations and move (2) to the subtitle or footnote.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Separating “what the data says” from “what you’re adding” reduces competition for attention and prevents secondary facts from diluting the main message. [@mintzer_simple_data_2024] recommends keeping restatements closely connected to the chart (annotations) and pushing other info farther away (subtitle/footnote) depending on how much it should shape interpretation.

- **The Principle:** Prioritize and stage information by proximity to the graphic
- **The Evidence:** [@mintzer_simple_data_2024]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand the main insight first, then optionally read supporting details
- **Data Type:** Simple story with a dominant takeaway plus supplementary facts (e.g., “80% had prior reports”)
- **Audience:** Mixed-attention audiences (some skim, some read deeply)

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The “context” is necessary to avoid misinterpretation of the visual (i.e., without it the chart is misleading).
- **Reason:** In that case, the context must be elevated closer to the chart rather than deferred [@mintzer_simple_data_2024].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Less immediate visibility for secondary insights.
- **The Risk:** Readers who don’t scroll/read footnotes may miss nuance.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Packing multiple unrelated facts into the chart area (or into one long subtitle) so everything competes.
- **Why it fails:** The key contrast becomes harder to perceive; the “simple data” story stops feeling simple [@mintzer_simple_data_2024].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The subtitle/annotation area reads like a paragraph with multiple ideas and no hierarchy.
- **The Test:** Identify the single core takeaway in one sentence. If it’s not the closest text to the data, you’ve violated the rule.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Cut nonessential sentences from annotations and move them to a footnote.
- **Best Fix:** Use a hierarchy: on-chart annotation for the core gap, subtitle for interpretive context that should guide reading, footnote for details that can wait (e.g., prevention links, source notes) as in [@mintzer_simple_data_2024].
