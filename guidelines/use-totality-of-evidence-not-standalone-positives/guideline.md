## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Base claims on the totality of evidence across studies, not on one statistically significant result.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

The paper emphasizes that many teams test similar questions and it is misleading to spotlight a single significant finding; multiple testing across the field inflates the chance that some study will report significance even under null, reducing PPV for isolated claims.

- **The Principle:** Evidence aggregation reduces the “one lucky study” problem
- **The Evidence:** [@ioannidisWhyMostPublished2005]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Decide what is likely true and stable in the literature
- **Data Type:** Research questions with multiple published studies
- **Audience:** Guideline developers, reviewers, clinicians, policymakers

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** A genuinely novel question with only one study available
- **Reason:** Synthesis is not possible yet; claims should be explicitly tentative. [@ioannidisWhyMostPublished2005]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Requires more time and access to broader literature
- **The Risk:** If synthesis itself is biased (e.g., based on biased constituent studies), conclusions can still mislead. [@ioannidisWhyMostPublished2005]

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** “Vote counting” only significant vs non-significant studies without considering field context
- **Why it fails:** The paper stresses that power, bias, and R shape credibility; simple tallies can misrepresent truth. [@ioannidisWhyMostPublished2005]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** A conclusion cites one “positive” paper while ignoring the rest of the literature
- **The Test:** Ask: “Are other studies on the same question considered, and is the conclusion aligned with the total pattern?”

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a structured review of existing studies and how the new result fits
- **Best Fix:** Conduct (or rely on) confirmatory, low-bias synthesis of high-quality studies and interpret claims in that full context. [@ioannidisWhyMostPublished2005]
