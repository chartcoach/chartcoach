## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

When comparing two options, show Drug A as the baseline absolute frequency and show Drug B as the incremental increase/decrease from Drug A (not only a second absolute total).

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Presenting an explicit baseline plus a highlighted change focuses attention on the difference that decision-makers must evaluate (the “delta”), rather than forcing them to compute it themselves.

- **The Principle:** Make the comparative difference perceptually explicit
- **The Evidence:** The study’s formats presented Drug A as absolute risk/benefit and Drug B as incremental change (e.g., “5 fewer out of 100”), and pictographs with highlighted incremental blocks supported higher gist and verbatim understanding than text/tables [@taitEffectFormatParents2010].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Determine how switching from A to B changes outcomes
- **Data Type:** Two-group comparisons with small-to-moderate deltas across multiple outcomes
- **Audience:** Lay decision-makers reviewing research consent information

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Users must know the total (absolute) risk of Drug B and will not have a way to derive it.
- **Reason:** Incremental-only presentation can obscure totals unless the baseline and delta are both visible and interpretable; ensure the baseline remains available (the study provided summary screens and allowed review) [@taitEffectFormatParents2010].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More explanation may be needed to ensure users understand “compared to Drug A”
- **The Risk:** Users may confuse “incremental change” with “total risk” if the visual emphasis is unclear [@taitEffectFormatParents2010].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Presenting two absolute numbers without visually emphasizing the difference.
- **Why it fails:** Users must compute the delta themselves, increasing cognitive burden and risking misinterpretation; the paper’s approach explicitly highlighted the change for Drug B [@taitEffectFormatParents2010].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users can state absolute frequencies for A but misstate how B differs from A (direction or magnitude).
- **The Test:** Ask “Compared to A, how many more/fewer out of 100 for B?”; if many fail, the delta is not being communicated clearly [@taitEffectFormatParents2010].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add an explicit “Compared to Drug A…” delta statement beside the Drug B display.
- **Best Fix:** In pictographs, keep Drug A’s baseline blocks visible and mark the changed subset for Drug B with a distinct outline/border treatment (as in the study’s incremental pictograph) [@taitEffectFormatParents2010].
