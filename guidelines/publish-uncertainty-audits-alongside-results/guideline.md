## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Alongside outcome estimates, provide a structured summary of key threats to validity (bias risks and methodological weaknesses) that could change the estimate or its uncertainty.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Without an audit, observers must infer uncertainty from intuition, which is vulnerable to predictable errors; explicit summaries of methodological threats (e.g., lack of blinding, selection effects) create a profile of uncertainty that is otherwise hidden [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Judging how much to trust a study’s conclusions.
- **Data Type:** Empirical studies and trials where design choices affect bias and uncertainty.
- **Audience:** Policymakers, practitioners, and informed publics relying on study claims.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** A message constrained to a single headline-length alert.
- **Reason:** The audit may not fit; provide a pointer/link to the full structured uncertainty summary instead [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Requires discipline-wide agreement on audit dimensions and extra reporting effort.
- **The Risk:** Can surface uncomfortable limitations that invite controversy if institutions do not stand behind transparent reporting [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Listing limitations as an unstructured paragraph of caveats.
- **Why it fails:** Readers cannot tell which threats are most serious or recurrent, and may discount the entire section [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Readers overgeneralize from a single flaw (“so it’s useless”) or miss serious bias sources entirely.
- **The Test:** Ask readers to name the top 2–3 threats to the result; if they cannot, the uncertainty profile is not legible [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Convert “limitations” into a checklist of named bias categories with low/unclear/high risk.
- **Best Fix:** Use a standardized risk-of-bias style table and connect each flagged threat to how it could shift the estimate or widen uncertainty [@fischhoffCommunicatingScientificUncertainty2014].
