## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

For audiences that include African American veterans under 65, minimize required calculations and interpretive math steps; make numeric conclusions explicit.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Lower objective and subjective numeracy increases the chance that patients will misunderstand probabilities, tradeoffs, or counts—especially when they must compute, convert, or compare numbers themselves.

- **The Principle:** Computation burden drives misunderstanding under low numeracy
- **The Evidence:** After adjusting for covariates, younger African Americans had lower objective numeracy and lower subjective numeracy than younger Whites [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understanding probabilities/benefits/harms to decide (screening, medications, procedures)
- **Data Type:** Percentages, frequencies, ratios, risk reductions, medication dosing schedules
- **Audience:** Younger (\<65) African American veterans in primary care [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013]

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** A clinical task inherently requires patient-performed calculations (e.g., home titration protocols) and cannot be simplified further
- **Reason:** The computation is part of safe execution; simplification must be balanced with accuracy and safety [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Less detail about underlying math and assumptions
- **The Risk:** Patients who want full numeric detail may feel information is incomplete [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Providing more numbers (extra columns, multiple statistics) to “increase transparency”
- **Why it fails:** Additional numeric fields can overwhelm lower-numeracy readers and reduce correct interpretation [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The design requires converting between percent and count, comparing small differences, or combining multiple values to reach a conclusion.
- **The Test:** Identify every step that requires arithmetic or probability reasoning; if the message needs more than one step, it likely exceeds the numeracy capacity of part of the audience [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Provide the computed result in words next to the numbers (“This option has the lower risk.”).
- **Best Fix:** Redesign the numeric presentation so users can answer the key question without calculation, then validate comprehension with younger African American veterans [@rodriguezHealthLiteracyNumeracy2013].
