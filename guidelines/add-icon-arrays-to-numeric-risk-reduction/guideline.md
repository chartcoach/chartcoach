## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Add icon arrays alongside numerical medical risk information when communicating baseline risk and risk with treatment.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Icon arrays support mapping probabilities onto countable individuals, which increases accuracy when people translate medical risks into expected frequencies. In experiments, adding icon arrays improved accurate understanding of risk reduction across participants, with a particularly strong benefit for relatively low-numeracy individuals.

- **The Principle:** Visual grounding of probabilities in countable units
- **The Evidence:** [@galesicUsingIconArrays2009]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand how much a treatment/screening changes risk (risk reduction)
- **Data Type:** Baseline risk vs treated risk (two-state outcome, e.g., event vs no event)
- **Audience:** Patients/general audiences, especially groups with lower numeracy (including older adults)

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** You cannot show both baseline and treated risks (only one state can be displayed).
- **Reason:** The paper’s comprehension gains were tested with icon arrays showing baseline and treatment risk; it does not establish benefits for one-sided displays.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** More space and visual complexity than numbers alone
- **The Risk:** Viewers may focus on the picture and skip the exact numeric statement

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Replacing numbers with an icon array but omitting the numeric values entirely
- **Why it fails:** The tested benefit was for icon arrays added to numerical descriptions for comprehension; the paper does not claim numbers can be dropped without consequence [@galesicUsingIconArrays2009].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users can describe the picture but cannot answer “out of 1,000, how many…?” consistently.
- **The Test:** Ask users to report expected counts with and without treatment; check whether their implied risk reduction is correct.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a paired icon array (without treatment / with treatment) next to the numeric statement.
- **Best Fix:** Present both numeric values and paired icon arrays that depict the same denominator and highlight affected individuals consistently [@galesicUsingIconArrays2009].
