## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Do not treat decluttering or adding focus annotation as a trust-building tactic; validate trustworthiness separately.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Trust judgments depend on more than visual cleanliness; highlighting and narrative can be interpreted as biased framing.

- **The Principle:** Visual rhetoric can trigger suspicion even when it improves recall
- **The Evidence:** Ajani et al. found no strong evidence that decluttering or adding focus reliably improved trustworthiness ratings; qualitative comments noted focused graphs could seem to “have an agenda” [@ajaniDeclutterFocusEmpirically2022a].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Maintaining credibility while communicating a conclusion
- **Data Type:** Any explanatory chart where the designer chooses what to emphasize
- **Audience:** Skeptical or persuasion-aware viewers

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Your evaluation metric is memorability/clarity rather than perceived impartiality.
- **Reason:** Focus cues can still improve recall and clarity even if trust gains are not reliable [@ajaniDeclutterFocusEmpirically2022a].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** You may need additional corroborating elements beyond design (e.g., methodological transparency) to earn trust.
- **The Risk:** Over-emphasis may reduce perceived neutrality for some viewers [@ajaniDeclutterFocusEmpirically2022a].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding more emphasis and narrative text to “convince” viewers.
- **Why it fails:** It can amplify perceived agenda and does not address the underlying basis for trust judgments observed in participant comments [@ajaniDeclutterFocusEmpirically2022a].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Viewers say the chart feels “pushy,” “biased,” or “agenda-driven.”
- **The Test:** Ask viewers whether the chart feels like it’s telling them what to think; if yes, consider whether your focus devices are too strong for the context [@ajaniDeclutterFocusEmpirically2022a].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Tone down the emphasis (keep one highlight but reduce assertive wording).
- **Best Fix:** Separate “what the data shows” from “recommended action” and ensure the focused conclusion is explicitly tied to the highlighted evidence in the chart [@ajaniDeclutterFocusEmpirically2022a].
