## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Assume viewers will look at embellishments; make any non-data imagery relevant to the chart rather than filler.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Explain the principle at work here. Connect the rule to human perception or clear communication. Cite your sources.

- **The Principle:** Salient imagery attracts gaze and consumes viewing time.
- **The Evidence:** Eye-tracking showed participants spent substantial on-screen time on Holmes charts in embellishment regions (including purely non-data imagery), yet comprehension time did not increase [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

This advice is designed for specific moments.

- **User Goal:** Reading a chart in a communication setting where imagery is present.
- **Data Type:** Any chart where non-data imagery occupies meaningful area.
- **Audience:** Typical readers who visually explore the whole graphic.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

List the specific scenarios where you should ignore this rule.

- **Scenario:** You include no embellishment (plain charts).
- **Reason:** The guidance is about attention allocation to imagery; it is irrelevant when there is no non-data imagery to draw gaze [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

Be honest about the downsides.

- **The Sacrifice:** Less freedom to add purely decorative elements.
- **The Risk:** If embellishment is irrelevant, you may be spending viewers’ attention budget on content that doesn’t support the message (the paper shows they do look at it) [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

Describe the common anti-patterns or "lazy fixes" people use that don't actually solve the problem.

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding background art or cartoons “just to make it fun” without tying it to subject or structure.
- **Why it fails:** The paper’s results are consistent with viewers processing imagery; irrelevant imagery would plausibly waste that processing without the demonstrated recall benefit tied to meaningful images [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Large, high-contrast imagery competes visually with the data marks.
- **The Test:** Temporarily hide the data marks—if the remaining imagery still reads as a strong standalone scene, it will likely attract gaze first and should be made semantically supportive rather than arbitrary [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Reduce or remove purely decorative regions that do not cue subject, categories, or trend.
- **Best Fix:** Convert embellishment into “dual-coded” elements that also communicate structure (where imagery aligns with the data encoding), mirroring the integrated approach used in the Holmes charts studied [@batemanUsefulJunkEffects2010a].
