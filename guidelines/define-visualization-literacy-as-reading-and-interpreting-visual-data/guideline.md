## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Define visualization literacy explicitly as the ability to read and interpret visually represented data and extract information from data visualizations.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

A test (or training) can only be valid if its tasks and items align with a precise construct definition; ambiguous definitions lead to mismatched tasks and unclear score meaning. The VLAT paper refines prior definitions to remove ambiguity and broaden beyond narrowly framed “patterns/trends/correlations,” enabling systematic blueprinting of tasks and items.

- **The Principle:** Construct definition drives blueprint alignment
- **The Evidence:** [@leeVLATDevelopmentVisualization2017]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Designing a literacy assessment, curriculum, or evaluation plan for “can users understand charts?”
- **Data Type:** Any (the rule is about defining the construct, not a dataset)
- **Audience:** Non-expert/general users (18+)

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** You are measuring *production* skills (e.g., “can users create appropriate visual encodings?”).
- **Reason:** The VLAT definition intentionally emphasizes consumption (reading/interpretation), not authoring or manipulation. [@leeVLATDevelopmentVisualization2017]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** You may exclude important authoring/interaction competencies from “literacy.”
- **The Risk:** Stakeholders may expect the term “literacy” to include making visualizations, which this definition does not cover. [@leeVLATDevelopmentVisualization2017]

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Using vague wording like “effective/efficient/confident” or “well-established charts.”
- **Why it fails:** It makes it unclear what tasks belong in the assessment and undermines interpretability of scores. [@leeVLATDevelopmentVisualization2017]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Task lists feel arbitrary or overly narrow (e.g., only trend questions), and you cannot justify exclusions.
- **The Test:** Try writing a test blueprint; if you cannot map every item to the definition without hand-waving, the definition is too vague. [@leeVLATDevelopmentVisualization2017]

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Replace any ambiguous construct phrasing with “read, interpret, extract information.”
- **Best Fix:** Rebuild your blueprint (contents × tasks) from the clarified definition, then regenerate items accordingly. [@leeVLATDevelopmentVisualization2017]
