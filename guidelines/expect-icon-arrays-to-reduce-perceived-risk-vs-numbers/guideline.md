## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

If you need perceptions to stay neutral, do not assume icon arrays are perception-neutral; verify whether they reduce perceived seriousness/helpfulness compared to numeric-only presentations.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

In the study, baseline risks and screening helpfulness were rated lower when presented via icon arrays than when presented numerically (ratios of whole numbers). Seeing many unaffected individuals may draw attention to the denominator and reduce perceived seriousness and perceived benefit.

- **The Principle:** Salience of unaffected cases can attenuate perceived risk/benefit
- **The Evidence:** [@galesicUsingIconArrays2009]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Judge how serious a risk is, and how helpful a screening/treatment is
- **Data Type:** Proportional risks presented as either numeric ratios or icon arrays
- **Audience:** Patients/general public where subjective seriousness influences decisions

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** Your explicit goal is to reduce exaggerated fear or debunk overstated treatment benefits.
- **Reason:** The paper suggests icon arrays may be useful when the intent is to counter inflated perceptions by making unaffected individuals salient [@galesicUsingIconArrays2009].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Potentially weaker motivation for protective actions if lower perceived seriousness leads to disengagement
- **The Risk:** Stakeholders may view the visualization as “downplaying” risk even if numerically accurate

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Using icon arrays to “make it clearer” without checking for shifts in perceived seriousness/helpfulness
- **Why it fails:** The paper shows the format can change perceived seriousness and perceived helpfulness even when the underlying numbers are identical [@galesicUsingIconArrays2009].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users say the risk “doesn’t seem that serious” after viewing the icon array compared to a numeric statement.
- **The Test:** Run a brief perception check (seriousness/helpfulness ratings) comparing icon arrays vs numeric ratios for the same risk.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Present both formats (numeric ratios plus icon arrays) so users can triangulate.
- **Best Fix:** If neutrality is required, pilot-test formats and choose the one that preserves intended perception while maintaining comprehension; document the chosen denominator and format to avoid unintended framing effects [@galesicUsingIconArrays2009].
