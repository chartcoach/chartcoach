## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Add signals that cue learners what to focus on and how to organize the material (e.g., emphasize key words in speech, use visual arrows, provide headings/outline).

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Signals guide selecting and organizing processes, reducing incidental processing caused by poorly directed attention amid complex or cluttered material.

- **The Principle:** Signaling effect
- **The Evidence:** Signaled multimedia led to better transfer than unsignaled multimedia in the reported study [@mayerNineWaysReduce2003].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Follow a multi-part explanation without getting lost.
- **Data Type:** Multimedia lessons with many facts, confusing graphics, or high risk of focusing on nonessential details.
- **Audience:** Learners prone to distraction or unfamiliar with the structure of the domain.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The lesson is already minimal, highly coherent, and visually unambiguous.
- **Reason:** Additional cues may be unnecessary if there is little risk of mis-selection or mis-organization.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Additional design work and possible visual clutter.
- **The Risk:** Over-signaling can distract if cues become another competing layer.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Adding decorative highlights everywhere (arrows/colors for many elements).
- **Why it fails:** It can create new incidental processing rather than reducing it [@mayerNineWaysReduce2003].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users attend to vivid but irrelevant details or can’t articulate the lesson’s main structure.
- **The Test:** Ask users to summarize the key steps; if summaries omit core steps or are disorganized, signaling is likely insufficient.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Add a small number of cues for the most important elements (key-term emphasis, a few arrows).
- **Best Fix:** Combine verbal and visual signaling to support selecting and organizing (e.g., headings/outline + targeted arrows + spoken emphasis) [@mayerNineWaysReduce2003].
