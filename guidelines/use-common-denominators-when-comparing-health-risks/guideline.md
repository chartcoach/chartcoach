## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Use the same denominator when presenting multiple risks (e.g., compare “5 out of 100” to “15 out of 100,” not “1 out of 20” to “3 out of 100”).

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Keeping denominators constant reduces cognitive effort and makes ratios directly comparable without requiring conversion.

- **The Principle:** Minimizing computational burden in ratio comparison
- **The Evidence:** [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Compare risk magnitudes across groups or options accurately
- **Data Type:** Proportions/risks shown as frequencies or percentages
- **Audience:** General public or patients, especially under time pressure

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** You must use a fixed, domain-standard base (e.g., “per 100,000” for rare events) for regulatory or reporting consistency.
- **Reason:** A mandated reference class may be required even if it’s not base-10.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** You may need to rescale data and rewrite labels.
- **The Risk:** Over-rounding during conversion can blur small but meaningful differences.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Mixing “%” with “1 in X” or “per 1,000” in the same comparison.
- **Why it fails:** Forces readers to mentally convert formats, increasing confusion and error. [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Two compared risks display different bases (e.g., 1/20 next to 3/100).
- **The Test:** Ask “Can the audience compare values without doing any math?”

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Convert all values to the same denominator and relabel (e.g., all “out of 100”).
- **Best Fix:** Choose a base-10 denominator that matches the risk size (e.g., per 100 for common risks; per 10,000 for rarer ones) and use it consistently. [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]
