## Write chart text in plain language that readers grasp immediately <!-- role: advice -->

Use straightforward wording in titles, descriptions, and annotations, phrased as you would explain the chart to a friend. Avoid overly official or technical phrasing that slows understanding.

## Clear phrasing reduces confusion and lowers the barrier to entry <!-- role: reason -->

Readers often decide within seconds whether to engage with a chart; complicated language can deter them or cause misinterpretation. Plain, familiar words help readers form the right mental model quickly.

**Mechanism:** Simple wording reduces linguistic decoding effort, freeing attention for interpreting the data.

**Evidence:** Straightforward phrasings improve chart readability, and rewording official dataset language into friendlier explanations helps readers understand faster [@muth_text_in_data_visualizations_2022].

**Notes:** Clarity can require time spent rewriting, but it improves comprehension for many readers.

## Apply to all explanatory text that shapes interpretation <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Understand what the chart shows and what it means.
- **Task:** Orient, interpret encodings, and extract the takeaway.
- **Data:** Any; especially when concepts are abstract or domain-specific.
- **Chart Setting:** Explanatory graphics, newsroom charts, or public-facing dashboards.
- **Audience:** Mainstream audiences or mixed literacy levels.
- **Success Criterion:** Readers can paraphrase what the chart shows without jargon.

## When technical wording may be necessary <!-- role: exceptions -->

**Break it when:** The audience is specialized and expects technical terms as the most precise shared vocabulary. **Why:** Replacing standard terms can reduce precision or credibility for expert readers.

## Trade precision-first language for approachability <!-- role: costs -->

**Sacrifice:** Some formal exactness in the most prominent text. **Risk:** Over-simplification can blur important definitions. **Mitigation:** Keep the title conversational and place precise definitions in less prominent text elements.

## Common writing mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

**Mistake:** Copy-pasting the dataset’s formal variable description into the title or subtitle. **Why it fails:** It raises the reading burden and hides the message.

## Quick checks <!-- role: check -->

**Failure Sign:** The title feels like documentation rather than a sentence a person would say. **Quick Check:** Read the title aloud; if it sounds unnatural, rewrite. **Stronger Test:** Ask a non-expert what the chart shows after reading only the title; confusion indicates jargon.

## Fixes when wording is too complex <!-- role: fix -->

- Rewrite the core explanation as a simple sentence describing what the reader sees.
- Replace bureaucratic phrases with common words while keeping meaning.
- Move technical definitions from the title into the description or notes.
- Add a short annotation that translates a key term into plain language.
