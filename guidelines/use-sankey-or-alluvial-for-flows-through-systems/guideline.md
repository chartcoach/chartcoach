## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Use a Sankey or alluvial diagram when you need to show flows—how volume moves through a system or how categories shift between states.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Explain the principle at work here. Connect the rule to human perception or clear communication.

- **The Principle:** Connected bands create a direct visual mapping from source to destination, making transitions and pathways the primary read.
- **The Evidence:** The post recommends flow diagrams for visualizing how things move through a system or how opinions change, naming alluvial and Sankey diagrams and describing typical use cases [@muth_chart_types_guide_2025].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

This advice is designed for specific moments.

- **User Goal:** Understanding transitions (e.g., people switching jobs or voting preferences) or flows of volume (money, energy, traffic).
- **Data Type:** Source→target connections with magnitudes or category transitions.
- **Audience:** Mainstream readers, when the core story is movement/transition [@muth_chart_types_guide_2025].

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

List the specific scenarios where you should ignore this rule.

- **Scenario:** Your goal is simply comparing totals by category, not pathways.
- **Reason:** Flow diagrams prioritize connections; the post points to bars/columns for comparing amounts and shares [@muth_chart_types_guide_2025].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

Be honest about the downsides.

- **The Sacrifice:** Harder to read precise values than simple bar charts.
- **The Risk:** Terminology and interpretation can be confusing; even designers disagree on the Sankey vs alluvial distinction [@muth_chart_types_guide_2025].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

Describe the common anti-patterns or "lazy fixes" people use that don't actually solve the problem.

- **The Wrong Fix:** Debating diagram naming instead of ensuring the audience understands the flows.
- **Why it fails:** The post notes the distinction is not consistently agreed upon; clarity of what’s flowing matters more [@muth_chart_types_guide_2025].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** The main takeaway requires tracing from sources to destinations, not just reading standalone values.
- **The Test:** If readers must answer “where did this come from / where did it go,” a flow diagram is appropriate; if they just need “how much,” it’s not [@muth_chart_types_guide_2025].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Reframe the visualization as a flow diagram (Sankey/alluvial) when pathways are the story.
- **Best Fix:** If pathways are not essential, replace with bars/stacked bars to focus on totals or shares instead [@muth_chart_types_guide_2025].
