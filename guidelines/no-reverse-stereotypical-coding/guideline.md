## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
If you use pink and blue in a gender chart, never reverse the stereotypical assignment (e.g., never use Pink for Men and Blue for Women). Ideally, avoid the combination entirely, but if used, stick to the cultural norm or abandon the colors.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Reversing these colors is dangerous for data accuracy. Readers have a strong intuitive association with "Pink = Women" and "Blue = Men." If a chart flips these to challenge stereotypes, readers often skip the legend and interpret the data "the right way round" based on intuition [@muth_gendercolor_2018]. This can lead to complete misunderstanding of the data (e.g., thinking the situation for women is better than it is).

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Ensuring accurate data reading.
*   **Data Type:** Gender comparisons.
*   **Audience:** Any audience accustomed to Western cultural norms.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** None.
*   **Reason:** The risk of misinterpreting the data values is too high. If you want to challenge stereotypes, use different colors, not reversed colors.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** You lose the ability to make a "clever" ironic statement about gender colors.
*   **The Risk:** By adhering to the norm (if you must use these colors), you unfortunately reinforce the stereotype, even while ensuring clarity.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Using pink and blue as "brand colors" for a publication indiscriminately, leading to charts where pink randomly represents men in one instance and women in another.
*   **Why it fails:** Inconsistency confuses the reader, as seen in *Quartz* charts where authors agreed on the palette but not the assignment [@muth_gendercolor_2018].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** A chart containing pink and blue bars/lines.
*   **The Test:** Look at the chart without the legend. What is your gut reaction to "Who is winning/higher?" Now look at the legend. If your gut reaction was wrong, the design has failed.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Swap the colors back to the stereotype to ensure readability.
*   **Best Fix:** Change the colors entirely to a neutral pair (e.g., Orange and Teal) to avoid the association issue completely.
