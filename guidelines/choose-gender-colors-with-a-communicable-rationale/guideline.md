## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Select gender-category colors based on a rationale you can explain (and that supports the story’s message), not just personal preference or defaults. [@muth_gendercolor_2018]

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

Explain the principle at work here. Connect the rule to human perception or clear communication.

- **The Principle:** A defensible mapping turns color from decoration into meaning; it aligns design choices with editorial intent.
- **The Evidence:** The post describes The Telegraph’s purple/green choice as grounded in UK suffrage movement colors and notes that “having a reference… helped seal the deal,” improving confidence and alignment with the equality message. [@muth_gendercolor_2018]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

This advice is designed for specific moments.

- **User Goal:** Interpret gender comparisons (especially about equality/inequality) with minimal stereotype signaling.
- **Data Type:** Any chart where color encodes men vs. women and the topic is socially sensitive (e.g., pay gaps).
- **Audience:** News/readers who may question framing; stakeholders who need to approve the design choice. [@muth_gendercolor_2018]

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

List the specific scenarios where you should ignore this rule.

- **Scenario:** Rapid, low-stakes exploratory work where the palette will not be published.
- **Reason:** The post’s rationale focus is aimed at publication choices and message framing; internal drafts can prioritize speed. [@muth_gendercolor_2018]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

Be honest about the downsides.

- **The Sacrifice:** More time spent researching, aligning, and documenting the choice.
- **The Risk:** If the rationale is obscure or not communicated, readers may not benefit from it and may still need explicit labeling. [@muth_gendercolor_2018]

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

Describe the common anti-patterns or "lazy fixes" people use that don't actually solve the problem.

- **The Wrong Fix:** Picking “random different colors” to avoid pink/blue but offering no consistent reason or story-level alignment.
- **Why it fails:** Without an explainable basis, the palette can feel arbitrary and won’t help “seal the deal” with collaborators or support the intended message the way The Telegraph’s approach did. [@muth_gendercolor_2018]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** When asked “Why these colors for genders?”, no one on the team can answer beyond “it looked nice.”
- **The Test:** Write a one-sentence justification for the mapping; if you can’t, the palette is likely arbitrary and should be reconsidered. [@muth_gendercolor_2018]

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Agree on a non-stereotyped pair and add a brief note in the style guide or project doc explaining the choice.
- **Best Fix:** Research a context-relevant reference (as in the suffrage-inspired palette example) and document both the source and the intended message the colors should reinforce. [@muth_gendercolor_2018]
