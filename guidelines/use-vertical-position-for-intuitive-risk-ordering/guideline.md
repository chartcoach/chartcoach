## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Map larger risk magnitudes to higher vertical positions (e.g., risk ladders or vertically oriented comparisons).

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

People readily interpret “higher on the page” as “more,” which supports quick ordinal judgments of which risks are larger.

- **The Principle:** Spatial metaphor alignment for magnitude (“higher = more”)
- **The Evidence:** [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Rank risks or see where one risk falls relative to others
- **Data Type:** Multiple risks spanning a range of magnitudes
- **Audience:** General public (e.g., environmental/health risk context displays)

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The display is primarily horizontal due to layout constraints and cannot preserve a clear “higher=more” mapping.
- **Reason:** Forced rotation may reduce intuitive reading if not redesigned. [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Vertical space.
- **The Risk:** Overcrowding labels if many risks are included.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Random ordering of risks on a list or ladder.
- **Why it fails:** Breaks the intuitive mapping and hinders quick judgments. [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** High risks appear below low risks or ordering is inconsistent.
- **The Test:** Ask “Does moving upward always mean higher risk?” If not, redesign.

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Sort risks by magnitude and place them bottom-to-top.
- **Best Fix:** Use a risk ladder with clear tick marks and anchored reference risks to provide context. [@lipkusNumericVerbalVisual2007]
