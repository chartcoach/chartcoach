## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
Write the headline as a declarative statement or hypothesis that tells the reader exactly what the chart proves, rather than a generic description of the topic.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Headlines act as hypotheses that the chart then proves. According to [@muth_better_charts_2017], this approach has multiple cognitive advantages:
*   **The Principle:** attention guidance and retention.
*   **The Evidence:** Readers immediately know what to focus on, saving them time. Furthermore, writing out the central statement helps readers remember the message better than a generic title would [@muth_better_charts_2017].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
This advice applies to explanatory visualizations where the author has a specific insight to convey.
*   **User Goal:** Communicating a specific finding or narrative (e.g., "The iPhone was the most successful product").
*   **Audience:** Readers who need to grasp the main point quickly.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** Exploratory dashboards or neutral reporting.
*   **Reason:** If the goal is to let the user find their own insights without bias, a descriptive title (e.g., "Sales by Year") is more appropriate.

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** You lose neutrality. The chart becomes an argument rather than just a display of data.
*   **The Risk:** If the data does not clearly support the headline, the chart will appear misleading or biased.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Using a topic-based title like "iPhone Sales 2007-2014."
*   **Why it fails:** It tells the reader *what* is being shown, but not *why* it matters or what the insight is [@muth_better_charts_2017].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Does the title end with a question mark or sound like a category?
*   **The Test:** Read the title, then look at the chart. Does the chart immediately act as "proof" for the sentence you just read?

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Rewrite the title to answer the question "What is the point of this chart?"
*   **Best Fix:** Identify the most critical piece of information you want the reader to remember and format it as a full sentence (e.g., change "Temperature Changes" to "Summers Are Getting Hotter") [@muth_better_charts_2017].
