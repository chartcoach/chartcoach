## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->
If your goal is to present a risk in a less alarming way (e.g., to alleviate false fears), use an icon array. Do not rely solely on numerical descriptions if you want to minimize perceived severity.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->
Visual displays force the viewer to see the denominator (the unaffected people) alongside the numerator (the affected people). Numerical descriptions often cause people to focus strictly on the "affected" count, leading to "affect-laden mental imagery."
*   **The Principle:** Denominator Neglect / Salience.
*   **The Evidence:** Participants perceived identical medical risks as *less* serious when presented via icon arrays compared to numerical text. Similarly, the helpfulness of screenings was rated lower in visual conditions than in numerical ones [@galesic_using_2009].

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->
*   **User Goal:** Debunking exaggerated fears or explaining low-probability side effects where patients might otherwise be irrationally afraid.
*   **Data Type:** Risk ratios (e.g., X people out of Y).
*   **Audience:** Patients or the general public who may be prone to "false alarms" regarding medical statistics.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->
*   **Scenario:** When the goal is to encourage urgent action or highlight a severe threat.
*   **Reason:** Because icon arrays dilute the perceived seriousness, they might discourage necessary behavior change (e.g., getting a critical screening) if the user perceives the risk as negligible [@galesic_using_2009].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->
*   **The Sacrifice:** Emotional urgency.
*   **The Risk:** You may unintentionally downplay the benefits of a medical treatment. The study found that treatments were perceived as *less* helpful when shown visually because the risk reduction looked less dramatic.

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->
*   **The Wrong Fix:** Using only the numerator in visuals (e.g., showing only the "dead" people).
*   **Why it fails:** This removes the context of the unaffected population, which is the primary mechanism by which icon arrays lower risk perception.

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->
*   **Visual Sign:** Does the visualization show the "whole" population, or just the "part" that is affected?
*   **The Test:** Does the visualization make the risk look "scary" or "manageable"?

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->
*   **Quick Fix:** Ensure the array displays all individuals (affected and unaffected) to provide proper context.
*   **Best Fix:** If the risk appears too low, consider increasing the total number of icons (see related guideline on denominator size).
