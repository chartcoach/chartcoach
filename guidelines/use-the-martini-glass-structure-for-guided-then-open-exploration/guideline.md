## The Rule <!-- role: advice -->

Start with a single-path narrative that communicates key observations, then explicitly transition to a free-exploration mode.

## The Logic <!-- role: reason -->

The paper identifies the martini glass as a common pattern: a “stem” of tight author-driven flow followed by a “glass” that widens into interactive exploration. This sequencing helps readers build context first and reduces the risk of unproductive digressions during exploration.

- **The Principle:** Constrain early narrative flow to establish meaning, then expand interaction for discovery.
- **The Evidence:** [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010]

## Where to Apply <!-- role: context -->

- **User Goal:** Learn the intended story and then interrogate or personalize it through interaction.
- **Data Type:** Complex or unfamiliar data where readers need onboarding.
- **Audience:** General audiences; readers new to the topic.

## When to Break It <!-- role: exceptions -->

- **Scenario:** The primary objective is quick, linear communication (e.g., a non-interactive presentation).
- **Reason:** The stem-to-glass transition adds complexity that may not be needed when no exploration is desired [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010].

## The Price <!-- role: costs -->

- **The Sacrifice:** Extra design work to create a clear transition and an exploration state.
- **The Risk:** If the “open” phase is not well oriented, readers may still get lost [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010].

## Common Mistakes <!-- role: mistakes -->

- **The Wrong Fix:** Opening interactivity immediately, before any narrative context.
- **Why it fails:** Readers lack the interpretive frame to know what to look for, leading to confusion rather than insight [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010].

## How to Check <!-- role: check -->

- **Visual Sign:** Users start interacting right away but can’t explain what the visualization is “about.”
- **The Test:** Verify that the first-time path contains a clear sequence of observations before any broad exploration controls appear [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010].

## How to Fix <!-- role: fix -->

- **Quick Fix:** Gate advanced controls until after the initial narrative steps.
- **Best Fix:** Script an initial linear walkthrough (annotations/text + highlights/transitions), then reveal broader filters/search/slider controls with explicit prompts [@segelNarrativeVisualizationTelling2010].
