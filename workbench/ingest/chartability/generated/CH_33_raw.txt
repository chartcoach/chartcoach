## Guidance

Ensure geometries and large text have a contrast ratio of at least 3:1 against their background, and regular text maintains a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1.

## Why

Users must be able to easily identify content using their senses, especially sight. Sufficient contrast ensures text and graphical elements are legible and distinguishable for a wide range of users, particularly those with low vision or certain color vision deficiencies. Low contrast is a pervasive accessibility issue, severely hindering comprehension and usability of data visualizations.

### Core Principle

Content must be perceivable through visual distinction.

## When it applies

- Any visualization or interface containing text or graphical elements.
- When visual clarity and legibility are critical for understanding information.
- Especially important for audiences with low vision, certain color vision deficiencies, or cognitive impairments who benefit from clear visual separation.

## Exceptions

None known for fundamental legibility and distinction. While advanced experimental models (like APCA for WCAG 3.0) aim for more perceptually accurate contrast calculations, these are not yet standard requirements.

## Trade-offs

Achieving high contrast might sometimes limit aesthetic color choices or require careful selection of brand colors. However, clarity and accessibility should be prioritized over purely decorative considerations, as insufficient contrast can render a visualization unusable for a significant portion of the audience.

## Signs of Trouble

- **The Squint Test:** If you squint at the visualization, do text or chart elements become difficult or impossible to read against their background?
- **Contrast Checker Failure:** When checked with a tool like WebAIM Contrast Checker, do any text or graphical elements fall below the 4.5:1 (for regular text) or 3:1 (for large text/graphics) thresholds?
- **Feedback:** Users report difficulty distinguishing labels, values, or chart components.
- **Washed-out appearance:** The visualization appears dull or difficult to parse, even to those without known vision impairments.

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach:** Use a contrast checker tool (e.g., WebAIM Contrast Checker) to test existing foreground and background color combinations. Adjust one or both colors directly until the required ratios are met. Prioritize critical text and interactive elements.
- **Moderate approach:** Adopt a pre-designed, high-contrast color palette that is known to meet accessibility standards. Ensure these palettes are applied consistently across all text and graphical elements, including interactive states (e.g., hover, focus).
- **Comprehensive approach:** Integrate automated contrast checks into your design system and development workflow. Use design tokens and programmatic checks to enforce contrast requirements during design and development. Conduct user testing with individuals with low vision to ensure real-world effectiveness and gather feedback on legibility.
