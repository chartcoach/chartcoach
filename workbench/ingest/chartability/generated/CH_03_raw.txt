## Guidance

Maintain a minimum contrast ratio of 4.5:1 for regular text and 3:1 for large text (18pt/24px or 14pt/18.66px bold) and graphical objects against their background. This applies to all chart geometries, text, and interactive components.

## Why

Insufficient contrast makes content difficult or impossible to perceive for users with low vision, color vision deficiencies, or older users. It significantly increases cognitive load as users strain to distinguish elements, hindering comprehension and leading to frustration. Adhering to contrast guidelines ensures fundamental access and readability for a broad audience.

### Core Principle

Perceivable information must be clearly distinguishable from its surroundings to enable all users, especially those with low vision, to access and understand content.

## When it applies

-   When displaying any text within a visualization (titles, labels, annotations, legends).
-   When using graphical elements such as lines, bars, shapes, or interactive components.
-   For all chart types and any audience, ensuring universal readability.
-   When colors are used to distinguish different elements or convey information.

## Exceptions

None known for fundamental contrast requirements for text and graphical objects as per WCAG standards. This is a baseline requirement for accessibility. While there are ongoing discussions about alternative contrast models (e.g., APCA in WCAG 3.0), current standards remain critical.

## Trade-offs

-   **Aesthetic Flexibility**: May restrict the choice of colors for branding or aesthetic preferences, requiring careful selection to meet contrast ratios while maintaining visual appeal.
-   **Cognitive Load for Designer**: Requires more deliberate color palette design and validation during development.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **The Squint Test:** If you squint at the visualization, do text or graphical elements disappear or blend into the background?
-   **Automated Tool Failures:** Contrast checkers (like WebAIM) report failures for text or non-text contrast against WCAG AA or AAA guidelines.
-   **Eye Strain:** Viewers report difficulty reading labels or distinguishing chart elements, often leading to fatigue.
-   **Hidden Information:** Critical information conveyed by graphical elements (e.g., lines, bar outlines) is not visible due to low contrast.

## How to Improve

-   **Quick approach:**
    *   Use a contrast checker (e.g., WebAIM Contrast Checker) to identify failing colors.
    *   Adjust the lightness/darkness of foreground or background colors minimally until the required contrast ratio is met. Prioritize higher contrast for smaller text.
-   **Moderate approach:**
    *   Redesign the color palette for the visualization from scratch with accessibility in mind, using tools like Viz Palette or Chroma. These tools can help identify color combinations that meet contrast requirements and are colorblind-friendly.
    *   Apply contrast ratios systematically across all text and graphical elements, ensuring consistency.
-   **Comprehensive approach:**
    *   Integrate contrast-aware color selection and validation into your design system and development workflow.
    *   Explore advanced color models like APCA (Accessibility Perceivable Contrast Algorithm) for future-proofing, noting that current standards are still WCAG 2.x based.
    *   Provide user-selectable themes (e.g., high-contrast mode) that automatically apply accessible contrast ratios.
