## Guidance

Ensure that geometries and large text (18pt / 24px regular, or 14pt / 18.66px bold) have a contrast ratio of at least 3:1 against their background. Regular text must maintain a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1.

## Why

Low contrast significantly hinders readability and perceivability for a broad range of users, including those with low vision, color vision deficiencies, or cognitive impairments, as well as users in challenging viewing environments (e.g., bright sunlight, older screens). Meeting minimum contrast requirements reduces visual strain and cognitive load, making visualizations accessible and understandable to a wider audience.

### Core Principle

Ensure all users can clearly distinguish essential information regardless of their visual abilities or viewing conditions.

## When it applies

- When any text (labels, titles, annotations) is displayed in a visualization.
- When graphical components (bars, lines, shapes, icons) are used to convey information.
- In any context where information must be clearly distinguishable from its background.

## Exceptions

None. Sufficient contrast is a fundamental requirement for perceivable content. While the *method* of measuring contrast may evolve (e.g., WCAG 3.0's APCA), the *need* for adequate contrast remains absolute.

## Trade-offs

Adhering strictly to current WCAG 2.x contrast ratios might not perfectly reflect perceptual reality for all users, as newer research (e.g., APCA/SPCA) suggests limitations in the underlying color perception model. This may sometimes lead to colors being "technically" compliant but still perceived as low contrast by some, or conversely, colors deemed non-compliant that are perceivable. However, current standards represent the widely accepted baseline.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Faded Appearance:** Text or graphical elements appear washed out, dim, or blend into the background, making them hard to discern.
- **Difficulty Reading:** Users report difficulty reading labels, titles, or distinguishing chart elements, especially in varying lighting conditions or on different display types.
- **Contrast Checker Failure:** Automated contrast checker tools report ratios below the required 4.5:1 for regular text or 3:1 for large text/graphical elements.
- **Visual Strain:** Viewers squint, lean closer, or report eye fatigue when trying to interpret the visualization.

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach:** Use a contrast checker tool (e.g., WebAIM Contrast Checker) to identify elements that fail minimum contrast requirements. Adjust the hue, saturation, or lightness of foreground and/or background colors of the failing elements slightly until they pass. Focus on meeting WCAG AA first.

- **Moderate approach:** Implement a design system or color palette that includes predefined high-contrast color combinations. For dynamic visualizations, ensure that interactive states (e.g., hover, focus) also maintain sufficient contrast against their initial state and background. Consider providing a "high-contrast mode" toggle.

- **Comprehensive approach:** Integrate accessibility into the color selection and design process from the outset. Use modern perceptual contrast algorithms (e.g., APCA for WCAG 3.0 candidates) in addition to WCAG 2.x measurements. Continuously test with diverse user groups, including those with low vision, and iterate on color choices to optimize perceivability for all.
