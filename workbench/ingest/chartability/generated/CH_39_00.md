## Guidance

Ensure geometries and large text (18pt or 14pt bold, or larger) have a contrast ratio of at least 3:1 against their background. Regular text must have a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 against its background.

## Why

This guideline is critical to ensure that content is perceivable for all users, especially those with low vision. Sufficient contrast allows users to easily identify content using their senses, primarily sight, reducing visual strain and cognitive load. Low contrast is a leading cause of accessibility failures, directly impacting the ability to read text, distinguish chart elements, and understand information.

### Core Principle

User must be able to easily identify content using their senses: sight, sound, and touch.

## When it applies

This guideline applies universally to all visual elements within any data visualization, including text, chart geometries (bars, lines, points), and interactive components, across all chart types and presentation mediums.

## Exceptions

While the principle of sufficient contrast is always important, the specific WCAG 2.1 contrast models have known limitations (e.g., as noted by Myndex Research's APCA/SPCA work). WCAG 3.0 is expected to introduce more advanced models that consider size and perceptual angles. These are ongoing developments in *how* contrast is measured, not an exception to the *need* for contrast itself. Therefore, no direct exceptions to the guideline's intent are known.

## Trade-offs

Adhering to strict contrast ratios may sometimes limit aesthetic choices or branding palettes, requiring adjustments to chosen color schemes. This involves balancing visual appeal with fundamental accessibility requirements.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **The Squint Test:** Text or chart elements become difficult to read or distinguish from their background when viewed quickly or with slightly narrowed eyes.
-   **Color Blending:** Adjacent colors in a chart (e.g., between a bar and its background, or text and a chart element) appear to merge, making boundaries unclear.
-   **Automated Audit Failures:** Accessibility testing tools (like WebAIM Contrast Checker) report contrast ratio failures for text or non-text elements.

## How to Improve

-   **Quick approach:** Use a contrast checker tool (like WebAIM's) to identify specific low-contrast areas. Adjust the problematic foreground or background colors slightly until they meet the minimum 3:1 or 4.5:1 ratio. This provides immediate readability improvement.
-   **Moderate approach:** Redesign the chart's color palette using color theory principles that prioritize high contrast while maintaining aesthetic coherence. For data visualization, consider using sequential or diverging palettes that inherently offer good contrast steps. Explore alternative visual encodings (e.g., adding textures, shapes, or thicker strokes) to provide redundancy, especially for non-text elements where exact contrast can be harder to achieve.
-   **Comprehensive approach:** Integrate contrast checking into your design system and development workflow. Establish a set of pre-approved accessible color palettes. For interactive visualizations, ensure that focus states, hover states, and selections also maintain the required contrast ratios against both the regular state and the background. Educate designers and developers on the importance of contrast from the initial design phase.
