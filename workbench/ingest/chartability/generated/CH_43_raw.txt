## Guidance

Ensure all text and graphical elements in your visualization meet minimum contrast ratio requirements against their background. Specifically, regular text must have a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1, while large text (18pt/24px or 14pt/18.66px bold) and meaningful graphical objects (like chart geometries) must have at least a 3:1 contrast ratio.

## Why

Sufficient color contrast is fundamental for perceivability, allowing users to easily distinguish content. It significantly impacts accessibility for individuals with low vision, color vision deficiencies, or age-related vision changes. Without adequate contrast, text becomes unreadable and essential chart elements blend into the background, making the visualization unusable. This is consistently identified as the most frequent accessibility failure in data visualizations.

### Core Principle

Perceivable content is fundamental for all users; visual information must be easily discernible by those with varying visual abilities to ensure equitable access and understanding.

## When it applies

- When displaying any text within a visualization (titles, labels, annotations, legends).
- When using graphical components (lines, bars, points, areas) to convey information.
- Across all chart types and data presentations.
- For all audiences, especially those with low vision or color vision deficiencies.
- In any medium (static, interactive, print, screen).

## Exceptions

None known under current WCAG 2.1 guidelines for informative content. Decorative elements that convey no information might be exempt, but this is rare for visualization components. While future standards (WCAG 3.0) may update evaluation models, current requirements should be met.

## Trade-offs

Adhering to high contrast ratios can sometimes limit aesthetic choices or restrict the use of certain brand-specific color palettes. It may require a more deliberate selection of foreground and background colors to ensure compliance while maintaining visual appeal.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Fuzzy Text:** Text looks blurry or difficult to read, especially smaller fonts, even for users with good vision.
- **Blending Elements:** Chart lines, bars, or data points appear to merge with the background or with each other, making them hard to distinguish.
- **Eyestrain:** Users report discomfort or strain when trying to read or interpret the visualization.
- **Contrast Checker Flags:** Automated tools like WebAIM Contrast Checker report contrast ratios below WCAG 2.1 AA thresholds for regular text (4.5:1) or non-text elements (3:1).
- **High Failure Rate:** If you haven't explicitly checked contrast, there's a very high probability (per Elavsky et al., 2022) your visualization fails this common accessibility criterion.

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach:** Use an online contrast checker (e.g., WebAIM Contrast Checker) to test foreground and background color pairs for all text and graphical elements. Adjust colors until they meet at least WCAG 2.1 AA standards (4.5:1 for regular text, 3:1 for large text/graphics). This is a minimal effort check that can immediately identify and fix major issues.

- **Moderate approach:** Incorporate contrast checking into your design system or development workflow. Select a pre-approved, WCAG-compliant color palette for your visualizations. For interactive elements, ensure state changes (e.g., hover, focus) also maintain sufficient contrast. This proactively prevents low contrast issues.

- **Comprehensive approach:** Beyond WCAG 2.1 AA, aim for AAA contrast where possible (7:1 for regular text, 4.5:1 for large text). Explore tools like Viz Palette (under the "learn" section) to evaluate entire palettes for contrast and legibility across different elements and states. Continuously test with real users, especially those with low vision, to gather feedback on perceivability.
