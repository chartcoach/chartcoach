## Guidance

Ensure regular text has a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 against its background, and large text (including chart geometries and large text elements) has at least 3:1 contrast. This applies to all meaningful visual elements in the visualization.

## Why

Sufficient contrast is fundamental for readability and perceivability, especially for individuals with low vision, color vision deficiencies, or age-related vision changes. Without adequate contrast, text and critical visual elements become difficult or impossible to distinguish, increasing cognitive load and hindering comprehension. WCAG standards specifically address these ratios based on extensive research to ensure broad accessibility.

### Core Principle

Perceivable information must be clearly distinguishable from its surroundings to enable all users, regardless of visual ability, to access and understand content without undue effort.

## When it applies

-   When any text is used in a visualization (labels, titles, annotations, legends).
-   When any graphical elements (bars, lines, points, areas) convey information and need to be distinguished from the background or other elements.
-   When designing for any audience, as low contrast impacts even those with typical vision under certain conditions (e.g., bright glare).

## Exceptions

-   Purely decorative graphical elements that convey no information (e.g., background patterns with no semantic value) are exempt from contrast requirements.
-   Logotypes or brand names which have specific, mandated color requirements may be exempt, but surrounding content should compensate to maintain overall accessibility.
-   Elements that are inherently part of an image or video where the contrast is not controllable by the author (e.g., a photo of real-world objects) are exempt.

## Trade-offs

-   Strict adherence to contrast ratios might limit aesthetic choices or brand color palettes, requiring designers to find alternative colors or introduce outlines/shadows to achieve compliance.
-   The WCAG 2.1 contrast model has known limitations (as discussed in the original checklist's notes), meaning a technically compliant solution might still be less-than-ideal for some users, or future standards (WCAG 3.0) may propose different metrics.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **The "Squint Test":** If you squint at your chart, do text or chart elements blend into the background or other adjacent elements, making them indistinct?
-   **Automated Tool Warnings:** Contrast checker tools (like WebAIM) report failures for text or non-text elements.
-   **User Reports:** Users complain that text is hard to read or certain parts of the chart are indistinct.
-   **Overlapping Elements:** When elements are close, do they become indistinguishable due to insufficient contrast between them?

## How to Improve

-   **Quick approach:** Use a contrast checker tool (e.g., WebAIM Contrast Checker) to identify failing color combinations and adjust foreground/background colors minimally until they meet WCAG 2.1 AA requirements (4.5:1 for regular text, 3:1 for large text and non-text elements).
-   **Moderate approach:** Redesign the color palette by selecting colors that inherently provide good contrast across the entire visualization. Consider using a limited palette of high-contrast colors for critical elements and applying textures or outlines redundantly for non-text elements to ensure distinction even if color alone might be borderline.
-   **Comprehensive approach:** Integrate accessibility-first color palette generation into your design system, using tools or algorithms that ensure WCAG-compliant contrast ratios by default for all text and graphical elements. Develop guidelines for developers to ensure dynamic states (hover, focus, selection) also maintain sufficient contrast, and consider offering user-adjustable contrast themes.
