## Guidance

Ensure graphical objects and large text have at least a 3:1 contrast ratio against their background, and regular text maintains a minimum 4.5:1 contrast ratio.

## Why

Sufficient contrast is critical for all users to easily distinguish visual elements from their background, reducing eye strain and improving readability. This is especially important for individuals with low vision, color vision deficiencies, or cognitive impairments, as well as for viewing in suboptimal lighting conditions or on lower-quality screens.

### Core Principle

Ensuring adequate visual differentiation between foreground and background elements is fundamental for universal perceivability, allowing users with diverse visual abilities to accurately distinguish and interpret content.

## When it applies

This guideline applies universally to all text (labels, titles, annotations) and meaningful graphical components (chart geometries, icons, interactive controls) within any data visualization, regardless of chart type, data, or medium.

## Exceptions

None, as contrast is a fundamental aspect of perceivability for all visual information. While ongoing research (e.g., WCAG 3.0's APCA) aims for more perceptually accurate contrast models, the WCAG 2.x standards remain the current benchmark for compliance.

## Trade-offs

Achieving high contrast may sometimes restrict color palette choices, potentially impacting aesthetic flexibility or adherence to specific brand guidelines. However, these trade-offs are typically outweighed by the significant accessibility and usability benefits.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **The Squint Test:** If you squint at the visualization, do important text or graphical elements blend into their background or become hard to discern?
-   **Automated Checker Failures:** Automated accessibility tools (like WebAIM Contrast Checker) report contrast ratios below WCAG 2.1 standards for text (4.5:1) or non-text elements (3:1).
-   **User Feedback:** Users report difficulty reading text or distinguishing chart components, especially in bright sunlight or on lower-quality screens.

## How to Improve

-   **Quick approach:** Use a contrast checker tool (e.g., WebAIM Contrast Checker) to test foreground and background color pairs. Adjust colors until they meet the minimum WCAG 2.1 contrast ratios (4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text and graphics).

-   **Moderate approach:** When designing color palettes, prioritize contrast from the outset. Consider using a limited palette of high-contrast colors for critical elements and ensure background elements do not interfere with foreground content.

-   **Comprehensive approach:** Integrate contrast checks into your design system and development pipeline. Utilize accessible color generators or tools that provide real-time contrast feedback during design. For interactive charts, ensure focus and hover states also maintain sufficient contrast.
