## Guidance

Geometries and large text must have >3:1 contrast against background. Regular text must have >4.5:1 contrast against background.

## Why

Sufficient contrast ensures that text, graphical components, and data elements are perceivable and distinguishable for users with varying visual abilities, especially those with low vision. Without adequate contrast, content can become unreadable, leading to frustration, misinterpretation, and exclusion.

### Core Principle

Content must be perceivable and distinguishable from its surroundings to all users.

## When it applies

-   When displaying any text (labels, titles, annotations, legends) in a visualization.
-   When using graphical elements (lines, shapes, icons, data marks, interactive states) to convey information.
-   Crucial for all audiences, particularly those with low vision or certain color perception differences.

## Exceptions

-   Contrast is not required for purely decorative elements that convey no information.
-   Contrast is not required for logotypes or brand names, though good contrast is still recommended for legibility.
-   Components that are inactive or whose appearance is not controlled by the author are exempt.

## Trade-offs

-   **Aesthetic choices:** Achieving sufficient contrast may limit color palette options or clash with branding guidelines, requiring creative solutions.
-   **Design complexity:** Balancing aesthetic design with strict contrast requirements can add complexity to the design and development process.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **Squint Test:** If you squint at the visualization, do text or graphical elements become indistinguishable from their background or adjacent elements?
-   **Faded Appearance:** Text or data marks appear 'faded' or blend into the background, making them hard to discern without effort.
-   **Common Failure:** Low contrast is reported as the most common accessibility failure (88% of audits by Chartability), indicating its widespread prevalence.

## How to Improve

-   **Quick approach:** Use a contrast checker tool (e.g., WebAIM Contrast Checker) to test foreground and background color pairs. Adjust colors until they meet the 4.5:1 ratio for regular text and 3:1 for large text and graphical objects. Prioritize critical text and data elements first.

-   **Moderate approach:** Integrate contrast checks into your design system or development workflow to prevent low-contrast elements from being published. Offer users a high-contrast mode or alternative color palettes that automatically meet accessibility standards.

-   **Comprehensive approach:** Design with contrast in mind from the outset, considering not just color, but also texture, size, and shape as redundant encodings. Stay informed about evolving contrast models (like APCA/WCAG 3.0) which aim for more perceptually accurate calculations, but acknowledge these are not yet standard.
