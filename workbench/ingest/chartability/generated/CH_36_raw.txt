## Guidance

Geometries and large text must have >3:1 contrast against the background, and regular text must have >4.5:1.

## Why

Sufficient contrast is fundamental for readability and perceivability for all users, especially those with low vision or other visual impairments. Without adequate contrast, text and graphical elements become indistinguishable from their background, hindering comprehension and making information inaccessible. Low contrast is frequently identified as the most common accessibility failure in data visualizations.

### Core Principle

Meaningful elements must be easily distinguishable from their surroundings for all users, regardless of visual ability.

## When it applies

- When displaying any text, labels, or graphical elements within a visualization.
- When designing for users with low vision or varying visual acuity.
- When aiming for WCAG compliance (levels AA or AAA).

## Exceptions

- Decorative elements that convey no information may not strictly adhere, but such elements are rare in data visualization where most visual cues are informative.
- Logos or brand names do not need to meet contrast requirements.

## Trade-offs

- Strict adherence to contrast ratios can sometimes limit aesthetic color choices or brand palette flexibility.
- Achieving high contrast with a wide range of colors for complex categorical data can be challenging.

## Signs of Trouble

- Text or chart elements appear faint or blend into the background, making them hard to read or distinguish.
- Running a contrast checker tool (e.g., WebAIM Contrast Checker) reports ratios below WCAG 2.1 AA standards (4.5:1 for regular text, 3:1 for large text and non-text elements).
- Users report difficulty reading labels, legends, or perceiving data points.

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach:** Use a contrast checker tool (like WebAIM Contrast Checker) to identify and adjust problematic foreground/background color pairs until they meet WCAG 2.1 AA standards (4.5:1 for regular text, 3:1 for large text/graphics). Prioritize critical text and interactive elements.
- **Moderate approach:** Redesign your core color palette to inherently include high-contrast options. Consider providing an optional 'high contrast mode' as a user setting to automatically apply a compliant palette.
- **Comprehensive approach:** Integrate automated contrast checking into your visualization development and design system workflows. Educate designers and developers on contrast best practices and the nuances of perceptual contrast (e.g., considering WCAG 3.0's APCA/SPCA efforts), moving beyond simple luminance ratios where appropriate.
