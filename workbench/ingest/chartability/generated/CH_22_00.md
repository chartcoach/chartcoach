## Guidance

Ensure chart geometries and large text have a contrast ratio of at least 3:1 against their background, and regular text has a ratio of at least 4.5:1.

## Why

Low contrast makes text and graphical elements difficult or impossible to read and distinguish for users with low vision, color vision deficiencies, or cognitive impairments. Adhering to contrast standards significantly improves readability and accessibility for a wide range of users. It is also one of the most common accessibility failures, occurring in 88% of audits performed by the Chartability authors.

### Core Principle

All information and user interface components must be presentable to users in ways they can perceive.

## When it applies

-   Whenever text (labels, titles, annotations) or graphical elements (bars, lines, points, backgrounds) are used in a visualization.
-   When content must be readable or distinguishable by users with low vision or certain cognitive disabilities.

## Exceptions

-   **Incidental elements:** Decorative elements that convey no information, inactive user interface components, or elements that are part of a logo or brand name are exempt from contrast requirements.
-   **Meaning conveyed by other means:** If the change in state for an interactive element is communicated through other redundant indicators (e.g., a change in stroke thickness of at least 2px, a dash pattern, or a marker), then contrast color difference for that state change might not be strictly required.

## Trade-offs

-   Achieving high contrast might constrain aesthetic choices or force deviations from brand-specific color palettes.
-   It can be challenging to maintain sufficient contrast across a complex chart with many data points or layers without making the chart feel overly busy.
-   Current WCAG 2.x contrast models have known limitations, and while WCAG 3.0 aims to improve this, it is not yet standard.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **The Squint Test:** If you squint at the visualization, do text or chart elements blend into the background or into each other?
-   **Automated Checker Warnings:** A contrast checking tool (like WebAIM Contrast Checker) reports failures for text or graphical objects when testing their foreground and background colors.
-   **Readability Complaints:** Users report difficulty reading text or distinguishing chart elements, especially on different screens, in varied lighting conditions, or with vision impairments.

## How to Improve

-   **Quick approach:** Use a contrast checker (e.g., WebAIM Contrast Checker) to identify failing color pairs. Adjust the lightness or darkness difference between foreground text/elements and their background until the required 4.5:1 (for regular text) or 3:1 (for large text and graphical objects) ratio is met.

-   **Moderate approach:** Systematically select color palettes that are designed for high contrast and accessibility from the outset. Prioritize distinct luminance values over mere hue differences for critical elements to ensure visual separation regardless of color perception.

-   **Comprehensive approach:** Integrate accessibility directly into your design system and development workflow to ensure contrast is a foundational consideration. This may involve providing users with options to switch to a high-contrast theme or dynamically adjust contrast settings, respecting user preferences from operating systems or browser extensions.
