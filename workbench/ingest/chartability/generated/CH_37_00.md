## Guidance

Ensure that geometries and large text in your visualization have a contrast ratio of at least 3:1 against their background. Regular-sized text must have a minimum contrast ratio of 4.5:1 against its background.

## Why

This guideline is fundamental for perceivability, especially for users with low vision, color vision deficiencies, or those viewing content in challenging environments (e.g., bright light, small screens). Without sufficient contrast, textual information becomes unreadable and graphical elements become indistinguishable, rendering the visualization incomprehensible. It's consistently one of the most common accessibility failures, significantly hindering access to information.

### Core Principle

Users must be able to clearly distinguish visual elements from their background to perceive information accurately and with reduced cognitive effort.

## When it applies

This guidance applies universally whenever any text, graphical objects (like bars, lines, points, icons), or user interface components are displayed against a background in a visualization. This includes axis labels, titles, legends, annotations, and all data-encoding marks.

## Trade-offs

Adhering to contrast requirements may sometimes limit aesthetic color choices. However, ensuring basic perceivability is a non-negotiable accessibility and usability requirement that outweighs purely aesthetic preferences. Creative design solutions can often meet contrast standards without sacrificing visual appeal.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **The Squint Test:** If you squint at your visualization, do any text elements become unreadable or do chart elements blend into the background or each other?
-   **Faint Elements:** Do any text labels, axis lines, grid lines, or chart shapes appear faint, washed out, or difficult to discern from their immediate surroundings?
-   **Automated Audit Failures:** Automated accessibility checkers (like WebAIM Contrast Checker) report contrast ratios below the WCAG 2.1 AA minimums (4.5:1 for regular text, 3:1 for large text and graphics).

## How to Improve

-   **Quick Fix:** For existing visualizations, use a color picker tool to identify the exact foreground and background colors of problematic elements. Use a contrast checker tool (e.g., WebAIM Contrast Checker) to find compliant alternative colors for these elements and implement the change. Prioritize critical text and interactive elements.

-   **Moderate Approach:** When designing new visualizations or performing a significant redesign, choose your color palette carefully from the outset. Utilize color palette generators or design systems that incorporate contrast checking (e.g., WCAG-compliant palettes). Design a default color scheme that ensures all text and graphical elements meet minimum contrast ratios, and provide options for a high-contrast mode if needed.

-   **Comprehensive Approach:** Integrate automated contrast checking into your development workflow and design system. Ensure that all visualization components (charts, labels, legends, tooltips) programmatically generate colors that meet WCAG standards, and that user-configured or dynamic color applications are also validated. Provide users with the ability to adjust contrast or switch to high-contrast themes within the application, overriding default styling if necessary.
