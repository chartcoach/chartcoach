## Guidance

Ensure that geometries and large text maintain a contrast ratio of at least 3:1 against their background, and regular text has a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1.

## Why

Sufficient contrast is fundamental for readability and accessibility. Users, especially those with low vision, cognitive impairments, or in challenging viewing conditions (e.g., bright sunlight, older screens), rely on clear distinctions between foreground and background elements to perceive information accurately. Low contrast is a pervasive accessibility failure that can make visualizations illegible and unusable for a significant portion of the audience.

### Core Principle

Perceivability is paramount: all critical visual information must be distinguishable by users through adequate visual separation from its surroundings.

## When it applies

- For any text, labels, or annotations within a visualization.
- For all meaningful graphical objects, such as bars, lines, points, and map regions, that convey information.
- Whenever visual elements need to be differentiated from their background or adjacent elements.

## Exceptions

None. Legibility through sufficient contrast is a foundational accessibility requirement for all visual content.

## Trade-offs

Achieving high contrast might sometimes limit aesthetic choices or require adjustments to brand color palettes. However, these trade-offs are generally outweighed by the significant improvement in accessibility and usability for a wider audience. The perceived limitations of current WCAG 2.x models are being addressed in WCAG 3.0, aiming for more perceptually accurate contrast evaluation, but current standards remain critical.

## Signs of Trouble

- **The Squint Test:** If you squint at the visualization, do text elements or chart geometries blend into the background or other elements, becoming difficult to distinguish?
- **Visual Fatigue:** Do users report eye strain or difficulty reading text after prolonged viewing?
- **Automated Audit Failures:** Automated accessibility checkers (like WebAIM Contrast Checker) report contrast failures for text or non-text elements.
- **Indistinguishable Elements:** Text or chart elements appear washed out, faded, or too similar in brightness to their surroundings.

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach:** Adjust the lightness or darkness of foreground and background colors to meet the minimum contrast ratios. Use a contrast checker tool (like WebAIM Contrast Checker) to get immediate feedback. Prioritize meeting 4.5:1 for regular text and 3:1 for large text/geometries first.

- **Moderate approach:** Redesign the color palette, ensuring that all foreground/background color pairs, including those used for chart elements and their interactive states, adhere to the WCAG guidelines. Consider using a limited, high-contrast palette, potentially one that has been tested for accessibility.

- **Comprehensive approach:** Integrate contrast checking into the design and development workflow. Establish a design system with pre-approved high-contrast color palettes and clear guidance on their application. Educate designers and developers on the importance of contrast and how to consistently apply accessible color choices. Regularly audit visualizations for contrast issues as part of the quality assurance process.
