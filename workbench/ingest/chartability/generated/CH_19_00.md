## Guidance

Geometries and large text (18pt / 24px regular, or 14pt / 18.66px bold and larger) must have a contrast ratio greater than 3:1 against their background. Regular text (smaller than the above) must have a contrast ratio greater than 4.5:1 against its background.

## Why

Poor contrast makes visual content difficult or impossible to perceive for many users, particularly those with low vision, color vision deficiencies, or cognitive disabilities. It increases the cognitive load for all users by forcing them to strain to discern information. Adhering to contrast guidelines ensures fundamental perceivability and is a cornerstone of inclusive design, being one of the most common accessibility failures observed in real-world audits.

### Core Principle

Ensure perceivable distinction for all visual content. All essential visual information must be easily discernible from its background and surrounding elements to guarantee universal access and comprehension.

## When it applies

- When displaying any text (labels, titles, annotations, legends) in a data visualization.
- When rendering any graphic elements (bars, lines, points, shapes, backgrounds) that convey information or are interactive.
- In any data visualization where visual information is critical for understanding.

## Exceptions

No exceptions to the fundamental need for sufficient contrast are known. While evolving standards (like WCAG 3.0's APCA) may refine how contrast is calculated, the principle of ensuring perceivable distinction remains paramount.

## Trade-offs

- **Aesthetic Constraints:** May limit aesthetic color choices, as some brand colors or preferred palettes might not meet contrast requirements.
- **Design Complexity:** Requires careful consideration of all foreground and background color combinations, which can add complexity to design systems.

## Signs of Trouble

- **The Squint Test:** If you squint at the visualization, do text or graphic elements blend into their background or disappear?
- **Tool Failure:** Using a contrast checker (e.g., WebAIM Contrast Checker) reveals that text or graphic elements fail WCAG AA or AAA compliance.
- **Feedback:** Users report difficulty reading labels, distinguishing chart elements, or complain of eye strain.

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach:** Use a color picker tool to sample the foreground and background colors of problematic elements, then use an online contrast checker (like WebAIM's) to find compliant alternatives by slightly adjusting hue, saturation, or luminosity until the minimum 3:1 or 4.5:1 ratio is met.
- **Moderate approach:** Redesign the visualization's color palette with contrast in mind from the outset. Use a color palette generator that includes contrast checking features to ensure all color combinations (data elements, text, backgrounds) meet accessibility standards. Prioritize a limited set of high-contrast brand colors.
- **Comprehensive approach:** Integrate automated contrast checks into the development workflow and design system. Provide users with options for high-contrast themes or alternative color palettes directly within the application, allowing them to customize the visual presentation to their individual needs. This addresses diverse user preferences beyond minimum compliance.
