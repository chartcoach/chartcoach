## Guidance

Ensure geometries and large text have a contrast ratio of at least 3:1 against their background, and regular text maintains a minimum contrast ratio of 4.5:1.

## Why

Low contrast significantly impairs the readability and distinguishability of content, especially for individuals with low vision, color perception differences, or in suboptimal viewing conditions. It is a fundamental requirement for making visualizations perceivable and is frequently identified as a critical barrier in accessibility audits.

### Core Principle

Content must be easily perceivable by all users, and sufficient contrast is a foundational element for effective visual communication and accessibility.

## When it applies

-   Whenever text, icons, or graphical elements (e.g., chart lines, bars, data points) are displayed.
-   For all types of data visualizations where visual information needs to be clearly distinguished from its background or adjacent elements.
-   For any audience, especially those with visual impairments.

## Exceptions

-   Logos or brand names are not required to meet contrast requirements.
-   Purely decorative elements that convey no information are exempt.
-   Current contrast ratio models (like WCAG 2.x) have recognized limitations (e.g., as noted by Myndex Research's APCA/SPCA work). Future standards (WCAG 3.0) are expected to introduce more perceptually accurate models, which may alter specific numerical requirements. However, adherence to current WCAG 2.x standards remains critical for compliance.

## Trade-offs

Achieving high contrast can sometimes limit creative color choices or brand aesthetic preferences. Designers may need to prioritize readability and accessibility over purely subjective visual appeal.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **Signal A:** Text or graphical elements appear faded, blurry, or blend seamlessly into their background, making them difficult to discern.
-   **Signal B:** Users express difficulty reading labels, distinguishing chart elements, or interacting with controls.
-   **Signal C:** Automated contrast checker tools (e.g., WebAIM Contrast Checker) report contrast ratios below 4.5:1 for regular text or 3:1 for large text and graphical objects.
-   **Signal D:** When viewed by individuals with low vision or using simulated low vision settings, critical information becomes unreadable.

## How to Improve

-   **Quick approach:** Use a contrast checker tool to identify failing foreground/background color combinations and adjust them minimally to meet WCAG 2.1 AA requirements. Ensure text colors are at least 4.5:1 and non-text elements (like chart geometries) are 3:1 against their background.
-   **Moderate approach:** Develop and enforce an accessible color palette as part of your design system, ensuring all predefined color combinations for text, UI elements, and data marks meet contrast standards. Use tools that simulate various forms of color vision deficiency to cross-verify.
-   **Comprehensive approach:** Integrate automated contrast testing into your development workflow. Design color systems that are perceptually uniform and validate them rigorously across various contexts and user settings. Educate designers and developers on the importance and methods of achieving sufficient contrast, considering not only static states but also hover, focus, and selected states.
