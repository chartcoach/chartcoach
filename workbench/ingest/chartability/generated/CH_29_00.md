## Guidance

Ensure all text and visual elements have sufficient contrast against their background. Specifically, geometries and large text must have a contrast ratio greater than 3:1 against their background, while regular text must maintain a contrast ratio greater than 4.5:1.

## Why

High contrast makes text and visual elements readable and distinguishable, especially for users with low vision or certain color vision deficiencies. This reduces cognitive strain and improves comprehension for a wider audience, ensuring that critical information is accessible. Low contrast is a leading cause of accessibility failures in data visualizations.

### Core Principle

The ability to differentiate between foreground and background elements based on their luminance difference is fundamental to visual perception and information access. Without sufficient contrast, elements merge, making content unperceivable.

## When it applies

- When displaying any text (labels, titles, annotations, legends, etc.) within a visualization.
- When using graphical objects or chart elements (lines, bars, points, areas) that convey meaningful information.
- Across all chart types, data types, and presentation mediums (static, interactive, print, screen).

## Exceptions

- Purely decorative elements that convey no information and are not critical for understanding the visualization may not require strict contrast ratios.
- Logos or brand marks may be exempt if they do not function as UI components and their context provides a clear understanding of their meaning.
- Incidental elements in complex images or purely stylistic flourishes that are not essential to communication.

## Trade-offs

Strict adherence to contrast guidelines can sometimes limit aesthetic choices or brand color palettes, requiring creative solutions to maintain both visual appeal and accessibility. Finding a balance often involves iterating on color selections or implementing accessible themes.

## Signs of Trouble

- **The Squint Test:** If you squint at the visualization, do text or important graphic elements disappear or become indistinguishable from their background?
- **Subtle Hues:** Are similar light or dark colors used for adjacent elements or text on a background of similar lightness, making them hard to tell apart?
- **Failing Contrast Checker:** Does a contrast checker (e.g., WebAIM Contrast Checker) report ratios below the required minimums for text (4.5:1) or non-text elements (3:1)?
- **Eye Strain:** Do users report difficulty reading text or distinguishing chart elements, or do they visibly strain to interpret the visualization?

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach:** Use a contrast checker tool (such as the WebAIM Contrast Checker) to identify elements that fail to meet the 4.5:1 (regular text) or 3:1 (large text/geometries) minimum contrast ratios. Adjust the luminance of foreground or background colors incrementally until all critical elements pass. Prioritize clarity and readability over subtle aesthetic nuances.
- **Moderate approach:** Implement a design system that includes a predefined set of WCAG-compliant color palettes for text, interactive states, and data encoding. Design primary, secondary, and accent colors with tested contrast-safe combinations. Consider offering a toggle for a high-contrast mode or dark theme that automatically applies accessible palettes.
- **Comprehensive approach:** Integrate automated accessibility testing tools (e.g., Axe-core, Lighthouse) into your development and CI/CD workflows to catch contrast issues early and consistently. Develop user preferences that allow individuals to adjust contrast levels, text sizes, or switch to alternative accessible themes, providing robust flexibility while ensuring core accessibility requirements are met by default.
