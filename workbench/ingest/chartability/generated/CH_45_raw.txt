## Guidance

Ensure regular text has a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 and graphical components (geometries, large text) maintain a minimum 3:1 contrast against their background.

## Why

Insufficient contrast makes text and visual elements difficult or impossible to distinguish for users with low vision, color vision deficiencies, or even in challenging viewing conditions. Adhering to contrast standards reduces cognitive load and ensures content is perceivable to a wider audience, upholding ethical and legal accessibility requirements.

### Core Principle

Meaningful visual information must be distinguishable from its background and surrounding elements to be perceivable by all users.

## When it applies

-   When displaying any text content within a visualization (e.g., titles, labels, annotations).
-   When using graphical elements (e.g., shapes, lines, data points, icons) that convey meaning or are interactive.
-   In all interactive and static visualizations across any medium, including print and screen.

## Exceptions

None known to the necessity of contrast itself. While the WCAG 2.x contrast model has recognized limitations (as noted in the `notes` section of the original guideline, and addressed in ongoing WCAG 3.0 work), the fundamental requirement for sufficient contrast remains.

## Trade-offs

Achieving sufficient contrast might sometimes limit purely aesthetic color choices, as accessibility standards prioritize perceivability over design preferences that could hinder access. The current WCAG 2.x model, based on relative luminance, may not always perfectly reflect human perception, leading to situations where a technically compliant color pair might still feel suboptimal, and vice-versa.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **The Squint Test:** If you squint at the visualization, can you still clearly read all text and distinguish all meaningful graphical elements from their backgrounds and adjacent elements?
-   **Automated Check Failure:** An accessibility checker (like WebAIM Contrast Checker) reports failures for text or non-text contrast ratios below 4.5:1 or 3:1, respectively.
-   **Subtle Text/Element Blending:** Text or graphic edges appear to subtly blend into the background or adjacent elements, making them hard to discern, especially in non-ideal viewing conditions or for users with low vision.

## How to Improve

-   **Quick approach:** Use a contrast checker tool (e.g., WebAIM Contrast Checker) to identify failing color combinations. Adjust foreground or background colors by increasing lightness/darkness differences to meet the minimum 4.5:1 for regular text and 3:1 for large text/graphical elements. Prioritize critical information and interaction elements first.
-   **Moderate approach:** Implement a design token system for colors that includes pre-checked contrast-compliant palettes for all standard UI elements, text, and data encodings. Consider offering a high-contrast mode as an alternative display option for users who require it.
-   **Comprehensive approach:** Integrate contrast checks into your visualization development pipeline (e.g., as part of unit tests or linting). Research and apply more perceptually uniform color spaces (e.g., CIELAB, Lch) during color selection, as these can offer more predictable control over perceived contrast. Stay informed about evolving standards like WCAG 3.0 (APCA/SPCA) for advanced and more perceptually accurate contrast evaluation.
