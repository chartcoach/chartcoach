## Guidance

Geometries and large text must have at least a 3:1 contrast ratio against their background, and regular text must have at least a 4.5:1 contrast ratio.

## Why

Sufficient contrast is crucial for readability and discriminability of visual information. For users with low vision, color vision deficiencies, or those in challenging viewing environments, low contrast significantly increases the cognitive effort required to perceive and understand content, often making it impossible. Adhering to contrast standards ensures basic perceivability and reduces cognitive load for a broad audience.

### Core Principle

Information should be perceivable to all users, regardless of visual ability, by ensuring adequate distinction between foreground and background elements.

## When it applies

- When displaying any text, including titles, labels, annotations, and legends.
- When displaying graphical elements such as bars, lines, points, shapes, or boundaries that convey meaning.
- When any two adjacent visual elements need to be differentiated from each other, or from their background.

## Exceptions

None known. Minimum contrast ratios are a foundational accessibility requirement with no generally accepted exceptions for visual content where information is conveyed.

## Trade-offs

Achieving high contrast may sometimes limit aesthetic choices or require adjustments to existing brand color palettes. However, the benefits of improved accessibility and usability for a wider audience typically outweigh these aesthetic constraints. Compromising on contrast risks excluding a significant portion of potential users.

## Signs of Trouble

- **The Squint Test:** If you squint at the visualization, do any text or graphical elements become difficult to distinguish from their background or from adjacent elements?
- **Tool Failure:** A contrast checker tool reports a failure to meet WCAG AA or AAA minimum contrast ratios for text or graphical elements.
- **Common Failure:** Auditors frequently identify low contrast as one of the most prevalent accessibility issues in visualizations.
- **Eyestrain/Fatigue:** Users report difficulty reading or experience eye strain, especially after prolonged viewing.

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach:** Use a contrast checker tool (e.g., WebAIM Contrast Checker) to identify all failing text and graphical elements. Make minimal adjustments to their foreground or background colors until they meet the required contrast ratios (4.5:1 for regular text, 3:1 for large text/geometries). Prioritize meeting AA standards first, then AAA for enhanced accessibility.

- **Moderate approach:** Redesign the color palette for your visualization, prioritizing accessibility from the outset. Use contrast-checking tools as part of your design process to proactively select color combinations that meet or exceed WCAG standards. Consider offering users high-contrast themes as an alternative viewing option.

- **Comprehensive approach:** Integrate contrast compliance into your design system and automated testing pipelines. Educate designers and developers on accessible color theory and best practices. Continuously monitor emerging contrast evaluation methods (like APCA/SPCA) to future-proof your designs, while ensuring current adherence to established standards.
