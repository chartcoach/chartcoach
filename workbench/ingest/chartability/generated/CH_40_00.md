## Guidance

Ensure geometries and large text (18pt / 24px regular, or 14pt / 18.66px bold and larger) have a contrast ratio of at least 3:1 against their background. Regular text must have a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 against its background.

## Why

Sufficient contrast is fundamental for making visual content perceivable and reducing cognitive effort for all users, particularly those with low vision, color vision deficiencies, or age-related vision changes. Without adequate contrast, text and graphical elements become difficult or impossible to distinguish from their background, hindering comprehension and usability.

### Core Principle

Sufficient contrast is fundamental for making visual content perceivable and reducing cognitive effort for all users, particularly those with low vision.

## When it applies

- For all text and graphical elements within a visualization, including chart geometries (e.g., bars, lines, points, pie slices), labels, annotations, legends, and interactive states.
- When designing color palettes for any data visualization.
- Across all mediums (print, screen, static, interactive) and for all audiences.

## Exceptions

None known. Adhering to contrast requirements is a fundamental aspect of accessible design. While evolving standards like WCAG 3.0 are exploring new perceptual contrast models (e.g., APCA), the current WCAG 2.1 guidelines remain the widely adopted benchmark.

## Trade-offs

Achieving high contrast across a complex visualization, especially when using a wide color palette or nuanced data encodings, can be challenging and may sometimes limit purely aesthetic choices. It may require careful selection of colors and, at times, a reduction in the total number of distinct colors used.

## Signs of Trouble

- **The Squint Test:** If you squint at your chart, do different text labels or graphical elements blend into their background or into adjacent elements?
- **Contrast Checker Failure:** A digital contrast checker tool (like WebAIM's) reports contrast ratios below 3:1 for large text/graphics or below 4.5:1 for regular text.
- **User Feedback:** Users report difficulty reading text, distinguishing data points, or perceiving the boundaries of graphical shapes, leading to eye strain or confusion.
- **Visual Clutter with Interaction:** When interactive elements change state (e.g., on hover/focus), the new state has insufficient contrast with the previous state or surrounding elements.

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach:**
  *   Use a contrast checker tool (e.g., WebAIM Contrast Checker) to identify specific low-contrast elements.
  *   Adjust the foreground or background colors of problematic elements minimally until they meet the 3:1 or 4.5:1 WCAG 2.1 requirements. Prioritize essential text and interactive elements.
  *   Increase font size for marginal text to meet "large text" criteria if minor readability issues persist.

- **Moderate approach:**
  *   Redesign the visualization's color palette to systematically use high-contrast combinations. Consider a base palette for data marks and a separate, high-contrast palette for text and interactive elements.
  *   For categorical data, use textures, patterns, or distinct shapes in addition to color. This provides redundancy for color perception and enhances contrast for all users.
  *   Integrate contrast checking directly into your design and development workflow (e.g., using browser developer tools, design software plugins).

- **Comprehensive approach:**
  *   Implement user-selectable high-contrast themes or modes within your application. This allows users to customize the visualization's appearance to best suit their needs.
  *   Explore more advanced contrast evaluation models like APCA (Accessible Perceptual Contrast Algorithm), which aim for more perceptually uniform contrast measurements, understanding that these are not yet part of WCAG 2.1.
  *   Build a design system that enforces contrast standards for all components and provides guidance on accessible color choices to designers and developers.
