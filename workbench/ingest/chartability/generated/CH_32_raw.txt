## Guidance

Ensure all text, graphical objects, and user interface components have sufficient color contrast against their background. Regular text must maintain a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1, while large text (18pt/24px or 14pt/18.66px bold) and graphical objects (e.g., chart geometries, icons, interactive states) require a minimum contrast ratio of 3:1.

## Why

Sufficient contrast is fundamental for readability and perceivability, especially for users with low vision, color vision deficiencies, or older adults. Low contrast significantly increases cognitive load and can make it impossible for some users to discern important information, leading to misinterpretation or complete inaccessibility. It is often the most common accessibility failure in data visualizations.

### Core Principle

Maintain distinct visual separation between foreground and background elements to ensure information is perceivable and readable by all users, minimizing the effort required to distinguish visual cues.

## When it applies

- When any text is used (titles, labels, annotations, axes, legends, tooltips).
- When graphical elements (e.g., bars, lines, points, areas, icons, interactive states) convey information.
- For all visual interfaces where elements need to be distinguishable from their surroundings or from each other.
- Applies universally across all chart types, data types, audiences, and display mediums.

## Exceptions

- **Purely Decorative**: Elements that are purely decorative, convey no information, and are not interactive (e.g., a background pattern) are exempt.
- **Inactive UI Components**: Components in a disabled state that are not interactive are exempt from contrast requirements, though their disabled state should be perceivable.
- **Logotypes**: Text that is part of a logo or brand name.
- **Incidental**: Parts of an image that contain graphical objects but are not critical for understanding the content, such as a casual photograph.

## Trade-offs

- **Aesthetics**: Adhering to strict contrast ratios can sometimes limit color choices, potentially impacting aesthetic preferences or brand guidelines.
- **Color Palettes**: It may be challenging to find color palettes that satisfy contrast requirements across all elements, especially for complex visualizations with many categories or subtle data variations.
- **Expressiveness**: Relying solely on color for contrast might restrict the use of color for other data encodings.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **"Squint Test" Failure**: If, when you squint at the visualization, text or graphical elements become indistinguishable from their background or adjacent elements.
-   **Automated Tool Flag**: Contrast checkers (like WebAIM's) report failures for text or non-text elements when audited.
-   **User Feedback**: Users report difficulty reading text, distinguishing chart elements, or identifying interactive states.
-   **Common Failure**: Low contrast is statistically the most common accessibility failure encountered in audits of data visualizations.

## How to Improve

-   **Quick approach**:
    -   **Adjust Colors**: Use a contrast checker (e.g., WebAIM Contrast Checker) to quickly select foreground and background colors that meet the 4.5:1 ratio for regular text and 3:1 for large text and graphical objects. Prioritize text and critical data elements.
    -   **Increase Text Size**: For text that struggles to meet 4.5:1, consider increasing its size to qualify as "large text" (18pt/24px or 14pt/18.66px bold), which only requires a 3:1 ratio.
-   **Moderate approach**:
    -   **Redesign Color Palettes**: Systematically review and adjust your entire visualization color palette to ensure all foreground/background combinations meet WCAG contrast requirements. Consider using predefined accessible palettes (e.g., from tools like Viz Palette or Chroma.js) or carefully construct a custom palette with contrast in mind.
    -   **Add Borders/Outlines**: For non-text graphical objects (like bars, data points) with low inherent contrast, add a thin, highly contrasting border or outline to improve their perceivability without drastically altering their primary fill color.
-   **Comprehensive approach**:
    -   **Integrate Contrast Checking into Workflow**: Implement automated contrast checks within your design and development pipeline (e.g., using Axe DevTools) to catch issues early and ensure continuous compliance.
    -   **Offer User-Controlled Themes**: Provide options for users to switch to high-contrast themes or customize foreground/background colors and text treatments, ensuring maximum flexibility and adherence to individual preferences.
    -   **Re-evaluate Visual Hierarchy**: Use contrast strategically to emphasize important information and de-emphasize secondary elements. A well-designed contrast hierarchy guides the eye and reduces cognitive load, improving overall clarity and accessibility.
