## Guidance

Ensure that text and graphical elements have sufficient contrast against their background. Specifically, geometries and large text (18pt or 14pt bold and larger) must maintain a contrast ratio of at least 3:1, while regular-sized text should have a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1.

## Why

Sufficient contrast is fundamental for the perceivability of all visual content. Users must be able to easily identify elements using their sight. Low contrast makes text difficult to read and graphical elements hard to distinguish, significantly increasing cognitive load and posing a major barrier for individuals with low vision, color vision deficiencies, or cognitive disabilities. As the most common accessibility failure, addressing contrast directly impacts a broad user base.

### Core Principle

Legibility and discriminability of visual information rely on sufficient luminance contrast between foreground and background elements.

## When it applies

Always, for all visual elements in any visualization, especially text, data marks, interactive components, and any other meaningful graphical information.

## Exceptions

None known. Adhering to minimum contrast ratios is a fundamental accessibility requirement for all visual content.

## Trade-offs

Potentially limits aesthetic color choices or strict adherence to brand color palettes if they do not meet required contrast ratios. However, the accessibility and usability benefits typically outweigh these aesthetic or branding compromises.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **Elements blend:** Visual elements, including text, data points, or chart geometries, appear to blend into their background or into adjacent elements, making them hard to distinguish.
-   **Text is difficult to read:** Users report difficulty or strain when reading labels, annotations, or other textual information.
-   **Automated failures:** Automated accessibility checkers (such as WebAIM Contrast Checker) report contrast failures for text or graphical components.
-   **The Squint Test:** If you squint at the visualization, important details or text disappear or become indistinguishable.

## How to Improve

-   **Quick approach:** Use a contrast checker tool to identify specific failing foreground/background color combinations. Adjust the luminance of one or both colors to meet the minimum required contrast ratios (4.5:1 for regular text, 3:1 for large text and graphical objects). Prioritize fixing text elements first, then essential graphical components.
-   **Moderate approach:** Redesign the visualization's color palette to systematically ensure all potential foreground/background combinations (including states like hover/focus) meet WCAG contrast guidelines. Consider developing or adopting a pre-tested accessible color palette. For interactive visualizations, offer an optional high-contrast mode.
-   **Comprehensive approach:** Integrate contrast validation into your design system, component library, and development pipelines. Use accessible color libraries or generate palettes programmatically that guarantee sufficient contrast. Conduct regular accessibility audits with contrast as a key criterion and educate all designers and developers on contrast principles and testing methodologies.
