## Guidance

Ensure text has a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 against its background, and large text (18pt/24px normal, or 14pt/18.66px bold) or graphical objects (such as chart geometries) have a minimum contrast ratio of 3:1.

## Why

Low contrast is a primary barrier to perceiving information, particularly for individuals with low vision, color vision deficiencies, or older users. Adhering to contrast guidelines ensures that essential content is distinguishable, reducing cognitive load and making visualizations accessible to a wider audience.

### Core Principle

Perceivable content must be easily identifiable through sensory input.

## When it applies

-   To all text content within a visualization, including titles, labels, annotations, and legends.
-   To all graphical elements that convey meaning, such as lines, bars, points, or area fills, where their distinction from adjacent colors or background is critical to understanding.
-   When designing for any audience, as good contrast benefits everyone.

## Exceptions

-   Logos or brand names are not required to meet contrast requirements.
-   Text that is part of an inactive user interface component.
-   Text or graphical elements that are purely decorative and convey no information.
-   A particular presentation of text or images of text is essential to the information being conveyed.

## Trade-offs

-   May limit aesthetic color choices, especially when trying to incorporate a wide range of distinct colors or specific brand palettes.
-   Can make the design process more complex, requiring careful selection and testing of color combinations.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **The Squint Test:** If you squint at the visualization, do text labels or data elements blend into the background or other adjacent elements?
-   **Faint Outlines:** Are the borders of chart elements barely visible against their fill or background?
-   **Hard-to-Read Text:** Do any text elements (titles, labels, legends) require extra effort to read, especially on certain backgrounds?
-   **Automated Audit Failures:** Does an accessibility checker (like WebAIM Contrast Checker) report contrast failures for text or graphical elements?

## How to Improve

-   **Quick approach:** Use a contrast checker tool (e.g., WebAIM Contrast Checker) to identify failing color combinations for text and graphical elements. Adjust the luminosity of foreground or background colors (making foreground darker or background lighter, or vice-versa) until minimum contrast ratios are met. Prioritize critical text (4.5:1) and main data elements (3:1).

-   **Moderate approach:** Redesign the color palette of the visualization, focusing on a palette that inherently includes colors with sufficient contrast. Consider using high-contrast color schemes (e.g., a dark theme with light text/elements, or vice versa) or colorblind-safe palettes that are also high-contrast. Ensure interactive states (hover, focus) also maintain contrast requirements.

-   **Comprehensive approach:** Integrate contrast checks into the visualization development workflow and design system. Establish clear contrast guidelines for all visualization components (text, data marks, UI elements) and provide designers and developers with tools and pre-approved palettes that adhere to these standards. Consider offering user-selectable high-contrast modes.
