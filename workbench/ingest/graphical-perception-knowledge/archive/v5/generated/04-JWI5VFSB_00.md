## Guidance

Use donut charts and pie charts interchangeably for showing part-to-whole relationships. Donut charts are no less accurate for reading proportions than traditional pie charts.

## Why

Experiments show that removing the center of a pie chart—the vertex where angles meet—does not harm a viewer's ability to accurately judge proportions. This suggests people rely more on the arc length of the outer edge and the area of the slice, both of which are preserved in a donut chart. The common assumption that the central angle is the most critical perceptual cue is likely incorrect.

### Core Principle

The effectiveness of a part-to-whole chart like a pie or donut relies more on area and arc length than on the central angle.

## When it applies

- When choosing between a pie chart and a donut chart for showing part-to-whole relationships.
- When you want to use the center space of a pie chart for additional information (like a total value or an icon) and are concerned about impacting readability.

## Exceptions

- If the donut is so thin that it becomes just an arc (e.g., inner radius >95% of outer radius), accuracy may slightly decrease compared to a standard pie or a donut with a thicker ring.

## Trade-offs

- While perceptually equivalent for judging proportions, a pie chart visually emphasizes the concept of a "whole" more strongly due to its filled center. A donut chart de-emphasizes the center and can be used to reclaim that space for contextual information.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Unnecessary Debate:** Your team spends significant time debating whether a pie or donut chart is "more correct," assuming a large perceptual difference exists.
- **Center Avoidance:** You avoid using the valuable central space in a donut chart for a title or summary value out of fear that it will "distract" from the angles, which are not the primary cue anyway.

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach: Swap It.** If you have a pie chart, feel free to convert it to a donut chart to reclaim the central space for a title, summary value, or icon. You will not lose accuracy in doing so.
- **Moderate approach: Optimize the Donut.** Use a donut chart with a moderate hole size (e.g., an inner radius between 40-60% of the outer radius). This provides ample space in the center for context while maintaining clearly visible segments.
