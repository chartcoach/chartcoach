## Guidance

To increase user engagement and appeal, prefer Dorling or contiguous cartograms, as they are generally better liked by viewers.

## Why

Beyond pure task performance, user experience and reception are critical for effective communication. The source study included a subjective attitude survey which found a clear preference for Dorling and contiguous cartograms. Participants rated them higher on scales of helpfulness, organization, elegance, and clarity. When asked to choose a cartogram type to continue working with, a vast majority selected Dorling or contiguous, indicating a strong preference developed through use.

### Core Principle

A visualization that is perceived as engaging, elegant, and usable is more likely to be explored and trusted by the audience.

## When it applies

-   When audience reception, aesthetic appeal, and engagement are important goals, for instance in journalism, public-facing dashboards, and presentations.
-   When choosing between two cartogram types that have similar analytical performance for your specific task.

## Exceptions

-   If the primary task demands the unique strengths of a non-contiguous cartogram (perfect shape preservation) or a rectangular cartogram (strict schematic topology), then analytical performance should take priority over subjective preference.

## Trade-offs

-   Prioritizing user preference may mean sacrificing performance on certain tasks. Dorling cartograms, while well-liked, perform poorly on location and adjacency tasks. Contiguous cartograms, also well-liked, distort shapes.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **Negative Feedback:** Users describe your rectangular or non-contiguous cartogram as "confusing," "boring," "ugly," or "difficult to use."
-   **Low Engagement:** You observe that users do not spend much time with the visualization or do not explore it.

## How to Improve

-   **Consider a Switch:** If your primary task is comparison or summarization, consider switching from a rectangular or non-contiguous cartogram to a Dorling or contiguous one to improve user reception.
-   **Mitigate Weaknesses:** If you choose a subjectively preferred type like a Dorling cartogram, be sure to mitigate its known analytical weaknesses. For example, add labels or interaction to help with location and adjacency tasks, balancing its aesthetic appeal with improved usability.
