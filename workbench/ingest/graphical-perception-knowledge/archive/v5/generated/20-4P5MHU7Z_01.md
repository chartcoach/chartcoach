## Guidance

To help your audience make accurate comparisons between bars, place the bars they need to compare directly next to each other.

## Why

The visual distance between bars directly impacts comparison accuracy. The further apart two bars are, the more difficult and error-prone the comparison becomes. This is because the human visual system struggles to project the height of one bar across a wide gap to compare it with another. This "Separation Effect" is particularly strong when comparing shorter bars.

### Core Principle

Make the most important comparisons the easiest to perform visually. Minimize the perceptual work required by reducing distance and removing obstacles.

## When it applies

- When designing a bar chart where a primary goal is for the audience to compare the magnitudes of specific categories.
- This is especially critical when the values being compared are small relative to the axis scale, as the comparison error increases more dramatically with distance for shorter bars.

## Exceptions

- If the bars have a natural and meaningful order (e.g., a time series, or alphabetical for easy lookup) that takes precedence over a specific comparison task, keeping that order may be more important. In this case, consider adding interactive features or annotations to aid comparison.

## Trade-offs

- Re-sorting the chart to group bars for comparison might break a different, meaningful order (like alphabetical or geographical). You trade easy lookup or conventional ordering for easy comparison.

## Signs of Trouble

- **The Eye-Dart:** Viewers must rapidly move their eyes back and forth between two distant bars to judge which is taller.
- **The Finger-Ruler:** Viewers feel the need to use their finger or a physical object to bridge the gap between two bars to compare their heights.
- **Arbitrary Sort Order:** The bars are sorted alphabetically or by some other arbitrary dimension, forcing important comparison pairs to be far apart.

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach: Add Reference Lines.** Interactively or statically, add a subtle reference line from the top of one bar that extends across the chart. This provides a visual guide to aid the comparison.

- **Moderate approach: Re-sort or Group the Bars.** Re-sort the chart to bring the key comparison bars closer together. For example, sort by value. If a few specific comparisons are key, create a "grouped bar chart" where those categories are clustered together.

- **Comprehensive approach: Use a Dumbbell Plot.** If the primary task is to show the difference or gap between pairs of values (e.g., '2023 vs 2024' for multiple categories), switch to a dumbbell plot. This chart type is explicitly designed to show the relationship between two points, making the comparison the central visual element.
