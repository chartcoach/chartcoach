## Guidance

Consciously and deliberately choose the y-axis range (including its starting point) to produce a perceived effect size that matches the data's real-world importance. Do not rely on software defaults.

## Why

The y-axis is not a neutral frame; it is a powerful rhetorical device. By zooming in (truncating the axis) or zooming out (extending the axis far beyond the data), you directly control the perceived magnitude and importance of the patterns shown. There is no single "correct" or "unbiased" axis for all data. A 2° change in your office thermostat is trivial; a 2° change in global temperature is catastrophic. The axis should reflect this contextual meaning, not a rigid, domain-agnostic rule.

### Core Principle

Visualization design is an act of communication and persuasion, not just neutral presentation. The designer is responsible for the message the viewer perceives.

## When it applies

-   Any time you are creating a chart with a quantitative axis (e.g., bar, line, area, scatter plot).
-   When deciding whether to follow the "start at zero" rule or to intentionally break it.

## Exceptions

-   When using automated chart-generating software where you have no control over the axis ranges. In this case, be critical of the output and prepared to rebuild the chart if the default axis is misleading.

## Trade-offs

-   **Following Convention vs. Telling the Story:** Adhering strictly to a "start-at-zero" rule is safe but may fail to communicate a subtle but important finding. Intentionally truncating the axis tells a stronger story but requires careful judgment and justification, and risks accusations of manipulation if done carelessly.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **Default-Axis-Itis:** The axis range has "ugly" numbers (e.g., starts at 13.7 and ends at 89.2) because it was automatically generated by software. No conscious choice was made.
-   **Context Mismatch:** The visual effect is dramatic, but the real-world effect is trivial (or vice-versa). For example, a chart showing a company's 0.1% market share gain looks like a massive spike.
-   **Lack of Justification:** You cannot articulate a clear reason why the current axis range was chosen over any other possible range.

## How to Improve

-   **Minimal: Question the Default.** Before publishing, stop and ask: "Why is the axis set this way? What would happen if I doubled the range? What if I halved it?" Force yourself to make an active choice.

-   **Moderate: Establish a Meaningful Baseline.** The axis doesn't always have to start at zero, but it should start at a meaningful point of comparison. This could be the prior year's average, a target goal, or a clinically significant threshold. The baseline provides context for the values being shown.

-   **Comprehensive: Perform a "Sensitivity Analysis".** Create multiple versions of your chart with different axis ranges (e.g., zero-based, default-range, and a manually truncated "story" range). Compare them side-by-side. This will make you keenly aware of how much rhetorical power the axis holds and help you choose a range that is both effective and defensible. Document your final choice and the reason for it.
