## Guidance

When the task is to compare the overall similarity or correlation between two bar charts, arrange them in a mirrored, back-to-back layout rather than a standard side-by-side layout.

## Why

The human visual system has a dedicated and highly efficient mechanism for detecting symmetry. By arranging two charts (e.g., bar charts) in a mirrored fashion, their combined form creates a single, larger shape. The degree of symmetry in this combined shape serves as a powerful perceptual proxy for the degree of correlation between the two datasets. A highly correlated pair creates a highly symmetric shape, which is easier to perceive than mentally comparing two separate patterns.

### Core Principle

Leverage the brain's built-in perceptual strengths. Instead of forcing viewers to perform a difficult cognitive task (pattern matching), transform the data into a format that a highly-tuned perceptual system (symmetry detection) can solve automatically.

## When it applies

- The primary task is to judge **overall correlation or similarity** between two datasets.
- You are comparing **exactly two datasets**.
- The chart type lends itself to mirroring, such as **bar charts**.

## Exceptions

- **Comparing more than two datasets.** A mirrored layout is fundamentally a binary comparison and does not scale to three or more series.
- **Task is to find a specific value or the largest change.** Other layouts, like animation or overlaid charts, are better for these tasks.
- **Audience unfamiliarity.** The layout is unconventional and may require a moment of orientation for viewers accustomed to standard small multiples.

## Trade-offs

- **Limited Scalability:** This technique is inherently limited to comparing two series.
- **Unconventional:** May be less immediately intuitive to a general audience without a brief period of adaptation compared to standard small multiples.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Incorrect Correlation Judgments:** Viewers struggle to determine which of several pairs of charts is more "similar" or "consistent."
- **Point-by-Point Comparison:** Viewers resort to slowly comparing individual bars one by one, rather than perceiving the overall pattern.
- **Animation is Ineffective:** If you're using animation for a correlation task, viewers will struggle because motion highlights individual deltas, not the global pattern.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Use an Adjacent Layout.** If a mirrored layout is too complex to implement, a standard side-by-side (adjacent) small multiple layout is better than a stacked layout. Ensure the charts share a common baseline and y-axis.
- **Comprehensive Approach: Implement a Mirrored Layout.**
  - For two horizontal bar charts, place them back-to-back, sharing a central y-axis. One chart's bars extend to the left, and the other's extend to the right.
  - The resulting "butterfly" or "pyramid" shape's symmetry will reveal the correlation.
