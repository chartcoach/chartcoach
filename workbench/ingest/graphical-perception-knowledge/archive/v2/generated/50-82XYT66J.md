## Guidance

To help your audience compare aggregate properties of two data series—such as their average value or overall range—stack the charts vertically rather than placing them side-by-side or overlaying them.

## Why

A vertically stacked layout allows the eye to easily scan down and compare corresponding bars and the overall "shape" of each group. This alignment supports our ability to perceive the "center of mass" or overall extent of each set of values. Research shows this arrangement leads to more precise judgments of group-level statistics like mean and range.

In contrast, overlaying the charts forces our visual system to work much harder to separate the two groups, making it difficult to assess each one as a whole. Side-by-side layouts are better than overlays but still require more effort than a clean vertical alignment.

## When it applies

- When the primary task is to compare **aggregate properties** (e.g., "Which group has the higher average?" or "Which data set has a wider spread?").
- When using small multiples of bar charts (or similar charts) to compare two distinct groups or categories.
- When you want to encourage a "big picture" comparison between entire sets of data, not just individual points.

## Exceptions

- If the primary task is to compare **individual corresponding items** (e.g., "For which month was the difference between Group A and B the largest?"), superimposing (overlaying) or animating the charts can be more effective.
- When vertical screen space is extremely limited and a side-by-side layout is the only practical option.

## Trade-offs

- A stacked layout consumes more vertical screen space than an adjacent or superimposed layout.
- While optimal for set-level (global) comparison, this layout is less effective for identifying specific item-level (local) differences, which are often clearer in a superimposed view.

## Evaluate

- [ ] Two charts meant for group-level comparison are overlaid (superimposed) on the same axes.
- [ ] Two charts are placed far apart or side-by-side when a vertical stack is possible and the task is aggregate comparison.

## Repair

1.  If the charts are superimposed, separate them into two distinct charts.
2.  Arrange the two charts one above the other, ensuring their common axes are perfectly aligned for easy scanning.
3.  If vertical space is a major constraint, a side-by-side (adjacent) layout is a better fallback than superimposing them for this specific task.
