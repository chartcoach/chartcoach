## Guidance

When encoding two different categories with shape in a single scatterplot, use one "closed" shape (like a circle `●` or square `■`) and one "open" shape (like a plus `+` or cross `x`).

## Why

Our brains automatically process "open" and "closed" shapes as distinct perceptual categories. When you use two shapes from the *same* category (e.g., a circle and a square, both closed), they create more visual interference for each other. This makes it slower and more error-prone for a viewer to tell them apart, especially in a dense or cluttered chart. By picking one shape from each category, you reduce this interference and make the groups easier to distinguish.

## When it applies

- When using shape to encode two or more categories in a single chart, most commonly a scatterplot.
- This is especially helpful in dense or cluttered charts where many marks are close together.
- The benefit is most pronounced for tasks that require judging numerosity ("Which group has more points?") or identifying trends ("Which group has a stronger correlation?").

## Exceptions

- **More than 2-3 categories:** Finding enough distinct and visually pleasing open/closed shapes can become difficult and create a noisy chart. Consider using color as the primary encoding instead.
- **Iconic shapes:** If your shapes have inherent meaning (e.g., using a car icon for data about cars), that meaning is more important than the open/closed distinction.
- **Very sparse plots:** If all points are far apart, discrimination is already easy, and the benefit of this guideline is minimal.

## Trade-offs

- You might sacrifice a specific aesthetic if you have a stylistic preference for using only one type of shape (e.g., all geometric, closed shapes). This trades a bit of stylistic consistency for improved perceptual clarity.

## Evaluate

- [ ] A chart uses shape to distinguish between two or more categories.
- [ ] All the shapes used belong to the same category (e.g., all are "closed" like circles, squares, and triangles, or all are "open" like crosses and asterisks).

## Repair

1.  Identify the two main categories you want viewers to distinguish.
2.  Change the shape for one of the categories to be from the opposite class. For example, if you are using circles and squares (both closed), change the squares to plus signs (open).
3.  Ensure the chosen shapes are clearly visible and rendered at a sufficient size.
