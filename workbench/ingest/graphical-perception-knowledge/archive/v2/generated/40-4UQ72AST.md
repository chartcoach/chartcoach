## Guidance

For long, ranked lists of data, wrap the bar chart into multiple columns so that all items are visible on the screen at once, eliminating the need for scrolling.

## Why

Standard, long bar charts require scrolling, which is slow and makes it difficult to see the overall distribution of values. Research shows that wrapping bars into multiple columns provides a powerful middle ground. It offers accuracy comparable to a standard bar chart but with significantly faster task completion times because no interaction is needed to see all the data.

## When it applies

- When visualizing a ranked list with more items than can comfortably fit on a single screen (e.g., more than a few dozen).
- When both the accuracy of individual value comparisons and the speed of interaction are important.
- When you want to give the audience a sense of the overall distribution of the entire list at a glance.

## Exceptions

- If the precise ranking of every single item is paramount and must be easily scannable in a single linear flow, a standard scrolled bar chart might still be preferable despite being slower.
- If the dataset is very small and already fits on one screen without scrolling, wrapping is unnecessary.
- If the primary task is only to estimate the average value of the entire list, other chart types like a Zvinca plot or histogram might be even faster.

## Trade-offs

- **Harder cross-column comparison:** Comparing bars in different columns is more difficult than comparing adjacent bars in a single list, as they no longer share an identical, continuous baseline.
- **Reduced resolution:** The horizontal space available for each bar is reduced, as the total chart width is divided among the columns. This can make it slightly harder to distinguish between values that are very close.

## Evaluate

- [ ] The chart requires a vertical scrollbar to view all items.
- [ ] It is impossible to see the first-ranked and last-ranked items on the screen at the same time.
- [ ] Users find it cumbersome to compare items at the top of the list with items at the bottom.

## Repair

1.  Reconfigure the chart to wrap the list of bars into multiple columns, ensuring the entire chart fits within the screen's height.
2.  Adjust the number of columns to find a good balance between bar width (for clear visual resolution) and the number of rows.
3.  If wrapping is not technically feasible, consider an alternative compact representation like a treemap, but be aware that judging area is generally less accurate than judging length.
