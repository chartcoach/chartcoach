## Guidance

When visualizing multiple time series, use an overlaid **shared-space** layout for local comparisons (e.g., finding the highest value at one point in time). Use a juxtaposed **split-space** layout (like small multiples) for global comparisons (e.g., comparing overall trends or shapes).

## Why

Overlaid lines in a shared space make it fast to compare values at a single point because the eye only has to travel vertically. However, they can become a cluttered mess, making it hard to follow individual trends. Splitting the series into separate charts (small multiples) eliminates clutter and makes it easy to compare overall shapes and trends, but requires more eye movement to compare specific points in time. Matching the layout to the task makes the visualization more effective and efficient to read.

## When it applies

- You are visualizing two or more time series.
- The primary task is either a **local comparison** (e.g., "Which company's stock was highest on June 1st?") or a **global comparison** (e.g., "Which product has the fastest overall growth trend?").

## Exceptions

- If you only have two or three series with minimal overlap, a shared-space layout can often work well for both local and global tasks.
- When vertical space is extremely limited, horizon graphs can be a compact split-space alternative, though they may require more cognitive effort to read.

## Trade-offs

- **Shared Space (Overlaid):** Optimizes for local comparison at the cost of potential visual clutter and difficulty in tracking individual lines, especially with many series.
- **Split Space (Small Multiples):** Optimizes for global trend comparison and avoids clutter at the cost of making precise, point-in-time comparisons more difficult and slower.

## Evaluate

- [ ] A chart with many (e.g., 5+) overlapping and crisscrossing lines is being used to show overall trends.
- [ ] A grid of small multiples is being used primarily to ask users to compare values at a specific, single point in time.

## Repair

1.  **If you have a cluttered overlaid chart for trend analysis:** Switch to a small multiples layout, giving each time series its own chart within a grid.
2.  **If you have small multiples for point-in-time comparison:** Combine the series into a single, overlaid line chart. Use a legend or direct labels to identify the series.
3.  **As a follow-up refinement:** For small multiples, ensure all charts share the same x-axis and y-axis scales for accurate comparison. For overlaid charts, consider adding interactive tooltips that reveal values on hover to aid identification.
