## Guidance

Donut charts are a perceptually sound alternative to pie charts for showing part-to-whole relationships. Do not hesitate to use a donut chart where you might otherwise use a pie chart.

## Why

Contrary to common wisdom, the human eye does not rely solely on the central angle to judge proportions in circular charts. Research shows that **arc length** and **segment area** are equally, if not more, important perceptual cues.

Since a donut chart preserves both arc length and area, removing the center of the pie does not harm a viewer's ability to estimate the values. The accuracy of a donut chart is statistically equivalent to that of a pie chart. The empty center also provides a useful space for a summary value, a title, or an icon.

## When it applies

- When visualizing the composition (part-to-whole relationship) for a small number of categories (typically 2-5).
- When you are choosing between a pie chart and a donut chart for a dashboard or infographic.
- When you want to use the central space for a summary statistic (e.g., the total), an icon, or a title.

## Exceptions

- **When the donut is extremely thin.** If the inner radius is very large (e.g., >90% of the outer radius), the chart becomes mostly an arc, and the area cue is lost. This can slightly decrease accuracy. A moderate hole size is most effective.
- **When precise comparison is critical.** Like pie charts, donut charts are not ideal for precise comparisons between slices, especially when values are close. A bar chart is almost always better for that task.

## Trade-offs

- The main trade-off is the same as for pie charts: you sacrifice some perceptual precision (compared to a bar chart) for a very clear part-to-whole metaphor.
- The center space can be a tempting place to put distracting information. Use it judiciously for a single, key piece of information.

## Evaluate

- [ ] A pie chart is used when a donut chart could have made better use of the central space.
- [ ] Team members or stakeholders express hesitation to use donut charts, citing debunked beliefs that they are less accurate than pie charts.

## Repair

1.  **Switch to a donut.** If you are using a pie chart, simply convert it to a donut chart. A moderate inner radius (e.g., 40-60% of the outer radius) is a safe and effective choice. Use the newly available center space for a key metric.
2.  **Consider a bar chart.** If the primary goal is for the audience to compare the size of the parts to each other (rather than to the whole), replace the pie or donut chart with a sorted bar chart for greater perceptual accuracy.
3.  **Educate your team.** If you encounter resistance, explain that research confirms donut charts are just as effective as pie charts because viewers rely more on area and arc length than on the central angle.
