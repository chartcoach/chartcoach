## Guidance

When comparing values between two series on a bar chart, overlay explicit difference markers onto a grouped bar chart. This provides the context of the original values while making the change between them immediately visible.

## Why

This approach offloads the cognitive work of calculating differences from the viewer to the chart itself. Standard grouped bar charts require you to mentally compare two bars to estimate their difference, which is slow and error-prone. By explicitly encoding the difference with a line or dot, you make this key comparison task faster and more accurate.

Research shows that users overwhelmingly prefer this hybrid design because it provides the "full story"—the absolute values of both series and the change between them—in a single, easy-to-read view.

## When it applies

- When a primary task for your audience is to compare values between two series, such as year-over-year, before-and-after, or actual-vs-target.
- For bar charts with categorical or sequential data (e.g., sales by region, revenue by month).
- In dashboards where users need to quickly understand "what's changed" at a glance.

## Exceptions

- When comparing **more than two series**, adding overlays for every pair can quickly become cluttered and confusing.
- If the primary task is simply to read the absolute value of each bar and comparison is not important, the overlays may add unnecessary visual noise.

## Trade-offs

- This design adds more visual elements ("ink") to the chart compared to a simple grouped bar chart, which could feel slightly more cluttered in a dense layout.
- It may require more effort to create, as it is a less common chart type in many standard tools.

## Evaluate

- [ ] Does the chart require viewers to mentally subtract one bar from another to see the size of the change?
- [ ] Does the chart show *only* the difference, hiding the original magnitude of the values being compared?
- [ ] Is it difficult to tell if a change is large or small in absolute terms?

## Repair

1.  **Start with a grouped bar chart.** Create a standard grouped bar chart that places the bars for the two series you are comparing side-by-side for each category.
2.  **Calculate the differences.** For each category, compute the difference between the value in the first series and the second series.
3.  **Add the overlay.** Add a visually distinct mark (like a horizontal line, a dot, or a small arrow) that explicitly encodes the calculated difference. Position this mark so it is clearly associated with the bar group for that category. This gives viewers the difference without removing the context of the original bars.
