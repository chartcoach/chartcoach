## Guidance

When visualizing data across both space and time, match the chart's layout to the primary analysis task.

- Use **small multiples** (juxtaposing space) for comparing different locations at a single point in time.
- Use a **single map with time-series glyphs** (juxtaposing time) for identifying trends over the entire time series.

## Why

This principle minimizes cognitive load by grouping information in a way that aligns with the user's question.

- **Small multiples** place all spatial information for a single time step in one view, making it easy to scan across locations.
- A **single map** with glyphs (like mini bar or line charts) places all temporal information for a single location in one spot, making it easy to see its history at a glance.

Forcing a layout to perform the wrong task requires the viewer to mentally collate scattered information, which is slower, more difficult, and more error-prone.

## When it applies

- You are visualizing two or more variables across both geographic space (e.g., countries, states) and a time series (e.g., years, months).
- The primary analytical task is to identify correlations or trends.
- You are choosing between a series of maps (small multiples) and a single map that encodes time within glyphs.

## Exceptions

When the task involves analyzing a **subset of time** (e.g., a 3-year period) and a **subset of space** (e.g., a specific region), there is no single best approach. The choice becomes a trade-off:
- If the spatial component is more complex (many locations), favor **small multiples**.
- If the temporal component is more complex (many time steps), favor a **single map**.

## Trade-offs

- **Small multiples** make it difficult to follow one location's trend over the full time series, as it requires scanning across multiple disconnected maps. They also consume more screen space.
- A **single map** with glyphs makes it difficult to see the overall spatial pattern at a single moment in time, as the relevant information is a small part of a larger glyph at each location.

## Evaluate

- [ ] Is the primary task to compare locations at one time point, but the visualization uses a single map with complex glyphs?
- [ ] Is the primary task to identify trends over a full time series, but the visualization uses small multiples, forcing users to scan across many charts?

## Repair

1.  **Identify the primary task.** Is the most important question about "what happened in 2022?" (a spatial comparison) or "what is the history of this location?" (a temporal trend)?
2.  **For spatial tasks**, switch to small multiples. If you are using a single map, change it to a series of simpler maps, one for each time step.
3.  **For temporal tasks**, switch to a single map. If you are using small multiples, consolidate them into one map where each location has a glyph (e.g., a mini line or bar chart) that shows its history.
4.  **For mixed tasks**, consider providing an interactive control that lets the user switch between a small multiple view and a single map view.
