## Guidance

For simple part-to-whole comparisons with a small number of categories (e.g., 3-7), use a pie chart or a bar chart instead of a single-level treemap.

## Why

Research shows that when estimating the proportion of a category, viewers make significantly larger errors when reading from a single-level treemap compared to a pie chart. The area judgments required for treemaps, especially with varying rectangle aspect ratios, are less precise than the angle and area judgments in pie charts for this specific task.

## When it applies

- When your primary task is for viewers to understand, estimate, or compare the proportions of a few categories that make up a whole.
- When you are visualizing non-hierarchical data.
- When the number of categories is low (e.g., under 8).

## Exceptions

- **Hierarchical Data:** This guideline does **not** apply when visualizing hierarchical (nested) data. Treemaps are specifically designed for showing multiple levels of a hierarchy within a whole, a task for which pie charts are completely unsuitable.
- **High Cardinality:** When you have a very large number of categories, a treemap can be more effective at showing the general distribution and identifying the largest components, even if individual value estimation is poor.

## Trade-offs

- **Space Efficiency:** A treemap can be more space-efficient than a pie chart or bar chart, especially as the number of categories increases. However, this efficiency comes at the cost of perceptual accuracy for individual comparisons.
- **Familiarity:** Pie charts and bar charts are more familiar to a general audience than treemaps, potentially reducing cognitive load.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Misleading Proportions:** Viewers misjudge the relative sizes of the rectangles in the treemap, especially when comparing a tall, thin rectangle to a wide, short one.
- **Inaccurate Takeaways:** When asked to estimate percentages from a treemap, viewer answers have a high error rate.
- **Viewer Hesitation:** Viewers take a long time to answer questions about the proportions shown in a treemap.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Add Direct Labels.** If you must use a treemap for non-hierarchical data, add direct labels with percentages or absolute values to each rectangle. This provides an "escape hatch" for viewers, allowing them to read the exact values instead of relying on less accurate perceptual judgments of area.

- **Moderate Redesign: Switch to a Stacked Bar Chart.** A 100% stacked bar chart maintains the part-to-whole concept and uses length, which is easier to compare for the segments at either end of the bar.

- **Comprehensive Redesign: Switch to a Simple Bar Chart.** If the primary task is to compare the categories, a standard bar chart is the most perceptually accurate option. All bars share a common baseline (zero), making length comparisons straightforward and precise. You lose the explicit visual representation of the "whole," but gain significant accuracy in comparison.
