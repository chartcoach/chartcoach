## Guidance

When the primary task is to quickly and accurately estimate the average value of a large, ranked list, use a Zvinca plot (a type of dot plot).

## Why

Zvinca plots encode values using position on a common scale, which is a highly effective visual channel. For an aggregation task like estimating the average, the human visual system can quickly perceive the "center of mass" of the cluster of dots, leading to fast and accurate judgments. Using dots instead of bars reduces the total amount of ink on the page, which may also facilitate easier overall visual estimation.

## When it applies

- The main goal is to understand the overall distribution and estimate the average (mean) of a large set of quantitative values.
- The list of items is long and needs to be displayed compactly on a single screen.
- Task completion speed is a high priority.

## Exceptions

- If the primary task is to precisely compare the values of two specific items or determine the exact rank of one item. For these tasks, length-based encodings like bar charts are more accurate.
- If the dataset has many items with identical or nearly identical values, overplotting (dots drawn on top of each other) can become an issue, obscuring the true density of values.

## Trade-offs

- **Comparison Accuracy:** You sacrifice the high precision of comparing individual items that length-based charts (like bar charts) provide.
- **Familiarity:** Zvinca plots are less common than bar charts, which might require a small amount of additional cognitive effort for viewers to understand initially.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Slow Averaging:** Users are taking a long time to estimate the average value from a visualization, possibly because they are mentally trying to read and average many individual bars.
- **Inaccurate Summaries:** When asked about the overall trend or average, users' estimates are wildly inaccurate, suggesting the chart type (e.g., a treemap or packed bars) is poorly suited for the task.

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach:** If you are using a bar chart, add a reference line indicating the average value. This directly provides the summary statistic but doesn't improve the user's ability to perceive it from the chart itself.
- **Moderate approach:** Replace a chart with a complex layout (like packed bars) with a simpler Zvinca plot or dot plot. This provides a common scale and simplifies the visual information, making averaging easier.
- **Comprehensive approach:** If summary tasks are a primary goal for a dashboard or report, provide a dedicated Zvinca plot or a statistical summary chart (like a box plot) in addition to any charts meant for individual item lookup or comparison.
