## Guidance
To enable accurate comparison and ranking of categorical data, use a bar chart instead of a pie chart.

## Why
Humans are significantly better at accurately judging and comparing lengths along a common baseline (the primary visual task in a bar chart) than they are at judging angles, areas, or arc lengths (the tasks required by a pie chart). This perceptual advantage makes bar charts more effective for almost all comparison and ranking tasks.

## When it applies
- When the primary goal is for the user to compare values between categories.
- When the user needs to rank categories from highest to lowest.
- When there are more than 2-3 categories, making angle comparisons in a pie chart increasingly difficult.

## Exceptions
- When the goal is to show a part-to-whole relationship with only two categories (e.g., 75% vs 25%), a pie chart can be effective and immediately recognizable.
- When you need to emphasize that the values sum to a meaningful total (100%). However, a stacked bar chart can often serve this purpose with greater clarity for individual components.

## Trade-offs
- **Gains:** Greatly increased accuracy and speed for comparison and ranking tasks.
- **Sacrifices:** May be less effective at communicating a simple part-to-whole message for 2-3 categories than a pie chart. A bar chart can take up more space than a pie chart, especially if category labels are long.

## Signs of Trouble
- **The "Is it bigger?" Question:** Viewers are unable to confidently tell which of two similarly-sized slices in a pie chart is larger.
- **Multiple Legends:** A pie chart requires a separate color legend, forcing viewers to look back and forth to decode the chart.
- **3D or Exploded Slices:** The pie chart uses 3D effects or has "exploded" slices, which further distort the angles and areas, making accurate perception impossible.
- **More than 5 slices:** The pie chart has many small slices, becoming a "rainbow wheel" that is impossible to interpret.

## How to Improve
- **Quick Fix: Switch to a Bar Chart.** The most effective improvement is to change the chart type from a pie chart to a bar chart.
- **Moderate Approach: Sort and Label.** If you switch to a bar chart, sort the bars in descending or ascending order (unless there is a natural ordering to the categories). Add direct data labels to the bars to facilitate precise lookups.
- **Comprehensive Approach: If you must use a Pie Chart...** If required to use a pie chart, limit it to 2-3 categories, add direct percentage labels to each slice, and never use 3D or exploding effects. For more categories, a bar chart is always a better alternative.
