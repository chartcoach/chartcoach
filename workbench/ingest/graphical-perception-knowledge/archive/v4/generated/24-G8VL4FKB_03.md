## Guidance

When the primary task is to analyze the difference or balance between two series (e.g., imports vs. exports), do not rely on viewers judging the gap between two lines. Instead, explicitly calculate the difference and plot it directly in its own chart.

## Why

Judging the fluctuating vertical distance between two lines is an extremely difficult and error-prone perceptual task. The human visual system tends to judge the *minimum* distance between the lines, not the vertical distance, leading to gross misinterpretations of the magnitude and trend of the difference. As demonstrated by Cleveland & McGill (1984), this can obscure even very dramatic patterns. Plotting the difference directly converts the task to a much more accurate position judgment against a stable zero-baseline.

## When it applies

- When visualizing two time-series where the relationship or difference *between* the lines is more important than their individual absolute values.
- Classic examples include import/export charts (showing trade balance), birth/death rates (showing population growth), or revenue/costs (showing profit).
- Any dual-axis chart where the intent is to compare the two series.

## Exceptions

- If the absolute values of both series are just as important as the difference, it can be effective to show both the original chart (with the two lines) and the separate difference chart together. This provides both context and a clear view of the relationship.

## Trade-offs

- **Loss of Context:** A separate difference chart removes the context of the original series' magnitudes. A profit of $100k might be huge for a small company (with revenue of $200k) but tiny for a large one (with revenue of $2B).
- **Space:** Creating a separate difference chart requires more vertical screen or page space.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Wandering Gaps:** The space between the two lines appears to narrow or widen, but it's hard to tell by how much, when, or where.
- **Peak Obscurity:** A significant peak or trough in the *difference* is barely noticeable in the original chart because it's just a subtle change in the gap.
- **Crossover Confusion:** Viewers have trouble identifying the exact point where one line overtakes the other (where the difference would cross zero).

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Add Shading.** Add a shaded region in the area between the two lines. While this still relies on a poor perceptual task (area/length judgment), it makes the quantity being judged more salient and can help highlight where the gap is largest.

- **Comprehensive Redesign: Create a Difference Chart.** Create a second chart, typically positioned directly below the original. In this new chart, plot the result of `Series A - Series B` against a horizontal zero-line. This directly and accurately visualizes the trend and magnitude of the difference, converting the difficult length task into a simple, accurate position task.
