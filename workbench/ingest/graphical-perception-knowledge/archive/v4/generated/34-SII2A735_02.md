## Guidance

When one data category in a chart is the primary focus of analysis and others serve as context, encode the primary category with a "closed" shape (e.g., a circle ●) and the contextual categories with "open" shapes (e.g., plus signs +).

## Why

Research shows that closed shapes are processed more quickly and accurately as targets. However, they are also more susceptible to perceptual interference from other closed shapes. Pairing a closed-shape target with open-shape distractors leverages the processing speed advantage of the closed shape while minimizing the perceptual interference from the contextual shapes, making the primary category stand out.

## When it applies

- In multi-class scatterplots where the analytical goal is asymmetrical—that is, focused on one category against the backdrop of others.
- For example, when analyzing "our product's" performance (primary, closed shape) versus "competitors'" (context, open shapes).

## Exceptions

- If all categories are of equal importance, this approach can create an unintended and misleading visual hierarchy. In that case, balancing the salience of all shapes is more important.
- When the primary target category has very few data points, an open shape might be too visually light; a closed shape could give it necessary presence.

## Trade-offs

- This approach intentionally creates a visual hierarchy by making the closed shape more salient. This is a negative consequence if all categories should be perceived as equally important.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Unbalanced Focus:** In a chart where all categories are meant to be equal, the category encoded with a closed shape (among open ones) draws too much viewer attention.
- **Reverse Struggle:** When the primary target is an open shape and the distractors are closed shapes, the task can become more difficult because the context is more salient than the target.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix:** Identify the single most important category in your chart's narrative. Change its symbol to a simple, filled circle and change all other category symbols to plus signs or crosses.
- **Moderate Redesign:** In addition to changing the shape, you can further enhance the focus on the primary category using color (e.g., a saturated color for the primary, gray for the context) to create a clear figure-ground relationship.
