## Guidance

When the primary task is to identify correlation and trends over a long period (i.e., all available time steps), use a single map that encodes the entire time series within a glyph at each location (e.g., a miniature bar chart or sparkline).

## Why

This design is faster and less error-prone for long-term trend analysis. It juxtaposes all temporal information for each location, making it easy to see trends like correlation and monotonic evolution over time with a single glance at a glyph. This reduces the cognitive load of having to scan and integrate information from multiple separate maps.

## When it applies

- When the task is to characterize the correlation and its evolution over the entire duration of the dataset (e.g., "For each state, what was the correlation trend between 2000 and 2009?").
- This holds true whether the spatial scope is a single location, a region, or all locations.

## Exceptions

- If the primary task is to compare spatial patterns at a *single point in time*, this approach is much slower and more error-prone than using small multiples.

## Trade-offs

- This design makes it harder to compare the state of all locations at a single moment in time, as that information is "buried" within each time-series glyph.
- The complexity of the glyphs can increase with the number of time steps, potentially leading to visual clutter.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Slow Performance:** Users are slow to identify long-term trends because they have to scan back and forth between many small multiple maps.
- **Memory Errors:** Users struggle to remember patterns from one map to the next, making it difficult to synthesize a temporal trend.
- **Visual Fragmentation:** The temporal story is broken up across many small, separate views, preventing a holistic understanding of trends.
- **User Complaints:** Viewers comment that it's difficult to "observe evolution over time" with small multiples.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Add a "Sparkline" Overview.** If using small multiples, provide a small sparkline or summary glyph next to each location's label that summarizes its trend, offering a temporal overview.
- **Comprehensive Redesign: Switch to a Single Map with Time-Series Glyphs.** Redesign the visualization to use a single base map. At each location, place a glyph (like a miniature bar chart or line chart) that encodes the entire time series for that location. This directly juxtaposes the temporal information, aligning with the trend-finding task.
