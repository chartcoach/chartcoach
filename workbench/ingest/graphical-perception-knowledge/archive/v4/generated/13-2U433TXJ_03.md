## Guidance
For tasks requiring rapid visual comparison of many time series, consider using colorfields (1D heatmaps). They can enable faster performance than line charts or horizon graphs.

## Why
Experiments on time series similarity perception found that participants were able to make judgments fastest when using colorfields. This suggests that encoding values as color strips allows for quick, high-level "gestalt" comparisons that are faster than tracing the shapes of line charts or decoding the layers of horizon graphs.

## When it applies
- When performance and speed are critical, such as in an interactive system where a user must quickly review dozens or hundreds of potential pattern matches.
- For overview tasks where identifying general patterns of similarity is more important than analyzing fine-grained details of the signal.

## Exceptions
- This speed advantage may not apply if the similarity logic is complex. Colorfields are perceptually sensitive to temporal warping (stretching/shifting) and are less suitable for that type of comparison.
- If precise value comparison is needed, the continuous position encoding of a line chart is superior to the binned color encoding of a colorfield.

## Trade-offs
- **Speed vs. Detail:** Colorfields are faster for overviews but sacrifice the fine-grained detail and precise shape representation available in a line chart.
- **Speed vs. Invariance:** While potentially faster, colorfields are not a good choice for similarity that needs to be invariant to temporal warping. You may be trading appropriate perceptual logic for speed.

## Signs of Trouble
- **Detail Loss:** Users complain they cannot see the exact shape or small fluctuations of the signal in the colorfield view.
- **Warping Sensitivity:** Users using a colorfield are incorrectly rejecting patterns that are good matches but are slightly stretched or compressed in time.

## How to Improve
- **Quick Fix: Increase Vertical Space.** Ensure the colorfield strips have sufficient height so that color variations are clearly visible.

- **Moderate Redesign: Provide a Detail-on-Demand View.** Use colorfields in a gallery view for rapid scanning and selection, but allow the user to click or hover to see a more detailed line chart of the selected item and its closest matches.

- **Comprehensive Approach: Test with Your Task.** Since performance is task-dependent, run a small, informal test with your users and data to confirm that colorfields provide a speed benefit for your specific similarity criteria before committing to them in a production system.
