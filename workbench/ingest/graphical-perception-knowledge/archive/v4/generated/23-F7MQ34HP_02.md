## Guidance
For general-purpose visualization of multiple time series, prefer standard line charts (for fewer series) or small multiples (for more series).

## Why
While specialized techniques like horizon graphs or braided graphs exist, studies show that simple line charts and small multiples are more versatile and perform consistently well across a range of common analytical tasks, from local value lookups to global trend comparisons. They have a lower cognitive barrier to entry and are more robust for general audiences.

## When it applies
- When designing a dashboard or tool where users will need to perform a variety of tasks on the same time series data.
- When the audience may not be familiar with more advanced or abstract chart types like horizon graphs.
- When a reliable, all-around performer is needed, rather than a chart hyper-optimized for a single, specific task.

## Exceptions
- When screen space is extremely limited and data density is very high, a more compact representation like a horizon graph might be necessary, despite its higher cognitive load for some tasks.
- When a very specific task is the sole priority (e.g., only local comparisons), a shared-space chart might be marginally faster than small multiples.

## Trade-offs
- **Gains:** Versatility, reliability across tasks, and ease of interpretation for a general audience.
- **Sacrifices:** May not be the absolute fastest or most space-efficient technique for every single specialized task.

## Signs of Trouble
- **User Confusion:** Viewers are unsure how to read a more complex chart (e.g., a horizon graph), asking what the different colors or bands mean.
- **Slow Performance on Simple Tasks:** A complex chart type is used, and it slows down performance on what should be a simple, quick task (e.g., using a horizon graph for a max value lookup).
- **Inflexible Design:** The chosen chart type excels at one task but is very poor for other, equally important tasks that users need to perform.

## How to Improve
- **Quick Fix: Default to Simplicity.** Start with a simple line chart. If it becomes cluttered, switch to small multiples. Only consider more complex chart types if there is a compelling, specific need that these simple forms cannot meet.
- **Moderate Approach: Provide a Toggle.** If a specialized view (like a horizon graph) is useful for a specific expert task, consider providing it as an alternative view that users can switch to, but keep the simpler form (small multiples or a line chart) as the default.
