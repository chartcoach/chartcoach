## Guidance

Stack bar charts vertically (one above the other) when the primary task is for a viewer to compare the overall mean value or the data range (minimum to maximum) between two or more sets of data.

## Why

A stacked arrangement aligns the charts along a shared axis, enabling viewers to make highly precise vertical comparisons. The research suggests that viewers can mentally "slice downward" to compare bar lengths and form an accurate impression of the overall set-level properties like mean and range. This arrangement leads to more precise and reliable judgments than side-by-side or overlaid layouts for these specific summary tasks.

## When it applies

- When comparing two or more sets of categorical data using bar charts.
- When the key analytical task is judging which set has a higher average value (mean) or a wider spread (range).
- When creating small multiple layouts where the primary goal is to compare summary statistics across the charts.

## Exceptions

- When the task is to compare *individual corresponding items* across charts (e.g., "which category had the biggest change?"). Prior research has shown that a superposed (overlaid) or animated chart can be more effective for this type of focused, item-to-item comparison.
- When vertical screen or page space is severely limited, a side-by-side (adjacent) arrangement may be a necessary compromise.

## Trade-offs

- **Space:** A stacked layout uses more vertical screen space compared to placing charts side-by-side (adjacent) or overlaying them (superposed).
- **Task Specificity:** While optimal for mean and range comparisons, this layout may be less effective for other tasks, such as judging correlation between the sets, where a mirrored layout might be better.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Side-by-Side Struggle:** When looking at two side-by-side bar charts, viewers have difficulty telling which one has a higher average value without carefully inspecting individual bars.
- **Inconsistent Judgements:** Different people arrive at different conclusions about which chart has the wider range, indicating the comparison is not perceptually clear.
- **Overlay Obscurity:** If using overlaid bars, viewers complain that the chart is too cluttered to get a sense of the overall value for each set.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Add Guide Lines.** If charts must remain side-by-side, add faint horizontal guide lines that span across both charts to help the eye compare bar heights. This can mitigate some of the perceptual difficulty.
- **Moderate Redesign: Stack Vertically.** Change the layout from adjacent (side-by-side) or superposed (overlaid) to stacked (one on top of the other). Ensure the x-axes are perfectly aligned. This directly applies the finding of the research for a significant improvement in perceptual precision.
- **Comprehensive Approach: Enable Task-Specific Layouts.** In an interactive context, recognize that no single layout is best for all tasks. Consider providing controls to let users switch between arrangements (e.g., a toggle between a stacked view for mean comparison and a superposed view for finding the largest difference).
