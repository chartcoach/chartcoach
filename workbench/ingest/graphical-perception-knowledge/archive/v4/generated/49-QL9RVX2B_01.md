## Guidance

When performance (speed and accuracy) is a top priority for a data-literate audience, consider using novel circular area charts (such as a "circular slice" chart) as a high-performance alternative to a traditional pie chart.

## Why

An experimental study found that a novel circular chart, where values were encoded by the area of sliding circles, was read both faster and with lower error than a standard pie chart for part-to-whole comparison tasks. This challenges the assumption that familiar charts are always the most effective and suggests that alternative designs can be perceptually superior.

## When it applies

- The primary goal is maximizing the speed and accuracy of part-to-whole estimations.
- The visualization is intended for a data-literate audience (e.g., an internal analytics team) that is open to learning new chart types.
- The visualization will be used repeatedly, justifying the initial learning cost.

## Exceptions

- **General Audience:** For a broad, general audience or a one-off visualization, the universal recognizability of a pie chart is often more valuable than a marginal performance gain from a novel chart. The cognitive effort required to learn the new form can cause confusion and negate any benefits.
- **Simplicity is Key:** If the goal is immediate, effortless communication with no room for ambiguity, stick with a highly conventional chart like a bar chart or pie chart.

## Trade-offs

- **Familiarity vs. Performance:** You trade the instant recognizability and low learning curve of a pie chart for potential gains in reader speed and accuracy.
- **Cognitive Load:** A novel chart type increases the initial cognitive load for the viewer as they must first understand how to read it before they can interpret the data it shows.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Viewer Confusion:** Viewers express confusion, asking "What does this chart mean?" or "How do I read this?"
- **Incorrect Interpretation:** Viewers misinterpret the visual encoding, leading to flawed takeaways from the data.
- **Design Distraction:** The novelty of the chart design distracts from the data itself; viewers focus on the "cool chart" rather than the message.

## How to Improve

- **Minimal: Annotate Clearly.** If you use a novel chart, provide a clear title and a brief, explicit annotation explaining the encoding. For example: "Each segment's area represents its share of the total."

- **Moderate: Gradual Introduction.** Introduce the new chart type in a low-stakes environment first. You could also pair it with a more familiar chart (like a bar chart) showing the same data to help viewers learn the new form.

- **Comprehensive: Prioritize Convention for Broad Audiences.** If your audience is general or the context requires zero learning curve, stick with a more conventional and perceptually effective chart type, such as a bar chart, which is both familiar and highly accurate for comparison tasks.
