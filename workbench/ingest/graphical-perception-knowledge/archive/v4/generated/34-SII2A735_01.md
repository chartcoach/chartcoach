## Guidance

In a multi-class scatterplot, avoid encoding different categories with shapes that are all from the same perceptual group. For example, do not use only "closed" shapes (like circles, squares, and triangles) to represent three different categories.

## Why

Shapes from the same perceptual group (e.g., all closed or all open) create more perceptual interference with each other. This makes it harder for a viewer to mentally isolate one category and perform analytical tasks like judging its numerosity (how many points?), distribution, or trend, as the other "similar" shapes compete for attention and clutter the view.

## When it applies

- For multi-class scatterplots where viewers need to perform analysis tasks (like judging correlation, density, or numerosity) on individual categories within the plot.
- This is especially important in charts with moderate to high data density, where symbols overlap or are close together.

## Exceptions

- When the primary task is simply to see the overall combined distribution of all points, and the distinction between categories is secondary.
- In very sparse plots where categories are well-separated by position, reducing the potential for interference.

## Trade-offs

- Following this guideline may limit your palette of available shapes, potentially conflicting with aesthetic or branding requirements that favor a "cleaner" look of using only simple, closed shapes.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Analytical Struggle:** Viewers report that it's "hard to see the trend for the squares because of all the circles getting in the way."
- **Task Errors:** Accuracy is low or response time is high for tasks like "Which category has more points in the upper-right quadrant?" or "Does the 'triangle' category show a positive correlation?".
- **Visual "Noise":** One category's marks feel like noise that obscures the signal of another, rather than a distinct group.

## How to Improve

- **Minimal Change:** If your chart uses circles and squares to show two categories, change the squares to plus signs. This breaks the "all closed shapes" interference and makes each category easier to isolate visually.
- **Systematic Review:** Audit your organization's or tool's default shape palettes. Ensure that for N > 1 categories, the default shapes are a mix of open and closed types to minimize perceptual interference out-of-the-box. For example, a default palette could be [circle, plus, square, cross, triangle].
