## Guidance

While length-based charts are generally superior for comparison, treemaps can be a surprisingly effective choice for visualizing large, non-hierarchical ranked lists, especially when space is limited.

## Why

Conventional wisdom holds that area-based judgments are perceptually less accurate than length-based judgments on a common scale. However, this study suggests that for treemaps, this disadvantage may not be as severe as presumed. A potential reason is that modern squarified treemap layouts are deterministic and organize rectangles by size, meaning an item's position provides a secondary (and strong) perceptual cue to its rank, which may aid interpretation. Their primary advantage is their exceptional space efficiency.

## When it applies

- You need to visualize a very large ranked list (e.g., hundreds of items) in a compact, static space.
- The task is to get a general sense of the distribution, identify the top items, and make approximate comparisons, rather than precise ones.
- The data is a flat list (non-hierarchical), which is a common but technically "off-label" use for treemaps.

## Exceptions

- When the primary task requires high-precision comparisons of value. A bar chart (scrolled or wrapped) will always be more accurate.
- When the primary task is to estimate the average value of the list. Treemaps performed poorly for this task.
- When the audience is completely unfamiliar with treemaps, as the layout can be unintuitive at first glance.

## Trade-offs

- **Accuracy for Space:** You trade the high perceptual accuracy of a bar chart for the superior space-efficiency of a treemap.
- **Comparison Difficulty:** Comparing the areas of two non-adjacent rectangles, especially those with different aspect ratios, is perceptually challenging.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Inaccurate Comparisons:** Users are making significant errors when asked to compare the values of two items, especially if the items are of similar size.
- **Misinterpretation:** Users are trying to find a hierarchical relationship in the data that does not exist, because they associate treemaps with hierarchical structures.

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach:** Add direct labels (value and category) to each rectangle in the treemap. This provides an "escape hatch" for users to read exact values instead of relying solely on area perception.
- **Moderate approach:** If precise comparisons are more important than space-efficiency, switch to a wrapped bar chart. It is more accurate and still fits in a compact space.
- **Comprehensive approach:** Use a treemap as part of a linked-view system. The treemap can serve as a dense overview and navigation tool, and selecting a rectangle can show its precise details and value in a separate panel or tooltip.
