## Guidance

When building perceptual models to power visualization recommendation systems or automated design tools, use triplet matching comparison tasks to gather similarity judgments from users. Avoid methods like 2D spatial arrangement.

## Why

The way human similarity judgments are collected fundamentally affects the quality and reliability of the resulting perceptual model. The research found that triplet matching tasks (e.g., "Is this shape more similar to shape A or shape B?") produce the most robust, lowest-variance, and accurate perceptual models. In contrast, seemingly intuitive methods like having users drag and drop items on a 2D canvas ("spatial arrangement") are fast but produce noisy, unreliable data that fails to capture the true dimensionality of perceptual spaces like color.

## When it applies

- This is a methodological guideline for researchers and developers who are building visualization recommendation systems, automated palette generators, or any tool that relies on a quantitative model of human graphical perception.
- When you need to create a "perceptual kernel" or distance matrix for a set of visual variables (e.g., a new shape or icon set).

## Exceptions

- If speed and cost are the absolute highest priorities and low-fidelity results are acceptable, faster methods like pairwise Likert ratings might be a reasonable compromise. The paper notes they fare well and are much faster to collect than exhaustive triplets.
- Spatial arrangement might be acceptable only if the perceptual space is known to be 2D and high precision is not required.

## Trade-offs

- **Cost and Time:** Triplet matching requires significantly more judgments to build a model compared to pairwise ratings or spatial arrangement, making it more expensive and time-consuming to execute.
- **Robustness and Accuracy:** The higher upfront cost of triplet matching is traded for a much more reliable and accurate perceptual model that will lead to better downstream design recommendations.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Inconsistent Models:** A perceptual model built from user judgments shows high variance between subjects, indicating the collection method was not reliable.
- **Poor Predictive Power:** A model of univariate encodings (e.g., separate models for shape and color) fails to accurately predict how bivariate encodings (shape + color) are perceived. The study found triplet matching models were best at this.
- **Dimensionality Mismatch:** A model built using a 2D spatial arrangement task produces strange results for color, because color perception is inherently three-dimensional (hue, saturation, lightness).

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach: Use Pre-existing Robust Models.** Instead of building your own model, use palettes and perceptual kernels from research that were generated using robust methods like triplet matching. The paper's authors open-sourced their kernels for this purpose.

- **Moderate approach: Use Pairwise Likert Ratings.** If triplet matching is too costly, use pairwise Likert scale ratings (e.g., "Rate the similarity of A and B on a scale of 1-9"). This method performed reasonably well in the study and is much faster to collect.

- **Comprehensive approach: Implement Triplet Matching.** Design a crowdsourced or lab study that presents users with triplets of visual stimuli and asks them to choose which of two items is more similar to a third reference item. Use the resulting judgments to build a high-fidelity perceptual kernel.
