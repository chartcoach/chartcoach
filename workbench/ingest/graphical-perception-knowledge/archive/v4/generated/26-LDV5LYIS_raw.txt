## Guidance

For the task of visually assessing the correlation between two quantitative variables, prefer a scatterplot over a parallel coordinate plot.

## Why

Research shows that people can judge correlations more accurately and more quickly when using scatterplots. Viewers can distinguish a wider range of correlation levels (e.g., strong vs. moderate positive correlation) in scatterplots. In contrast, parallel coordinate plots can introduce a perceptual bias, causing viewers to underestimate the strength of correlations.

## When it applies

- The primary goal is for the audience to understand the strength and direction of the relationship between two quantitative variables.
- You are choosing between a scatterplot and a parallel coordinate plot for visualizing a two-variable relationship.

## Exceptions

- When the primary task is to find patterns, clusters, or outliers across **many variables** (typically 3 or more) at once. Parallel coordinate plots are designed for multivariate analysis, and in that context, they can be more effective than trying to interpret a large scatterplot matrix. The weakness described here applies specifically to judging a single pairwise correlation.

## Trade-offs

- **Scatterplot:** Excellent for accurately visualizing the relationship between two variables. However, it does not scale well to many variables; a scatterplot matrix is needed, which can become overwhelming.
- **Parallel Coordinate Plot:** Scales to display many variables simultaneously. However, this comes at the cost of making pairwise correlation assessment less accurate and intuitive.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Task Mismatch:** A parallel coordinate plot is being used when the central question is simply "How correlated are variable A and variable B?".
- **Ambiguous Patterns:** In a parallel coordinate plot, the crossing lines make it difficult to determine if the relationship between two adjacent axes is positive, negative, or non-existent.
- **Viewer Confusion:** Viewers express difficulty in judging the relationship strength or direction from a parallel coordinate plot, or their interpretation is inaccurate.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Add explicit annotations.** If you must use a parallel coordinate plot, add a clear annotation stating the correlation coefficient (e.g., "r = 0.85") between key pairs of axes to compensate for the perceptual weakness.

- **Comprehensive Redesign: Switch to the right chart for the task.** If the main goal is to show the correlation between two variables, replace the parallel coordinate plot with a scatterplot. If multiple pairwise correlations are important, use a scatterplot matrix.
