## Guidance

Recognize that the aspect ratio of bars in a bar chart can systematically bias a viewer's perception of their value. Tall, skinny bars tend to be underestimated, while short, wide bars tend to be overestimated.

## Why

Recent research has shown that the shape of a bar influences how its magnitude is perceived. Unlike the common belief that bar charts are always accurately perceived, their aspect ratio introduces a systematic perceptual bias. This can lead viewers to misjudge the data, with tall bars appearing smaller than they are and wide bars appearing larger.

## When it applies

- When designing any bar chart where the bars are not perfect squares.
- When accurate judgment of bar length is critical.
- When comparing values across different bar charts that may have different aspect ratios.

## Exceptions

- When the exact magnitude is not as important as the relative ranking of the bars.
- When direct data labels are present on all bars, providing a way for users to override their perceptual judgment by reading the exact values.

## Trade-offs

- Forcing bars to be square to eliminate bias may lead to layouts that are impractical, space-inefficient, or aesthetically awkward.
- Using a standard, non-square aspect ratio is common practice, but carries this risk of perceptual bias.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Extreme Aspect Ratios:** The bar chart consists of very tall, skinny bars or very short, wide bars.
- **Inconsistent Ratios:** A dashboard or report uses multiple bar charts with different aspect ratios, introducing variable biases and making comparisons between charts unreliable.
- **High-Stakes Decisions:** The bar chart is being used to inform important decisions where small misperceptions of value could have significant consequences.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Add Data Labels.** The most immediate mitigation is to add direct data labels to the bars. This allows viewers to read the true value, though it does not fix the underlying perceptual bias.

- **Moderate Approach: Aim for Square-ish Ratios.** When designing the chart, try to make the aspect ratio of the bars as close to square as is practical. This may involve adjusting the chart's overall width, height, and the spacing between bars.

- **Comprehensive Redesign: Switch to a Dot Plot.** To eliminate aspect ratio bias entirely, consider replacing the bar chart with a dot plot. A dot plot encodes value using only the position of a dot, which is not subject to the same shape-based perceptual biases as the length and area of a bar.
