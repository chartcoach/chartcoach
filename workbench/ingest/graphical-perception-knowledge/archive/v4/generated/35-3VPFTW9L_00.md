## Guidance

When using color to distinguish between categories in a multiclass scatterplot, don't just assign colors from a palette in their default order. Systematically match colors to classes to maximize the perceptual separability between spatially adjacent or overlapping groups.

## Why

Default color assignment is arbitrary and often results in visually similar colors being assigned to neighboring point clusters. This makes it difficult for viewers to distinguish between classes, slowing down analysis and increasing the risk of misinterpretation. An optimized assignment improves the speed and accuracy of identifying and separating class structures.

## When it applies

- When visualizing labeled data with multiple categories (classes) on a scatterplot.
- When class clusters overlap or are in close spatial proximity.
- When the primary task is to identify, count, or compare the distribution of different classes.

## Exceptions

- If class labels have strong, universally understood semantic color associations (e.g., political parties, specific brands), these semantic mappings may take precedence over a purely perceptual optimization. In this case, you can pre-assign those fixed colors and optimize the remaining assignments.

## Trade-offs

- A purely perceptual optimization may conflict with aesthetic preferences or brand guidelines. The assignment that is most "separable" might not be the most visually harmonious.
- Manual optimization is a tedious trial-and-error process. Automated optimization requires specific tooling.

## Signs of Trouble

- **The Squint Test:** If you squint at the chart, do different point clusters blur together into a single, indistinguishable color blob?
- **Neighboring Ambiguity:** Are two clusters that are close to each other assigned similar colors (e.g., a light blue and a medium blue), making their boundary unclear?
- **User Confusion:** Do viewers struggle to count the number of distinct classes or frequently misidentify points in overlapping regions?

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Manual Swap.** Identify the two most confusing, adjacent classes in the scatterplot. Find their colors in the legend and swap one of their colors with a color from a distant, non-confusing class. Repeat for the next most confusing pair.
- **Moderate Approach: Evaluate with Simulators.** After assigning colors, use a color vision deficiency simulator (like the one in Viz Palette) to check if adjacent classes remain distinct for all users. Manually adjust any color pairs that become indistinguishable.
- **Comprehensive Approach: Use an Optimization Algorithm.** Employ a computational method (like the genetic algorithm described in the source paper) that analyzes both the spatial distribution of points and the perceptual differences in the color palette to find an optimal assignment automatically.
