## Guidance

For comparing quantitative values, encode data using length on a common baseline (as in a bar chart) rather than angle (as in a pie chart).

## Why

According to the perceptual hierarchy popularized by Mackinlay (1986), humans can judge and compare lengths with much greater accuracy than they can judge angles. While a pie chart's primary encoding is angle, a bar chart uses length, which is a more perceptually effective channel. This means viewers are less likely to make errors when reading a bar chart.

## When it applies

- When comparing the magnitude of different categories.
- When ranking categories from largest to smallest.
- When showing part-to-whole relationships where comparison between parts is important.

## Exceptions

- **Single, Simple Proportion:** If the goal is to highlight a single, simple part-to-whole relationship (e.g., "25% of users completed the survey") and high precision is not required, a pie chart can be an acceptable and highly familiar choice.
- **Familiarity:** If an audience is extremely familiar with pie charts for a specific reporting context, changing to a bar chart might cause temporary confusion, even if the bar chart is more effective.

## Trade-offs

- **Familiarity:** Pie charts, despite their perceptual flaws, are very common. Using a bar chart for part-to-whole relationships might feel less intuitive to some general audiences.
- **Focus:** A pie chart forces a part-to-whole interpretation, whereas a bar chart is more flexible. If the part-to-whole context is the single most important message, a pie chart makes that context unavoidable.

## Signs of Trouble

- **The "Which is bigger?" Test:** You or your audience cannot easily tell which of two slices in a pie chart is larger.
- **Legend Crutch:** Viewers must constantly look back and forth between the legend and the chart, or rely on data labels, to understand the values.
- **Multiple Pies:** You are using multiple pie charts side-by-side to show change over time or comparison across groups. This is a task for which pie charts are particularly ill-suited.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Add Direct Labels.** If you must keep the pie chart, add direct percentage and/or value labels to each slice. This provides an "escape hatch" for the user, allowing them to read the values instead of trying to interpret the angles.
- **Moderate Redesign: Sort the Slices.** Sorting the pie chart's slices from largest to smallest can make comparisons between adjacent slices slightly easier.
- **Comprehensive Redesign: Switch to a Bar Chart.** The most effective fix is to replace the pie chart with a bar chart, sorted by value. This leverages the more accurate "length" channel and makes all comparisons trivial.
