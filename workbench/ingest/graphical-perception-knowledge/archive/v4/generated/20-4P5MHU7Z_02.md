## Guidance
Avoid designing a visualization where the primary task requires viewers to compare the lengths of two segments that are stacked directly on top of each other.

## Why
When two segments are stacked, viewers are susceptible to a "part-to-whole" cognitive bias. Instead of comparing the top segment to the bottom segment, their perception is unconsciously influenced by comparing the top segment to the *total length* of the combined stack. This leads to a systematic and significant underestimation of the true ratio between the two segments.

## When it applies
- In stacked bar charts or divided bar charts where you need to compare the size of the component parts.
- When asking questions like, "What percentage of Segment B is Segment A?" where A is stacked on B.

## Exceptions
- If only a very rough "is it bigger or smaller?" judgment is needed and precision is not a goal.
- If the segments are so disproportionate (e.g., one is 10x the other) that the bias is negligible compared to the obvious difference.

## Trade-offs
- While the solutions fix the comparison bias, they can make it harder to see the total sum of the segments or require more chart space.

## Signs of Trouble
- **The Task:** The chart's primary purpose is to compare the size of stacked components (e.g., comparing Q1 sales to Q2 sales within a single yearly bar).
- **The Bias:** When tested, viewers consistently underestimate the ratio. For example, when comparing a 50-unit segment to another 50-unit segment (a 100% ratio), their answers might cluster around 50% (the part-to-whole ratio of 50/100).

## How to Improve
- **Quick Fix: Add a Gap.** Introduce a small, visible gap between the segments. This visually breaks the "whole" bar, disrupting the part-to-whole bias and encouraging a more direct segment-to-segment comparison.
- **Moderate Redesign: Switch to a Grouped Bar Chart.** Instead of stacking the segments, place them side-by-side as individual bars on a common baseline. This allows for accurate, unbiased comparison.
- **Comprehensive Redesign: Plot the Comparison Directly.** Create a chart that directly visualizes the comparison of interest. For example, if you want to show the ratio of Segment A to Segment B, calculate that ratio and plot it directly on a bar or dot plot.
