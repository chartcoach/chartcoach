## Guidance

When using shape to encode different data categories in a single chart, combine "open" shapes (e.g., +, ×, *) with "closed" shapes (e.g., ●, ■, ▲) to make them easier to tell apart.

## Why

The human visual system pre-attentively groups open shapes and closed shapes into distinct perceptual categories. Using a mix of these types makes one group "pop out" from the other, reducing the cognitive effort needed to distinguish between data series, especially in dense or cluttered plots.

## When it applies

- When using multiple shape encodings in a single visualization, such as a multi-class scatterplot.
- When the primary task for the viewer is to distinguish between the different categories or to analyze the properties of one category in the presence of others.

## Exceptions

- If a chart is very sparse and the data categories are already well-separated by position, the choice of shape is less critical.
- When brand guidelines strictly limit the available shapes to only one type (e.g., only circles and squares are permitted).

## Trade-offs

- Some open shapes (like asterisks) can be more visually complex or "heavy" than simple closed shapes, which might affect the overall aesthetic balance of the chart.
- This approach intentionally creates perceptual distance, which may be undesirable if the goal is to show a subtle or unified relationship between categories.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Category Confusion:** Viewers have difficulty telling which mark belongs to which category, especially at a glance or in a cluttered chart.
- **Slow Interpretation:** Viewers have to consciously search for all marks of a given category instead of perceiving them as a single, cohesive group.
- **The "All-the-Same" Effect:** In a plot that uses only closed shapes (e.g., circles, squares, triangles), the different categories visually blend together into a single, hard-to-parse cloud of points.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix:** If you are currently using two closed shapes (e.g., circle and square), change one of them to an open shape (e.g., circle and plus sign). This simple swap can significantly improve discriminability.
- **Moderate Redesign:** Systematically review your default shape palette. For a 3-category plot, instead of using circle, square, and triangle, use a mix like circle, plus sign, and square.
- **Comprehensive Approach:** For a multi-category plot, create a shape palette that deliberately pairs open and closed shapes to maximize the perceptual distance between all categories being displayed. Test this palette in a realistic, cluttered chart to confirm its effectiveness.
