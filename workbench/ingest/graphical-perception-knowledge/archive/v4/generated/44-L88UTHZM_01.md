## Guidance

When comparing the magnitude of different categories, especially for ranking or assessing differences, use a bar chart instead of a pie chart.

## Why

People are much better at accurately judging and comparing lengths from a common baseline (in a bar chart) than they are at judging angles or areas (in a pie chart). This makes comparisons in bar charts faster, more accurate, and less prone to error. Pie charts often lead to perceptual distortion, especially when slices are of similar size or not aligned to a vertical or horizontal axis.

## When it applies

- When a user needs to compare the values of different parts of a whole.
- When the task is to rank categories by value.
- This is especially critical when there are more than 2-3 categories or when the differences between categories are subtle.

## Exceptions

- A pie chart may be acceptable for showing a simple part-to-whole relationship with only **two or three** distinct, very different slices (e.g., 75% vs 25%). In these limited cases, the "part of a whole" metaphor can be intuitive and the perceptual error is minimal.
- When the goal is simply to communicate that one part is a majority of the whole, without needing precise comparisons.

## Trade-offs

- A bar chart may take up more screen real estate, especially if category labels are long (though a horizontal bar chart mitigates this).
- Some audiences may have a strong (though misguided) expectation for pie charts to be used for any data that represents percentages or composition.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Similar Slices:** The chart contains multiple slices of similar size, making it impossible to tell which is larger without reading labels.
- **The 5-Slice Rule:** The pie chart has more than 5 slices, creating significant visual clutter and cognitive overload.
- **Non-Aligned Comparison:** It's difficult to compare two slices that are not adjacent to each other (e.g., comparing a slice at 2 o'clock with one at 7 o'clock).
- **Label Dependency:** The chart is completely unreadable without direct data labels on every single slice.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Simplify and Label.** If you absolutely must use a pie chart, ensure it has 3 or fewer slices, add direct percentage labels, and sort the slices from largest to smallest starting at the 12 o'clock position to provide a consistent baseline.

- **Moderate Redesign: Switch to a Donut Chart.** Convert the pie chart to a donut chart. While still based on angle/arc length, the empty center can reduce the tendency to misinterpret area and encourages focus on the arc length, which is marginally better for comparison.

- **Comprehensive Redesign: Use a Bar Chart.** Replace the pie chart with a bar chart, with categories on the y-axis and values on the x-axis. Sort the bars from largest to smallest (unless there is a natural order to the categories) to make ranking effortless.
