## Guidance

For interactive scenarios where the highest possible accuracy in comparing ranked values is the top priority, use a standard scrolled bar chart.

## Why

A standard bar chart provides two key perceptual advantages for comparison:
1.  **Common Baseline:** All bars start from the same position (zero), making their lengths directly and accurately comparable.
2.  **High Resolution:** Each bar can use the full width of the chart area, maximizing the visual resolution and making even small differences in length easier to perceive.
This combination leads to the most accurate judgments of value, at the cost of interaction time.

## When it applies

- The most critical user task is to make precise comparisons between values or determine the exact rank of items in a long list.
- The visualization is interactive, allowing for scrolling.
- Accuracy is more important than the speed at which users can complete tasks.

## Exceptions

- When task completion time is a critical factor. The need to scroll makes this one of the slowest options.
- When the visualization must be static (e.g., for print or a non-interactive report) and the list is too long to fit on one page. In this case, a wrapped bar chart is a better alternative.
- For users with motor control impairments, the requirement to scroll can present a significant accessibility barrier.

## Trade-offs

- **Speed and Effort:** You achieve the highest accuracy at the cost of significantly longer task completion times and increased user effort (scrolling).
- **Global Context:** It is impossible to see the entire list at once, making it difficult to get a sense of the overall distribution without scrolling through the whole dataset.

## Signs of Trouble

- **User Frustration:** Users complain that it takes too long to find information or compare items that are far apart in the list.
- **Task Abandonment:** In time-sensitive scenarios, users may give up on tasks that require extensive scrolling.
- **Accessibility Issues:** Users with motor control difficulties are unable to effectively navigate the list.

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach:** Implement an interaction where users can "select" or "pin" an item to keep it visible while they scroll to another item for comparison.
- **Moderate approach:** If speed is also a concern, switch to a wrapped bar chart. This sacrifices some accuracy (due to multiple baselines and lower resolution) but dramatically improves task time by eliminating scrolling.
- **Comprehensive approach:** Provide both a scrolled bar chart for detailed, accurate lookups and a more compact summary chart (like a Zvinca plot or histogram) to show the overall distribution.
