## Guidance

For tasks that require comparing the magnitude of different categories, use a bar chart instead of a pie chart.

## Why

Bar charts encode quantitative values using length and position along a common axis, which humans perceive with high accuracy. Pie charts encode values using angle and area, which are much harder to judge precisely. This perceptual difference means that viewers can make faster and more accurate comparisons with a bar chart.

## When it applies

- The primary goal is to compare values between different categories.
- You need to show the ranking of categories.
- You have more than 2-3 categories.
- The values of the categories are relatively close to each other.

## Exceptions

- **Simple Part-to-Whole:** If you have only two or three categories and the primary goal is to show a simple part-to-whole relationship (e.g., "Category A makes up about a quarter of the total"), a pie chart can be acceptable. However, a stacked bar chart often serves this purpose just as well or better.
- **Binary Choice:** For showing a single percentage of a binary choice (e.g., 75% Yes vs. 25% No), a single-slice pie chart (or "pac-man" chart) can be visually effective.

## Trade-offs

- **Showing the "Whole":** A bar chart doesn't inherently visualize the total sum of the parts as obviously as a pie chart does (which always sums to 100%). To compensate, you can add a "total" annotation or use a 100% stacked bar chart if the part-to-whole relationship is critical.
- **Audience Expectation:** In some business or non-technical contexts, audiences may have a strong expectation for pie charts. Choosing a bar chart might require a slight cultural shift, but it is a shift toward greater clarity and accuracy.

## Signs of Trouble

- **The "Which is bigger?" question:** Viewers are struggling to determine which of two slices is larger, especially if they are not adjacent.
- **Many Slices:** The pie chart has more than 5 slices, creating a cluttered and unreadable "rainbow wheel."
- **3D or Exploded Slices:** The use of 3D perspective or exploded slices distorts the angles and areas, making accurate perception impossible and misleading the viewer.
- **Label-Only Reading:** The chart is only useful because of its data labels; the visualization itself adds no value and may even detract from understanding.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Add Direct Percentage Labels.** If you cannot change the chart type, add clear, legible percentage labels directly onto or next to each pie slice. This gives users an escape hatch to read the numbers directly.

- **Moderate Redesign: Convert to a Bar Chart.** Re-create the visualization as a bar chart. This is the most direct and effective improvement. Ensure the y-axis starts at zero.

- **Comprehensive Approach: Convert to a Sorted Bar Chart.** Re-create the visualization as a bar chart and sort the bars in ascending or descending order (unless there is a natural order to the categories). Sorting reduces the cognitive effort required to find the largest/smallest values and understand the ranking.
