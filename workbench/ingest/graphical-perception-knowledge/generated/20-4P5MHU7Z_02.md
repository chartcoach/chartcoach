## Guidance

When viewers need to compare the values of different bars, place those bars as close to each other as possible, preferably adjacent. Avoid separating them with wide gaps or intervening distractor bars.

## Why

Humans find it more difficult and less accurate to compare the heights of bars that are separated by a large distance. The mental effort required to span the gap and hold one height in memory to compare with another introduces perceptual error. This effect is especially strong when the bars being compared are short, as the relative error becomes larger.

### Core Principle

Make the most important comparisons the easiest to see. Minimizing the visual distance between objects being compared reduces the cognitive and perceptual load, leading to faster and more accurate judgments.

## When it applies

- When the primary task for the viewer is to compare or rank the values represented by different bars.
- When designing the layout and sorting order of a bar chart.

## Exceptions

- If the bars must be sorted by a different, meaningful order (e.g., alphabetically, geographically, or temporally) that is more important to the chart's purpose than the direct value comparison. Even in this case, consider ways to mitigate the separation effect.

## Trade-offs

- Strictly grouping bars for comparison may disrupt another meaningful order (e.g., alphabetical). You may sacrifice scannability on one dimension (like finding a specific category by name) to improve comparison accuracy on another (value).

## Signs of Trouble

- **Ruler Use:** Viewers have to use their finger, a cursor, or a physical ruler to track a bar's height across the chart to compare it with a distant bar.
- **Hesitation:** Comparisons between distant, similarly-sized bars feel like rough estimations rather than confident judgments.
- **Key Comparisons are Far Apart:** The most important comparisons your chart is meant to enable are between bars located on opposite ends of the chart.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Use Interactive Highlighting.** In an interactive chart, use hover or selection to highlight the specific bars being compared, and potentially draw a reference line between their tops. This helps the eye connect them.

- **Moderate Redesign: Re-sort the Chart.** If the existing order is not critical, sort the bars by value. This naturally places bars of similar height next to each other, making fine distinctions easier.

- **Comprehensive Redesign: Use Small Multiples or a Dot Plot.** If many non-adjacent comparisons are needed, consider a different chart. A dot plot can make it easier to scan for values. Small multiples can be used to group related categories, bringing them closer for comparison while maintaining a larger organizational structure.
