## Guidance

When designing visualizations for tasks that require accurate recall of absolute values, be aware that viewers are likely to systematically misremember them. They tend to overestimate small values and underestimate large values, pulling their memories toward the middle of the range.

## Why

This bias is likely caused by a combination of perceptual and cognitive factors. Human memory tends to compress ranges, a phenomenon related to "central tendency bias," where judgments are pulled toward the average of a set. When recalling a value from a chart, the memory of that value is biased toward the center of the axis or the average of the other values present.

### Core Principle

Human memory for visualized data is not a perfect recording; it is a biased reconstruction influenced by the context of other values and the scale itself.

## When it applies

-   In any visualization where the precise recall of absolute quantitative values is important for decision-making.
-   When users must compare a value seen in a chart to a standard or threshold remembered from elsewhere.
-   In dashboards or reports where users may glance at a chart and make decisions based on their memory of it later.

## Exceptions

-   When the primary task is to judge relative ranks or general trends, and the accuracy of absolute values is not critical.
-   When all data points are clustered in the middle of the range, as the bias is weakest for median values.

## Trade-offs

-   Adding elements to counteract this bias, such as data labels or reference lines, can increase visual clutter.
-   Guiding attention to specific values might cause users to neglect other parts of the data.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **Skewed Decisions:** Decisions made based on recalled chart values are consistently skewed (e.g., risks associated with high values are downplayed, or the significance of low values is inflated).
-   **Misremembering Extremes:** When questioned, users consistently recall extreme values as being less extreme than they actually were.
-   **Failed Validations:** When a user's recalled value is checked against the actual data, there is a systematic pattern of overestimation for low values and underestimation for high values.

## How to Improve

-   **Quick Approach: Add Data Labels.** For the most critical data points (especially highs and lows), add direct text labels. This provides an escape hatch, allowing users to read the exact value instead of relying on their biased perception and memory.
-   **Moderate Approach: Add Reference Lines.** Add static reference lines for key statistical markers like the mean, median, or a critical threshold. These lines provide explicit anchors that can help reduce the bias from viewers mentally "guessing" the center of the range.
-   **Comprehensive Approach: Use Interactive Highlighting.** Allow users to hover over or click on a mark to reveal its exact value and its relationship to other data points (e.g., "This value is 35% above the average"). This encourages active exploration rather than passive, memory-based judgment.
