## Guidance

For tasks requiring the comparison of trends, shapes, or values across a wide span of a time series (a "dispersed" comparison), use a split-space technique like small multiples. Give each time series its own dedicated chart, arranged in a grid.

## Why

Overlaid line charts become cluttered and difficult to read as the number of series increases or as lines cross frequently. This "spaghetti" effect makes it perceptually and cognitively demanding to follow a single series' trajectory. Small multiples eliminate this visual clutter by giving each series its own space. This allows the viewer to easily perceive the shape and trend of each series individually, and then compare them across charts.

### Core Principle

Reduce cognitive load by preventing visual clutter. When tracking individual items is important, separation is more effective than superposition.

## When it applies

- The primary task is a **dispersed comparison**, such as "Which series showed the most volatility?" or "Compare the overall trend of Series A to Series B."
- The number of time series is moderate to high, where overlap would be a significant problem in a shared-space chart.
- You want to encourage comparison of the overall shape and pattern of each series.

## Exceptions

- When the primary task is a precise, local comparison at a single point in time. In this case, a shared-space chart is faster because it does not require eye movement between charts.
- When the absolute magnitude and direct comparison of values are more critical than the individual shapes of the series.

## Trade-offs

- **Prioritizes clarity for individual series:** This approach excels at making each series easy to read and follow.
- **Sacrifices precision of direct comparison:** Comparing the exact values of two series at the same point in time requires the viewer to mentally project from one chart to another, which is less accurate and slower than a direct vertical comparison in a shared-space chart.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Chart Overload:** The chart is a single plot with a tangled mess of overlapping, hard-to-distinguish lines (a "spaghetti chart").
- **Task Mismatch:** Viewers are asked to describe the trend of a single series but cannot visually isolate it from the others.
- **Legend-Heavy:** The chart relies on a large, complex color legend, and viewers must constantly refer back to it to understand the chart.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Highlight a Series.** If you must use a shared-space chart, use interactivity or color to allow a user to select and highlight one or more series, pushing all others to the background (e.g., as thin gray lines). This temporarily simulates the clarity of a split-space chart.

- **Comprehensive Redesign: Convert to Small Multiples.** Re-design the visualization to use small multiples. Break the single chart into a grid of smaller charts, one for each time series. Ensure all small multiples share the same x-axis and y-axis scales to allow for valid comparison.
