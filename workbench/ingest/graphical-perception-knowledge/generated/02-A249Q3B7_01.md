## Guidance

When the primary goal is to identify trends or correlations across the entire duration of a time series, use a single map and place a time-series glyph (like a miniature line or bar chart) at each location.

## Why

This design juxtaposes all temporal information for each location, making it easy to perceive trends and patterns within a single, self-contained glyph. It avoids the high cognitive load of scanning and mentally integrating information across multiple, spatially separated maps.

### Core Principle

Make the most important comparisons the easiest to see. When the task is temporal trend analysis, the visualization's structure should prioritize the juxtaposition of time.

## When it applies

- When the primary task is to understand the temporal evolution of variables at various locations (e.g., "What was the trend in the relationship between literacy and working hours for each state over the last decade?").
- When analyzing data over many time steps.

## Exceptions

- If the time series is very short (e.g., two or three points), the advantage is diminished, and small multiples may be equally effective as the cognitive load of scanning is low.

## Trade-offs

- **Spatial Clutter:** This approach is less effective for understanding spatial patterns at a single moment in time, as that information is distributed across many different glyphs.
- **Occlusion:** The glyphs themselves can create visual clutter and overlap, especially in dense geographic areas, potentially obscuring each other or the underlying map.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Cross-Map Scanning:** Viewers are seen scanning back and forth between multiple maps in a small-multiple layout to try and piece together a temporal trend for a single location.
- **Inability to See Trends:** When asked about the overall trend, users of a small-multiple layout express difficulty, uncertainty, or give incorrect answers.
- **User Feedback:** Users say "I can't follow the trend for Texas" when looking at a series of small maps.

## How to Improve

- **Moderate Redesign: Consolidate to a Single Map.** Re-design the visualization from small multiples to a single base map. At each location, place a glyph (e.g., a mini line chart, bar chart, or sparkline) that encodes the variable's values over the entire time period.

- **Quick Fix: Animate the Small Multiples.** If you must use small multiples, provide an animation that cycles through the time steps. While animation has its own significant drawbacks for analysis, it can sometimes help in perceiving simple trends by presenting the temporal sequence in place.
