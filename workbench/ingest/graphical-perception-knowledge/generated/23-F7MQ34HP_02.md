## Guidance

To help users judge and compare the overall trend or rate of change (slope) of multiple time series, both simple line graphs and small multiples are effective and efficient choices.

## Why

Estimating a trend involves assessing the overall direction and steepness of a line.
- **Simple line graphs** allow for the direct comparison of slopes within the same coordinate system. If the lines do not overlap excessively, it can be intuitive to see which line is steepest.
- **Small multiples** reduce visual clutter, allowing the viewer to clearly see the shape of each individual trend before making a comparison. This can be particularly helpful when there are many series.

The research shows that both approaches are performant, suggesting the choice can be guided by other factors, such as the number of series and the potential for clutter.

### Core Principle

Match the visualization's complexity to the task's perceptual needs. For trend estimation, simpler representations that clearly show line orientation are superior to complex encodings.

## When it applies

- The primary task is to judge the rate of change or overall trend, such as "Which product had the fastest sales growth?" or "Identify all series with a negative trend."
- You are choosing between different types of line chart visualizations.

## Exceptions

- Neither approach is ideal if there are so many series that a simple line graph becomes an unreadable "spaghetti chart" and a small multiples grid becomes too large to view at once. In such cases, analytical or aggregative methods may be needed.

## Trade-offs

- **Simple Line Graph:**
    - **Pro:** Enables direct, in-place comparison of slopes.
    - **Con:** Can become cluttered with many series, obscuring trends.
- **Small Multiples:**
    - **Pro:** Eliminates clutter, making each trend easy to see individually.
    - **Con:** Requires viewers to shift their gaze and make comparisons across different charts, which can be slightly more demanding.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Complex Encodings:** The visualization uses a complex technique (like horizon graphs) that requires cognitive effort to decode, slowing down the primary task of trend estimation.
- **Obscured Lines:** In a simple line graph, the series of interest is so frequently overlapped by other lines that its overall trend is difficult to perceive.
- **Inconsistent Scales:** In small multiples, the y-axes are not synchronized, making visual comparison of slopes invalid and misleading.

## How to Improve

- **If using a complex chart (e.g., braided, horizon):** Simplify the visualization to either a simple line graph or small multiples. This will likely improve the speed and ease of trend estimation.
- **If using a cluttered simple line graph:**
    - **Quick Fix:** Use color or interactivity to highlight one or two series at a time.
    - **Comprehensive Redesign:** Convert the chart to small multiples to eliminate overlap entirely.
- **If using small multiples:** Ensure all individual charts share an identical y-axis scale to make slope comparisons valid.
