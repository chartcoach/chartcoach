## Guidance

To enable accurate comparison and ranking of quantities, encode them using **position along a common scale** (as in a bar or dot chart) rather than using **angle** (as in a pie chart).

## Why

Humans perceive differences in position along a common scale far more accurately and with less bias than they perceive differences in angles. Using position-based charts like bar charts reduces interpretation errors and makes it easier for viewers to correctly rank values and judge their relative magnitudes.

### Core Principle

Humans judge quantities more accurately by comparing positions along a common scale than by comparing angles, areas, or colors.

## When it applies

- When the primary task is to compare or rank the values of different categories.
- When visualizing parts of a whole (composition).
- When precision and low error rates are important for the audience's task.

## Exceptions

- **Single, Simple Proportion:** When showing a single part-to-whole relationship where the value is very close to a familiar fraction like 25%, 50%, or 75%, a pie chart can be intuitive as it leverages our ability to judge right angles.
- **Low Precision Needed:** If the goal is simply to convey a general "part-of-a-whole" message without any need for precise comparison (e.g., "Category A is a small part of the total"), a pie chart may be acceptable.

## Trade-offs

- A bar chart may take up more space (especially vertical space) than a single, compact pie chart.
- A pie chart more strongly emphasizes the "part-of-a-whole" nature of the data. This can be mitigated in a bar chart by adding a "Total" annotation or using a 100% scale.

## Signs of Trouble

- **The "Is it bigger?" Test:** Can you instantly and confidently tell which of two similarly-sized pie slices is larger? If it takes more than a moment of effort, angle is failing as an effective encoding.
- **Ranking Difficulty:** Is it hard to quickly rank all the slices from smallest to largest?
- **Cognitive Strain:** Do you find yourself trying to mentally rotate or move slices to compare them?

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Add Data Labels.** If you must use a pie chart, add direct percentage or value labels to each slice. This provides a "perceptual escape hatch," allowing users to read and compare the numbers instead of judging the flawed angles.

- **Comprehensive Redesign: Switch to a Bar or Dot Chart.** Re-encode the data using a bar chart or dot plot. Sort the bars from largest to smallest to make ranking and comparison even easier and faster. This directly applies the more accurate perceptual task of judging position.
