## Guidance

When asking viewers to judge the overall similarity or correlation between two bar charts, arrange them in a mirrored (center-aligned) layout rather than a standard adjacent (side-by-side) layout.

## Why

The human visual system is exceptionally sensitive to bilateral (mirror) symmetry. Arranging two charts as mirror images of each other leverages this powerful, pre-attentive perceptual mechanism. This allows for a more holistic and efficient comparison of the overall pattern between the two charts, making it easier to assess their similarity (correlation) than when the brain has to compare two identical, translated patterns.

### Core Principle

Align your visualization design with the innate strengths of the human visual system. Using mirror symmetry for comparison tasks taps into a highly efficient perceptual pathway.

## When it applies

-   When the primary task is for a viewer to assess the overall **correlation** or pattern similarity between two datasets.
-   When using **bar charts** arranged as small multiples.
-   When comparing exactly two datasets, as the mirrored layout is inherently pairwise.

## Exceptions

-   **For finding a single largest change (max delta):** Other layouts, like animation or overlaid charts, are more effective for this task. While mirrored layouts were slightly better than adjacent layouts, they were not the best for max delta.
-   **When the metaphor is unfamiliar:** A mirrored layout, where one axis is reversed, can be confusing to audiences who are not expecting it. Standard adjacent layouts are more conventional and may be safer for general audiences.
-   **When comparing more than two datasets:** The bilateral symmetry of a mirrored layout does not scale to three or more datasets.

## Trade-offs

-   **Conventionality vs. Performance:** You may sacrifice the familiarity of a standard side-by-side layout for a modest but significant gain in perceptual performance.
-   **Axis Complexity:** One of the two charts will have a reversed axis, which requires a moment of cognitive re-orientation for the viewer.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **Inaccurate Judgments:** Viewers struggle to correctly identify which of two chart pairs is more "similar" or "correlated."
-   **Slow Comparisons:** Viewers take a long time to compare two side-by-side charts, often scanning back and forth between individual bars.
-   **Over-reliance on Calculation:** Viewers feel the need to estimate individual values to judge similarity, rather than perceiving the pattern holistically.

## How to Improve

-   **Quick Fix: Reduce Noise.** If using a standard adjacent layout, ensure that the means and standard deviations of the data in the charts are comparable, as this was a control in the experiment to isolate the perception of correlation.

-   **Moderate Redesign: Implement a Mirrored Layout.** Modify your side-by-side bar chart layout. Keep the left-hand chart as is (e.g., with the y-axis on the left and bars growing to the right). For the right-hand chart, place its y-axis on the right and have its bars grow to the left, towards the center. This creates the mirrored effect.

-   **Comprehensive Redesign: Offer Layout Choices.** If building an interactive tool, allow users to toggle between adjacent and mirrored layouts. This empowers them to choose the conventional layout for general viewing and switch to the higher-performance mirrored layout for focused correlation analysis.
