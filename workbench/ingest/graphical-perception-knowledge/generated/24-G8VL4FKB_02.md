## Guidance

When encoding quantitative values for comparison, prefer using **length** (e.g., bar charts) over **area** (e.g., bubble charts, treemaps).

## Why

Humans are notoriously bad at accurately judging 2D area. We tend to underestimate the size of larger areas relative to smaller ones, a perceptual bias described by Stevens's Power Law. This means if one bubble has twice the data value of another, viewers will perceive it as less than twice as large. Length judgments are far less biased and therefore lead to more accurate comparisons.

### Core Principle

Choose visual encodings that people can decode most accurately for the task at hand. The accuracy of human perception varies significantly by visual encoding channel.

## When it applies

- When the primary task is to make precise comparisons or rankings of quantitative data.
- When using bubble charts, proportional symbol maps, or treemaps.

## Exceptions

- **General Magnitude Only:** When you only need to show a general sense of magnitude (e.g., 'big', 'medium', 'small') and precise comparison is not important.
- **Constrained Space:** Area-based charts like treemaps can be very space-efficient for showing hierarchical part-to-whole data, but comparing the sizes of the individual (especially non-adjacent) rectangles remains perceptually difficult.

## Trade-offs

- **Space Efficiency:** Bar charts can be less space-efficient than area-based charts like treemaps or bubble charts, which can pack many data points into a compact area.
- **Additional Dimensions:** Bubble charts allow for encoding two other variables on the x and y axes, whereas a simple bar chart encodes only one quantitative value and one categorical value.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Scaling Confusion:** Are you scaling the radius or the area of your circles? Scaling by radius is a common mistake that creates a quadratic error in the perceived area. A circle with 2x the radius has 4x the area. Proper scaling should be `radius = sqrt(value)`.
- **Difficult Comparisons:** Is it hard to tell if 'Bubble A' is 10% or 50% bigger than 'Bubble B' without reading a tooltip? If the judgment isn't easy, the encoding is not effective for comparison.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Add Data Labels.** If you must use an area-based chart, label all areas (bubbles, squares) with their exact values. This allows users to read and compare the numbers, overriding their flawed perceptual judgments.
- **Comprehensive Redesign: Switch to a Length-Based Chart.** Convert the visualization to a bar chart or dot plot. This switches the perceptual task from a difficult area judgment to a more accurate length/position judgment, dramatically improving comparison accuracy.
