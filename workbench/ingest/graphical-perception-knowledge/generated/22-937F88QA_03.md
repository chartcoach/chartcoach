## Guidance

Do not universally ban the rainbow colormap. Instead, evaluate it as a specialized tool that is highly effective for some tasks (quantitative lookups, gradient perception) but demonstrably ineffective for others (geometric pattern recognition).

## Why

The long-standing and valid critique of rainbow colormaps centers on their non-perceptual ordering and creation of false boundaries, which hinders the interpretation of shape and form. However, the very properties that make them bad for patterns (strong, non-monotonic hue variation) make them excellent at combating simultaneous contrast illusions. This leads to higher accuracy in value lookups. The colormap's effectiveness is a direct trade-off based on the user's primary task.

## When it applies

- When choosing a colormap for a continuous quantitative map where users may need to perform multiple, competing analytical tasks.
- When your team or organization has a blanket rule to "never use rainbow," this guideline provides the evidence for a more nuanced, task-based policy.

## Exceptions

- **Never use a rainbow colormap if the primary task is identifying fine-grained geometric shapes or patterns.** The false boundaries it creates will actively mislead the user.
- **Never use a standard rainbow colormap without mitigation if accessibility is a primary concern.** It is not perceivable by users with common red-green color vision deficiencies.

## Trade-offs

- The primary trade-off is stark: choosing rainbow **maximizes accuracy for value lookup** while simultaneously **degrading performance for pattern recognition** and **creating accessibility barriers**. This choice must be made consciously.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Dogmatic Rejection:** A team has a blanket policy to "never use rainbow" and applies it even in situations where the primary task is quantity estimation, for which it is the most accurate tool.
- **Task Mismatch:** A rainbow colormap is being used on a complex map where the primary goal is to understand the shape or structure of the data.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Document the Rationale.** If you choose to use a rainbow colormap for its lookup accuracy, add a note to the visualization explaining *why* it was chosen and acknowledging its known limitations for pattern perception.
- **Moderate Redesign: Provide Task-Based Alternatives.** Instead of a single, static map, provide a user control to switch between two views:
  1.  A **"Read Values"** view using a rainbow colormap.
  2.  A **"See Patterns"** view using a perceptually-uniform diverging colormap.
- **Comprehensive Approach: Implement a Task-Aware System.** Design the visualization system to automatically recommend or switch the colormap based on the analytical task the user initiates. For example, activating a "measure" tool could trigger a rainbow scheme, while activating a "profile" tool could trigger a diverging scheme.
