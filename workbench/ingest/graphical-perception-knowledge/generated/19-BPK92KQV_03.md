## Guidance

When using small multiples for comparing values, arrange them side-by-side (adjacent) rather than one above the other (stacked). An adjacent layout ensures corresponding marks share a common baseline, which is critical for accurate perception.

## Why

Humans are far more accurate at comparing the positions or lengths of objects when they are aligned along a common scale or baseline. When charts are stacked vertically, corresponding data points (e.g., bars for the same category) are horizontally separated and unaligned. This forces the viewer to make a difficult and error-prone judgment of length or position without a shared reference point. An adjacent (side-by-side) layout aligns these reference points, making the comparison task much easier and more accurate.

### Core Principle

Position on a common scale is the most accurate visual encoding for quantitative comparison. Design your layouts to maximize the use of common baselines.

## When it applies

-   When using small multiples (bar charts, line charts, etc.) where the primary goal is to **compare** specific values or judge **correlation** between the charts.
-   Any time you need viewers to make precise judgments between corresponding data points in different charts.

## Exceptions

-   **When comparison is secondary:** If the main goal is simply to show the individual trend or distribution of many different series, and direct comparison is not critical, stacking might be an acceptable use of vertical space.
-   **Extreme space constraints:** If you have very wide charts and extremely limited horizontal space, stacking might be the only option. In this case, acknowledge that you are sacrificing comparative accuracy for layout constraints.

## Trade-offs

-   **Space Usage:** Adjacent layouts consume more horizontal space than stacked layouts. Stacked layouts are more space-efficient vertically. You are trading screen real estate for perceptual accuracy.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **Unaligned Baselines:** In your small multiples layout, the bars or points you want users to compare do not start at the same position.
-   **Viewer Complaints:** Users say it's "hard to tell the difference" or that they have to "use a ruler" to compare your charts.
-   **Slow Task Performance:** It takes users a long time to answer simple comparison questions about your charts.

## How to Improve

-   **Quick Fix: Re-orient the Layout.** Change your layout from `flex-direction: column` to `flex-direction: row`. Arrange the small multiples horizontally (side-by-side) instead of vertically (stacked). This simple change will immediately align the baselines and improve comparability.

-   **Moderate Approach: Add Guiding Lines.** If you absolutely must use a stacked layout due to space constraints, add interactive guidelines. For example, on hover, draw a faint horizontal line across all charts at the level of the hovered mark to provide a temporary common scale.

-   **Comprehensive Approach: Rethink the Chart Type.** If comparing many series is the goal, a stacked small multiple layout may be the wrong approach entirely. Consider an alternative visualization like a dot plot, a slope chart, or an overlaid line chart that places all data on a common scale from the outset.
