## Guidance

When visualizing a long, sorted list of values (e.g., more than can fit on one screen), consider using a wrapped bar chart layout. This technique breaks the single list into multiple columns, allowing all items to be visible simultaneously without scrolling.

## Why

Wrapped bar charts maintain the use of length as the primary visual encoding, which is highly accurate. By eliminating the need for scrolling, they significantly reduce task completion time compared to a standard, long bar chart. This offers a practical compromise between the high accuracy of a scrolled bar chart and the speed of a non-interactive chart.

### Core Principle

Make the most important comparisons the easiest to see. By placing all data on one screen, wrapped bars facilitate at-a-glance distributional awareness, even if it makes fine-grained comparisons across columns slightly harder.

## When it applies

- When you need to display a ranked list that is too long to fit on a single screen.
- When both reasonable accuracy and fast completion time are important.
- When the primary task is to understand the overall distribution, find a specific item's rank, or make general comparisons.

## Exceptions

- If precise comparison between any two bars in the entire list is the absolute top priority, a single scrolled bar chart is superior. Despite being slower, it provides a single common baseline for all items, which yields the highest possible accuracy.
- For extremely large datasets (thousands of items), other density-based visualizations may become more appropriate than any bar chart variant.

## Trade-offs

- **Accuracy vs. Speed:** You sacrifice a small amount of comparison accuracy (for items in different columns) for a large gain in speed and the ability to see the entire distribution at once.
- **Space:** Comparison across columns is more difficult because the bars do not share a common baseline.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Endless Scrolling:** Users have to scroll extensively to find an item or compare items at opposite ends of a list.
- **Lost Context:** Users cannot see the overall shape of the distribution because only a fraction of it is visible at any time.

## How to Improve

- **Moderate Redesign: Wrap the Bars.** Instead of a single long, scrolling list of bars, break the list into several columns that fit on one screen. Each column gets its own baseline. This is the core of the wrapped bar chart approach.
