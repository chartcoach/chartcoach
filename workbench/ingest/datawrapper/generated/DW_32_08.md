## Guidance

When text is placed over other visual elements like gridlines, data lines, or colored map areas, add a subtle outline (or "halo") to the text that matches the chart's background color.

## Why

The outline creates a small, clean buffer around the letters, effectively "carving out" space and preventing background elements from visually interfering with the letterforms. This simple technique dramatically improves legibility and creates a cleaner, more polished and professional look without adding visual clutter.

## When it applies

- Whenever text (e.g., annotations, map labels, direct data labels) sits on top of any non-background element, especially thin lines like gridlines.

## Exceptions

- If text is placed entirely in empty white space with no elements passing through or behind it, an outline is unnecessary.

## Trade-offs

- A very thick or high-contrast outline can be distracting in its own right and create a "sticker" effect. The effect should be subtle—just enough to separate the text from what's behind it.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Vibrating Text:** Gridlines running through your labels make them hard to read and appear to "vibrate" against the text.
- **Lost Letters:** Parts of letters become illegible or get lost against a similarly-colored background element or a busy texture.
- **Messy Intersections:** The point where a data line or gridline crosses through a text label looks messy and unintentional.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Enable the Outline.** In your charting tool, find the settings for text annotations or labels and look for an "outline," "stroke," or "halo" option. Enable it and set its color to your chart's background color (e.g., white) and its thickness to 1-2 pixels.
- **Alternative: Manual Placement.** If your tool doesn't support halos, carefully place your labels so they fall in empty spaces between gridlines or other elements. This is more time-consuming and less flexible.
