## Guidance

Whenever feasible, place labels directly next to the lines, bars, or areas they describe, instead of using a separate color legend.

## Why

Forcing a reader to look back and forth between a legend and the data itself increases cognitive load. This visual search task, however brief, slows down interpretation and consumes mental energy that could be spent understanding the data's message. Direct labels make the chart self-contained and immediately decodable.

### Core Principle

Minimize the cognitive work required to decode the visualization by co-locating labels with the data they describe.

## When it applies

-   For charts with a small number of categories or series (typically fewer than 5-6), especially line charts, pie charts, and some bar charts.

## Exceptions

-   May not be feasible in very dense charts with many overlapping series (e.g., a "spaghetti" line chart). In such cases, a separate legend, ideally an interactive one that highlights a series on hover, might be a better choice to avoid clutter.
-   When labels are too long to fit neatly within the chart area.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **The Back-and-Forth:** You find yourself (or watch others) repeatedly glancing between the legend and the main chart area.
-   **Color Matching:** The primary task becomes "match the blue in the legend to the blue line on the chart."

## How to Improve

-   **Moderate Approach:** Remove the chart's default legend. Add text labels at the end of each line, or inside/next to each major bar or pie slice. If possible, match the label color to the data series color for an even stronger visual connection. Many modern charting tools have a "direct labeling" option.
