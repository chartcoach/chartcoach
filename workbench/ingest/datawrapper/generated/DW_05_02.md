## Guidance

To provide a more detailed, truthful, and less interpreted view of the data, use an unclassed (continuous) color scale.

## Why

Unclassed scales represent data with higher fidelity, as they don't group values into artificial bins. This approach reveals subtle variations, outliers, and the nature of transitions between areas (e.g., whether a change is smooth or abrupt). It is the "most exact representation of the data model possible," empowering readers to do their own exploration and answer more nuanced questions, such as "How does my specific region compare to its immediate neighbors?" rather than just "Which broad category is my region in?".

## When it applies

- When the goal is to show the overall pattern or distribution of the data in its full complexity.
- When you want to enable reader-driven exploration and discovery of local patterns or outliers.
- When you want to present the data with maximum transparency and minimum designer interpretation.
- When creating interactive maps where users can hover to get exact values via tooltips, mitigating the difficulty of reading values from a gradient.

## Exceptions

- When the primary goal is to communicate a simple, specific message based on statistical thresholds (e.g., above/below average).
- In static formats (like print) where accurate value look-up is critical and tooltips are not available.

## Trade-offs

- It can be perceptually more difficult for a viewer to look up a precise value or range by matching a color to a continuous legend.
- The map may appear more complex, potentially increasing cognitive load for viewers looking for a simple takeaway.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Banding Artifacts:** A classed map shows harsh, blocky transitions between regions that are actually very similar in value, creating a misleading visual boundary.
- **Hidden Outliers:** An important outlier is visually indistinguishable from other high values because they have all been grouped into the same top-level class (e.g., "50%+").

## How to Improve

- **Switch to a Continuous Scale:** Change the map's color scale from "stepped" to "continuous" in your visualization tool.
- **Choose a Good Interpolation:** Ensure the color gradient transitions smoothly and perceptually. A linear interpolation in a perceptually uniform color space (like L*a*b*) is often a good choice.
