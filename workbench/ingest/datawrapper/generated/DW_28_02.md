## Guidance

When your data has a two-level hierarchy (e.g., main categories and subcategories), assign a distinct hue to each main category. Then, use different shades or tints of that hue for its respective subcategories.

## Why

This technique leverages the Gestalt principle of similarity. The shared hue visually connects all subcategories to their parent, creating clear groups that are processed pre-attentively. The variation in shade then allows for distinction *within* the group. This creates an intuitive visual hierarchy that is easier to decode than a flat palette of many different colors.

## When it applies

- When you need to show both membership in a larger group and distinction within that group.
- For stacked bar charts, grouped bar charts, or treemaps with a clear categorical hierarchy.

## Exceptions

- **Too Many Subcategories:** If you have more than 3-4 subcategories per main category, the shades may become too difficult to tell apart. In this case, other encoding methods like faceting (small multiples) might be better.
- **Deep Hierarchies:** This technique works best for a simple two-level hierarchy. For deeper trees, other visualization types (like a treemap or sunburst) might be necessary.

## Trade-offs

- This approach reduces the total number of unique hues you can use, as you are "reserving" shades of one hue for a single group.
- It requires careful color selection to ensure that the shades are distinct enough from each other, and that a dark shade of one hue isn't confused with a light shade of another.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Flat Palette for Hierarchy:** Your chart uses a disparate collection of colors for items that belong to logical groups, forcing the viewer to mentally reconstruct the hierarchy.
- **Ambiguous Shades:** The dark shade of your "blue" category looks very similar to the light shade of your "purple" category, causing confusion.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Manually Group Your Colors.** Identify your main groups. Assign a distinct hue to each. Then, for each group's members, manually select lighter and darker versions of the parent hue.
- **Comprehensive Approach: Use a Hierarchical Color Tool.** Use a tool or script that can generate hierarchical palettes. This involves defining a parent color and then programmatically generating perceptually distinct steps away from it for the child elements.
