## Guidance

Whenever possible, place labels directly on or next to the data they describe (e.g., on lines, bars, or pie slices) and remove the separate color legend.

## Why

A separate color legend forces the reader to perform a mentally taxing lookup task: find the category in the legend, remember its color, then scan the chart to find that color. This process is slow, inefficient, and a significant barrier for colorblind users who may struggle to match the colors. Direct labels eliminate this work entirely, making the chart faster and easier to understand for everyone.

### Core Principle

Reduce cognitive load by co-locating labels and the data they describe. Don't make your reader work to connect the dots.

## When it applies

-   In most charts with a manageable number of categorical series, particularly line charts, area charts, pie charts, and simple bar charts.
-   When you want to make a chart as easy to read as possible at a glance.

## Exceptions

-   In highly dense charts where there is no clear space for labels without them overlapping (e.g., a scatterplot with thousands of points from multiple categories). In these cases, interactive tooltips are a better solution.
-   On complex maps, where direct labeling can quickly lead to clutter and illegibility.

## Trade-offs

-   Placing labels effectively can be challenging. It may require manual adjustment or sophisticated labeling algorithms to prevent labels from overlapping with each other or with important data points.
-   Direct labels can sometimes make a chart feel more cluttered than a clean chart area with a separate legend, though the usability gain usually outweighs this.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **The Back-and-Forth:** You or other viewers have to repeatedly look between the chart and the legend to understand what's being shown.
-   **Legend Ambiguity:** The legend contains two colors that are very similar, making the matching task difficult or impossible for some users.
-   **Orphaned Data:** The chart contains a legend, but it's positioned so far from the data that the connection is weak.

## How to Improve

-   **Quick approach:** In a line chart, add text labels at the end of each line. In a pie chart, add labels next to or inside each slice.
-   **Moderate approach:** Carefully design your chart layout to create space for direct labels. You may need to adjust the axis range or the chart's aspect ratio.
-   **Comprehensive approach:** Use a tool (like Datawrapper) that has built-in, sophisticated algorithms for automated direct labeling, and make it your default method for annotating categorical charts.
