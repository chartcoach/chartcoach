## Guidance

All text and important visual marks should have a high enough contrast ratio against their background to be easily readable. The W3C recommendation is a minimum ratio of **4.5:1 for normal text** and **3:1 for large text**.

## Why

Low contrast makes text and visuals difficult or impossible to read, especially for people with low vision or in non-ideal viewing conditions (e.g., screen glare, low-light). Ensuring high contrast is a fundamental requirement for creating accessible and effective visualizations.

### Core Principle

Design for legibility and accessibility. Information that cannot be clearly seen is useless.

## When it applies

- Any text on a visualization: titles, labels, annotations, legends, source lines.
- Any visual marks (lines, dots) that need to be distinguished from the background.
- This is a universal guideline that applies to all charts, mediums, and audiences.

## Exceptions

- **Logos & Incidental Text:** Text that is part of a logo or is purely decorative and conveys no information is exempt from this requirement. However, this should be used with extreme caution in data visualization.

## Trade-offs

- Extremely high contrast (e.g., pure black `#000000` on pure white `#FFFFFF`) can sometimes cause eye strain for some readers. Using an off-black on an off-white is often more comfortable while still meeting contrast requirements.
- Aesthetic goals might favor subtle, low-contrast palettes, but these must be sacrificed for the sake of readability and accessibility.

## Signs of Trouble

- **The Squint Test:** You have to squint or lean in to read text on the chart.
- **Disappearing Text:** Light grey text on a white background, or medium-colored text on a similarly-colored background, becomes nearly invisible.
- **Fails a Check:** A contrast checking tool reports a ratio below the recommended 4.5:1 threshold for your text and background colors.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Darken the Text.** The easiest way to increase contrast is usually to darken the foreground color (text) or lighten the background color. Change light grey text to a darker grey or black.
- **Moderate Redesign: Use a Contrast Checker.** Use a web-based or browser-integrated tool (like WebAIM's) to check all your color pairs. Adjust colors until they meet the minimum WCAG ratios.
- **Comprehensive Approach: Build an Accessible Palette.** Design your entire color palette from the start with contrast in mind. Define specific text colors, background colors, and accent colors that are all pre-checked for accessibility compliance. Avoid placing text on top of complex image or gradient backgrounds without a solid-colored "scrim" behind the text.
