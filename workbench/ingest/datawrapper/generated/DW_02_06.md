## Guidance

Always include a clearly visible source for your data, typically in the footer of the visualization.

## Why

Citing your source is crucial for establishing credibility and trust with your audience. It demonstrates transparency about where your information comes from and allows interested readers to verify your data or explore it further for their own purposes. It is a fundamental and non-negotiable practice of ethical data communication. A source acts as proof and shows respect for the work of the original data collectors.

## When it applies

-   Always. Every chart based on data not originally generated by the author for that specific purpose should cite its source.

## Exceptions

-   None for public-facing or professional work. The only plausible exception is for purely internal, informal sketches where the source is implicitly and universally understood by the immediate audience (e.g., a quick chart of team data shared within that team).

## Signs of Trouble

-   **"Where did this come from?":** The first question a skeptical viewer asks is about the data's origin.
-   **Missing Footer:** The chart has a title, axes, and a main body, but no information in the lower-left or lower-right corner.

## How to Improve

-   **Quick Fix:** Add a line of text at the bottom of your chart that says "Source:" followed by the name of the organization, person, or publication that provided the data (e.g., "Source: U.S. Census Bureau, 2020 American Community Survey"). If possible, make the source name a hyperlink to the original data table or report.
