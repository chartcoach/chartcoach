## Guidance

Ensure that the colors in a categorical palette are perceptually distinct, meaning an average viewer can easily and unambiguously tell them apart, even when they are used for small chart elements.

## Why

The entire purpose of using color to encode categories is to allow viewers to differentiate them. If colors are too similar, the encoding fails. Viewers will struggle to match the data marks to the legend, leading to misinterpretation, frustration, and a higher cognitive load. This problem is magnified for small marks (like dots in a scatterplot) and for viewers with color vision deficiencies.

### Core Principle

Each color in a categorical palette should be an unambiguous representative of its category. If there's any doubt about which category a color represents, the palette is not effective.

## When it applies

-   Any time you use color to represent discrete categories (e.g., parties, countries, product types).
-   Especially critical when chart elements are small (dots, thin lines) or when there are many categories.

## Exceptions

-   When using different shades of a single hue to represent an ordered sequence or magnitude. In that case, the colors are *meant* to be related, not distinct in the same way as a categorical palette.

## Trade-offs

-   A highly discriminable palette may not always be the most aesthetically harmonious. Prioritizing clarity over pure aesthetics is usually the correct trade-off for data visualization.
-   Finding a large number of truly distinct colors (> 8-10) is extremely difficult.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **The Squint Test:** If you squint at your chart, do different colored categories blur into a single visual group?
-   **Subtle Neighbors:** Does your palette contain two very similar colors (e.g., a light green and a lime green, or a magenta and a purple)?
-   **Colorblind Simulation Failure:** When you check your chart with a colorblindness simulator (like in Viz Palette), do two or more colors become nearly identical?
-   **Back-and-Forth:** Do you find yourself repeatedly glancing between a chart element and the legend to be sure which category it is?

## How to Improve

-   **Quick Fix: Use a Pre-built, Tested Palette.** Instead of picking colors by hand, use a color palette designed for data visualization, such as those in Datawrapper, ColorBrewer, or Tableau. These are built to maximize perceptual distance.
-   **Moderate Approach: Check and Tweak.** Use a tool like Viz Palette to test your current color choices. If two colors are too similar, adjust the hue, saturation, or lightness of one of them until they are clearly distinct, both for standard vision and in simulations.
-   **Comprehensive Approach: Encode with Something Else.** If you have too many categories for color to be effective (>10), don't force it. Recode the data using a different visual channel. For example, use small multiples (a grid of charts) or use direct labels and a neutral color instead of relying on a complex color legend.
