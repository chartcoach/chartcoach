## Guidance

For pie slices that are too small to comfortably contain their data label, place the label outside the chart and connect it to its slice with a thin leader line.

## Why

Pie charts, especially those with several slices, will often have thin "sliver" slices. There is simply not enough physical space inside these slivers to place a readable label. Attempting to do so results in tiny, illegible font sizes, text overflowing the slice boundaries, or poor color contrast. External labeling solves this problem by moving the text to an open area, allowing it to be displayed at a legible size.

## When it applies

- You are creating a pie chart.
- One or more slices are too small to fit their label.
- You have labels that are too long to fit, regardless of the slice size.

## Exceptions

If you have so many small slices that the external labels become a cluttered "spiderweb" of lines, this is a sign that the pie chart is the wrong chart type. In this case, the solution is not better labeling but choosing a different chart (like a bar chart) or grouping the small slices.

## Trade-offs

- **Prioritizes:** Legibility and clarity of all labels.
- **Sacrifices:** The clean, self-contained look of having all labels inside the pie. Leader lines can add some visual complexity.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Unreadable Labels:** Labels inside slices are tiny, compressed, or overlap the slice borders.
- **Clashing Text:** The color of the label text does not have sufficient contrast with the color of the slice behind it, making it hard to read (an accessibility failure).
- **Long Labels Truncated:** A long category name is cut off or awkwardly wrapped to fit inside a slice.

## How to Improve

- **Use External Labels with Leader Lines:** In your charting tool, choose the option to place labels outside the pie. Position the text in a clear space and ensure the line connecting it to the slice is unambiguous. This is particularly effective for small slices and long category names.
