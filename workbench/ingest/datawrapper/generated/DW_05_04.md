## Guidance

Before finalizing a choropleth map or heatmap, always start your design and exploration process by visualizing the data with an unclassed (continuous) color scale.

## Why

An unclassed map acts as a diagnostic tool, revealing the true, underlying distribution of your data without any simplification. It shows you where the clusters, gaps, and outliers are. By examining this raw view first, you can make a conscious and informed decision about whether classification is necessary and, if so, where to place the class breaks to best preserve or highlight important features. This workflow prevents you from accidentally obscuring key insights by relying on arbitrary or default classification schemes that may not be appropriate for your specific dataset.

### Core Principle

Understand your data's distribution before you simplify it.

## When it applies

- When beginning the design process for any visualization that encodes a continuous quantitative value with color, such as a choropleth map or a heatmap.
- When you are unsure what classification method (e.g., quantiles, equal interval, natural breaks) is most appropriate for your data.

## How to Improve

- **Default to Continuous:** In your visualization tool, set the color scale to "continuous" as your first step.
- **Analyze the Distribution:** Look at the unclassed map alongside a histogram of the data. Identify if the data is skewed, if there are natural clusters, or if there are significant outliers.
- **Make an Informed Choice:** Based on this analysis, decide if a classed map is needed to tell your story. If so, use your understanding of the distribution to choose a classification method (or set custom breaks) that is purposeful and defensible. If not, stick with the nuanced unclassed view.
