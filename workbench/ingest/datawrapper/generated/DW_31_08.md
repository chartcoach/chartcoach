## Guidance

In a stacked column or bar chart, arrange the segments so that the most important category for comparison is placed on the baseline (the bottom of a column or the start of a bar).

## Why

The segment resting on the baseline is the only one whose size can be accurately judged and compared across the different stacks. The other segments are "floating," making it perceptually difficult to compare their lengths. By strategically placing the most important segment on the baseline, you are making the most critical comparison for your story the easiest and most accurate one for your audience to perform. Using a highlight color on this segment further directs viewer attention.

### Core Principle
Design the chart to answer the most important question most easily. Guide the viewer's eye and leverage perceptual strengths.

## When it applies
- When creating any stacked chart (column, bar, area).
- When one specific component's trend or comparison is the primary message of the chart.

## Exceptions
- If there is a natural or conventional ordering of the segments that would be confusing to change (e.g., ordering by age group, or a 'strongly agree' to 'strongly disagree' Likert scale). In this case, the conventional order may take precedence over placing the most important segment at the bottom.

## Trade-offs
- You can only give one segment the "prime real estate" on the baseline. Other segments will remain difficult to compare.

## Signs of Trouble
- **The Floating Story:** The chart's title or main insight is about a segment that isn't on the baseline.
- **Re-ordering Confusion:** A viewer might be confused if the legend order doesn't match the stacking order, so ensure they are consistent.
- **Mismatched Focus:** The colors or annotations highlight a middle segment, while the easiest-to-read segment at the bottom is unrelated to the main point.

## How to Improve
- **Quick Fix: Use a Stronger Color.** If you can't change the order, use a bold, saturated highlight color on the most important segment to help it stand out, and de-saturate the other colors.
- **Moderate Redesign: Reorder the Segments.** Change the stacking order of your data series to place the most important one at the bottom (for columns) or beginning (for bars). Update the legend to match the new stacking order.
- **Comprehensive Redesign: Use a Different Chart.** If you need to compare two or more segments accurately, a stacked chart is the wrong choice. Switch to a grouped bar chart or a line chart.
