## Guidance

If the primary goal is to compare values between categories, choose a chart type that uses position along a common scale (e.g., bar charts, dot plots) instead of one that uses color, angle, or area (e.g., stacked bars, pie charts).

## Why

Humans are significantly better at accurately judging and comparing lengths from a common baseline than they are at comparing angles, areas, or color intensities. By switching from a chart like a pie or stacked bar to a standard bar chart, you are changing the encoding from a less effective one to the most effective one for comparison tasks, leading to more accurate interpretation. This change often has the side effect of reducing the need for multiple colors.

### Core Principle
Encode your most important comparisons using the most accurate perceptual channels. For quantitative comparison, that channel is position along a common scale.

## When it applies

- When the primary task for the viewer is to compare the values of different categories.
- When you are using a stacked bar/area chart and need to compare components that are not on the baseline.
- When you are using a pie chart to ask viewers to compare slice sizes.

## Exceptions

- When the primary goal is strictly to show a part-to-whole relationship and the exact comparisons are less important, a stacked bar or pie chart might be used (though a bar chart is often still better).
- When a table is more appropriate for precise lookups.

## Trade-offs

- **Gained:** Greatly increased accuracy of comparisons; often a cleaner and less ambiguous chart.
- **Sacrificed:** The explicit visual representation of a "whole" that a pie chart or 100% stacked bar provides. A grouped bar chart may also take up more space than a stacked bar chart.

## Signs of Trouble

- **The Stacked Comparison Struggle:** In a stacked bar chart, you find yourself trying to compare the size of two segments in the middle of different stacks. This is a nearly impossible perceptual task.
- **The "Is it bigger?" Pie Chart Debate:** Viewers are arguing over whether the blue slice is slightly bigger than the red slice in a pie chart.

## How to Improve

- **Comprehensive Redesign: Switch Chart Types.**
  - **If using a Pie Chart:** Change it to a bar chart, sorted by value.
  - **If using a Stacked Bar Chart:** Change it to a grouped bar chart so each category gets its own bar starting from zero. Alternatively, use a dot plot.
  - This fundamentally changes the chart's encoding to prioritize accurate comparisons.
