## Guidance

Employ the color grey for background elements, secondary data series, or unselected items to make your primary, color-coded data points stand out more effectively.

## Why

Grey acts as a neutral backdrop that reduces visual noise and directs the viewer's attention to the most important parts of the story, which should be rendered in more saturated "highlight" colors. This creates a clear visual hierarchy, telling the reader what to look at first.

### Core Principle

Create visual hierarchy through the selective use of color and contrast. Not all data is equally important; use color to guide the eye to the focal point of the visualization.

## When it applies

- When highlighting a specific category or data point in a chart.
- To show general context data (e.g., other countries' trend lines while focusing on one).
- In interactive charts, to show what is currently not selected or hovered over.
- For non-data elements like grid lines, axis lines, and annotations that should be present but not dominant.

## Exceptions

- On dark-themed visualizations, a dark or light grey serves the same purpose. The principle is to use a low-saturation, neutral color for de-emphasis relative to the background.

## Trade-offs

- Overusing grey without a strong highlight color can make a chart feel dull or washed out. The technique relies on the contrast between the neutral grey and a vibrant highlight.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Lack of Focus:** All elements in the chart have equal visual weight, making it hard to know where to look first.
- **Visual Noise:** Grid lines, axis labels, or secondary data series are as visually prominent as the main data, creating clutter and distraction.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Tone Down Gridlines.** Change your gridlines and axis lines from black to a light grey. This is often the single biggest improvement you can make to reduce clutter.
- **Moderate Redesign: Create a Highlight.** Pick the most important category or data point in your chart. Color it with a strong, saturated hue and change all other categories to shades of grey.
- **Comprehensive Approach: Use "Warm" or "Cool" Greys.** To avoid a cold, clinical feel, use greys with a slight tint of color. A warm grey (with a hint of red/orange/yellow) can feel more inviting. A very light, desaturated color (like a pale yellow or blue) can also serve as a sophisticated alternative to pure grey.
