## Guidance

Do not use color as the only visual means of distinguishing categories. Supplement or replace color with other indicators like shapes, patterns, or line styles (dashes).

## Why

Relying solely on color to convey information excludes the 8% of men and 0.5% of women with some form of color vision deficiency (CVD). By providing a redundant visual cue—such as making one line solid and another dashed—you ensure that all users can interpret your chart, including those with CVD and those who view it on a monochrome screen or printout.

### Core Principle
Design for accessibility by providing multiple ways to interpret information. Information conveyed by color should also be available through other visual means.

## When it applies

- Always, but especially when creating visualizations for a broad, public audience.
- When you have a small number of categories to distinguish (2-4).
- When you anticipate the chart may be printed in black and white.

## Exceptions

- This technique has very low cardinality; it is not scalable. Do not try to use 10 different patterns or 10 different shapes, as they will become impossible to tell apart. It is best used for a handful of categories.
- Overuse of heavy patterns can create significant visual noise ("chart junk") and moiré patterns that are distracting.

## Trade-offs

- **Gained:** Greatly improved accessibility; robustness to grayscale printing.
- **Sacrificed:** Can add visual clutter if not designed with subtlety; limited to a small number of categories.

## Signs of Trouble

- **The Grayscale Test Fail:** When you print your chart in black and white (or use a grayscale simulator), two or more categories become indistinguishable.
- **Colorblind Simulator Fail:** When viewed through a colorblindness simulator (like for deuteranopia or protanopia), key distinctions in the chart disappear.

## How to Improve

- **Moderate Redesign: Add a Redundant Encoding.**
  - **For Line Charts:** In addition to color, vary the line style (e.g., solid, dashed, dotted).
  - **For Scatter Plots:** In addition to color, vary the symbol shape (e.g., circle, square, triangle, cross).
  - **For Bar/Area Charts:** In addition to color, overlay a subtle, low-contrast pattern or texture.
