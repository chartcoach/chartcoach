## Guidance

When the main goal is to allow readers to find specific information or individual values, use a table rather than a chart.

## Why

Tables structure data in a way that facilitates direct lookup. Unlike charts, which are optimized for showing patterns and relationships, tables present data in a simple row-and-column format that makes it easy to scan for and retrieve precise values, such as the data for a specific city, company, or date.

### Core Principle

Design for the primary task. If the task is lookup, the design should optimize for finding specific items quickly and accurately.

## When it applies

- When your audience needs to find data points that apply specifically to them (e.g., their location, age group, industry).
- When the data is structured text (names, teams, states) rather than purely quantitative.
- When providing a reference or a directory-like resource.
- When the audience needs to know the exact value of a piece of data.

## Exceptions

- If the table becomes excessively long and the lookup task can be better served by an interactive tool (like a search bar that returns a single result), a full table might not be necessary.
- If identifying an overall pattern is more important than finding a single value, a chart is more appropriate.

## Trade-offs

- Tables are poor at revealing overall trends, patterns, outliers, or the "shape" of the data. By choosing a table, you prioritize precision and lookup over high-level visual discovery.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Constant Questions:** Readers are asking for specific numbers that are not easily read from your chart.
- **"Where am I?":** Users have difficulty finding the data point relevant to their own context in a visualization.
- **Text Clutter:** You have a long paragraph of text that is just listing structured data that would be easier to read in a table format.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix:** If you have a chart, add a small summary table with key values or provide a link to the full dataset in a table format.
- **Comprehensive Approach:** If the primary task is lookup, replace the chart entirely with a well-formatted table. Ensure columns are clearly labeled and consider adding search or sort functionality to aid lookup.
