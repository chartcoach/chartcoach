## Guidance

Do not use colors that are stereotypically associated with racial or ethnic groups. This includes avoiding black for Black people, white for white people, yellow for Asian people, and red for Indigenous peoples.

## Why

These color associations are rooted in outdated and often racist historical classification systems. Using them, even unintentionally, can perpetuate harmful stereotypes, reduce complex identities to a single physical trait, and make readers feel disrespected or misrepresented. The goal is to represent people respectfully, not to reinforce historical biases.

### Core Principle

Visual choices should actively work to dismantle stereotypes, not reinforce them. Representing human groups requires a higher degree of care to ensure the visualization is inclusive and respectful.

## When it applies

- When creating any visualization that categorizes data by race or ethnicity, including bar charts, line charts, pie charts, and maps.

## Exceptions

None. While there might be rare, complex narrative reasons to subvert this, the risk of causing harm far outweighs potential benefits. It is safer and more respectful to always avoid this practice.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Direct Mapping:** Your color legend shows a direct and obvious link between a color and a racial stereotype (e.g., a yellow swatch for "Asian").
- **Historical Echoes:** The color choices resemble those found in colonial-era or pseudoscientific "race maps."

## How to Improve

- **Comprehensive approach:** Select a categorical color palette where the colors are abstract and have no connection to physical appearance or historical stereotypes. Use tools like `Viz Palette` or `ColorBrewer` to find a balanced, non-referential set of colors. Assign these colors arbitrarily to the groups in your data.
