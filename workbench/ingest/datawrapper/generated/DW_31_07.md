## Guidance

If your category labels are long, use a horizontal bar chart (or stacked bar chart) instead of a vertical column chart.

## Why

We read text horizontally from left to right. Column charts, which arrange categories along a horizontal axis, leave very little space for labels. This forces designers to resort to poor solutions like rotating text vertically, angling text, or truncating labels, all of which severely harm readability and increase cognitive load. Horizontal bar charts arrange categories along the vertical axis, providing generous space for long, fully-formed, and easily readable labels.

### Core Principle
Prioritize readability. Orient your chart in a way that allows labels to be read in their natural, horizontal orientation.

## When it applies
- Any time your categorical data has labels that are more than one or two short words long.
- When designing charts for an international audience, as translated labels may be longer than the original English.

## Exceptions
- If the categories have a natural, fixed horizontal order (like a time series) that would be confusing to show vertically. In this case, you may need to reconsider using a column chart or find ways to shorten the labels.

## Trade-offs
- The chart's orientation changes from vertical to horizontal, which may be less conventional for certain data types (e.g., showing population by country). However, the gain in label readability almost always outweighs this minor break in convention.

## Signs of Trouble
- **Rotated Text:** Labels on the x-axis are turned 90 degrees.
- **Angled Text:** Labels are diagonal, making the reader tilt their head.
- **Truncated Text:** Labels are cut off with ellipses (e.g., "Manufacturing...").
- **Overlapping Text:** Labels run into each other.
- **Tiny Font:** The font size for labels has been shrunk to an unreadable size to make them fit.

## How to Improve
- **Quick Fix: Abbreviate Labels.** Manually shorten the labels, but be careful not to introduce ambiguity. Provide the full labels in a tooltip or a separate table.
- **Moderate Redesign: Switch to a Bar Chart.** The simplest and most effective solution is to change the chart type from a column chart to a horizontal bar chart.
- **Comprehensive Redesign: Re-think the Grouping.** If labels are long because they are descriptive sentences, perhaps the categories themselves can be defined more concisely.
