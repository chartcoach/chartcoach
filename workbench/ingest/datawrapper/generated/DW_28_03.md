## Guidance

To draw attention to a specific data point, category, or region, render it in a distinct, often saturated, color. Render all other 'contextual' data in a single, muted color, such as gray.

## Why

This approach minimizes cognitive load by telling the viewer exactly where to look. The highlighted element stands out immediately due to its unique color (a "preattentive attribute"), while the grayed-out elements provide necessary context for comparison without competing for attention. It's a powerful way to guide a narrative and make a clear point.

## When it applies

- When your primary goal is to tell a story about one specific part of the data in relation to the whole.
- In presentations or reports where you want to control the narrative and focus the audience's attention.
- When annotating a specific point or trend in a chart.

## Exceptions

- **Exploratory Tools:** If the goal is to encourage free exploration and comparison between all categories equally (e.g., in an interactive dashboard for analysts), this technique would be counterproductive as it imposes a specific narrative.
- **Multiple Highlights:** Highlighting more than 2-3 categories with different colors dilutes the effect and can become as confusing as using no highlighting at all.

## Trade-offs

- You sacrifice the ability to easily distinguish between the non-highlighted categories. They blend into the background, which is the point, but means detailed comparisons among them become impossible.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Competing Colors:** Your chart tries to make a point about one region's performance, but all regions are colored with different, bright hues, forcing the reader to search for the one you're talking about.
- **Everything Is Important:** The chart uses multiple bright, "urgent" colors, creating a visually loud and stressful experience where nothing stands out because everything is trying to.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Pick a Focus, Mute the Rest.** Decide what the single most important story in your chart is. Give that data series a strong color. Change every other data series to a light or medium gray.
- **Moderate Approach: Add an Annotation.** After highlighting a series in color, add a text annotation directly on the chart that explains *why* it's highlighted. This connects the visual emphasis to the narrative insight.
