## Guidance

For charts with a few distinct categories, embed the color legend directly into the title, subtitle, or descriptive text by coloring the words that name the categories.

## Why

This technique seamlessly integrates the legend into the chart's narrative, reducing visual clutter by eliminating a separate legend element. It teaches the viewer the color meanings as they are reading the chart's primary message, making the decoding process feel more natural and less like a separate step.

### Core Principle

Combine text and graphics to create a single, cohesive explanatory unit.

## When it applies

- Best suited for charts with a low number of categories (typically 2 to 4) that can be naturally woven into a sentence.
- Highly effective in journalistic or narrative visualizations where the title and description play a key role in framing the data.

## Exceptions

- When there are too many categories to list gracefully in a sentence.
- When the category names are long or complex, making the title unwieldy.
- If your brand or style guide forbids using blue for non-hyperlink text, avoid this technique for blue categories, as users may perceive it as a link.

## Trade-offs

- **Potential for Redundancy:** The source article suggests it can still be a good idea to keep a traditional legend as well, to serve as a reminder for viewers who start reading in the middle of the chart.
- **Accessibility:** Text color must have sufficient contrast. Additionally, screen readers will not announce the color, so the text must make sense on its own without the color information.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Confused Clicks:** Users try to click on blue-colored text in the title, assuming it's a hyperlink.
- **Unreadable Title:** The category colors are too light, making the title difficult to read.
- **Clunky Phrasing:** The title or description reads awkwardly because it has been forced to include all the category names.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Color Key Nouns.** In your title or subtitle, identify the nouns for your main categories and apply the corresponding color to just those words.

- **Moderate Approach: Add a Fallback Legend.** Use the integrated title-legend, but also include a small, traditional color key elsewhere as a secondary reference point. This provides reinforcement and a clear lookup target.

- **Comprehensive Approach: Test for Ambiguity.** Before publishing, ask a colleague to review the chart. Do they understand the colored text is a legend? Do they find any of the colors confusing (e.g., blue for links)? Adjust based on feedback.
