## Guidance

If your pie chart contains multiple small, individually unimportant slices, group them into a single, larger slice labeled "Other" or "Miscellaneous".

## Why

A proliferation of small slices makes a pie chart cluttered, difficult to label, and hard to read. By consolidating these minor categories, you simplify the visualization, reducing the number of slices and colors. This decluttering makes the remaining, more significant slices larger and their proportions easier to perceive, focusing the reader's attention on the most important parts of the data.

### Core Principle

Simplify visualizations by removing or aggregating low-importance details to amplify the main message.

## When it applies

- You are creating a pie chart from data with high cardinality (many categories).
- Many of these categories represent very small percentages of the total.
- The individual identity of these small categories is not critical to your story.

## Exceptions

- When the point of the story is the "long tail" itself—that is, the existence of many small players is the key finding. In this case, a bar chart sorted from largest to smallest would be a far more effective way to visualize this distribution.

## Trade-offs

- **Prioritizes:** Simplicity, readability, and focus on the main categories.
- **Sacrifices:** The ability to see the value of every individual category in the chart. This detail can be provided in a table or in the text if necessary.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Sliver Overload:** Your chart has numerous thin slices that are hard to distinguish and label.
- **Lost Focus:** The presence of many small slices distracts from the larger, more important slices that are central to your message.
- **Exceeds Slice Limit:** Your chart violates the "five slices max" rule, and the excess slices are all small.

## How to Improve

- **Aggregate Minor Categories:** In your data, create a new category called "Other." Sum the values of all the small slices you wish to group and assign that sum to the "Other" category. Remove the original small categories from the data used to build the chart. The resulting pie chart will be cleaner and more effective.
