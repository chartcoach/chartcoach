## Guidance
To make a data point the primary focus of your visualization, assign it a color that is more saturated, brighter, and has a higher contrast against the background than any other element.

## Why
Our visual system is hard-wired to notice outliers. A single, saturated color in a field of neutral or desaturated colors is a powerful preattentive attribute that the brain processes automatically, without conscious effort. This ensures that the viewer's attention is captured and directed to the most important part of the visualization immediately.

### Core Principle
The eye is drawn to contrast. Maximize the contrast (in saturation, brightness, and/or hue) of the element you want to emphasize relative to its surroundings.

## When it applies
- When you have a single, primary message or data point you want every viewer to see.
- In explanatory graphics and data storytelling, where you are guiding the audience through a specific narrative.
- In dashboards or monitoring displays, to flag key metrics that require attention.

## Exceptions
- **Aesthetic Constraints:** In some contexts, a very bright, saturated color might clash with a brand palette or be considered too garish. A darker, but still distinct, color can also work.
- **Cultural Connotations:** Be mindful of the cultural meaning of colors. A bright red might signal "danger" or "loss" in a Western context, which may not be your intent.

## Trade-offs
- A very strong highlight color can completely dominate the visualization, causing viewers to overlook important context provided by de-emphasized elements.

## Signs of Trouble
- **The "Everything is Important" Chart:** The chart uses multiple, competing saturated colors, resulting in visual chaos. If everything is emphasized, nothing is.
- **Weak Highlight:** Your chosen highlight color is too similar to the other colors (or gray) and fails to stand out.
- **Unintended Emphasis:** An element you did not intend to be the focus (like a bright red axis line from a default template) is inadvertently drawing the most attention.

## How to Improve
- **Quick Fix: Boost the Saturation.** Take your existing highlight color and increase its saturation and/or brightness until it clearly stands out from the other elements.
- **Moderate Approach: Pair with De-emphasis.** Ensure the power of your highlight color is amplified by de-emphasizing everything else. Change all other elements to a neutral gray or a highly desaturated version of your highlight color.
- **Comprehensive Approach: Test for Salience.** Use a "squint test" (squint your eyes and see what stands out) or convert the image to grayscale to check if your highlighted element still has the highest contrast in terms of brightness/luminance. This ensures the emphasis works even without color information.
