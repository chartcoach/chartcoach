## Guidance

To create a balanced and efficient layout, place pie charts within narrow containers, such as sidebars or margin columns, rather than in full-width content areas.

## Why

Pie charts have a relatively fixed, squarish aspect ratio. Unlike bar charts, which can stretch horizontally or vertically, a pie chart does not gracefully expand to fill a wide, rectangular space. Placing a pie chart in a full-width container results in large, awkward amounts of negative space to its left and right. Using a narrower container that more closely matches the chart's aspect ratio results in a more professional and space-efficient design.

## When it applies

- You are designing a page layout for a report, article, or dashboard that includes a pie chart.
- The layout is flexible (e.g., a multi-column grid on a webpage or in a print document).

## Exceptions

- In a single-column, mobile-first design, the chart may need to take the full width of the screen. In this case, the excess white space is unavoidable, but it is less noticeable on a narrow screen.
- When the chart itself is part of a larger composition with annotations and callouts that require extra horizontal space.

## Trade-offs

- **Prioritizes:** Efficient use of space and a balanced, professional page layout.
- **Sacrifices:** The potential for the chart to be a large, dominant centerpiece of the page.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Wasted Space:** Your pie chart is centered in a wide column, with significant, empty gaps on either side.
- **Unbalanced Layout:** The page feels "empty" around the chart, and the flow of text is awkwardly interrupted by the large white block containing the chart.

## How to Improve

- **Adjust Your Layout:** If your publishing system allows, restructure your page to use multiple columns. Place your main text in a wider column and the pie chart in a narrower adjacent column or sidebar. This creates a more compact and visually appealing arrangement.
