## Guidance

On an axis with complex or relative units (like an index or percentage difference), add an explanatory annotation next to a representative tick mark to show readers how to interpret the values.

## Why

Complex units like "indexed difference" are not intuitive. A small annotation, such as labeling the "-5" tick as "-5% from 2023," provides a concrete example that teaches readers how to decode the entire axis. This makes the chart more self-contained and significantly lowers the barrier to understanding.

### Core Principle

Provide a key or legend for your encoding, even for axes. Don't assume readers understand your units.

## When it applies

-   When your axis doesn't use simple, universally understood units like dollars, temperature, or absolute counts.
-   This is vital for indices, standardized scores, log scales, or any custom-calculated metric being shown to a general audience.

## Exceptions

This is unnecessary for standard, easily understood units where the meaning is clear from the axis title (e.g., "Temperature (°C)", "Revenue ($M)", "Population").

## Signs of Trouble

-   **Unit Confusion:** Viewers are unsure what a value of "-10" actually means in the real world.
-   **Decoding Effort:** Viewers have to stop and mentally perform calculations to understand the scale.
-   **Over-reliance on Text:** The chart's main description needs a long paragraph just to explain the y-axis.

## How to Improve

-   **Quick Fix:** Add a single text annotation near a prominent gridline. Combine the numerical value with a plain-language explanation (e.g., "-10 means 10% lower than today").
-   **Comprehensive Approach:** Integrate the explanation seamlessly into the axis design, as shown in the Datawrapper example where "-5% from 2023" is placed clearly and elegantly next to the -5% tick.
