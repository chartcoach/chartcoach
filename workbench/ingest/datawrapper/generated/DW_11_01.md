## Guidance

When seeking a unique aesthetic, use a color picker tool to extract palettes from sources you find visually appealing. This could be another data visualization, a piece of art, a photograph, or a magazine cover.

## Why

This method provides a shortcut to a harmonious and often unique color scheme. It leverages the color expertise of designers, artists, and photographers. However, it is crucial to remember that this is only a starting point; these colors must be tested and often adapted for a data visualization context.

### Core Principle

Inspiration is a valid starting point for design, but it must be followed by rigorous validation to ensure fitness for purpose.

## When it applies

- When you want a more custom or evocative palette than standard options provide.
- When you are trying to match the mood or theme of your data (e.g., using earthy tones for an environmental chart).
- When you're in an exploratory phase and looking for creative ideas.

## Exceptions

- Do not use this method if you need a palette that is guaranteed to be perceptually uniform for scientific or analytical purposes. In those cases, use palettes generated with perceptually uniform color spaces.
- If the source image has poor contrast or uses colors that are not distinct, it's a poor candidate for this method.

## Trade-offs

- Colors that look great in a photograph or painting may not work well for discrete categories in a chart. You will likely need to adjust the brightness and saturation of the sampled colors to ensure they are distinguishable and accessible.
- This method prioritizes aesthetics, which may come at the cost of perceptual clarity if not carefully managed in a follow-up step.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Context Mismatch:** Colors taken from a dark, moody painting look out of place or unreadable on a chart with a bright white background.
- **Lost Distinctions:** The sampled colors are too similar in lightness, making them hard to tell apart in a bar chart or on a map.
- **Legend Deception:** The colors look great as large swatches in the color picker tool but become indistinguishable when used as small points or thin lines in your chart.

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach:** Use an online tool (like Adobe Color's "Extract Theme") to automatically generate a palette from an image.
- **Moderate approach:** After extracting colors, paste their hex codes into a palette validation tool (like Viz Palette). Check for colorblind safety and perceptual distance issues, and manually adjust individual colors to improve their performance.
- **Comprehensive approach:** Use the sampled palette as a starting point. Convert the colors to a perceptually uniform color space (like LCH or Oklab). Adjust the Lightness and Chroma values to create a set of colors that are both harmonious and clearly distinguishable for your specific chart type and purpose.
