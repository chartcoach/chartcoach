## Guidance

If you are visualizing the components of only a single total, use a standard bar or column chart where each component gets its own bar. Avoid using a single stacked column.

## Why

A standard bar chart aligns all components to a common baseline, making comparison between them simple and accurate. It also typically makes better use of space. A single stacked column is essentially a divided bar, but presented vertically it can look awkward and be space-inefficient. While the bottom segment is easy to read, comparing any other two segments requires a more difficult mental comparison of their lengths, a task that a standard bar chart makes trivial for all components.

### Core Principle
Choose the simplest, most direct visualization for the task. For comparing parts of a single whole, a bar chart is more direct than a single stacked bar.

## When it applies
- When your data consists of several categories that sum to one single total (e.g., breaking down the budget of a single department).

## Exceptions
- If you are building a "waterfall chart," which is a special type of chart that uses floating columns to show cumulative changes to a total.
- If the single stacked bar is part of a larger dashboard or system where this format is used consistently for a specific purpose (e.g., as a custom KPI indicator).

## Trade-offs
- A simple bar chart does not visually represent the total sum as a single entity, whereas the single stacked bar's total height does represent the total. However, the total can easily be stated in the title or an annotation.

## Signs of Trouble
- **A Lonely Tower:** Your visualization consists of a single, tall, skinny stacked column.
- **Difficult Comparisons:** To compare two segments, you have to read the data labels; you can't do it easily just by looking.
- **Wasted Space:** The chart has a lot of empty white space to the left and right of the single column.

## How to Improve
- **Quick Fix: Rotate and Label.** If you must keep the format, consider making it a single horizontal stacked bar. Ensure all segments are clearly labeled with their value.
- **Moderate Redesign: Switch to a Bar Chart.** Change the chart type to a standard bar chart (horizontal bars are often best, especially for long labels). Sort the bars by size to make comparison and ranking even easier.
