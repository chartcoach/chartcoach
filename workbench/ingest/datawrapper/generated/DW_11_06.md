## Guidance

Use a Large Language Model (like ChatGPT or Claude) to generate color palette ideas by describing the theme, mood, or context of your visualization. Treat the hex codes generated by the LLM as a raw starting point for a more rigorous design and validation process.

## Why

LLMs can be a quick and creative brainstorming partner, translating abstract concepts (e.g., "a trustworthy palette for a financial report," "a warm, autumnal palette") into concrete hex codes. This can help overcome creative blocks and provide novel combinations you might not have considered.

### Core Principle

Leverage generative AI as a creative assistant, not a final authority. Its output is inspiration, not a finished, validated product.

## When it applies

- At the very beginning of the color selection process, when you're looking for inspiration and ideas.
- When you are stuck or want to explore unconventional thematic palettes.

## Exceptions

- **Do not** use an LLM's output directly in a final visualization without extensive validation. LLMs have no inherent, reliable understanding of color perception, perceptual uniformity, or accessibility.

## Trade-offs

- This method is very fast for idea generation but creates an output that absolutely requires further validation and refinement.
- Relying on it too heavily can lead to functionally poor palettes that look good as text but fail in practice. The generated palettes often lack perceptual balance.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Naive Output:** The LLM gives you a list of generic color names ("blue, green, red...") without specific hex codes, or provides hex codes that are overly simple (e.g., `#FF0000`, `#00FF00`).
- **Immediate Failure:** You use the generated palette directly and it immediately fails colorblindness checks or contains colors with wildly different brightness levels.
- **Vague Prompts, Vague Results:** You give a prompt like "give me colors" and get a generic, uninspired rainbow palette.

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach:** Prompt the LLM with a specific theme and ask for a list of hex codes. For example: "Give me five hex codes for a color palette with a 'serene forest' theme."
- **Moderate approach:** Take the list of hex codes from the LLM and immediately plug them into a validation tool like Viz Palette. Use the tool's feedback to manually adjust, reorder, or replace colors to fix accessibility and perceptual issues.
- **Comprehensive approach:** Use the LLM for thematic keywords only. If it suggests a palette for "sea glass," use "sea glass color palette" as a search term to find human-curated, pre-validated palettes. Or, use the theme to guide your own palette creation process in a perceptually uniform color space.
