## Guidance

When you need to compare the composition of two or more different groups or time periods, use a chart type like a stacked bar chart, not multiple pie charts.

## Why

Each pie chart represents its own 100% total. Placing two pie charts side-by-side encourages a comparison that is perceptually very difficult. Viewers must compare non-aligned angles and areas between two separate objects, which is an error-prone task. A stacked bar chart, by contrast, places the different totals in a comparable format (as bars of different lengths) and allows for easier (though still imperfect) comparison of the component parts.

## When it applies

- You need to compare part-to-whole relationships across multiple groups, categories, or time points (e.g., comparing market share in different countries, or poll results over two years).
- The totals for each group might be different, and this difference is also important to show.

## Exceptions

- There are no known exceptions where multiple pie charts are superior to alternatives for comparing compositions. While they are commonly used, they are not effective.

## Trade-offs

- **Prioritizes:** Accurate comparison of compositions across different groups.
- **Sacrifices:** The simple, self-contained "pie" metaphor for each group. The stacked bar chart is a more complex visual but is far more effective for this specific task.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Side-by-Side Pies:** You have two or more pie charts displayed next to each other, with the implied goal of having the reader compare them. The reader's eyes have to jump back and forth between the charts to try and discern changes in the slices.

## How to Improve

- **Switch to a Stacked Bar Chart:** Represent each group or time period as a separate bar. Each bar's total length can represent the total for that group, and the segments within the bar represent the shares. This makes it easier to compare both the totals and the compositions. For even more accurate comparison of the individual components, consider a grouped bar chart.
