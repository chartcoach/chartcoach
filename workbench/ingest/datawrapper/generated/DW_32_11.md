## Guidance

Format numbers to be as simple as is appropriate for the context. Avoid showing excessive decimal places and use standard suffixes to abbreviate large numbers (e.g., "12.8k" for 12,831, "12.8m" for 12,831,283).

## Why

Unnecessary precision adds visual clutter and cognitive load without improving understanding. Readers rarely need, or remember, numbers with many decimal places or a long string of digits. Simplified, rounded numbers are easier to scan, compare, and recall, making your visualization more effective and approachable.

## When it applies

- In any text element that displays numbers: axis labels, data point labels, annotations, and tooltips.

## Exceptions

- **High-Stakes Precision:** When high precision is scientifically, financially, or engineeringly critical and the audience expects it (e.g., pharmaceutical dosages, financial statements to the cent).
- **Small Ranges:** When the key differences in your data only appear after the decimal point, more precision is required to show the variation.
- Even in these cases, consider if the full precision is needed everywhere, or if it can be reserved for tooltips while the main labels are simplified.

## Trade-offs

- Rounding and abbreviation inherently remove some information. You must ensure that the rounding does not distort the data's message, create false equivalences, or violate the expectations of an expert audience.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Decimal Overload:** Your labels show numbers like `22.428%` or `12,831,783.92`.
- **Spurious Precision:** The data displays a level of precision that is not meaningful or real in the underlying measurements (e.g., survey results reported to three decimal places).
- **Jagged Alignment:** In a table or a list of labels, numbers with varying numbers of decimal places do not align correctly on the decimal point, looking messy.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Change the Number Format.** In your charting tool's settings, change the default number format to show zero or one decimal place. Enable a format that abbreviates large numbers (e.g., using `k` for thousands, `m` for millions).
- **Moderate Approach: Use Smart Formatting and Tooltips.** Use a "smart" number format that removes unnecessary trailing zeroes (e.g., `27.0%` becomes `27%` while `22.4%` stays as is). Move the full, precise numbers into interactive tooltips, so they are available on demand without cluttering the main view.
- **Comprehensive Approach: Define Precision by Context.** Establish rules for number formatting based on the data type and magnitude. For example: percentages get one decimal place, dollar amounts over $1M are abbreviated to one decimal place (e.g., $3.4m), and population counts are rounded to the nearest thousand.
