## Guidance
De-emphasize secondary or contextual data points by reducing their color opacity, making them semi-transparent. Keep the most important data fully opaque (100% opacity).

## Why
Reducing opacity causes elements to recede into the background, making the fully opaque elements stand out by comparison. This creates a visual hierarchy without changing colors. It is especially effective in dense visualizations like scatter plots, as it can also help reveal patterns of overplotting by showing where transparent marks build up.

## When it applies
- In digital media (screen, interactive) where transparency is rendered cleanly.
- For dense plots like scatter plots, where it helps mitigate overplotting.
- As a subtle alternative to graying out when you want to retain the original hue of the de-emphasized elements.

## Exceptions
- **Print Media:** Transparency can be difficult to reproduce accurately in print, sometimes resulting in muddy or unexpected colors.
- **Overlapping Shapes:** In charts with overlapping areas, like stacked area charts or Venn diagrams, transparency will cause colors to mix and create new, unlegended colors that can confuse the viewer.
- **Background Complexity:** If the chart has a complex or colored background, transparency will cause the elements to blend with the background in unpredictable ways.

## Trade-offs
- **Color Distortion:** A semi-transparent color is not the same as the original. A transparent red over a white background will look pink, which may not match your legend.
- **Legibility Risk:** If opacity is set too low, the de-emphasized elements may become illegible or be missed entirely.

## Signs of Trouble
- **Muddy Colors:** Overlapping transparent elements create murky, brownish colors that are visually unappealing and hard to interpret.
- **Invisible Data:** De-emphasized elements are so transparent they are barely visible on the intended display.
- **Legend Mismatch:** The colors of the transparent elements on the chart no longer match the solid color swatches in your legend.

## How to Improve
- **Quick Fix: Use on Points and Lines Only.** Avoid using transparency on large, overlapping shapes (like areas in an area chart). It is safest on points and lines that do not have significant overlap.
- **Moderate Approach: Pair with a Legend.** If you use transparency, ensure your legend reflects it. Some tools allow you to show semi-transparent swatches in the legend itself, making the encoding explicit.
- **Comprehensive Approach: Use Gray Instead.** For most cases, especially with shapes or for print, using a solid light gray is a more robust and predictable method for de-emphasis than transparency. Reserve transparency for solving specific problems like overplotting in scatterplots.
