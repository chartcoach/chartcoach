## Guidance

Consciously choose the level of smoothing (e.g., using a moving average or LOESS regression) for your trend lines to match your communication goal.

## Why

Raw data, like daily polls, can be very "noisy" or "ragged," making it hard to see the underlying trend. Smoothing averages out this noise to reveal a clearer signal. However, too much smoothing can hide important short-term events or create a misleading sense of stability. The choice represents an editorial judgment about what is signal and what is noise.

## When it applies

- When visualizing noisy time-series data, such as daily polling data, stock prices, or sensor readings, where the goal is to show a general trend.

## Exceptions

- When every individual data point is important and the volatility itself is the story.
- When presenting to a technical audience that needs to see the raw, unaltered data for analysis.
- When there are very few data points, as smoothing can be misleading.

## Trade-offs

- **Clarity vs. Fidelity:** Smoothing increases the clarity of the overall trend but at the cost of losing detail and fidelity to the original data. A smoothed line is an interpretation, not the raw data itself.
- **Risk of Obscuring Data:** A heavily smoothed line might hide a significant but brief spike or dip in the data that could be an important part of the story.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Detached Line:** The smoothed line looks completely detached from the actual data points, suggesting it is a poor model of the data.
- **Noise Overload:** The line is so "ragged" and noisy that no trend is discernible at all.
- **Hidden Method:** A line is smoothed, but there is no mention of the method used, leaving the reader unable to judge its validity.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix:** Start with a modest level of smoothing, like a 7-day or 10-day moving average for daily data, and see if it clarifies the trend without excessively distorting it.
- **Moderate Approach:** Show the raw data points faintly in the background behind the smoothed line. This provides both the clear trend and the context of the original data's spread and volatility.
- **Comprehensive Approach:** Clearly state the smoothing method in the chart's subtitle or notes (e.g., "Data shown as a 10-day moving average"). For a sophisticated audience, you could visualize the confidence band around the smoothed line to show the range of statistical uncertainty.
