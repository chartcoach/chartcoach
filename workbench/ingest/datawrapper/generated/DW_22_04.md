## Guidance

Before finalizing a font for your data visualization, confirm that it supports all the special characters, symbols, and languages your data requires.

## Why

If your data includes characters that your chosen font doesn't have a glyph for (e.g., currency symbols like €, accented characters like 'é', or mathematical symbols like 'µ'), the browser or software will render a placeholder character—often a box known as "tofu" (□)—or fall back to a different font. This creates an unprofessional, broken appearance, can make your data unreadable, and undermines the credibility of your visualization.

### Core Principle

Ensure data integrity and visual consistency. The visual representation of your data should be complete and free of rendering errors.

## When it applies

-   Always, but especially when:
-   Your data involves multiple languages or non-English characters (e.g., German, Vietnamese, Cyrillic).
-   You are displaying financial data with different currency symbols (£, ¥, €, $).
-   You are using scientific or mathematical notations (µ, ±, °, ¹).
-   You need to include symbols like copyright (©), registered trademark (®), or arrows (→).

## Exceptions

-   There are no exceptions. If a character is in your data, the font must support it. The only alternative is to remove or replace the character in the data itself, which is often not an option.

## Trade-offs

-   **Font Choice vs. Character Support:** You may have to abandon a stylistically preferred font in favor of a less exciting but more comprehensive one. A "boring" font that renders correctly is always better than a "beautiful" font that fails.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **"Tofu" Squares:** You see square boxes (□), question marks (?), or other replacement characters in your chart where symbols or special letters should be.
-   **Font Goulash:** One symbol or character suddenly appears in a different font style (e.g., a serifed Euro symbol in a line of sans-serif text). This is a sign of font fallback.
-   **Missing Accents:** Words appear without their proper diacritics (e.g., "resume" instead of "résumé").

## How to Improve

-   **Quick Fix:** If you see a missing character, immediately switch to a comprehensive, widely-used font like Arial, Helvetica, or Noto Sans, which have excellent character support.
-   **Moderate Approach:** Before starting a project, list all potential special characters your data might contain. Use a font testing tool to check if your desired font supports them all. Paste a string of all required characters (e.g., "€£¥$ áéíóúüñ ±°¹²³") into a font previewer.
-   **Comprehensive Approach:** For projects involving diverse international data, make a highly comprehensive font family like Noto Sans or Inter the default in your design system. Document the importance of character set verification as a required step in the data visualization production checklist.
