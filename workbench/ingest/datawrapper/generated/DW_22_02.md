## Guidance

Use sentence case ("The quick brown fox") or title case ("The Quick Brown Fox") for titles, labels, and annotations. Avoid using all-caps ("THE QUICK BROWN FOX") for anything other than very short abbreviations.

## Why

Text set in all capital letters is significantly harder and slower to read. We recognize words not just by their individual letters, but by their overall shape, which is defined by the pattern of ascenders (like in 'h', 'd') and descenders (like in 'p', 'g'). All-caps text forms a uniform rectangular block, eliminating these distinctive shapes and forcing the reader to process words letter by letter. It can also be perceived as "shouting" and visually aggressive.

### Core Principle

Respect the reader's cognitive resources. Choose formatting that makes text as easy as possible to scan and comprehend, reducing unnecessary mental effort.

## When it applies

-   Chart titles and subtitles.
-   Axis labels and legend text.
-   Long annotations or descriptions.
-   Any text that is more than one or two words long.

## Exceptions

-   **Acronyms and Initialisms:** It is standard to use all-caps for acronyms like "USA," "GDP," or "NASA."
-   **Very Short Labels:** For extremely short, one-word labels in a UI context (e.g., a "BACK" button), all-caps can be acceptable if used consistently and sparingly, often with increased letter-spacing for better legibility. However, sentence case is still a safer choice.

## Trade-offs

-   **Stylistic Impact:** All-caps can be used deliberately to create a strong, impactful, or stylized graphic effect. However, this aesthetic choice comes at the direct cost of readability and should be avoided in purely informational contexts.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **Reader Fatigue:** You feel a sense of strain or effort when reading the text on the chart.
-   **"Shouting" Chart:** The visualization feels aggressive or loud, and the titles overpower the data.
-   **Slow Comprehension:** It takes longer than expected to understand what the chart title or labels are saying.

## How to Improve

-   **Quick Fix:** Re-type your titles and labels using sentence case. This is the simplest and most effective solution. For chart titles, you can also use title case, but sentence case is often preferred for its more modern and less formal feel.
-   **Moderate Approach:** Review all text elements in your visualization. Convert all-caps text to sentence case. If you need to create emphasis, use a heavier font weight (e.g., bold) instead of all-caps.
-   **Comprehensive Approach:** Establish a clear capitalization rule in your style guide. Mandate sentence case for all headings, labels, and annotations in data visualizations. This ensures consistency and prioritizes readability across all your published work.
