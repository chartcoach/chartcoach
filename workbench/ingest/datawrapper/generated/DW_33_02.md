## Guidance

Visualize polling data over time using a line chart to reveal trends and momentum.

## Why

A single poll is just a static snapshot. Public opinion is dynamic, changing in response to news and campaign events. A line chart is the most effective way to show the trajectory of support, providing crucial context about momentum, volatility, and the impact of events. This shifts the narrative from "who is ahead now" to "how has the race evolved."

### Core Principle

For time-series data, visualize the trend. The story is often in the change over time, not in a single point in time.

## When it applies

- When you have time-series data, such as multiple polls conducted over a period of weeks, months, or years.
- When the goal is to understand trends, momentum, or the impact of events on the data.

## Exceptions

- When the goal is simply to show the result of a single, specific poll (e.g., an exit poll on election day), although even then, comparison to a pre-election poll can be valuable.
- When there are only two or three time points, a slope chart or dot plot might be clearer than a full line chart.

## Trade-offs

- Can become cluttered and hard to read if there are too many overlapping lines (a "spaghetti chart").
- Requires access to and processing of historical data, which can be more work than showing a single poll.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Snapshot Fixation:** Reporting focuses solely on the latest poll results, presented in a static format like a bar or pie chart, with no historical context.
- **Lost Narrative:** A major campaign event is reported, but the accompanying visualization doesn't show its potential impact on the polls in the following days or weeks. The data and the story are disconnected.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Add Trend Context.** Alongside a chart of the latest poll, include text describing the trend (e.g., "Party X is up 3 points from last week and has been steadily rising for a month").
- **Moderate Redesign: Create a Line Chart.** Convert the polling data into a line chart showing the polling average for each major party/candidate over the course of the campaign.
- **Comprehensive Redesign: Use an Annotated Line Chart.** Create a line chart and use annotations, labels, and shaded regions to mark key political events (e.g., debates, scandals, policy announcements). This directly connects the data's movement to the real-world narrative, explaining *why* trends might be changing.
