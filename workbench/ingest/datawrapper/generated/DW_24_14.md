## Guidance

In tables, consider replacing or supplementing a column of numbers with in-cell bar charts to provide a quick visual guide to their magnitude.

## Why

A column of numbers requires readers to scan and mentally compare each value to understand their relative size. By representing those numbers as bars, you make comparison instantaneous. The eye can quickly see the largest, smallest, and intermediate values, allowing for rapid ranking and pattern detection while still keeping the data within the organized structure of a table.

## When it applies

- When you have a quantitative column that is particularly important for comparison.
- In tables where you want to provide a quick visual overview in addition to precise numbers.
- When you want to make it easy for readers to rank the rows by a specific metric.

## Exceptions

- If horizontal space is extremely limited, as bar charts inherently make a column wider than numbers alone. The article suggests applying this to only the most important columns.

## Trade-offs

- **Increased Width:** An in-cell bar chart requires more horizontal space than a simple number column.
- **Potential Clutter:** If overused on many columns, it can make the table look busy. It's most effective when used selectively on one or two key columns.

## Signs of Trouble

- **"Number Numbness":** Readers' eyes glaze over when looking at a large column of multi-digit numbers, making it hard to spot the largest or smallest values.
- **Slow Comparison:** It takes a noticeable amount of time for a user to determine the relative ranking of rows based on a specific column.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Add a Bar Chart Column.** Create a new column next to your key number column and insert a simple bar chart that visually represents the number. The bar should be scaled so the largest value in the column is at or near 100% width.
- **Comprehensive Approach: Integrate Bars and Numbers.** Combine the bar and number in a single column. The bar can serve as a background for the number, which can be aligned left or right depending on what provides the best readability. This is a very space-efficient and effective design.
