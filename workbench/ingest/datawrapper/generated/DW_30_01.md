## Guidance

In annotations referring to a specific data category, apply that category's color to the relevant words in the text. Be precise: color only the words that name the category.

## Why

This technique brings the legend directly to the data, creating an immediate visual connection between the textual insight and the graphical mark. It reduces cognitive load by eliminating the need for the viewer to scan back and forth between the annotation, the legend, and the data.

### Core Principle

Place explanatory information as close as possible to the data it describes.

## When it applies

- When writing callouts or annotations that highlight or explain a specific data point, line, or area.
- In any chart type (bar, line, scatter, etc.) where you are drawing attention to a particular category.

## Exceptions

When all category colors are too light or have poor contrast against the background, making them unsuitable for text. In this case, alternative methods should be used.

## Trade-offs

- **Legibility vs. Consistency:** The exact category color may not be legible as text. You may need to sacrifice exact color matching for readability by using a darker shade.
- **Precision:** Only the words that directly name the category should be colored. Coloring additional words can create ambiguity about what the color represents.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Illegible Text:** The colored text is hard to read because of low contrast with the background (WCAG AA requires a 4.5:1 ratio for normal text).
- **Ambiguous Coloring:** The entire annotation is colored, making it unclear if the color represents the category, a trend (e.g., "decrease"), or something else.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Darken the Text Color.** If a category color is too light (e.g., light yellow), use a darker shade of that same hue for the text to meet accessibility contrast ratios.

- **Moderate Approach: Use a Background Highlight.** Instead of coloring the text itself, use the category color as a background color for the relevant words. Use black or white text on top to ensure high contrast and readability.

- **Comprehensive Approach: Re-evaluate the Palette.** If many of your category colors are not suitable for text, your palette may not be versatile enough. Consider choosing a palette where each color has a corresponding, accessible darker shade for text use.
