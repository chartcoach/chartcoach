## Guidance

Display data from multiple pollsters simultaneously instead of relying on a single source.

## Why

Different polling organizations may have varying methodologies or inherent biases ("house effects") that lead to consistently different results. Any single poll can also be a statistical outlier due to random chance. Showing results from several polls provides a more complete and reliable picture, highlighting both the general consensus and the range of disagreement among pollsters.

### Core Principle

Aggregate evidence to provide a more robust and stable signal. Avoid drawing strong conclusions from a single, potentially noisy, data point.

## When it applies

- When reporting on topics where multiple sources of sampled data exist, such as election polling, economic forecasts, or public opinion on a specific issue.
- When there is known variation or methodological disagreement between data sources.

## Exceptions

- If only one reliable poll or data source is available.
- If the goal is to specifically analyze the methodology or results of a single, particular poll, in which case this context should be made clear.

## Trade-offs

- Increases information density and cognitive load for the viewer.
- Can be more labor-intensive to gather, clean, and aggregate data from multiple sources.
- May be confusing if polls differ wildly without any explanation for the variance.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Cherry-Picking:** A report focuses exclusively on one poll's results, especially if that poll supports a particular narrative, while ignoring other available polls with different findings.
- **Single-Source Syndrome:** All reporting on a topic is based on a single recurring poll, presented as definitive truth without acknowledging the existence or results of other pollsters.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Add Contextual Text.** In the text accompanying a single-poll chart, mention the results of other recent polls to provide context (e.g., "While this poll shows a 5-point lead, other polls this week have shown a lead between 2 and 7 points.").
- **Moderate Redesign: Use a Comparison Table.** Create a table that lists the results from multiple pollsters for each party/candidate. This allows for easy lookup and comparison. The example from the article uses split bars within a table.
- **Comprehensive Redesign: Visualize the Distribution.** Use a dot plot where each dot represents one pollster's result for a given candidate, showing the full distribution of poll results. Alternatively, calculate and visualize a polling average, while still showing the individual polls that contribute to it.
