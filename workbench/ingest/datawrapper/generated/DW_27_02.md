## Guidance

Avoid using a pie chart when you have more than five categories (slices). If you need to display a part-to-whole relationship with many shares, use a stacked bar or stacked column chart instead.

## Why

As the number of slices in a pie chart increases, the chart quickly becomes cluttered and difficult to interpret. Each slice becomes smaller, making their angles harder to judge and compare. Furthermore, finding space for legible labels becomes a significant challenge, often resulting in a messy web of leader lines or an unreadable legend. This increases the cognitive load on the reader.

## When it applies

- You are visualizing a composition (part-to-whole relationship).
- Your categorical data has high cardinality (more than 5 categories).

## Exceptions

If you can group the smaller slices into a single "Other" category to reduce the total slice count to five or fewer, a pie chart may still be viable. This works if the individual small slices are not important to the story.

## Trade-offs

- **Prioritizes:** Readability, clean labeling, and reduced cognitive load.
- **Sacrifices:** The ability to show every single category as a distinct slice in a circular format.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Cluttered Visuals:** Your pie chart has many thin "sliver" slices.
- **Labeling Nightmare:** Labels are overlapping, are too small to read, or require a complex set of lines pointing from the label to the slice.
- **Color Overload:** The chart uses too many colors, making it look like a "rainbow," which distracts the reader and makes it difficult to associate labels or legend keys with their corresponding slices.

## How to Improve

- **Switch to a Stacked Bar/Column Chart:** Convert the data into a stacked bar or stacked column chart. This format handles a larger number of categories more gracefully. It provides more space for clear, organized labeling next to each segment, resulting in a tidier and more professional appearance.
