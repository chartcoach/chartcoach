## Guidance

Limit the primary comparison goal of a stacked column chart to the overall totals and the single segment that rests on the shared baseline (the bottom-most segment).

## Why

Humans are very good at comparing the lengths of objects that share a common starting point (a baseline). In a stacked column chart, only the bottom segment has this property. It is perceptually difficult and inaccurate to compare the sizes of the other, "floating" segments because they all start at different heights. Focusing the chart's message on the totals and the bottom series aligns the visualization with what viewers can accurately perceive.

### Core Principle
Make the most important comparisons the easiest to perform. Aligning data to a common baseline is the most effective way to enable accurate visual comparison of magnitude.

## When it applies
- When creating a stacked column chart.
- When the primary task for the viewer is to compare the size of different components across several parent categories.

## Exceptions
- If the goal is to give a general, qualitative sense of the composition of totals without needing precise comparisons between the inner segments. For example, showing that a certain component is consistently the largest or smallest part of the total.

## Trade-offs
- This limits the utility of a stacked column chart. If you need to enable comparison for more than one or two segments, this chart type is not the right choice and another should be used.

## Signs of Trouble
- **Floating Comparisons:** The chart's title or annotations ask the reader to compare segments that are not on the bottom baseline (e.g., "Notice the growth in segment C").
- **Reader Confusion:** Viewers express difficulty in determining which of the inner segments is larger or how they change across the columns.
- **Over-reliance on Labels:** The chart is unreadable without data labels on every single segment, indicating that the visual encoding itself is failing.

## How to Improve
- **Quick Fix: Reorder and Highlight.** If you must use a stacked column chart, move the most important segment for comparison to the bottom and use a highlight color to draw attention to it.
- **Moderate Redesign: Switch to Grouped Bars.** Change the chart type to a grouped (or "split") bar chart. This gives every segment its own bar and a common baseline, making all comparisons easy and accurate.
- **Comprehensive Redesign: Use Small Multiples or a Dot Plot.** For comparing multiple parts across multiple totals, a series of small multiple bar charts (one for each segment) or a dot plot can be even clearer, especially with many categories or segments.
