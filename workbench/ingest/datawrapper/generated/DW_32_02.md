## Guidance

Design tooltips to show not just the numerical value, but also the category and unit it represents. Phrase them as a complete piece of information.

## Why

Informative tooltips serve as a "just-in-time" reminder of what the data point represents, reinforcing the chart's message and reducing cognitive load. Instead of just showing a number, a well-designed tooltip answers the question, "What am I looking at?" directly at the point of interaction.

## When it applies

- In any interactive visualization that uses tooltips to reveal data on hover or click.

## Exceptions

- None known. Providing more context in a tooltip is nearly always better, as long as it is concise.

## Trade-offs

- Requires a small amount of extra effort to configure the tooltip format, but has no significant downsides.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Orphaned Value:** A tooltip shows only a number, like `3.4`, without any descriptive text.
- **Ambiguous Data:** On a multi-series chart, it is unclear from the tooltip which series the hovered value belongs to without relying on color matching.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Customize the Tooltip Format.** Go into your chart editor's tooltip settings. Instead of the default format (e.g., `{{ value }}`), customize it to include the category name and the unit (e.g., `{{ series_name }}: {{ value }}% unemployed`).
- **Moderate Approach: Use Custom HTML.** For complex charts, use custom HTML in tooltips to provide a small summary table or multiple data points related to the hovered element. This can provide rich, structured information on demand.
- **Comprehensive Approach: Tell a Mini-Story.** Craft tooltip content to be a readable sentence, such as: "In **{{ year }}**, the unemployment rate for **{{ series_name }}** was **{{ value }}%**."
