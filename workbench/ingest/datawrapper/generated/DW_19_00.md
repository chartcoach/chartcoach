## Guidance

Make your chart title an active, declarative sentence that summarizes the key insight or story your data reveals.

## Why

A neutral title like "Bike Theft Statistics" requires the reader to interpret the data to find the story. A narrative title like "Berlin's unsolved bike thefts" delivers the main conclusion immediately. This reduces cognitive load and focuses the reader's attention on the most important finding, making the chart more effective as a communication tool.

## When it applies

-   When presenting a chart to a general audience.
-   When the primary goal is to communicate a specific finding quickly and clearly.
-   In storytelling contexts like articles, reports, and presentations.

## Exceptions

-   For exploratory data analysis or in scientific/technical reports where the audience is expected to draw their own conclusions, a neutral, descriptive title (e.g., "Reported vs. Solved Bike Thefts, 2004-2022") may be more appropriate to maintain objectivity.

## How to Improve

-   **Identify the 'so what?':** Look at your chart and ask yourself, "What is the single most important message here?"
-   **Write a sentence:** Turn that message into a concise, declarative sentence.
-   **Place it as the title:** Use this sentence as the main title of your chart. Use a more descriptive, neutral label as a subtitle if needed.
