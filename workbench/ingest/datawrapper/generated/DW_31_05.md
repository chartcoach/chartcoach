## Guidance

When your entire message can be summarized by one or two numbers, present them directly as text. Avoid creating a chart to show a single data point.

## Why

Charts are for showing patterns, relationships, and comparisons that are difficult to see in a table of numbers. A single number has no pattern or relationship to show. Creating a chart for it forces the reader to go through the process of decoding a visual (e.g., looking at an axis, interpreting a bar or slice) just to extract a piece of information that could have been read in a fraction of a second as text. This is an inefficient use of your reader's attention and your available space.

### Core Principle
Don't make the reader work to decode a visual when plain text is faster and clearer. Use the right tool for the job; sometimes that tool is a sentence, not a chart.

## When it applies
- When the key insight is a single number, percentage, or fraction (e.g., "Sales increased by 15%," or "3 out of 4 dentists agree").

## Exceptions
- When the single number is part of a larger, standardized dashboard format (e.g., a "gauge" chart or a single-bar KPI) where the visual consistency is important for quick scanning across many such indicators.
- When the visual itself is used for branding, decoration, or to create an emotional impact that goes beyond simply conveying the number.

## Trade-offs
- You lose the potential "graphical appeal" of a chart, but gain clarity and speed of comprehension.

## Signs of Trouble
- **One-Slice Pie:** You've created a pie chart with only one or two slices.
- **One-Bar Bar Chart:** Your bar chart has only a single bar.
- **Redundancy:** The chart's title or caption states the entire message, making the chart itself completely redundant (e.g., a title "25% of X" above a chart that shows 25%).

## How to Improve
- **Quick Fix: Delete the Chart.** Remove the chart entirely.
- **Moderate Redesign: Emphasize the Text.** Integrate the number into your document's narrative. Make it bold, use a larger font, or place it in a call-out box to give it the emphasis a chart might have provided. Example: "Our key finding: **47%** of all traffic comes from organic search."
