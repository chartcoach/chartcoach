## Guidance

If you are visualizing a composition with only two shares (e.g., "Yes" vs. "No"), consider forgoing a chart altogether and simply stating the key percentage directly in your text.

## Why

A pie chart with two slices effectively displays only one number, as the second is always the complement (100% minus the first number). Dedicating a full chart to this single piece of information can be an inefficient use of space and reader attention. A simple, declarative sentence is often more direct, faster to read, and integrates more smoothly into the narrative of your text.

## When it applies

- You are showing a part-to-whole relationship with only two categories.
- The key takeaway is a single percentage.

## Exceptions

- A visual element is required for design or layout purposes, and the pie chart's circular form is aesthetically desired.
- You are creating a dashboard or a series of small multiple visuals where consistency in displaying this type of data as a chart is important.

## Trade-offs

- **Prioritizes:** Conciseness, information density, and narrative flow.
- **Sacrifices:** A visual anchor for the number. Some readers may find a chart more engaging or memorable than text alone.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Superfluous Chart:** A pie chart on the page shows two slices, such as "42%" and "58%". The main takeaway is simply the number "42%", and the chart adds little extra insight while consuming significant space.

## How to Improve

- **Use Inline Text:** Remove the pie chart and integrate the key number directly into your supporting text. For example, instead of a chart showing 42% vs 58%, write "In our survey, 42% of respondents agreed." This is more efficient and keeps the reader focused on the story. You can make the number bold or use a callout to add emphasis if needed.
