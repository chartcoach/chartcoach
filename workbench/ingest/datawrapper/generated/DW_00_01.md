## Guidance

For charts with multiple categories, consider using a monochromatic color palette (different shades of a single hue) instead of a qualitative palette (multiple distinct hues).

## Why

Using shades of one color creates a more visually cohesive and less overwhelming chart than one with many competing hues. This can unify parts of a whole, such as segments in a stacked bar chart, and shift the viewer's focus toward the overall totals rather than the individual components. It's an effective way to reduce the "confetti" look of a chart with too many categories.

## When it applies

- In part-to-whole charts like stacked bars, stacked area charts, or pie charts.
- When the total value of the segments is more important than comparing individual segments with each other.
- When you want to achieve a more subtle, understated, and professional aesthetic.
- When categories have a natural order that can be mapped to light-to-dark shades.

## Exceptions

- When the primary task is for viewers to quickly and accurately identify and compare individual categories. Distinct hues are far more discriminable than different shades of one color.
- If you have more than 7-9 categories, as it becomes nearly impossible to distinguish that many shades of a single hue.

## Trade-offs

- **Gained:** A calmer, more cohesive aesthetic; a stronger visual emphasis on the sum of the parts.
- **Sacrificed:** Perceptual discriminability between categories. It is significantly harder for the human eye to distinguish between shades of blue than it is to distinguish between blue, orange, and green.

## Signs of Trouble

- **The "Confetti Party":** The chart uses so many bright, distinct hues that it looks chaotic and it's hard to focus on any single part of it.
- **Indistinguishable Shades:** You've used a monochromatic palette, but two adjacent shades are so similar they are impossible to tell apart.

## How to Improve

- **Moderate Redesign: Switch to a Monochromatic Palette.** Replace your multi-color qualitative palette with a sequential one. Most charting tools offer pre-built "monochromatic" or "sequential" palettes. Ensure there is enough perceptual difference between adjacent steps in the palette.
