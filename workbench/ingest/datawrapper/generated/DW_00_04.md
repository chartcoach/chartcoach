## Guidance

In part-to-whole charts, combine numerous small or insignificant categories into a single, consolidated group labeled "Other" or "Miscellaneous."

## Why

Displaying every single category can overwhelm the viewer with trivial detail, making the chart cluttered and hard to read. Grouping the smallest components into an "Other" category simplifies the visualization, reduces the number of required colors, and helps the audience focus on the most significant parts of the data.

## When it applies

- For part-to-whole charts like pie charts, stacked bar charts, treemaps, and area charts.
- When you have a "long tail" of categories that are individually too small to be meaningful to the main story.
- When the primary goal is to show the composition of the largest components.

## Exceptions

- When the "long tail" itself is the story (e.g., "The diversity of the market is increasing").
- In contexts where full transparency is required and every category must be reported individually, regardless of size. In this case, a bar chart sorted by value is a better choice than a pie chart.

## Trade-offs

- **Gained:** A simpler, cleaner, and more readable chart; increased focus on the most important categories.
- **Sacrificed:** The loss of detail about the individual categories that were merged together.

## Signs of Trouble

- **The Sliver Problem:** Your pie chart or stacked bar has a multitude of tiny, unreadable segments, each with its own color and a label that won't fit.
- **Legend Overrun:** The legend for your chart is longer than the chart itself because of all the small categories.

## How to Improve

- **Moderate Redesign: Consolidate the Tail.**
  1.  Decide on a threshold for inclusion (e.g., any category making up less than 2% of the total).
  2.  Create a new category called "Other." Sum the values of all categories that fall below your threshold and assign the total to the "Other" category.
  3.  In your chart, display the main categories plus your new "Other" category.
  4.  Best practice is to color the "Other" category in a neutral gray and place it last in the stacking order or at the end of a sorted list.
