## Guidance

When creating a stacked area chart, strategically order the layers so the most important category for your message, or the least volatile category, is at the bottom.

## Why

Humans can only accurately judge and compare values that share a common, straight baseline. In a stacked area chart, only the bottom-most series benefits from the stable, flat baseline of the x-axis. All other series are plotted on top of the fluctuating series below them, distorting their shape and making their true values and trends difficult to read. Placing your key series at the bottom makes its trend perceptually accurate and easy to follow.

### Core Principle

Make the most important comparison the easiest one to make. By giving the most important series a stable baseline, you are optimizing the chart for its primary purpose.

## When it applies

-   You are creating a stacked area chart.
-   There is one particular series whose trend is more important than the others.
-   Alternatively, if no series is more important, placing the largest or most stable (least volatile) series at the bottom can create a smoother baseline for the other series, slightly improving overall readability.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **Distorted Story:** The series you care most about is in the middle of the stack, and its visual shape doesn't match the story you're trying to tell from the data.
-   **Baseline Instability:** The bottom layer of your chart is a very small, volatile series, creating a "noisy" foundation for all the other categories.

## How to Improve

-   **Quick Fix:** In your charting tool, reorder the data series so that the most important one is listed first (as this usually places it at the bottom).
-   **Moderate Approach:** After reordering, use color to further emphasize the key series at the bottom. Make it a strong, saturated color while using more muted or analogous colors for the other series above it.
