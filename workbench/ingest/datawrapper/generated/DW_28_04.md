## Guidance

When visualizing binary data (e.g., yes/no, present/absent) or a small number of simple categories, consider using shades of gray (e.g., black, gray, and white) instead of distinct hues.

## Why

A grayscale palette is universally perceivable and eliminates any risk of misinterpretation due to color vision deficiency. It can also create a more sophisticated and less "busy" aesthetic, allowing other visual elements, like annotations or a single highlighted color, to stand out more effectively. It forces you to rely on brightness differences, which is a robust encoding channel.

## When it applies

- For binary data (e.g., yes/no, private/public).
- When you have only 2-3 categories that don't have strong, conventional color associations.
- As the "context" color in a chart that uses a single bright hue for highlighting.

## Exceptions

- **Branding:** If you must adhere to a brand style guide that requires specific colors.
- **Convention:** If the categories have strong, conventional color associations that aid interpretation (e.g., red/blue for political parties in the US), using grayscale might confuse the audience or strip away important context.

## Trade-offs

- You lose the ability to use color for emotional or symbolic association. A grayscale palette is neutral and "data-forward," which may not be appropriate for all communication goals.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Unnecessary Color:** Your chart uses two bright, saturated colors for a simple "before/after" comparison, making the graphic feel visually loud and distracting from the main message.
- **Accessibility Fail:** A chart uses red and green for a "pass/fail" dashboard, which is unreadable for the most common form of colorblindness. A simple light/dark gray scheme would have been instantly accessible.

## How to Improve

- **Quick Fix: Switch to Grayscale.** Change the category colors to two distinct shades of gray (e.g., dark gray and light gray, or black and light gray). Ensure they have a strong contrast ratio.
- **Moderate Approach: Use Grayscale Plus a Highlight.** If you have three categories, consider making two of them shades of gray and one of them a color to draw attention to it. This combines the clarity of grayscale with the attention-grabbing power of a highlight.
