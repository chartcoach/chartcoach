## Guidance

For a simple and highly effective two-color palette, combine blue with a complementary orange or red.

## Why

The combination of blue and orange/red leverages colors that fall on different confusion lines for all major types of colorblindness. While red-green blind individuals perceive the orange/red as a shade of olive or brown, it remains clearly distinct from blue. Similarly, blue-blind individuals see the pair as teal and pink, which are also distinct. This makes the pair a robust choice for binary or diverging data.

### Core Principle

Choose colors that are far apart in the color space for all types of color vision deficiencies.

## When it applies

-   When you need a two-color categorical palette.
-   When creating a diverging color scheme to show deviation from a central point.
-   When highlighting a specific category against a baseline.

## Exceptions

-   When brand guidelines strictly prohibit these colors. In this case, you must find another pair that has high contrast in both hue and lightness.
-   When the colors have a strong semantic meaning that conflicts with your data (e.g., using orange to represent something negative when it is culturally associated with warmth or safety).

## Trade-offs

-   This specific combination has become so common as a colorblind-safe default that it can sometimes feel unoriginal.
-   Poorly chosen shades (e.g., a very light orange and a very light blue) might lack sufficient contrast, so lightness must also be considered.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **Problematic Pairs:** Your chart relies on red vs. green, or blue vs. purple of similar lightness.
-   **Simulator Failure:** When checking your two-color palette in a colorblindness simulator, the colors merge into a similar shade.

## How to Improve

-   **Quick approach:** If you are using a problematic red-green palette, swap the green for a distinct blue.
-   **Moderate approach:** Replace your existing two-color palette with a well-tested blue and orange combination, ensuring the two colors also differ in lightness.
-   **Comprehensive approach:** Build a complete color system for your organization based on colorblind-safe principles, with the blue/orange combination as a core option for categorical data.
