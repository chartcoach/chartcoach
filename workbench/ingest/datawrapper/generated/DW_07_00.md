## Guidance

When selecting a palette, use blue as a foundational color for key categories, as it is the most reliably perceived hue across different forms of color vision deficiency.

## Why

Blue is the color that is perceived most consistently by people with normal vision and those with the most common forms of red-green colorblindness (protanopia and deuteranopia). Unlike red and green, which fall on a confusion line for these viewers, blue remains distinctly blue, making it a "safe" and predictable choice.

### Core Principle

Choose colors that are robust to perceptual shifts across different types of vision.

## When it applies

-   When choosing a single highlight color for a visualization.
-   When designing a categorical palette from scratch.
-   When you need to ensure at least one color in your chart is perceived as intended by the widest possible audience.

## Exceptions

-   When blue has a strong, conflicting semantic meaning in the context of your data (e.g., representing "cold" in a chart where that's not the intended message).
-   When designing for audiences with tritanopia (blue-yellow colorblindness), though this is much rarer. In this case, blue can be confused with teal, but it's still often a better choice than red/green combinations.

## Trade-offs

-   Over-reliance on blue can make palettes feel monotonous or generic, especially as the "blue and orange" combination has become a common default for accessibility.

## Signs of Trouble

-   **Hue Confusion:** Your chart's primary distinction relies on a green versus red comparison.
-   **Simulator Failure:** When viewed in a red-green colorblindness simulator, two key colors in your chart become nearly indistinguishable.

## How to Improve

-   **Quick approach:** If you have a problematic color pair (like red/green), swap the green for a distinct blue.
-   **Moderate approach:** Re-anchor your color palette around blue. If you need multiple colors, start with blue and choose other colors that have high contrast with it for all viewers (like a vibrant orange).
-   **Comprehensive approach:** Adopt a pre-vetted, colorblind-safe palette that uses blue as a primary color.
