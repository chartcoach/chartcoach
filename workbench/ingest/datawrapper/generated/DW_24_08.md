## Guidance

For tables with many columns, apply a subtle background color to every other row (known as "zebra striping" or "row banding").

## Why

In a wide table, it's easy for the eye to slip and jump to an adjacent row when scanning from a label on the left to a value on the far right. Zebra striping provides a persistent visual guide for each row, making it easier for the reader to stay on track and correctly associate data across the entire row.

## When it applies

- When a table has many columns, forcing the reader's eye to travel a long horizontal distance.
- When a table is dense with information.
- When you cannot reduce the number of columns.

## Exceptions

- **Narrow Tables:** Zebra striping is often unnecessary and can add distracting visual clutter to tables with only a few (2-3) columns, where the risk of row-jumping is low.
- **High-Contrast Rows:** If rows are already separated by strong grid lines or ample white space, striping may be redundant.

## Trade-offs

- Adds "non-data ink" to the table, which can be seen as visual noise if not implemented subtly. The effect must be gentle enough to guide without distracting.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Scanning Errors:** You or your users find yourselves accidentally reading data from the wrong row when looking at columns far from the row labels.
- **Eye Strain:** It feels mentally taxing to scan across a wide, dense table.

## How to Improve

- **Action:** Apply a very light grey background color (e.g., `#F5F5F5`) to every even-numbered or odd-numbered row in your table. The contrast between the striped and non-striped rows should be minimal—just enough to be perceivable.
