## Guidance
When a chart has many categories but only a few are relevant to your story, group the less important ones into a single category labeled "Other" or "All else." This "Other" category should be visually de-emphasized, typically with a neutral gray color.

## Why
Presenting too many categories at once overwhelms the viewer's working memory and makes a chart unreadable. By consolidating the "long tail" of less important data, you drastically reduce cognitive load, clean up the visual, and allow the viewer to focus their attention on comparing the few categories that matter.

### Core Principle
Respect the limits of working memory. Simplify visualizations by reducing the number of distinct elements the viewer must process and compare.

## When it applies
- When you have high-cardinality categorical data (more than 7-10 categories) and a clear focus on only a few of them.
- In part-to-whole charts like pie charts, stacked bar charts, or treemaps where the sum of parts is meaningful.
- When the individual values in the long tail are not important to the story.

## Exceptions
- **When the Long Tail is the Story:** If your narrative is specifically about the diversity or distribution of the smaller categories, grouping them would hide the key insight.
- **Lookup Tasks:** If the chart's purpose is to allow users to look up the specific value for any category, none of them should be hidden in an "Other" group.

## Trade-offs
- **Loss of Granularity:** You lose the ability to see the values and labels of the individual categories that have been grouped together. This is an editorial decision that permanently obscures that information from the viewer.

## Signs of Trouble
- **A Crowded Legend:** Your legend has a long, scrolling list of entries.
- **Illegible Labels:** There are so many slices in your pie chart or bars in your bar chart that the labels overlap or have to be omitted.
- **Rainbow Effect:** The chart is a riot of color, with a different hue for every one of a dozen or more categories, and no clear takeaway.

## How to Improve
- **Quick Fix: Manually Group in a Spreadsheet.** Before creating your chart, add a new column to your data. Use a formula (e.g., `IF`) to keep your main categories and relabel all others as "Other". Then, sum the values for this new group. Create your chart from this summarized data.
- **Moderate Approach: Set a Threshold.** Decide on a threshold (e.g., "any category under 5% of the total"). Group all categories that fall below this threshold into your "Other" group. This is a systematic way to simplify the data.
- **Comprehensive Approach: Interactive Grouping.** In an interactive visualization, you could show all categories by default but provide a control (e.g., a checkbox) that allows the user to "Group smaller categories." This offers the user both the detailed view and the simplified summary.
