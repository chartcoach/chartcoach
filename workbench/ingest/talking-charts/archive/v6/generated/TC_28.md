## Guidance

Be conscious of your design workflow: in a narrative-driven setting, ensure the visualization reinforces a known story; in an exploratory setting, remain open to evolving the visual message as you analyze the data.

## Why

The purpose of a visualization dictates the ideal workflow. If you are illustrating a known conclusion (e.g., from a finished report), your process should be focused on finding the clearest way to express that message. This is a "narrative-first" or communicative workflow. If you are exploring a new dataset to find insights, your process should be flexible and iterative, allowing the story to emerge from the data. This is an "exploratory" workflow. Confusing the two—such as forcing a preconceived narrative onto new data—can lead to biased analysis and weak storytelling.

### Core Principle

Know whether you are illustrating or investigating. Your process should change depending on your goal.

## When it applies

- At the beginning of any data visualization project.
- When working with a new or unfamiliar dataset.
- When collaborating in a team to define the project's goals.

## Exceptions

- Many projects are a hybrid. You might start with a loose hypothesis (narrative-first) but then explore the data to refine or even contradict that hypothesis (exploratory). Being able to switch between these modes is a key skill.

## Trade-offs

- **Efficiency vs. Discovery:** A narrative-first process is generally more efficient, as the goal is clear from the start. An exploratory process takes more time and may lead to dead ends, but it's also where unexpected discoveries are made.
- **Objectivity vs. Story:** A purely exploratory process is less biased, but it may not yield a clear or compelling story. A narrative-first process produces a clear story but risks confirmation bias.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Confirmation Bias:** In an exploratory project, you stop analyzing as soon as you find a chart that confirms your initial hypothesis, without looking for contradictory evidence.
- **Aimless Exploration:** You spend weeks creating endless charts from a dataset without ever converging on a core message or story.
- **Weak Story:** In a narrative-first project, the final visualization feels disconnected from the intended message because the data doesn't actually support it well.

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach:** At the project kickoff, explicitly state the goal: "Our goal is to find the clearest way to show Fact X" (narrative-first) or "Our goal is to explore this dataset to find out what's interesting" (exploratory).

- **Moderate approach:** If exploring, time-box your initial analysis. Give yourself a set amount of time for open-ended exploration before you must converge on 2-3 potential stories to develop further. If illustrating, start by creating a "bad" version of the chart that shows the opposite of your message to understand how the data could be interpreted differently.

- **Comprehensive approach:** Adopt a structured, dual-process workflow. For example, always begin with a defined period of open-ended exploratory analysis (e.g., using a tool like Tableau or Python) to identify potential stories. Once a story is selected, switch to a narrative-first production workflow (e.g., using a tool like Adobe Illustrator or D3.js) to craft the final, polished visualization.
