## Guidance

Before creating a data visualization, consider whether it is the most effective visual format for the story, or if an illustration, photograph, or even just text might work better.

## Why

A data visualization is a tool, not a universal solution. Not every story with numbers in it needs a chart. Sometimes, a single, powerful number in large type is more impactful. A story about an individual's experience might be better told with a photograph. A conceptual story might be clearer with an illustration. Choosing the right format depends on the core message and the communicative goal. Using a data visualization where it doesn't add value can distract from the story or feel forced.

### Core Principle

Use the right tool for the job. The goal is to tell a story effectively, not just to make a chart.

## When it applies

- At the very beginning of the storytelling process, before any design work has begun.
- When the data is very simple (e.g., just one or two numbers).
- When the story is more about a concept, a process, or a human experience than about quantitative comparison.

## Exceptions

- In contexts where charts are the expected medium of communication (e.g., a financial report, a scientific dashboard), replacing them with other formats may violate audience expectations.

## Trade-offs

- **Data-centrism vs. Narrative:** Insisting on a chart might put the focus on the numbers, whereas a photo might better center the human element of the story.
- **Effort:** Creating a bespoke illustration can be more time-consuming than generating a standard chart.

## Signs of Trouble

- **The "Chart for Chart's Sake":** A chart exists simply because there was data available, but it doesn't add any new insight or clarity to the story.
- **The "One-Number Chart":** A bar chart is used to display a single number, when just showing the number itself in a large font would be more direct and powerful.
- **The "Text is Clearer" problem:** It takes longer to understand the chart than it would to read the same information in a simple sentence.
- **Forcing a narrative:** The data is shoehorned into a visual format that doesn't fit, resulting in a confusing or weak graphic.

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach:** Ask this question: "If I remove this chart, is the story weaker?" If the answer is no, then the chart is likely unnecessary.

- **Moderate approach:** For a simple data point, try mocking up three versions: a simple bar chart, the number presented as a large "call-out" statistic, and the number used in a well-crafted sentence. Compare them and see which is most effective.

- **Comprehensive approach:** At the start of a project, hold a story planning session with writers, editors, photographers, and data visualization designers. Collectively decide on the best medium or combination of media to tell the story, rather than defaulting to a specific format based on job roles.
