## Guidance

When appropriate, frame your visualization to show real-world consequences or potential actions rather than just abstract data.

## Why

Presenting data, especially about problems like climate change, can leave viewers feeling helpless or overwhelmed. Viewers are more engaged and motivated when they understand how the data relates to tangible consequences or what actions could influence the outcome. Framing a visualization around solutions or showing how different choices lead to different futures makes the data feel more tangible and empowers the audience.

### Core Principle

Don't just show the problem; illuminate the path forward. Data is more powerful when it informs action.

## When it applies

- When visualizing data about societal or environmental problems (e.g., climate, health, inequality).
- When the goal is not just to inform, but also to persuade or motivate.
- When presenting future scenarios or projections.

## Exceptions

- In a purely diagnostic or analytical context, the goal might be simply to identify a problem, not to propose a solution.
- A heavy-handed focus on solutions can be perceived as advocacy, which may not be appropriate for all publication contexts (e.g., a neutral scientific journal).

## Trade-offs

- **Objectivity vs. Advocacy:** A solution-oriented frame is an explicit rhetorical choice and moves the visualization away from a neutral stance.
- **Complexity:** Visualizing potential actions and their consequences can be more complex than simply showing the existing data.

## Signs of Trouble

- **"Doom and Gloom":** The visualization effectively communicates a problem but leaves the viewer feeling hopeless or fatalistic.
- **"Okay, now what?":** The audience understands the data but is left with no sense of what they or others can do about it.
- **Abstract presentation:** The data is completely disconnected from any real-world impact or consequence.

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach:** Reframe your title or caption to be solution-oriented. Instead of "Global Emissions Are Rising," try "Rapid Shift to Renewables Could Still Meet Climate Goals."

- **Moderate approach:** Visualize two scenarios side-by-side: a "business-as-usual" path and an "action-taken" path. This visually demonstrates the impact of potential choices.

- **Comprehensive approach:** Create an interactive "explorable explanation" or a simulator where users can adjust variables (e.g., policy choices, personal actions) and see the resulting impact on the data. This directly connects actions to outcomes.
