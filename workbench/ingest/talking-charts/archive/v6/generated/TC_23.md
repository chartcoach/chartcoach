## Guidance

Make data feel more tangible and meaningful by connecting it to familiar, everyday experiences and personal relevance.

## Why

Abstract numbers and large-scale trends can feel distant and impersonal, causing viewers to disengage. When a visualization connects data to something the viewer knows—their local area, a memorable event, or a daily routine—it becomes personally relevant. This connection fosters emotional resonance and motivation, transforming the data from a dry fact into a meaningful story.

### Core Principle

Data has a human scale. Translate abstract quantities into terms that resonate with personal experience.

## When it applies

- When communicating about large-scale issues like climate change, public health, or the economy.
- When trying to motivate an audience to care about a topic or take action.
- When visualizing data for a specific geographic community.

## Exceptions

- In a purely scientific or technical context, emotional resonance and personal connection may not be the primary goal; analytical clarity is.
- Personalizing data too much can sometimes distract from the broader message if viewers focus only on their own situation.

## Trade-offs

- **Focus:** Highlighting personal or local relevance can sometimes draw attention away from the larger, systemic picture.
- **Universality:** A reference that is familiar to one audience may be meaningless to another.

## Signs of Trouble

- **"This doesn't affect me":** Viewers see the data as abstract and irrelevant to their own lives.
- **The "So What?" reaction:** The chart is factually correct but fails to evoke any sense of interest or concern.
- **Abstract units:** The data is presented in units that have no tangible meaning to a layperson (e.g., "gigatons of carbon").

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach:** Use an analogy in the title or caption to translate an abstract number into a familiar scale. For example, instead of "X square kilometers of deforestation," try "An area of forest the size of 3 football fields is lost every minute."

- **Moderate approach:** If possible, present the data at a local scale. Instead of a national or global map, show a map of the viewer's city or region. Use annotations to connect data points to well-known local events or landmarks.

- **Comprehensive approach:** Create a personalized or interactive visualization. For example, a "data calculator" that allows users to input information about their own lives (e.g., their location, their age) and see how the data relates to them directly.
