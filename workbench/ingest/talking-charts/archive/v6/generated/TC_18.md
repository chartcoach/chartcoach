## Guidance

For time-based data, use familiar formats like timelines and line charts with a clear, consistent time axis to help viewers understand sequences and trends.

## Why

Humans naturally understand time as a linear progression. Visualizations that align with this mental model—such as a line chart with time on the horizontal axis or a simple vertical timeline—are highly intuitive. Cluttered designs or charts that use inconsistent or confusing timescales can break this model, making it difficult for viewers to trace patterns, understand causality, or make sense of temporal data.

### Core principle

Align your visual representation of time with the way people intuitively understand it: as a consistent, linear sequence.

## When it applies

- When showing data that changes over time (e.g., stock prices, temperature, website traffic).
- When telling a story that has a chronological sequence of events.
- When you want to show a trend, seasonality, or a pattern of growth or decline.

## Exceptions

- For cyclical data (e.g., patterns within a 24-hour day or a calendar year), a radial or spiral chart might be more effective at showing the repeating pattern than a straight line. However, these are less familiar and require more explanation.

## Trade-offs

- A very long time series can take up a lot of horizontal space.
- Showing multiple time series with very different scales on the same chart can be challenging and may require dual axes, which are often confusing to readers.

## Signs of Trouble

- **The broken timeline:** The time axis is not continuous, has unexplained gaps, or uses an inconsistent scale (e.g., switching from years to months partway through).
- **Time as a circle:** Using a radial format for data that is not inherently cyclical, which can confuse viewers.
- **Interpretation difficulty:** Viewers struggle to answer simple questions like "What happened first?" or "Is this value going up or down?"
- **Cluttered design:** So many events are packed onto a timeline that it becomes unreadable.

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach:** Ensure your time axis is clearly labeled with units and runs from left to right (or top to bottom) in a consistent, unbroken sequence.

- **Moderate approach:** If the timeline is cluttered, break it into logical sections or use annotations to call out the most important events, de-emphasizing the rest. For a line chart with many lines, highlight the most important one and show the others in gray.

- **Comprehensive approach:** If the story involves multiple timescales or a very long period, consider an interactive approach. Show a high-level overview first, and then allow users to "zoom in" to a specific period for more detail. For storytelling, consider an animated sequence that builds the timeline step-by-step.
