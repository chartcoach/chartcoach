## Guidance

Iterate on the visualization design with input from different kinds of experts, such as domain specialists, communication experts, and data scientists.

## Why

Creating an effective visualization requires balancing multiple competing concerns: data accuracy, perceptual clarity, narrative coherence, and domain-specific nuance. It is rare for one person to have all the necessary expertise. A collaborative design process that integrates different perspectives—the domain expert who understands the data's meaning, the designer who understands visual perception, and the communicator who understands the audience—leads to a more robust and effective final product.

### Core Principle

Many eyes make a better chart. Diverse expertise uncovers blind spots.

## When it applies

- In any project where accuracy and clarity are critical.
- When working with complex, specialized, or sensitive data.
- Throughout the design process, from low-fidelity sketches to high-fidelity prototypes.

## Exceptions

- For very simple, low-stakes visualizations, a full collaborative review process may be overkill.
- In environments with extreme time constraints, extensive collaboration may not be feasible, but even a quick check from a colleague is better than none.

## Trade-offs

- **Time:** A collaborative and iterative process is more time-consuming than working alone.
- **"Too many cooks":** Without a clear decision-maker, conflicting feedback from different experts can lead to a muddled or compromised design.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Siloed work:** The data analyst, the designer, and the subject matter expert all work on their part of the project in isolation.
- **Late-stage feedback:** Feedback is only solicited after the visualization is completely finished, making changes difficult and costly.
- **Single point of failure:** The entire project's accuracy and clarity rest on the shoulders of a single person.
- **Shift in preference:** A design that was loved as a low-fidelity sketch is rejected as a high-fidelity prototype, indicating a lack of iterative feedback.

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach:** Before publishing, have a colleague from a different team or with a different skill set do a quick "sanity check" of your visualization.

- **Moderate approach:** Institute a formal, multi-stage review process. Have a domain expert review for content accuracy, a data visualization expert review for perceptual effectiveness, and an editor review for narrative clarity.

- **Comprehensive approach:** Adopt a co-design methodology from the start. Form a small, cross-functional team (e.g., a scientist, a designer, a writer) that works together from initial brainstorming through to final production. Use shared documents and frequent, informal check-ins to facilitate continuous collaboration.
