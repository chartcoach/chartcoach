## Guidance

Avoid assuming your audience is familiar with the topic, including domain-specific terms and geography. Provide clear labels and explanations.

## Why

Not all viewers share the same background knowledge. If a visualization requires prior familiarity with a concept (like the "gender pay gap"), a technical term, or geography, it can exclude a large portion of the audience and lead to misinterpretation or disengagement. Viewers who lack context may feel self-doubt or simply give up. Providing clear definitions and orientation cues makes your visualization more inclusive and accessible.

### Core Principle

Provide the necessary context within the visualization. Don't assume your viewer has read the background material or shares your expertise.

## When it applies

- When visualizing data on a specialized or technical topic for a general audience.
- When using acronyms or domain-specific terminology.
- When creating maps or charts that refer to geographic locations that may not be universally known.
- Even with seemingly common topics, as familiarity can vary widely.

## Exceptions

- When creating a visualization for a small, internal group of domain experts who all share a deep, common understanding of the topic and its terminology.

## Trade-offs

- **Brevity vs. Explanation:** Adding definitions and explanations takes up space and can make a visualization more text-heavy.
- **Pacing:** Explaining basic concepts may feel slow or redundant to expert viewers.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Use of unexplained jargon:** The chart or its labels use terms like "YoY growth," "EBITDA," or "RCP 8.5" without defining them.
- **Geographic ambiguity:** A map shows data for a region but doesn't include labels for countries or major cities to orient the viewer.
- **"What does this term mean?":** Viewers ask for definitions of key terms used in the chart.
- **Misinterpretation due to concept:** Viewers understand the chart's mechanics but draw the wrong conclusion because they misunderstand the underlying concept being measured.

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach:** Spell out acronyms and add a brief, parenthetical definition for any technical term. For example, "Gender Pay Gap (the difference in average earnings between men and women)."

- **Moderate approach:** On a map, ensure major cities, countries, or landmarks are labeled to provide orientation. Use a tooltip or a dedicated text box to provide a concise definition of the main concept being visualized.

- **Comprehensive approach:** Create a layered or "scrollytelling" piece that starts by explaining the core concept in simple terms before introducing the data. This "on-ramping" prepares the viewer to understand the visualization correctly.
