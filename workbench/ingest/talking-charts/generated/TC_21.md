## Guidance

When you have knowledge of your audience's specific needs, preferences, or common points of confusion, tailor the content and design accordingly.

## Why

A generic, one-size-fits-all approach often fails to connect with specific audience groups. Different cultures may have different color associations, and certain concepts like large numbers, probabilities, or dual-axis charts are notoriously difficult for lay audiences to grasp. By being mindful of these potential pitfalls and adapting your design—for example, by changing reading direction, testing color palettes, or finding ways to make large numbers more concrete—you can create a more effective and inclusive visualization.

### Core Principle

Effective communication is empathetic. It anticipates and addresses the specific needs and challenges of its audience.

## When it applies

- When designing for a specific cultural or linguistic group (e.g., considering reading direction, color symbolism).
- When visualizing very large or very small numbers, percentages, or probabilities for a general audience.
- When you have access to user feedback or analytics that reveal common points of confusion for your audience.

## Exceptions

- If you are designing for a completely unknown, global audience, it can be difficult to tailor content. In this case, sticking to the most universal and simple conventions is the safest approach.

## Trade-offs

- **Effort:** Tailoring content for multiple different audiences requires significant extra work.
- **Generalizability:** A design hyper-tailored to one group may not work as well for another.

## Signs of Trouble

- **Confusing Concepts:** The chart relies on viewers intuitively understanding a difficult concept, like what a "billion" or a "2% chance" really means.
- **Dual-Axis Confusion:** A chart with two y-axes causes viewers to compare the wrong lines or misinterpret scales.
- **Cultural Misreadings:** A color choice has an unintended negative connotation for a specific cultural group in your audience.
- **One-size-fits-all:** The same visualization is used for audiences with vastly different needs and backgrounds without any modification.

## How to Improve

- **Quick approach:** Avoid dual-axis charts; create two separate, single-axis charts instead. To make large numbers concrete, use relatable analogies (e.g., "One billion seconds is nearly 32 years").

- **Moderate approach:** When designing for a specific group, research their cultural conventions regarding color and layout (e.g., reading direction). If possible, get feedback from a member of that group.

- **Comprehensive approach:** Develop audience personas that document the needs, preferences, and literacy levels of your key user groups. Use these personas to guide the creation of tailored versions of your visualizations for each group.
